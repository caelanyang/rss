# David Sedaris: Lessons From Decades of Writing

David Sedaris has long been one of the funniest writers in the game, and he's got a method for how he does it. It starts with observation, always looking for what's surprising, ridiculous, or a little bit strange about the world. Then, every single day, he writes the best ideas down in a journal.

> 大卫·塞达里斯一直是业内最幽默的作家之一，他有一套独特的创作方法。首先是观察，他总是寻找生活中那些令人惊讶、荒谬或略显怪异的事物。然后，他每天都会把最好的想法记在日记里。

But there's also a willingness to put himself in absurd situations, like working as a Christmas elf at Macy's or cleaning up litter off English country roads for eight hours a day. He's sold more than 16 million books, and he's been writing for The New Yorker for more than 30 years.

> 但他也有乐于让自己置身于荒诞情境的倾向，比如在梅西百货扮演圣诞精灵，或者每天在英国乡村道路上清理八小时垃圾。他的作品销量超过1600万册，为《纽约客》撰稿也超过30年。

On writing without publishing: Sedaris started keeping a diary when he was 20, but didn't feel pressure to publish. He says there's a fundamental difference between writing and publishing. He never felt embarrassed to have a day job. His advice to young writers: you're 20 and this is your time to suck. Take chances and learn—don't think about publishing.

> 关于不发表作品的写作：塞达里斯20岁就开始写日记，但他并不觉得有发表的压力。他认为写作和发表之间存在着根本的区别。他从不为自己有份正职工作而感到尴尬。他对年轻作家的建议是：你才20岁，这是你尽情发挥、尝试的时期。抓住机会，不断学习——别想着发表。

On finding premises: The best essays come from noticing ridiculous things about yourself. His famous Anne Frank piece came from literally viewing her apartment like real estate—thinking "we should move the kitchen." The only question was how to handle it without being disrespectful. He found a way to be honest about his own absurd reaction.

> 关于寻找切入点：最好的文章往往源于对自身荒谬之处的觉察。他那篇著名的《安妮·弗兰克》文章，灵感就来源于他像看房地产一样审视安妮的公寓——心想“我们应该把厨房搬走”。唯一的问题是如何既不失礼又能表达这种想法。他找到了一种坦诚面对自己这种荒诞反应的方式。

On reading out loud: Sedaris reads his work to live audiences, and they are his real editors. He can feel when an audience drifts, when they're puzzled, when a joke lands. He even asked audiences to correct him on factual errors. He gets much better feedback than from reading alone to himself—audiences "pay to tell me everything I need to know."

> 关于朗读：塞达里斯会现场朗读自己的作品，而观众就是他真正的编辑。他能感受到观众何时走神，何时感到困惑，何时笑点奏效。他甚至会请观众纠正他作品中的事实错误。他从观众那里得到的反馈远比自己独自朗读要好得多——观众“花钱就是为了告诉我所有我需要知道的信息”。

On his writing method: He keeps a diary guide. When something happens that might be useful later, he notes it. He looks for absurd juxtapositions that give texture. He reads things aloud to hear the rhythm. Good writing has variety in voice and texture—even speaking a different language in an essay gives it texture.

> 关于他的写作方法：他会记日记。如果发生什么事，觉得以后可能会用得上，他就会记下来。他会寻找那些能丰富文章层次的、看似荒诞的并置。他会朗读文章，感受其中的韵律。好的文章应该在语调和质感上有所变化——甚至在文章中使用不同的语言也能增添文章的质感。

On specific details: "Good people vs good characters." Specific, strange details make writing memorable. Choosing funnier words. Stretching the story just a little. Finding the premise is the easy part—the challenge is finding the right details to make it work.

> 关于具体细节：“好人 vs 好角色”。具体而奇特的细节能让作品令人难忘。选择更幽默的词语。稍微扩展一下故事。找到故事的前提很容易——真正的挑战在于找到合适的细节让它成立。

On improvement: "Stop trying so hard" and "Be a great observer" are more important than technical skill. The best improvement comes from finding your unique voice through observation and putting yourself in situations most people avoid.

> 关于提升：“别太用力”和“善于观察”比技术更重要。最好的提升来自于通过观察找到自己独特的表达方式，并让自己置身于大多数人避之不及的环境中。
