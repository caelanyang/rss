Personal phones

It was illegal, from about 2021 through about 2025, for employees of financial 
firms to text or WhatsApp about work on their personal phones. The theory was 
thatUS securities regulations <https://bloom.bg/3TNzM8o> require firms to keep 
copies of “all communications sent” by their employees, “including inter-office 
memoranda and communications,” that are “related to [their] business as such.” 
So if a financial services employee sends an email to a client, or to a 
colleague, her firm needs to retain a copy of that email. And if she texts a 
client or colleague from her personal phone, then the firm won’t have a copy, 
which violates the law.


> **译文：** 从2021年到2025年左右，金融公司员工使用个人手机发送工作相关的短信或WhatsApp消息是违法的。其依据是美国证券法规<https://bloom.bg/3TNzM8o>要求公司保留员工发送的所有通信记录，包括“与公司业务相关的内部备忘录和通信”。

因此，如果一名金融服务员工向客户或同事发送电子邮件，公司需要保留该邮件的副本。但如果她使用个人手机向客户或同事发送短信，公司则无法保留副本，这违反了法律。

351 ETFs

An accidental but important feature of the US tax code is that exchange-traded 
funds don’t pay taxes when they sell stock. If you buy some stock for $100 and 
sell it a few years later for $500, you have $400 of capital gains and payabout 
$80 <https://bloom.bg/4lj6Pdn> of taxes. If you are an investor in a mutual 
fund, and the mutual fund buys some stock for $100 and sells it for $500, you 
have $400 of capital gains and pay about $80 of taxes: The mutual fund’s 
taxable transactions are passed on to you. [2]  <>


> **译文：** 美国税法中一个偶然但却重要的特点是，交易所交易基金（ETF）在出售股票时无需缴税。如果您以 100 美元的价格购买股票，几年后以 500 美元的价格出售，您将获得 400 美元的资本利得，并缴纳约 80 美元的税款。如果您是共同基金的投资者，而该共同基金以 100 美元的价格购买股票，并以 500 美元的价格出售，您也将获得 400 美元的资本利得，并缴纳约 80 美元的税款：共同基金的应税交易将转嫁给您。[2]


If you are an investor in an ETF, though, and the ETF buys some stock for $100 
and sells it for $500, you pay no taxes. Instead, when you eventually sell your 
shares in the ETF, you pay taxes on all of the gains that have accumulated in 
the ETF. But that tax deferral is quite valuable: Paying $80 in 20 years is 
better than paying $80 now, and if you die and leave your appreciated ETF 
shares to your heirs, thennobody pays the tax at all <https://bloom.bg/4wjVmjr>.


> **译文：** 如果您是ETF的投资者，而该ETF以100美元的价格买入股票，

然后以500美元的价格卖出，您无需缴纳任何税款。相反，当您最终出售ETF份额时，

您需要为ETF中累积的所有收益缴纳税款。但这种税收递延非常有利：20年后缴纳80美元总比现在缴纳80美元要好，

而且如果您去世并将增值后的ETF份额留给继承人，

那么任何人都无需缴纳任何税款。<https://bloom.bg/4wjVmjr>


Like I said, this is apparently an accident — it doesn’t seem as if Congress 
wrote the tax code quite intending this result — but it is a real and, at this 
point, quite beloved feature of ETFs. ETFs, Ioften <https://bloom.bg/4vJ0SLi> 
write <https://bloom.bg/3RqRTQU>, just are mutual funds that don’t pay taxes. 
There is not, as far as I can tell, much desire in government tochange it. ETFs 
are popular <https://bloom.bg/4vU4udG>, people like the fact that they don't 
have to pay taxes, it’s fine.


> **译文：** 正如我所说，这显然是个意外——国会似乎并非有意制定税法来达到这个目的——但这确实是ETF的一个真实且目前颇受欢迎的特点。ETF，正如我常说的，就是不用缴税的共同基金。

据我所知，政府并没有太多改变现状的意愿。ETF很受欢迎，人们喜欢不用缴税这一点，这很好。


It’s accidental from Congress’s perspective, but it is not accidental from the 
financial industry’s perspective. Quite a lot of work goes on beneath the 
surface of the simple story that I have laid out. The tax code doesn’t say 
“ETFs can sell stocks without triggering taxes.” What it says is thatin-kind 
creation and redemption of ETF sharesare not taxable transactions. Most stock 
ETFs have in-kind creation and redemption: A trading firm can acquire a basket 
of S&P 500 stocks and deliver them to an S&P 500 ETF to get back newly created 
shares of that ETF, or it can deliver shares of the ETF and redeem them for a 
basket of the underlying stocks. Because the ETF neversells the underlying 
stock — it delivers it in an in-kind exchange for its own shares — it doesn’t 
have to pay taxes; the in-kind exchange is not a taxable event. [3]  <>


> **译文：** 从国会的角度来看，这纯属偶然，但从金融行业的角度来看，这并非偶然。在我阐述的这个简单故事背后，隐藏着许多运作。税法并没有规定“ETF可以出售股票而无需缴税”。它规定的是，ETF份额的实物创建和赎回并非应税交易。大多数股票ETF都采用实物创建和赎回：交易公司可以购买一篮子标普500指数成分股，并将其交付给标普500指数ETF，以换取该ETF新创建的份额；或者，它可以交付ETF份额，并将其赎回为一篮子标的股票。由于ETF从未出售标的股票——它只是以实物交换的方式将其交付给自己的份额——因此无需缴税；实物交换并非应税事件。[3]


The ETF industry figured out that it could extend this logic to cover, not 
just inflows and outflows, but alsotrades. If an ETF wants to sell its ABC 
shares and buy XYZ shares, it won’t do that for cash. Instead, it will ask a 
trading firm to (1) hand it the XYZ shares in a “custom creation” transaction 
(trading firm gives ETF XYZ shares and gets back ETF shares) and (2) take back 
the ABC shares in a “custom redemption” transaction (trading firm gives ETF 
back its own shares and gets back ABC shares). The trading firm does the cash 
trading, but the ETF doesn’t, so it has no taxable transactions. These trades 
are often called “heartbeats <https://bloom.bg/4wVxrXg>,” because from the 
outside they look like money flowing into the ETF and quickly flowing back out 
again.


> **译文：** ETF行业发现，这种逻辑不仅可以用于资金流入和流出，还可以用于交易。如果一只ETF想要卖出其持有的ABC股票并买入XYZ股票，它不会直接用现金交易。相反，它会委托一家交易公司进行以下操作：(1) 通过“定制创建”交易将XYZ股票交给它（交易公司向ETF提供XYZ股票，并收回其持有的ETF股票）；(2) 通过“定制赎回”交易收回其持有的ABC股票（交易公司向ETF返还其持有的股票，并收回其持有的ABC股票）。交易公司进行现金交易，而ETF本身不进行现金交易，因此ETF没有应税交易。这些交易通常被称为“心跳交易”，因为从外部来看，它们就像资金流入ETF，然后迅速流出。


All of this suggests a question: Why should you ever pay taxes when you sell 
stock? ETF technology exists, and it is sort of backward and old-fashioned not 
to use it to defer taxes. Conceptually,what you should do 
<https://bloom.bg/3TxVoFH> is:


> **译文：** 这一切都引出一个问题：为什么卖股票还要缴税？ETF技术已经存在，不用它来延缓纳税未免有些落后和过时。从概念上讲，你应该这样做：

<https://bloom.bg/3TxVoFH>


 * Get yourself an ETF. 
 * Do an in-kind creation transaction, where you contribute your stock 
portfolio to the ETF. 
 * Now, instead of owning a bunch of stocks, you own one ETF, which owns the 
bunch of stocks.This exchange was a tax-free transaction: You contributed your 
stock portfolio and got back ETF shares, but it all happened in-kind, so you 
don’t pay any taxes. (It is called a 351 exchange, after thesection of the tax 
code <https://bloom.bg/4x2uNzj> that says it’s not taxable.) 
 * Now, if you want to sell one stock and buy another stock, you have the ETF 
do it. Or, rather, you have the ETF do a heartbeat transaction in which it gets 
rid of the old stock and acquires the new stock in in-kind redemptions and 
creations, so you don’t pay any taxes. 
 * You manage your portfolio however you want: You sell stocks you don’t like 
and buy stocks you do, you sell down concentrated appreciated positions and 
diversify, whatever, all without paying taxes, because all of it happens in an 
ETF. 
 * If you need money — not to buy stocks, but to buy houses or groceries or 
whatever — you sell some sharesof the ETF, which does trigger capital gains 
taxes. But you pay taxes only when you take money out for consumption, not when 
you rebalance your portfolio. 
 * Or not. Or you borrow against your portfolio <https://bloom.bg/4wlalJU> (of 
ETF shares) to fund your consumption, never sell your shares, never pay taxes, 
and leave the ETF shares to your heirs with a basis step-up. 
This is not tax or legal or investing advice, and there are some important 
nuances. I am casually assuming that you run the ETF, for instance, but in 
practice you’ll probably have some professional ETF manager running the ETF and 
doing the diversification. You’ll probably need a pretty big portfolio to get 
yourself an ETF; no one will do this for a $10,000 account. Also, Step 2 — 
transmuting your own portfolio into an ETF in a 351 exchange — is subject to 
some restrictions: You have tostart with a somewhat diversified portfolio; you 
can’t just plop one giant appreciated stock position into an ETF and then 
heartbeat it away into a diversified S&P 500 fund. [4]  <> This is a real 
restriction, as the most obvious use case for this technology is to diversify 
away your giant portfolio of low-basis appreciated stock that you got for 
founding a successful company. Still, if you’ve also been investing over the 
years, you might be able to create your personal ETF and then use it to 
diversify your holdings and trade tax-free.
