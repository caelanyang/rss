Artificial intelligence is forcing mathematicians to ask a surprisingly fundamental question: What is the human mind for?

> 人工智能正迫使数学家们提出一个令人惊讶的根本性问题：人类的思维是做什么用的？

Our latest AI feature, by freelance science writer Benjamin Skuse, explores a debate now dividing the math community. As AI systems become capable of tackling more of the discipline's technical work, mathematicians are reconsidering where people add value. Will the future belong to mathematicians whose greatest contribution is no longer producing proofs, but deciding which problems are worth solving?

> 我们最新一篇由自由科学撰稿人本杰明·斯库斯撰写的AI专题文章，探讨了目前在数学界引发的一场争论。随着人工智能系统能够处理更多数学领域的技术性工作，数学家们正在重新思考人类的价值所在。未来是否属于那些最大贡献不再是证明数学理论，而是决定哪些问题值得解决的数学家呢？

Ben, who has been following the recent surge of activity in the world of AI for math, describes three broad schools of thought. Some mathematicians welcome AI systems that can solve longstanding problems, even if humans can't fully understand the solutions. Others argue that mathematics derives its beauty from the human struggle to discover those answers, and view AI as merely a tool. A third group, led by figures like Terence Tao, sees AI as a collaborator that could expand the scale and ambition of mathematical research.

> 本一直关注着人工智能在数学领域的蓬勃发展，他总结出了三种主要的观点。一些数学家欢迎能够解决长期难题的人工智能系统，即使人类无法完全理解其解决方案。另一些数学家则认为，数学之美源于人类探索答案的过程，并将人工智能仅仅视为一种工具。第三类观点，以陶哲轩为代表，则将人工智能视为一种合作者，能够拓展数学研究的规模和目标。

The question reaches far beyond mathematics. As AI becomes a more adept collaborator in many fields, we're all renegotiating our roles. As Ben told me in an email, his own answer is clear: "I would rather we never obtain answers to the big questions if it's not us doing the math, at least in part." Otherwise, he says, "we have failed as a species to protect and nurture our inquisitive nature." — Eliza Strickland, AI editor

> 这个问题远不止数学那么简单。随着人工智能在许多领域成为更得力的合作伙伴，我们都在重新审视自己的角色。正如本在邮件中告诉我的，他的答案很明确：“如果不是我们自己进行数学运算，哪怕只是一部分，我宁愿我们永远无法找到那些重大问题的答案。” 否则，他说，“作为人类，我们未能保护和培养我们与生俱来的好奇心。”——伊莉莎·斯特里克兰，人工智能编辑
