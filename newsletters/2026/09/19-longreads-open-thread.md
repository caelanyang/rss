* Jeremy Stern profiles Mark Zuckerberg for Colossus . This is written in a deliberately gonzo style, where it's partly a profile and partly a first-person narrative about what it's like to profile Mark Zuckerberg. Which is a pretty good format: there are usually layers of PR people between a journalist and a rich or powerful subject, so a story that talks about the process of getting the story is more revealing than one that just shares the output. It's kind of like a Penn & Teller routine, where you're supposed to look for the ways you're being misdirected.

> **译文：** * Jeremy Stern为Colossus撰写了Mark Zuckerberg的人物特写。这篇文章刻意采用了“刚佐新闻”风格，一部分是人物特写，一部分是第一人称叙述，讲述为Mark Zuckerberg做特写是什么体验。这其实是个很好的格式：记者和富有或有权势的采访对象之间通常隔着层层公关人员，所以一篇讲述“获取故事的过程”的报道，比只分享最终产出的报道更能揭示真相。这有点像Penn & Teller的魔术表演，你应该去寻找那些你被误导的地方。

In addition to being a kind of postmodern piece that is profiling the concept of a CEO profile, it has some good details on Zuck, like his habit of level-skipping: asking his direct reports' direct reports for data to get a clearer look at what's going on. Which itself is a kind of media commentary of the sort that the article engages in: there are many skills that help executives rise in the ranks of big tech companies, and one of those is pairing flattering narratives with numbers that seem to confirm them.

> **译文：** 除了是一篇后现代式的、对“CEO特写”这一概念本身进行特写的文章之外，它还有一些关于Zuck的好细节，比如他“跳过层级”的习惯：向直接下属的直接下属索要数据，以便更清楚地了解正在发生的事情。这本身就是文章所从事的那种媒体评论：有许多技能能帮助高管在大型科技公司中晋升，其中之一就是将讨好的叙事与看似能证实这些叙事的数字配对。

So, of course, you want to periodically go one level down and talk to people who haven't perfected that skill.

> **译文：** 所以，你自然想定期往下跳一级，和那些还没把这套技能练到炉火纯青的人聊聊。

* Daniel Frank in Not Not Talmud on how world-historically bizarre Papua New Guinea is : it's a civilization of millions of people that had minimal direct contact with the outside world until 1930, due to a mix of historical contingencies. This was anecdotally true when Australians looking for gold mines explored what they believed to be the uninhabited/able interior of New Guinea, and has since been confirmed by genetic analyses: it's a unique population that was cut off from the rest of the world for an extraordinarily long time.

> **译文：** * Daniel Frank在Not Not Talmud上撰文，讲述巴布亚新几内亚在世界历史上有多么奇异：这是一个拥有数百万人口的文明，由于一系列历史偶然因素的交织，直到1930年才与外部世界有了极少的直接接触。当澳大利亚人寻找金矿、探索他们以为无人居住/无法居住的新几内亚内陆时，这一点在轶事层面得到了印证，此后基因分析也证实了这一点：这是一个独特的群体，与世界的其他部分隔绝了极其漫长的时间。

Many fun details here: their social norm was that the highest status accrued to whoever could give the biggest gifts without being fully reciprocated, so they had implicitly implemented a very progressive tax system that prevented people from accumulating meaningful wealth. And they had an economy, whose currency was shells—very valuable for people who are separated from the beach by mostly-impassable terrain, but very cheap to someone who has quinine and wants to do business. This is a great first contact story, and presumably the last one we'll have for a long time.

> **译文：** 这里有许多有趣的细节：他们的社会规范是，最高地位归属于那些能送出最大礼物而无需获得完全对等回报的人，因此他们实际上隐含地实施了一套非常进步的税收制度，阻止人们积累有意义的财富。他们有一个经济体，货币是贝壳——对于被几乎无法通行的地形与海滩隔开的人来说，贝壳非常珍贵，但对于拥有奎宁并想做生意的人来说，贝壳非常便宜。这是一个伟大的“首次接触”故事，大概也是我们很长一段时间内会有的最后一个。

* Tyler Cowen interviews Annie Lowrey on the "Time Tax," i.e. the amount of time people spend filling out forms, waiting for bureaucracies, etc. . The Diff has covered this phenomenon before, arguing that filling out forms is corvée labor , and this remains the party line: it is simply ridiculous that a ruinously expensive policy can be budget-neutral if the expense consists of wasted time rather than wasted money. But that's still a cost! And it's also a tax with unequal incidence; the need to fill out lots of forms in order to get government benefits or get out of obligations is an implicit subsidy to people who are educated but have low incomes.

> **译文：** * Tyler Cowen采访Annie Lowrey，讨论“时间税”，即人们花在填表格、等官僚机构等事情上的时间。The Diff之前已经报道过这一现象，认为填表格是一种徭役，而这仍然是本刊的立场：一项代价高昂的政策，如果其代价是浪费的时间而非浪费的金钱，就可以做到预算中性，这简直是荒谬的。但那仍然是一种成本！而且这也是一种税负分布不均的税；为了获得政府福利或摆脱义务而需要填写大量表格，这是对受过教育但收入较低的人群的一种隐性补贴。

The worst effect is on people who are at the bottom of the income and education distribution. Overall, Cowen's pushback is that this time-wasting serves as a rationing tool: if it takes a long time to apply for disability, or a specialized loan program, or to get out of a spurious traffic problem, then the people who avail themselves of this will be the ones who are more confident that they have a case. But there isn't necessarily a linear relationship, here: presumably, fraudsters will figure out which programs are worth the time it takes to abuse them, and everyone else doesn't have sample size of interactions to make this judgment.

> **译文：** 最糟糕的影响落在收入和受教育程度分布最底层的人身上。总体而言，Cowen的反驳是，这种浪费时间起到了配给工具的作用：如果申请残疾补助、专项贷款项目，或摆脱一个虚假的交通问题需要很长时间，那么利用这些项目的人将是那些更确信自己有理的人。但这里不一定存在线性关系：可以想见，骗子会弄清楚哪些项目值得花时间去滥用，而其他人并没有足够的交互样本来做出这种判断。

* And speaking of people wasting their time for no good reason, Alex Chalmers & Rob Wiblin in Works in Progress make the case for just putting your trash in the ground instead of sorting recycling . (They are, to be clear, making this case as a policy argument, not saying that failing to sort recycling is a form of civil disobedience.) This is one of those cases where it's hard to have good intuitions about scale. The upshot is that America produces a lot of trash, but there's a lot of America, such that the amount of garbage we've cumulatively put in landfills is under 0.01% of US land.

> **译文：** * 说到人们毫无正当理由地浪费时间，Alex Chalmers和Rob Wiblin在Works in Progress上主张，干脆把垃圾埋进地里，而不是分类回收。（需要说明的是，他们是在作为政策论点提出这一主张，并不是说不对垃圾进行分类回收是一种公民抗命。）这是那种很难对规模产生良好直觉的情况之一。结论是，美国产生大量垃圾，但美国也很大，以至于我们累计放入垃圾填埋场的垃圾量不到美国国土面积的0.01%。

It's not a blanket case against recycling: recycled metals are still valuable, but plastics mostly aren't. Which is a good reminder of how much energy consumption happens in out-of-the-way parts of the supply chain: some of your personal lifetime carbon footprint came from using stainless steel utensils, or buying a car or basically any appliance; converting ore into useful metal is an expensive process! Recycling is a visible ritual that's indifferent to energy costs, but it can be a reminder of those invisible energy costs instead.

> **译文：** 这并不是一概反对回收：回收的金属仍然有价值，但塑料大多没有。这很好地提醒我们，供应链中那些不起眼的环节消耗了多少能源：你个人一生碳足迹的一部分来自使用不锈钢餐具，或购买汽车，或基本上任何电器；将矿石转化为有用的金属是一个昂贵的过程！回收是一种可见的仪式，它对能源成本漠不关心，但它反而可以提醒人们那些看不见的能源成本。

* Dan Luu has a wonderful post on Bug Blindness . I have been meaning, for years, to write a rant about how much of Excel's behavior you have to model in your head in order to use it at maximum speed. Many other popular programs have such quirks, and there are millions of users who've learned subtle habits like pausing before starting to enter text into a form, toggling wifi off and back on, hitting refresh, etc. There are just many slightly suboptimal aspects to software, which are individually completely petty problems and collectively a big drag on productivity. And now many of them are solvable!

> **译文：** * Dan Luu有一篇关于“Bug盲区”的精彩文章。多年来我一直想写一篇吐槽，讲讲为了以最高速度使用Excel，你必须在脑子里建模多少Excel的行为。许多其他流行程序也有这种怪癖，有数百万用户学会了微妙的使用习惯，比如在开始往表单里输入文字之前先停顿一下、把WiFi关掉再打开、点击刷新等等。软件就是有许多略微次优的方面，单独来看都是完全琐碎的问题， collectively却是对生产力的巨大拖累。而现在其中许多问题都可以解决了！

* This week in Capital Gains: I noticed, over many years of reading about quants in the late 1980s and early 1990s, there'd somethings be a list of strategies they used, and that mixed in with the usual suspects ("statistical arbitrage," "convertible bond arbitrage") the list would often include "Japanese warrants." This turns out to have been a big, and for a while profitable, trade , where Japanese companies issued warrants along with bonds, and underpriced the warrants.

> **译文：** * 本周Capital Gains：在多年阅读关于1980年代末和1990年代初量化交易员的资料后，我注意到，有时会有一份他们使用的策略清单，而在那些常见的嫌疑犯（“统计套利”、“可转债套利”）之间，清单上常常会包括“日本认股权证”。这原来是一笔巨大的、且在一段时间内有利可图的交易，日本公司在发行债券的同时发行认股权证，并且对认股权证定价过低。

* From Read.Haus: will banks and other financial services companies globalize the way software companies have ? Banks were in the information technology business long before it had their name, so they're on a continuum with modern software companies. But banks can't globalize the way other firms can because the banking system is so close to a form of sovereignty: governments very carefully guard the privilege to create more money, and when they give it out, there are strings attached. In any kind of system where there's a feedback loop between money and power, that will mean that local banks will have an interest in keeping the political system friendly to them. In search, social media, short video, music streaming, etc., there just isn't as much at risk, so there are fewer barriers to globalization.

> **译文：** * 来自Read.Haus：银行和其他金融服务公司会像软件公司那样全球化吗？早在“信息技术”这个词出现之前，银行就已经在做信息技术业务了，所以它们与现代软件公司是一个连续谱系。但银行无法像其他公司那样全球化，因为银行体系与一种主权形式如此接近：政府非常小心地守护着创造更多货币的特权，而当它们授予这一特权时，是附带条件的。在任何一种货币与权力之间存在反馈回路的体系中，这都意味着本地银行会有兴趣让政治体系对它们友好。在搜索、社交媒体、短视频、音乐流媒体等领域，风险就没那么大，因此全球化的障碍也更少。
