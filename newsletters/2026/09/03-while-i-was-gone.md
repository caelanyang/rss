I just returned from a trip with my young family to Shenyang, my place of birth, so my almost two-year-old son can meet and hangout with his almost 95-year-old great grandmother. It was a filial piety trip. I will publish a dedicated post in the next few days to reminisce more about it. Meanwhile, I’m glad to be back in my quiet suburb with turkeys on the loose in my backyard.

> **译文：** 我刚带着年幼的家人从一趟沈阳之旅回来——那是我的出生地，让我快两岁的儿子能见见他快95岁的太姥姥，并和她一起玩耍。这是一趟尽孝之旅。接下来几天我会发一篇专门的帖子来回忆更多细节。与此同时，我很高兴回到我安静的郊区，后院还有火鸡在闲逛。

Of course, the world does not stand still, especially in tech and AI, while my own world was frozen between memories of a city I barely remember and dodging Meituan delivery scooters while pushing a stroller. So this post is a list of some fast-twitch reactions to news items that caught my attention while I was gone…

> **译文：** 当然，世界不会静止不动，尤其是在科技和AI领域，而我的世界却冻结在几乎记不清的城市回忆和推着婴儿车躲避美团外卖电动车之间。所以这篇帖子是一些快速反应的清单，针对我离开期间引起我注意的新闻……

Data Center Moratoriums

The wave of data center moratoriums in the US has become fast and furious. It will most definitely become a prominent election issue in this upcoming midterm, as I foresaw early this year . The most important change in this issue is how prominent governors, like Josh Shapiro of Pennsylvania (a Democrat) and Greg Abbott of Texas (a Republican), are starting to speak (or being forced by local political dynamics to speak) about restricting data center constructions. Both states have real data center footprint and growth potential, Texas in particular, so the political pressure their governors are facing is especially material.

> **译文：** 美国数据中心暂停令的浪潮已经变得又快又猛。正如我今年早些时候预见到的那样，这几乎肯定会成为即将到来的中期选举中的一个突出议题。这个问题最重要的变化是，像宾夕法尼亚州的Josh Shapiro（民主党人）和德克萨斯州的Greg Abbott（共和党人）这样有声望的州长，开始公开谈论（或被迫因地方政治动态而谈论）限制数据中心建设。这两个州都有实际的数据中心布局和增长潜力，尤其是德克萨斯州，因此其州长面临的政治压力尤其重大。

In the moratorium data center dashboard we’ve built, the latest update is now tracking 299 active moratoriums of various size and scale across the country. This free resource has been cited and linked in many places, from the Ezra Klein’s podcast to a widely-circulated New York Times op-ed by a union leader  arguing that data center construction is good for union and blue-collar jobs.

> **译文：** 在我们建立的数据中心暂停令仪表盘中，最新更新显示目前正在追踪全美299项不同规模和范围的有效暂停令。这个免费资源已被许多地方引用和链接，从Ezra Klein的播客到一篇广为流传的《纽约时报》评论文章——由一位工会领袖撰写，主张数据中心建设对工会和蓝领就业有利。

There is a lot of hand-wringing about this moratorium risk, which is a short-term overhang on the entire AI investment thesis. It is also a solvable problem, because the most common reason for the resistance is electricity/water cost and transparency in general.

> **译文：** 关于这种暂停令风险，有很多人忧心忡忡，这是对整个AI投资逻辑的短期压制因素。但这也是一个可以解决的问题，因为抵制的最常见原因通常是电力/用水成本以及整体透明度问题。

Good players who are building for the long-term, like Nebius, have developed a trustworthy playbook in building data centers the right way: invest in local communities, use technology to reduce power and water consumption, and most importantly, don’t hide your corporate identity behind NDAs and unnecessary secrecy. This approach is also not costly.

> **译文：** 像Nebius这样着眼长远、做正确事情的优秀企业，已经开发出一套值得信赖的以正确方式建设数据中心的打法：投资当地社区，用技术降低电力和用水消耗，最重要的是，不要用保密协议和不必要的保密来隐藏企业身份。这种方法成本也不高。

As the company shared during one of its previous earnings calls , a masterclass of data center economics, securing land and power only cost about 1% of the whole project, while physical construction is only about 20%. The vast majority of building an AI factory is what goes inside it – GPUs, memory, racks of servers, networking cables, etc.

> **译文：** 正如该公司在之前一次财报电话会议中分享的那样——那是一堂数据中心经济学的大师课——获取土地和电力只占整个项目成本的约1%，而实体建设仅占约20%。建设AI工厂的绝大部分成本在于内部的东西——GPU、内存、服务器机架、网络线缆等等。

Spending more to hire union laborers, fund local projects, and be a good corporate citizen for the long-haul is both a rounding error in the grand scheme of things and the right thing to do.

> **译文：** 花更多钱雇佣工会劳工、资助当地项目、做长远的好企业公民，从全局来看既是一个可以忽略不计的零头，也是正确的事情。

> AI infrastructure is becoming as foundational as electricity was a century ago.

> **译文：** > AI基础设施正变得像一个世纪前的电力一样具有基础性地位。

> The question is whether it's built with communities or on top of them.@JensenHuang  and @GavinSBaker  circled the same point this week: data centers can be a genuine win for the towns that host them,…

> **译文：** > 问题在于它是与社区共建，还是凌驾于社区之上。@JensenHuang和@GavinSBaker本周都围绕同一个观点：数据中心对所在城镇来说可以是一个真正的双赢……

> — Nebius (@nebiusai) September 2, 2026

Sadly, for every Nebius, there are probably 5 or 10 so-called neoclouds who are just pure speculators looking to make a quick buck in the AI boom. Those players should be weeded out, and these moratoriums could be the regulatory forcing mechanism that makes that happen.

> **译文：** 遗憾的是，每有一个Nebius，可能就有5到10个所谓的“新云”公司，它们只是纯粹的投机者，想在AI热潮中快速捞一笔。这些玩家应该被淘汰，而这些暂停令可能正是实现这一目标的监管强制机制。

OpenAI Agents Gone Rogue

The OpenAI agents' post-mortem has dominated my timeline. AI agents powered by frontier models “escaping” their confines and hacking whatever can be hacked is a real fear.

> **译文：** OpenAI智能体的事后复盘占据了我的信息流。由前沿模型驱动的AI智能体“逃脱”限制并入侵一切可入侵之物，这是一个真实的恐惧。

The issue is still raw with many more technical details that need to be shared, but one thing is clear: monitoring the chain of thoughts of agents throughout its activities, even (or especially) during evaluation and post-training, is a table stakes requirement for all agent swarms going forward. Had OpenAI’s post-training practice simply monitored what their agents were “thinking”, as they went off the guardrails, a lot of the damage could have been avoided.

> **译文：** 这个问题仍然很粗糙，还有更多技术细节需要分享，但有一点是明确的：在智能体的所有活动过程中监控其思维链——甚至在（或者说尤其是在）评估和后期训练阶段——是所有智能体集群未来必须具备的基本要求。如果OpenAI的后期训练实践仅仅监控了他们的智能体在偏离护栏时的“想法”，很多损害本可以避免。

One thing I am pleasantly surprised to see is that because of this incident, many voices  from  different  camps see an opportunity for the upcoming official US-China discussion on AI as part of Xi’s visit to Washington to be more substantive, less performative. Instead of meeting the very low expectations of this bilateral discussion being “talking about talking”, the OpenAI rogue agents incident presents a tangible topic to anchor a real discussion about bilateral and global cooperation on testing and securing AI agents’ cyber capabilities. That would be a great outcome from an otherwise very unfortunate and frightening incident.

> **译文：** 让我感到惊喜的一点是，因为这次事件，来自不同阵营的许多声音看到了一个机会：即将在习近平访问华盛顿期间举行的中美AI官方对话可以更加实质性、更少表演性。与其让这次双边对话停留在“谈论如何对话”这种极低预期上，OpenAI流氓智能体事件提供了一个具体话题，可以锚定关于双边和全球在测试和保障AI智能体网络能力方面合作的真正讨论。如果这样，那将是一次本来非常不幸和可怕的事件带来的极好结果。

NVIDIA-Hugging Face Marriage

NVIDIA is rumored to acquire Hugging Face. This deal is reminiscent of Microsoft’s acquisition of GitHub in 2018.

> **译文：** 据传NVIDIA将收购Hugging Face。这笔交易让人想起2018年微软收购GitHub。

Funny enough, I saw this deal coming three years ago, as a perfect marriage between the “hard power” and “soft power” of AI. NVIDIA is no doubt the “hard power” king of AI, as it is the dominant and leading supplier of all the hardware (and some software of course) of AI. Hugging Face, being the destination of choice for almost all developers and researchers to launch and host open weight models, holds the key to the “soft power” of AI. Strategically, supporting open source and open weight AI is critical to NVIDIA’s long-term success, so this deal is a no-brainer in my view and only a matter of time and price.

> **译文：** 有趣的是，我在三年前就预见到了这笔交易，它是AI“硬实力”和“软实力”的完美联姻。NVIDIA无疑是AI“硬实力”之王，因为它是AI所有硬件（当然还有一些软件）的主导和领先供应商。而Hugging Face几乎是所有开发者和研究人员发布和托管开源权重模型的首选平台，掌握着AI“软实力”的关键。从战略上讲，支持开源和开放权重AI对NVIDIA的长期成功至关重要，所以在我看来这笔交易是理所当然的，只是时间和价格的问题。

Speaking of price, I thought the final price tag, which could range between $12.9 to $14 billion , is worth dissecting. When Microsoft forked up $7.5 billion in 2018 dollars to buy GitHub, it was a hefty premium, but worthwhile in order to fix the broken relationships the tech giant had then with software developers and open source. So when I first pontificated on a possible NVIDIA-Hugging Face marriage in August 2023, just a few months after ChatGPT’s launch, I thought a $10 billion price tage would be reasonable (somewhere between GitHub’s $7.5 billion acquisition price and GitLab’s $11 billion IPO price.)

> **译文：** 说到价格，我认为最终的价格标签——可能在129亿到140亿美元之间——值得剖析。当微软在2018年以75亿美元收购GitHub时，那是一个高昂的溢价，但为了修复这家科技巨头当时与软件开发者和开源社区破裂的关系，这是值得的。所以当我在2023年8月——ChatGPT发布仅仅几个月后——首次高谈阔论NVIDIA与Hugging Face可能联姻时，我认为100亿美元的价格是合理的（介于GitHub的75亿美元收购价和GitLab的110亿美元IPO估值之间）。

Almost exactly three years later, the $12.9 - $14 billion price is certainly more than what I had predicted, but not unreasonably so. Even though Hugging Face’s rumored $150 million revenue is less than GitHub’s at the time of its acquisition, it is in the center of literally everything AI, so much so that the rogue OpenAI agents hacked it to cheat on its exam. That prominence deserves a healthy AI premium.

> **译文：** 几乎整整三年后，129亿至140亿美元的价格当然比我预测的要高，但也并非不合理。尽管Hugging Face传闻中的1.5亿美元收入低于GitHub被收购时的水平，但它正处于AI一切事物的中心，以至于流氓OpenAI智能体都入侵它来作弊考试。这种核心地位值得一个健康的AI溢价。

Moonshot-Hyperscaler Revenue Share

Thanks to Kimi K3’s growing capability and popularity, Moonshot AI is negotiating a revenue share of up to 30% with the Big 3 American hyperscalers , AWS, Azure and GCP . Moonshot likely already has similar revenue share commercial agreements with smaller inference cloud players like TogetherAI and BaseTen. Alibaba is trying to do the same with its Qwen models.

> **译文：** 得益于Kimi K3不断增强的能力和日益增长的受欢迎程度，Moonshot AI正在与美国三大超大规模云服务商——AWS、Azure和GCP——洽谈最高可达30%的收入分成协议。Moonshot可能已经与TogetherAI和BaseTen等较小的推理云服务商达成了类似的收入分成商业协议。阿里巴巴也在尝试对其Qwen模型采取同样的做法。

A new business model for monetizing open weight models is emerging. This should not be surprising if you tracked the licensing changes that came with the release of K3 and then Qwen 3.8. K3’s license asks for commercial agreements to be negotiated if the partner, who resells the model as a service to their customers, generates $20 million in trailing 12 months revenue. Qwen 3.8’s license is only moderately more generous than K3’s, pegging the revenue-to-commercial-agreement trigger at $50 million.

> **译文：** 一种将开放权重模型商业化的新模式正在兴起。如果你关注过K3及随后Qwen 3.8发布时伴随的许可条款变更，就不会对此感到意外。K3的许可协议要求，如果合作伙伴将模型作为服务转售给其客户，且过去12个月收入达到2000万美元，则需要协商商业协议。Qwen 3.8的许可条款仅比K3略微宽松，将触发商业协议的收入门槛设定为5000万美元。

> Commercial agreement threshold is $50 million trailing 12 months revenue (K3's is $20m)

> **译文：** > 商业协议触发门槛为过去12个月收入5000万美元（K3为2000万美元）

> "Branding clause" requirement is the same: 100 million monthly active or 20m monthly revenue

This approach is both inevitable and a double-edge sword. The world can’t count on VC funding, subsidies, and human generosity to keep using open weight models without paying. As these models become more capable (and more expensive to improve), developing a business model to sustain their open weight nature is necessary. These revenue share commercial agreements address this sustainability concern.

> **译文：** 这种做法既是必然之举，也是一把双刃剑。世界不能指望依靠风险投资、补贴和人类的慷慨来持续免费使用开放权重模型。随着这些模型能力不断增强（改进成本也越来越高），建立一种商业模式来维持其开放权重的特性是必要的。这些收入分成商业协议正是为了解决这一可持续性问题。

However, life is always less straightforward and tougher to navigate for Chinese labs making AI models. As Graham Webster and I wrote when K3’s new license was first unveiled , this new way to scale its revenue also opens itself up to more scrutiny and avail itself of American legal jurisprudence more so than ever before. Alibaba/Qwen too! How forcefully will DC insert itself into these commercial negotiations between Moonshot and American hyperscalers will be a major X factor.

> **译文：** 然而，对于中国AI模型实验室来说，前路总是更加曲折、更加艰难。正如Graham Webster和我在K3新许可协议首次发布时所写的那样，这种扩大收入的新方式也使其面临更多审视，并比以往任何时候都更多地置身于美国法律管辖之下。阿里巴巴和Qwen也是如此！华盛顿方面将在多大程度上介入Moonshot与美国超大规模云服务商之间的商业谈判，将是一个重大的不确定因素。
