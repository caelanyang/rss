Trends in energy, climate tech, and the power grid

7 September 2026

Hello! This week we have a wide range of stories for you: hurricane chasers, dead frog experiments, Spain’s power grid growing pains, ships running on green methanol, and China’s grip on the rarest of rare earth elements.

> **译文：** 你好！本周我们为你准备了丰富多样的报道：飓风追逐者、死青蛙实验、西班牙电网的成长烦恼、以绿色甲醇为燃料的船舶，以及中国对最稀有稀土元素的掌控。

My personal favorite is the tale of an argument about dead frogs that resulted in the invention of the world’s first battery. Writer Allison Marsh explains how the famous Alessandro Volta built the battery to prove that a friend was wrong about dead frogs harboring electricity in their bodies.

> **译文：** 我个人最喜欢的是一个关于死青蛙争论的故事，这场争论最终促成了世界上第一块电池的发明。作家Allison Marsh解释了著名的Alessandro Volta如何制造电池，以证明一位朋友关于死青蛙体内储存电力的观点是错误的。

In another story, hurricane scientists told us about their harrowing experience flying aircraft into the notorious “boundary layer” of hurricanes in an effort to collect data, and how new tools may mean they don’t have to do that anymore. They’ve found that earthquake sensors can be used instead, and could provide critical data about whether a hurricane will intensify.

> **译文：** 在另一篇报道中，飓风科学家向我们讲述了他们驾驶飞机闯入飓风臭名昭著的“边界层”以收集数据的惊险经历，以及新工具如何意味着他们或许不再需要这样做。他们发现地震传感器可以替代使用，并能提供关于飓风是否会增强的关键数据。
