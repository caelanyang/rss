Checking in with the Iran War after six months of widespread criticism and recent signs of American success.
> **译文：** 在历经六个月的广泛批评以及近期美国出现成功迹象之后，再来审视伊朗战争。
The War and Reasonable Doubt
Friday, September 4, 2026
Majid Saeedi/Getty Images
Almost as soon as the U.S. began the war in Iran, an international consensus decided that it was a terrible idea. And since then, the critics have had plenty of material to confirm that thesis. There has been confusing messaging from the White House, rifts with Israel, broken ceasefires, an MOU that lasted less than a month, reports of diminished munitions, unhappy allies, an overheating bond market, and of course, a steady din of Truth Social threats that are invariably walked back by the president 72 hours later.
> **译文：** 几乎在美国对伊朗开战的同时，国际社会就形成共识，认为这是一个糟糕的主意。自那以后，批评者们有大量素材来印证这一论断。白宫传递出混乱的信息、与以色列产生裂痕、停火协议破裂、一份谅解备忘录维持了不到一个月、弹药减少的报告、盟友不满、债券市场过热，当然还有Truth Social上持续不断的威胁言论，而这些言论总是在72小时后被总统本人收回。
But what if all of this ultimately works?
I think it’s worth keeping in mind that the net impact of the Iran War remains a largely unsettled question. So rather than surveying a sea of expert opinions deriding the war as a catastrophe  or an endless supply of Foreign Affairs essays explaining why the U.S. strategy is doomed to fail  , I will begin by citing not a think tanker, but cultural critic Chuck Klosterman, from his book But What If We’re Wrong? :
> **译文：** 我认为值得记住的是，伊朗战争的净影响在很大程度上仍是一个悬而未决的问题。因此，与其罗列众多专家意见将这场战争斥为灾难，或是引用无穷无尽的《外交事务》文章来解释美国战略为何注定失败，我首先要引用的不是智库学者，而是文化评论家查克·克洛斯特曼，出自他的著作《但如果我们错了呢？》：
But what about the things we’re all wrong about?
What about ideas that are so accepted and internalized that we’re not even in a position to question their fallibility? These are ideas so ingrained in the collective consciousness that it seems foolhardy to even wonder if they’re potentially untrue. Sometimes these seem like questions only a child would ask, since children aren’t paralyzed by the pressures of consensus and common sense.
> **译文：** 那些被如此广泛接受和内化的观念又当如何？我们甚至无法质疑它们的可错性。这些观念在集体意识中根深蒂固，以至于怀疑它们可能不真实都显得鲁莽。有时这些问题似乎只有孩子才会问，因为孩子不会被共识和常识的压力所束缚。
It’s a dissonance that creates the most unavoidable of intellectual paradoxes: When you ask smart people if they believe there are major ideas currently accepted by the culture at large that will eventually be proven false, they will say, “Well, of course. There must be. That phenomenon has been experienced by every generation who’s ever lived, since the dawn of human history.” Yet offer those same people a laundry list of contemporary ideas that might fit that description, and they’ll be tempted to reject them all. […]
> **译文：** 这种不协调造成了最不可避免的智识悖论：当你问聪明人是否相信当前被整个文化广泛接受的主要观念中有些最终会被证明是错误的，他们会说：“嗯，当然。一定有。自人类历史开端以来，每一代人都经历过这种现象。”然而，当你向同样这些人列出一长串可能符合这一描述的当代观念时，他们却倾向于全部否定。[……]
In her book Being Wrong , author Kathryn Schulz spends a few key pages on the concept of “naive realism.” Schulz notes that while there are few conscious proponents of naïve realism, “that doesn’t mean there are no naive realists.” I would go a step further than Schulz; I suspect most conventionally intelligent people are naïve realists, and I think it might be the defining intellectual quality of this era. The straightforward definition of naïve realism doesn’t seem that outlandish: It’s a theory that suggests the world is exactly as it appears. Obviously, this viewpoint creates a lot of opportunity for colossal wrongness (e.g., “The sun appears to move across the sky, so the sun must be orbiting Earth”). But my personal characterization of naive realism is wider and more insidious. I think it operates as the manifestation of two ingrained beliefs:
> **译文：** 在《犯错》一书中，作者凯瑟琳·舒尔茨用了几页关键篇幅讨论“天真现实主义”的概念。舒尔茨指出，虽然很少有自觉的天真现实主义拥护者，但“这并不意味着没有天真现实主义者”。我要比舒尔茨更进一步；我怀疑大多数传统意义上的聪明人都是天真现实主义者，我认为这可能是这个时代的标志性智识特质。天真现实主义的直白定义看起来并不那么古怪：它认为世界正如其表象所示。显然，这种观点为巨大的错误创造了大量机会（例如，“太阳看起来在天空中移动，所以太阳一定在绕地球运行”）。但我个人对天真现实主义的刻画更为宽泛、更为隐蔽。我认为它表现为两种根深蒂固信念的体现：
* “When considering any question, I must be rational and logical, to the point of dismissing any unverifiable data as preposterous,” and
> **译文：** * “在考虑任何问题时，我必须理性和逻辑，以至于将任何无法验证的数据斥为荒谬”，以及
* “When considering any question, I’m going to assume that the information we currently have is all the information that will ever be available.”
> **译文：** * “在考虑任何问题时，我将假设我们目前拥有的信息就是将来可获得的所有信息。”
If we grant for argument’s sake that Chuck is correct and naive realism is the defining intellectual quality of an era in which public intellectuals are terrified of criticism and chronically seek the comfort of measurable support for their arguments, then how might that bear on Iran discourse?
> **译文：** 如果我们为了论证而承认查克是对的，承认天真现实主义是这个时代的标志性智识特质——在这个时代，公共知识分子害怕批评，长期寻求可量化的支持来为自己的论点寻求安慰——那么这对伊朗问题的讨论意味着什么？
What’s clear and measurable after six months is that the war has taken longer than Trump said it would, messaging from the White House is reliably chaotic, domestic prices are rising, allies are reeling with bond problems of their own  , it’s been politically catastrophic for Republicans, and there’s no easy off-ramp from any of this. It’s rational and logical, then, to side with the former Chatham House director who recently called the war  “a strategic loss and an embarrassment” reflective of “a whipsaw foreign policy that doesn’t know its limits.”
> **译文：** 六个月后清晰且可量化的事实是：战争持续的时间比特朗普所说的更长，白宫的信息传递确实混乱，国内物价上涨，盟友因自身的债券问题而焦头烂额，这对共和党来说是政治灾难，而且这一切都没有简单的退出路径。因此，理性且逻辑的做法是站在前查塔姆研究所所长一边，他最近称这场战争是“战略损失和尴尬”，反映了“一种不知界限的来回摇摆的外交政策”。
The War at Six Months
As you can probably guess, I suspect those takes are simplistic and naive, or at least too confident at the current stage. For one, Iran’s long-term leverage has already been permanently degraded. American allies in the region have begun constructing billions of dollars’ worth of pipelines  to circumvent the Strait of Hormuz, and once those projects are complete, there is no unringing the bell for the IRGC and the current regime. Their longstanding leverage over the global economy will be diminished, leaving behind a regime that’s massively unpopular both domestically and regionally, with vanishing ability to defend its interests. Even Iraq, long a quasi-vassal of the Iranians, is building a pipeline  and partnering with the U.S.
> **译文：** 你大概能猜到，我认为这些看法过于简单和天真，至少在现阶段过于自信。首先，伊朗的长期杠杆已经被永久削弱。美国在该地区的盟友已开始建设价值数十亿美元的管道以绕开霍尔木兹海峡，一旦这些项目完工，对伊斯兰革命卫队和现政权来说，覆水难收。他们长期以来对全球经济的杠杆将被削弱，留下一个在国内和地区都极不受欢迎的政权，捍卫自身利益的能力也将消失殆尽。就连长期作为伊朗准附庸的伊拉克也在建设管道并与美国合作。
Establishing alternatives to Hormuz is a break with nearly a half-century of history that never would have happened without the war as a forcing function to focus minds and mobilize capital. I wrote about this inevitable outcome back in May . Previously, because 20% of the world’s oil transits the strait (in addition to petrochemicals, gas, helium, fertilizer, and more critical inputs), the Iranian regime has preserved its reign with the implicit threat of holding the global economy hostage and decimating the political fortunes of any U.S. leaders who undertake a prolonged effort at regime change. Indeed, as Karim Sadjadpour wrote for the Atlantic in March  , that strategy worked well for Iran in the past, and it’s exactly what the IRGC assumed would prevail in the present conflict.
> **译文：** 建立霍尔木兹海峡的替代通道是对近半个世纪历史的突破，如果没有这场战争作为聚焦思维和调动资本的强制力量，这一切永远不会发生。我在五月份就写过这一必然结果。此前，由于全球20%的石油经由该海峡运输（此外还有石化产品、天然气、氦气、化肥等关键物资），伊朗政权通过隐含的威胁——挟持全球经济、摧毁任何进行长期政权更迭努力的美国领导人的政治命运——来维持其统治。事实上，正如卡里姆·萨贾德普尔三月在《大西洋月刊》上所写，这一策略过去对伊朗行之有效，而这正是伊斯兰革命卫队认为在当前冲突中会再次奏效的策略。
The IRGC has failed, though—at least so far. Oil prices have been contained ( with the help of China  ), American markets have continued to grow, and Iran’s success in closing the strait appears to be waning as we reach the middle innings of the conflict. Bloomberg reported last week  that Goldman analysts have found that “oil exports from the Persian Gulf have recovered to around two-thirds of pre-war levels.”
> **译文：** 然而，伊斯兰革命卫队失败了——至少到目前为止。油价得到控制（在中国的帮助下），美国市场持续增长，随着冲突进入中盘阶段，伊朗封锁海峡的成功率似乎在下降。彭博社上周报道，高盛分析师发现“波斯湾石油出口已恢复到战前水平的三分之二左右。”
A natural follow-up to that data might be: “ That’s great the strait is now two-thirds open , but it was 100 percent open before the war! ” Yes, and now let’s consider what else was true before the war.
> **译文：** 对这些数据的一个自然追问可能是：“海峡现在恢复了三分之二开放，这很好，但战前可是百分之百开放啊！”是的，现在让我们考虑一下战前还有哪些事实。
Iran was cheating on the JCPOA as soon as it was signed  , and the regime of course continued enriching uranium after the JCPOA was scrapped. Over the past decade, its nuclear and ballistic missile capabilities had metastasized into an ominous problem for the stability of the Middle East and the world. In addition to either tacitly or actively sponsoring terror throughout the region, including the October 7th attacks  and Houthi attacks on American shippers in the Red Sea  , Iran was explicitly aligned with Russia  and China  and receiving military assistance from both, all while murderously subjugating a population of approximately 90 million people. And the problems, across every vector, were getting worse. Whatever the costs of the war have been, doing nothing came with costs, too.
> **译文：** 伊朗在《联合全面行动计划》签署后不久就开始违约，在该协议被废弃后，伊朗政权当然继续浓缩铀。过去十年间，其核能力和弹道导弹能力已经扩散成对中东和世界稳定的严峻威胁。除了在该地区明里暗里支持恐怖主义，包括10月7日袭击和胡塞武装在红海对美国船只的袭击之外，伊朗还明确与俄罗斯和中国结盟，并接受双方的军事援助，同时以残暴手段压迫着约9000万人口。而且，各个方向的问题都在恶化。无论战争的代价是什么，无所作为同样有代价。
The U.S. chose action. In response, Iran has largely failed to cripple the global economy while also proactively attacking countries like Bahrain, Qatar, the UAE, and Saudi Arabia, which has undermined the regime’s efforts to turn the Middle East against the U.S. Instead, those countries have pushed the U.S. to eradicate the regime for good  , and now it’s Iran that looks isolated and crippled. As Brett McGurk wrote at CNN this week  :
> **译文：** 美国选择了行动。作为回应，伊朗在很大程度上未能瘫痪全球经济，同时还主动攻击巴林、卡塔尔、阿联酋和沙特阿拉伯等国，这削弱了该政权试图让中东反对美国的努力。相反，这些国家推动美国彻底铲除该政权，现在看起来孤立和被削弱的是伊朗。正如布雷特·麦格克本周在CNN上所写：
According to the International Monetary Fund, Iran’s economy is expected to contract by more than 5% this year, with inflation approaching 70%. The value of Iran’s currency has fallen sharply. Prices for basic goods have soared. An Iranian Labor Ministry official recently estimated that more than one million jobs disappeared during the first three months of the war alone.
> **译文：** 据国际货币基金组织称，伊朗经济预计今年将萎缩超过5%，通胀率接近70%。伊朗货币大幅贬值。基本商品价格飞涨。伊朗劳工部一位官员最近估计，仅在战争的前三个月就有超过一百万个工作岗位消失。
These statistics matter because Iran’s ability to sustain the conflict ultimately depends on more than its remaining missile arsenal. Before the war, the Iranian regime confronted unprecedented public pressure against its repressive rule and failing economy. Thousands of Iranians were reportedly killed in the streets. […]
> **译文：** 这些统计数据之所以重要，是因为伊朗维持冲突的能力最终不仅仅取决于其剩余的导弹库。战前，伊朗政权就面临着前所未有的公众压力，针对其压迫性统治和失败的经济。据报道，数千名伊朗人在街头被杀。[……]
But the Guards require resources to rule by force, and this new fourth act — a military embargo — is something that Iran has never confronted before. Indeed, this is the first time since the 1979 Revolution that Iran has faced a quarantine on its economic lifeline through Hormuz.
> **译文：** 但革命卫队需要资源来以武力维持统治，而这第四幕——军事禁运——是伊朗从未面对过的。事实上，这是自1979年革命以来，伊朗首次面临通过霍尔木兹海峡的经济生命线被封锁。
Their way out is to stop threatening ships in the Strait and abandon what’s left of its mostly destroyed enrichment program. That’s a deal Trump would accept. Doing either, however, from the vantage point of the Guards would show weakness and threaten their rule.
> **译文：** 他们的出路是停止威胁海峡中的船只，并放弃其大部分已被摧毁的浓缩计划所剩的一切。这是特朗普会接受的协议。然而，从革命卫队的角度来看，采取其中任何一项行动都会显得软弱，并威胁到他们的统治。
From here, we’ll see. I’m not stupid enough to predict an imminent American victory in the middle of a conflict that has, by some measures, been ongoing for about 45 years. Anything can happen over the next few months, and U.S. costs are real in the meantime. The war has claimed at least 18 American lives  and required tremendous sacrifices from service members and their families . Regional bases have been decimated  , resources are being stretched thin  as the conflict prolongs, and the diminished stock of missile interceptors looks real . Meanwhile, spending continues, gas prices are as high as they were in 2022  , and Treasury yields remain volatile. Continued upheaval in that second area could strain the entire U.S. economy (as well as the government’s future debt service).
> **译文：** 从这里开始，我们拭目以待。我还没蠢到在一场按某些标准已持续约45年的冲突中途预测美国即将获胜。未来几个月任何事情都可能发生，而在此期间美国的代价是实实在在的。这场战争已造成至少18名美国人丧生，并让服役人员及其家人付出了巨大牺牲。地区基地遭到重创，随着冲突延长，资源被不断摊薄，导弹拦截器库存的减少看起来是真实的。与此同时，开支持续增加，汽油价格与2022年一样高，国债收益率依然波动。第二个地区的持续动荡可能拖累整个美国经济（以及政府未来的债务偿还）。
Where I disagree with many critics of the war is in their conclusion that any of those data points are evidence of a failed or failing war. I see that data as evidence that successfully prosecuting any war is incredibly chaotic and fraught with political, economic, and human downsides.
> **译文：** 我与许多战争批评者的分歧在于，他们得出的结论是，这些数据点中的任何一个都证明这场战争已经失败或正在失败。我认为这些数据恰恰证明，成功进行任何战争都是极其混乱的，且充满政治、经济和人员方面的代价。
Costs, Benefits and the Bet
Measured against the warnings from experts that were expressed at the outset of this conflict  , the U.S. is doing quite well. The price of oil has not skyrocketed and sparked a global economic crisis, while Trump has not committed 250,000 troops to taking Iran by land, risking tens of thousands of lives in the process. The U.S. strategy hasn’t been perfect, but it hasn’t been static, either.
> **译文：** 与这场冲突爆发之初专家们发出的警告相比，美国的表现相当不错。油价没有飙升并引发全球经济危机，特朗普也没有投入25万地面部队从陆路攻取伊朗，从而冒着数万人丧生的风险。美国的战略并非完美，但也不是一成不变。
The military eventually arrived at the right blockade strategy, and this week struck two Iranian oil tankers in a new “tanker-for-tanker” approach  that makes quite a bit of sense . Finally, interceptors notwithstanding, the stocks of offensive weapons are not a problem . My expectation is that the current semi-kinetic equilibrium will hold until the midterm elections in November, and from there, the U.S. will continue to escalate  in an effort to topple the Iranian regime altogether.
> **译文：** 军方最终找到了正确的封锁策略，本周以新的“油轮换油轮”方式打击了两艘伊朗油轮，这种做法相当有道理。最后，撇开拦截器不谈，进攻性武器的库存不是问题。我的预期是，当前这种半动能平衡状态将持续到11月中期选举，此后美国将继续升级行动，力求彻底推翻伊朗政权。
That’s the goal, that’s the off-ramp. And it’s also possible that Iran will accelerate that timeline .
> **译文：** 这就是目标，这就是退出通道。伊朗也有可能加速这一时间表。
The core question is of course whether all these risks are worth it. The U.S. rationale for attacking Iran was summarized by one writer in March as “We just sort of felt like it”  ; to this day, I think that’s how many people view the war. Riding high off the win in Venezuela, with Netanyahu egging them on, and with visions of taking Kharg Island that date to the 1980s  , Trump and his administration assumed it would be easy. And why not ? they thought.
> **译文：** 核心问题当然是所有这些风险是否值得。美国攻击伊朗的理由在3月被一位作者概括为“我们就是有点想打”；时至今日，我认为很多人就是这样看待这场战争的。在委内瑞拉大胜的余威中，在内塔尼亚胡的怂恿下，再加上自1980年代以来夺取哈尔克岛的愿景，特朗普及其政府以为这会很容易。他们心想，为什么不呢？
Through that lens, then sure, the war looks profoundly reckless, particularly in the context of constrained military resources, exhausted service members, and a looming confrontation with China. But for reasons I laid out in March  , I continue to see potential benefits that are obvious, and perfectly consistent with countering China during a cold war with Schrödinger’s characteristics .
> **译文：** 从这个角度看，这场战争当然看起来极其鲁莽，尤其是在军事资源受限、服役人员疲惫不堪、与中国对抗迫在眉睫的背景下。但基于我在3月阐述的理由，我依然看到其中显而易见的潜在好处，而且这些好处与在一场具有薛定谔式特征的内战冷战中对抗中国完全一致。
For one, in the wake of this war the U.S. is now entrenched as the world’s preeminent energy superpower and has gained strategic leverage that comes with that title . Perhaps that happened by accident, or perhaps not.
> **译文：** 首先，这场战争之后，美国如今已巩固了其作为世界首要能源超级大国的地位，并获得了伴随这一头衔而来的战略杠杆。这或许是偶然发生的，也或许不是。
But second, and contrary to explanations from Trump, Marco Rubio, Scott Bessent, and Pete Hegseth—who have no reason to state all of their objectives publicly—the war has always looked to me like a calculated bet that toppling a straightforwardly tyrannical Iranian regime, securing the Strait of Hormuz, and gaining leverage over the flow of regional energy exports is not only achievable but could also be a potentially powerful deterrent to Chinese aggression in the Taiwan Strait.
> **译文：** 其次，与特朗普、马尔科·卢比奥、斯科特·贝森特和皮特·赫格塞斯的解释相反——他们没有理由公开陈述所有目标——这场战争在我看来始终像一场精心计算的赌注：推翻一个彻头彻尾的暴虐伊朗政权、确保霍尔木兹海峡安全、并在地区能源出口流向问题上获得杠杆，不仅是可以实现的，而且可能成为对中国在台湾海峡侵略行为的潜在强力威慑。
China imports more than 50% of its oil from the Middle East, in addition to critical petrochemicals. Assuming the costs can be managed (and they have been so far), I’m completely comfortable with the U.S. using its military to throttle a murderous regime and chase that strategic upside.

Trump’s strategic intent aside, what should also be clear is that it’s required real mettle and conviction to stay committed to a comically unpopular war  that looks like it will cost the Republicans the midterm elections, which in turn could lead to any number of political headwinds for Trump personally. I wrote only two weeks ago  that I find Trump foreign policy exhausting, but if the president were as selfish and capricious as his loudest critics allege, he would have bailed months ago. Slinking away with a harebrained sequel to the JCPOA would have been easier, and probably smarter politically.
> **译文：** 抛开特朗普的战略意图不谈，同样应该清楚的是，坚持一场荒谬地不受欢迎的战争需要真正的勇气和信念——这场战争看起来将让共和党输掉中期选举，进而可能给特朗普个人带来各种政治逆风。我两周前刚刚写道，我觉得特朗普的外交政策令人精疲力竭，但如果总统真像他最激烈的批评者所声称的那样自私和反复无常，他几个月前就会抽身了。带着一个异想天开的JCPOA续集悄悄溜走本来更容易，在政治上也可能更明智。
Instead, the war continues. Iran’s leaders have already forfeited their long-term leverage by attempting to close the strait and are now being suffocated as the U.S. military opens shipping lanes for everyone else and closes them to Iran. We’ll see how much longer the regime and the IRGC can survive, whether the U.S. economy can remain stable, and how much more the U.S. will escalate in the months to come.
> **译文：** 然而，战争仍在继续。伊朗领导人试图封锁海峡，已经丧失了长期杠杆，如今美军为其他所有人开放航道、却对伊朗关闭航道，伊朗正在被窒息。我们将看看这个政权和伊斯兰革命卫队还能撑多久，美国经济能否保持稳定，以及美国在未来几个月还会升级到什么程度。
The ultimate goal appears to be removing a regime that has targeted U.S. troops and destabilized the Middle East for the past 40 years, and killed 30,000 Iranian protesters just last January.
> **译文：** 最终目标似乎是铲除一个过去40年来以美军为攻击目标、破坏中东稳定、并在去年1月杀害3万名伊朗抗议者的政权。
And if you’ve seen what’s happened to Venezuelan oil exports to China this year  , it’s probably not coincidence that decisively winning this war would leave China’s most prolific oil suppliers indebted to the U.S. military, which may also have final say over who and what passes through the Strait of Hormuz.

Through that lens, the war looks like a wildly ambitious assertion of American strength as a potentially catastrophic standoff with China looms in the background. And yes, the entire exercise could be a last gasp of hegemony. It could end horribly. But I would ignore the people telling you it already has.
> **译文：** 从这个角度看，这场战争看起来是对美国实力的一次野心勃勃的宣示，而与中国之间可能具有灾难性的对峙正在背景中逼近。是的，整个行动可能是霸权的最后一口气。它可能以可怕的方式结束。但我会忽略那些告诉你它已经结束的人。
