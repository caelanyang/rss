* Daniel Kolitz reflects on two episodes of economic disempowerment  in The Baffler: first, he lost his job as an SEO copywriter because LLMs could do it better.

> **译文：** * Daniel Kolitz在《The Baffler》杂志上反思了两段经济上失去自主权的经历：首先，他作为SEO文案撰稿人的工作丢了，因为大语言模型能做得更好。

> **译文：** 但最引人注目的部分是：“在我写这篇文章时，我欠Cleo $359.96，欠Albert $31.99，欠Brigit $62.99，欠Dave

> **译文：** 因此，这篇文章提出的更广泛的经济观点是站不住脚的：作为金融机构的客户，作者的价值远低于那些拥有五位数中段信用额度且总是按时还款的人，或那些申请抵押贷款的人。

> **译文：** 这些发薪日贷款公司并非某个更大阴谋的一部分，要把所有人都套牢在永久经济底层；它们只是金融体系中一个微小的角落，以令人压抑且运营上令人头疼的方式赚取小额利润。

* Steve Newman at Second Thoughts on why robotics is hard . One thing he points out early is that robotics demos are particularly misleading, because it's more straightforward to make a robot superhuman at one task than to make it good at tasks in general, but the robotics bull case rests on the idea that we'll be able to replace many different kinds of physical labor with robots. Another big issue is that their world models need to be so comprehensive, and that this also applies to training—the closer a task is to a real-world one rather than an incremental one ("bake a cake" vs "crack an egg") the more expensive verification gets. And we still have unanswered questions on form factors (one possibility here is that much of the world will use robots with legs, but the US will have more robots on wheels—the Americans with Disabilities Act means we are a nation of convenient ramps).

> **译文：** * Steve Newman在《Second
> Thoughts》上撰文探讨为什么机器人技术很难。他早期指出的一个问题是，机器人演示特别具有误导性，因为让机器人在单一任务上超越人类比让它在一般任务上表现出色更直接，但机器人看涨论调的基础在于我们能用机器人替代许多不同类型的体力劳动。另一个大问题是它们的世界模型需要极其全面，这也适用于训练——任务越接近现实世界任务而非增量任务（“烤蛋糕”对比“打鸡蛋”），验证成本就越高。而且我们在形态因素上仍有未解问题（一种可能是世界上大部分地区会使用带腿的机器人，但美国会有更多轮式机器人——《美国残疾人法案》意味着我们是一个遍布便利坡道的国家）。

* Data Colada has an investigation of a Dan Ariely paper with ridiculously good evidence of manipulation .

> **译文：** * Data Colada对Dan Ariely的一篇论文进行了调查，发现了荒谬到令人难以置信的操纵证据。

The paper purported to study whether or not students' grades were affected by setting their own deadlines, and further by how those deadlines were spread out.

> **译文：** 该论文声称研究学生自行设定截止日期是否影响成绩，以及这些截止日期的分布方式如何产生影响。

It doesn't seem to show that.

This kind of statistical sleuthing is delightful: they were able to reverse-engineer the exact manipulations made, and then go back and find reviewer comments asking for precisely those results!

> **译文：** 这种统计侦探工作令人愉悦：他们能够逆向工程出确切的操纵方式，然后回溯找到要求恰恰是这些结果的审稿人评论！

One reason psychology-adjacent fields are so vulnerable to fraud is that their outputs are so flattering to the reader.

> **译文：** 心理学相关领域如此容易受到欺诈的一个原因是，它们的产出对读者来说太讨喜了。

Imagine being the only person who knows that drawing little eyes on a piece of paper makes people more honest—you're wise, and everyone around you is just a meat machine running silly little programs.

> **译文：** 想象一下，你是唯一知道在纸上画小眼睛能让人们更诚实的人——你很睿智，而你周围的每个人都只是运行着愚蠢小程序的肉机器。

It's very hard to pull back from that and ask if the study replicates. (No .) The other reason is, of course, that most people in academia don't pause to ask if the paper they're reading is based on fabricated data. (There's actually another issue with the data: the study looks at granular measures of student grades, including participation.

> **译文：** 很难从中抽身出来，去问这项研究是否能被复制。（不能。）另一个原因当然是，学术界大多数人不会停下来问自己正在读的论文是否基于伪造数据。（数据其实还有另一个问题：该研究考察了学生成绩的细粒度指标，包括参与度。）

These are expressed as percentages.

The scores for participation include numbers like 92.3%, 89.6%, and 80.77%, the last of which is truncated, and to get these numbers out of a fraction with an integer numerator, it needs to have a denominator of 3,000.

> **译文：** 参与度分数包括92.3%、89.6%和80.77%等数字，最后一个被截断了，而要从一个整数分子的分数中得出这些数字，分母需要是3，000。

The class met fourteen times, and if we assume there isn't a participation score on exam day, this implies that students' class participation was rated on a ~230-point scale.

> **译文：** 这门课共上了十四次，如果我们假设考试当天没有参与度评分，这意味着学生的课堂参与度是在约230分的量表上评定的。

Meanwhile, if you look at the full data and focus on the subset of students who set their own deadlines, there isn't much of a correlation between students' scores on different components of their final grade.

> **译文：** 与此同时，如果你查看完整数据并关注那些自行设定截止日期的学生子集，就会发现学生期末成绩不同组成部分之间的得分并没有太大相关性。

So one compelling possibility is that these scores, too, were made up.

> **译文：** 因此，一个很有说服力的可能性是：这些分数也是编造的。

* A Read.

Haus reader asks if the AI trade is causing companies that are further from pure AI but still connected to the datacenter buildout, like Caterpillar, to be more volatile .

> **译文：** Haus 的读者问道，AI 交易是否正在导致那些离纯 AI 较远但仍与数据中心建设相关的公司（如 Caterpillar）的波动性加大。

There are two arguments that these stocks should be even more volatile than pure AI names, one fundamental and one behavioral.

> **译文：** 有两种论点认为这些股票的波动性应该比纯 AI 股票更大，一种是基本面层面的，一种是行为层面的。

On the fundamental side, the more disconnected some company is from the AI supply chain, the less likely it was to invest ahead of bottlenecks—someone selling GPUs has a very good sense of how big AI is, but for someone selling construction equipment, it starts as a minor boost to sales and then just keeps on growing.

> **译文：** 从基本面来看，某家公司与 AI 供应链的关联越弱，它就越不可能在瓶颈出现前提前投资——卖 GPU 的人对 AI
> 的规模有非常清晰的认识，但对于卖工程机械的人来说，这起初只是销售额的小幅提振，然后不断增长。

If they're producing at close to maximum capacity, they can ration with some combination of longer backlogs and higher prices, i.e. through some combination of higher margins and more predictable earnings.

> **译文：** 如果他们的生产接近最大产能，他们可以通过更长的积压订单和更高的价格来配给供应，即通过更高利润率和更可预测收益的某种组合来实现。

But this also means that a marginal drop in demand falls straight down to them.

> **译文：** 但这也意味着，需求的边际下降会直接传导到他们身上。

The behavioral reason is more fun: some traders in these stocks are AI-pilled, others think of them as cyclicals going through a particularly big cycle.

> **译文：** 行为层面的原因更有趣：这些股票中的一些交易者深受 AI 叙事影响，另一些则将其视为正在经历一个特别大周期的周期性股票。

And these two groups will anchor to completely different P/E ratios.

> **译文：** 而这两群人会锚定在完全不同的市盈率上。

So there's a big air gap between where a pure industrials analyst gets excited about a late-cycle cyclical and where an AI bull gets excited about a critical component provider in the early stages of an unprecedented buildout.

> **译文：** 因此，在纯粹的工业分析师对周期末段的周期性股票感到兴奋的位置，与 AI 多头对前所未有的建设浪潮早期阶段的关键组件供应商感到兴奋的位置之间，存在巨大的空档。

We actually saw some of this when DeepSeek hit: the GPU stocks did badly, but the gas turbine stocks got hit harder.

> **译文：** 我们实际上在 DeepSeek 冲击时看到了一些这种情况：GPU 股票表现糟糕，但燃气轮机股票受到的打击更大。

American Scoundrel: Roy Cohn's Dark Journey from Joe McCarthy to Donald Trump : One feature of technological and economic complexity is that it requires more detailed rules. When people are settling the frontier, it's pointless to enforce some kind of zoning; you only do that once they've clustered in cities and everyone's close by. Similarly, smaller communities need simpler laws around how workers get treated and how contracts are enforced, because social censure is enough to keep people mostly in line. As things get more complicated, you need more rules. This sometimes leads to characters who were born for a more freewheeling atmosphere, and adapt its norms to the letter of the law in a more settled state. And that describes Roy Cohn: anti-communist activist, lawyer, wheeler-dealer, white-collar crook, AIDS victim, and Trump mentor.

> Trump：技术和经济复杂性的一个特征是它需要更详细的规则。当人们在边疆定居时，执行某种分区规划毫无意义；只有当他们聚集在城市里、彼此靠近时，你才会这样做。同样，较小的社区需要更简单的关于工人待遇和合同执行的规则，因为社会谴责足以让人们大体上守规矩。随着事情变得更加复杂，你需要更多规则。这有时会催生出那些为更自由奔放的环境而生的人物，他们将其规范适应于一个更加定型的社会中的法律条文。而这正是对
