Rebecca (00:35-04:26):

I let my emotions drive much of my writing because I treat writing as therapy. Whenever I experience something difficult, heavy, or something that feels like I won’t recover from, one of the first things I do to grasp the negativity is split my psyche into two parts.

> **译文：** 我让情感驱动大部分写作，因为我把写作当作疗愈。每当我经历艰难、沉重或感觉无法恢复的事情时，我抓住这种负面情绪的首要方法之一，就是把我的心灵一分为二。

One part lives through the grief, panic, or fear, while the other floats detached on the outside, curiously observing. I ask myself: “What is my body doing right now? What is the rhythm of my crying? What’s happening to my breathing? How are these thoughts and feelings sitting on my chest?” Then I document them. This doesn’t make the sadness disappear, but articulating how it functions helps me grasp it and makes me legible to myself.

> **译文：** 一部分亲身经历悲伤、恐慌或恐惧，另一部分则超然漂浮在外，好奇地观察。我问自己：“我的身体此刻在做什么？我哭泣的节奏是怎样的？我的呼吸发生了什么变化？这些想法和感受是如何压在我胸口的？”然后我把它们记录下来。这并不会让悲伤消失，但将它的运作方式表达出来，能帮助我理解它，让我对自己变得清晰可读。

To explain how to translate this into narrative and engaging writing, I tell my students that feelings are not abstract concepts merely applied to the body, like love, hatred, or anger. Instead, all feelings begin with a physiological response; they are how your body reacts to information.

> **译文：** 为了解释如何将这种体验转化为叙事性和引人入胜的文字，我告诉学生们，感受并非仅仅附加于身体的抽象概念，比如爱、恨或愤怒。相反，所有感受都始于生理反应；它们是你的身体对信息作出的反应方式。

We didn’t start with theoretical descriptions. For example, if you’re writing about triumph, you could use words like “elation” or “victory,” but these convey nothing material about the character’s internal experience. So, we did an exercise where I passed out kindergarten-level silhouette diagrams of the human body. I instructed them, “Without using any abstractions, describe how triumph or joy feels. Imagine you just received a call about a promotion. Where does that sensation sit in your body? Describe only what is happening on a physical level.”

> **译文：** 我们并非从理论描述开始。例如，如果你要写胜利，你可以用“兴高采烈”或“胜利”这样的词，但这些词并不能传达角色内在体验的任何实质内容。于是，我们做了一个练习：我分发幼儿园级别的人体轮廓简图。我指示他们：“不使用任何抽象词汇，描述胜利或喜悦的感觉。想象你刚接到一个升职电话。那种感觉在你身体的哪个部位？只描述身体层面正在发生的事情。”

People reported an elevated heart rate, palpitations, and butterflies. One student even said, “I feel joy in my toes; my toes start to tingle.” While that might seem unusual, starting at the physical level allows for fascinating lateral connections to confusing and contradictory emotions. This leads us to the complex ways we feel things, because emotions are never one-dimensional. Joy is always mixed with something else.

> **译文：** 有人报告说心跳加速、心悸、心里七上八下。一位学生甚至说：“我在脚趾感受到喜悦；我的脚趾开始发麻。”虽然这看起来不寻常，但从身体层面出发，能引发对混乱而矛盾情感的迷人横向联想。这引导我们走向感受事物的复杂方式，因为情感从来不是单一维度的。喜悦总是与其他东西混合在一起。

In the case of triumph or elation, students also described shortness of breath or a desire to yell. An elevated heart rate, in particular, is also associated with fear and anxiety. About half the class admitted that when they receive genuinely good news, they immediately become scared. They worry about when the other shoe will drop, how they might mess up the opportunity, whether it’s truly what they wanted, or how they can replicate the success.

> **译文：** 就胜利或兴高采烈而言，学生们还描述了呼吸急促或想要大喊的冲动。尤其是心跳加速，也与恐惧和焦虑相关。大约一半的学生承认，当他们收到真正的好消息时，会立刻感到害怕。他们担心什么时候会有坏消息降临，担心自己会如何搞砸这个机会，担心这是否真的是自己想要的，或者担心如何复制这种成功。

This reveals a much more interesting “puddle” of multiple feelings occurring at once. This complexity is far more engaging to read than simply, “Bob got the call that he was promoted and felt great.” That gives the reader nothing.

> **译文：** 这揭示了一个更有趣的“水洼”——多种感受同时涌现。这种复杂性远比简单地写“鲍勃接到升职电话，感觉棒极了”更引人入胜。后者让读者一无所获。

What did you feel you needed to impart to your students to help them improve this skill? Is it merely a byproduct of repeated practice, or are there specific blockers that inhibit people from writing well about their own emotions?

Rebecca (04:44-06:44):

Two things are at play. First, it is observational. Ninety percent of good writing involves being very attentive to what is happening around you and writing with specificity about those observations, rather than relying on the theoretical. Basic practices, such as asking yourself throughout the day, “Where is this feeling sitting in my body? How am I physically responding to this?” already push your writing to a level past what a reader could easily imagine for themselves.

> **译文：** 有两件事在起作用。第一是观察力。好的写作有百分之九十在于对周围发生的事情高度关注，并以具体性记录这些观察，而非依赖理论。基本练习，比如整天问自己：“这种感觉在我身体的哪个部位？我对此的身体反应是什么？”就已经能把你的写作推到一个读者仅凭想象难以企及的高度。

The second is mixing your primary colors, to use a metaphor. It is not interesting when it is just red, blue, or yellow. I showed my students a diagram—an emotions color wheel—and we played with mixing opposites. For instance, fear and courage. I asked, “How could those both sit in the body at once? How could they be part of the same moment?” We explored love and revulsion, or joy and triumph versus panic and anxiety.

> **译文：** 第二是混合你的原色，打个比方来说。仅仅红色、蓝色或黄色并不有趣。我向学生展示了一张图表——一个情绪色轮——我们玩混合对立的游戏。例如，恐惧与勇气。我问：“这两者如何能同时存在于身体中？它们如何成为同一时刻的一部分？”我们探索了爱与厌恶，或者喜悦与胜利对比恐慌与焦虑。

We are always feeling multiple things at once, even in moments that are supposed to be pure and singular, such as getting married. I recall the moment my husband and I had our “first look” at the top of some library stairs. I tapped his shoulder, and he turned around. That should have been a moment of pure love, intimacy, and joy.

> **译文：** 我们总是同时感受多种情绪，即使在那些本应纯粹而单一的瞬间，比如结婚。我记得我和丈夫在图书馆楼梯顶上进行“第一眼”的那一刻。我轻拍他的肩膀，他转过身来。那本应是一个纯粹的爱、亲密和喜悦的时刻。

Yet, as I held him, I was also terrified—not of getting married, as I was incredibly excited—but because when you love someone so deeply, you are also scared of loss. Loving means dread, fear, and panic that this can be taken from you. Reaching for those opposites and having them collide in a singular experience always makes a scene far more fascinating.

> **译文：** 然而，当我抱着他时，我也感到恐惧——不是对结婚的恐惧，因为我无比兴奋——而是因为当你深爱一个人时，你也会害怕失去。爱意味着恐惧、害怕和恐慌，担心这一切会被夺走。触及这些对立面，让它们在单一体验中碰撞，总是能让场景变得远为迷人。

David (06:45-06:52):

You spoke about the importance of being an observer of the world. So far, you’ve discussed observing yourself. What about observing others?

> **译文：** 你谈到了作为世界观察者的重要性。到目前为止，你讨论的是观察自己。那观察他人呢？

Rebecca (06:53-09:14):

That’s tough for writers. Many people become writers because we are introverts. However, after college, I developed an attitude that every social interaction is interesting data gathering.

> **译文：** 这对作家来说很难。许多人成为作家是因为我们是内向者。然而，大学毕业后，我养成了一种态度：每一次社交互动都是有趣的数据收集。

This springs from an argument in ethics. Iris Murdoch, a philosopher I admire, argues in her essay The Sovereignty of Good that morality, goodness, and ethics come from stepping outside of yourself. It’s a vanishing of the ego when you view your own actions from someone else’s perspective. When you free yourself of petty biases, when you remove the blinders of your subjective experience of the world, that’s where goodness and compassion begin.

> **译文：** 这源于伦理学中的一个论点。我钦佩的哲学家艾丽丝·默多克（Iris Murdoch）在她的文章《善的至高无上》中论证，道德、善良和伦理来自于走出自我。当你从他人的视角看待自己的行为时，那是一种自我的消融。当你摆脱狭隘的偏见，当你摘下主观体验世界的眼罩，善良和同情便由此开始。

A parallel attitude is equally important for good craft. If you write solely from the confines of your own head, you might have one good story or novel in you. The best writers, however, are able to erase themselves and constantly ask how other people experience the world, and how they hold perspectives different from their own.

> **译文：** 一种平行的态度对精湛的技艺同样重要。如果你只在自己头脑的局限内写作，你可能只有一两个好故事或好小说可写。然而，最优秀的作家能够消解自我，不断追问他人如何体验世界，以及他们如何持有与自己不同的视角。

I try hard with all my main characters to give them flaws, perspectives, or ideologies that I don’t hold—alternate responses to questions I’m asking myself. You only gain that from constantly being in the world. This involves going to parties and talking to people, but also challenging yourself to learn surprising things from experts in fields outside your own.

> **译文：** 我努力为我所有的主要角色赋予我所不持有的缺陷、视角或意识形态——对我正在追问自己的问题给出不同的答案。你只有通过不断置身于世界中才能获得这些。这包括参加聚会、与人交谈，也包括挑战自己，从自己领域之外的专家那里学到令人惊讶的东西。

This is part of why, even as my career grows, I always want to be a teacher and remain in the classroom. I never want to go full-time as a writer, because then I’d be alone with my own expertise and thoughts, which would quickly stagnate. I prefer being in places where I’m surprised and challenged, where I am not the expert. This allows me to observe how others learn, research, and form opinions about the world.

> **译文：** 这也是为什么，即便我的职业生涯不断发展，我始终想成为一名教师，留在教室里。我从未想过全职写作，因为那样我会独自面对自己的专业知识和想法，很快就会停滞不前。我更愿意待在自己会被惊喜和挑战的地方，在那里我不是专家。这让我能够观察他人如何学习、研究，并形成对世界的看法。

Rebecca (09:37-13:46):

My next fantasy novel is a retelling of the Faust mythos, exploring art, politics, mass appeal, and mechanical reproduction. It’s also a love letter to different art forms, and what we lose when we abandon that human creativity—the slow, human process of creating art—which is highly relevant right now.

> **译文：** 我的下一部奇幻小说是对浮士德神话的重述，探讨艺术、政治、大众吸引力和机械复制。它也是一封写给不同艺术形式的情书，以及当我们放弃那种人类创造力——缓慢而人性化的艺术创作过程——时我们失去了什么，这在当下极具现实意义。

The issue is that I only write prose. I’m not a painter, a visual artist, or a singer. I realized I needed to deeply understand these different art forms to write compellingly about them, especially from the perspective of characters whose worldviews are shaped by their unique artistic practices.

> **译文：** 问题在于我只写散文。我不是画家、视觉艺术家或歌手。我意识到我需要深入理解这些不同的艺术形式，才能写出令人信服的作品，尤其是从那些世界观由其独特艺术实践所塑造的角色视角出发。

I started attending life drawing classes. I went to a three-week workshop as an introduction to figure drawing, and afterwards, I regularly returned to an artist collective near Boston that hosts weekly nude figure drawing sessions. You’re there with your sketchbook, and a model poses nude.

> **译文：** 我开始参加人体素描课程。我参加了一个为期三周的工作坊作为人物素描入门，之后我定期回到波士顿附近的一个艺术家团体，那里每周举办裸体人物素描活动。你带着速写本坐在那里，模特裸体摆出姿势。

The first time, I hadn’t realized this was part of the course. The model came in, dropped their clothes, and struck a pose like the Vitruvian Man. It’s amazing how quickly you get used to it. There’s nothing weird, embarrassing, or remotely sexual about it. You’re simply gazing at a fascinating human body, with all its curves and complexities, trying to capture the relationship between its important angles.

> **译文：** 第一次去的时候，我并不知道这是课程的一部分。模特走进来，脱下衣服，摆出维特鲁威人那样的姿势。令人惊讶的是，你很快就会习惯。这没有任何奇怪、尴尬或带有性意味的地方。你只是在凝视一具迷人的人体，带着它所有的曲线和复杂性，试图捕捉其重要角度之间的关系。

One key lesson was learning to “unsee”—to reduce the visual field to core dimensions like values, lines, or proportions. Beginning artists—and I’m still one—often try to capture everything, which results in a blob because they lack mastery over the relationships between those elements. My drawing instructor taught us to capture values by squinting a little, letting our vision blur, and focusing only on what was darker or lighter, and the various shades and values in relation to each other. He asked, “Where do we focus first to place heavy, dark values? Where is the light coming from, and where might we use an eraser to create highlights?”

> **译文：** 一个关键的课程是学会“视而不见”——将视野简化为核心维度，如明暗、线条或比例。初学艺术家——我现在仍然是——往往试图捕捉一切，结果画出一团模糊，因为他们无法掌控这些元素之间的关系。我的素描老师教我们通过微微眯眼来捕捉明暗，让视线模糊，只专注于哪些部分更暗或更亮，以及各种色调和明暗之间的相互关系。他问道：“我们首先应该关注哪里来放置厚重、深色的阴影？光线从哪里来？我们可以在哪里用橡皮擦出高光？”

Learning to reduce an object to its basic dimensions was something I had never done visually before. I then applied this to the perspective of a painter character, creating a fantastic and intimate scene where the model fails to appear. He asks his friend—for whom he holds complex, possibly romantic feelings—to step in and model instead, offering payment for the session.

> **译文：** 学会将物体简化为基本维度是我以前从未在视觉上做过的事情。然后我将这一点应用到一个画家角色的视角中，创造了一个奇妙而亲密的场景：模特没有出现。他请他的朋友——他对这位朋友怀有复杂且可能带有浪漫色彩的感情——代替模特，并为这次写生付费。

The intensity of being observed, broken down into parts—not for who you are, but for where a shadow falls on your face, or the geometric relationship between your nipples and your crotch. Undergoing that as the model, from the perspective of someone you care about and want to see you fully, created a rich, emotional scene. It was full of friction and insecurity, exploring how they looked at each other and truly saw each other. Metaphorically, this ties into the entire storyline’s theme of failures in perception and communication.

> **译文：** 被观察的强烈感受，被分解成各个部分——不是因为你这个人是谁，而是因为阴影落在你脸上的位置，或者你的乳头与胯部之间的几何关系。作为模特，从你在乎且希望他完全看到你的人的角度来经历这一切，创造了一个丰富而充满情感的场景。它充满了摩擦和不安全感，探索了他们如何注视彼此、真正看见彼此。隐喻性地，这与整个故事线中感知与沟通失败的母题相呼应。

> **译文：** 这与我被教导的一致。你学会画出实际存在的东西，而不是你以为自己看到的东西。这是一个重要的区别，因为我们“看到”的很多东西实际上是由记忆填补的。

> **译文：** 例如，当我转头看向房间的那一侧——我之前并没有在观察——我可能只捕捉到那里真实存在的10%。我的大脑然后从记忆中填补其余部分。我以前见过的房间、沙发和桌子会自动从我已有的数据中填充进来。

A similar phenomenon occurs when writing prose. If you stay only within your subjective experience, your own memories, or your preconceived notions about the world, you automatically populate some of the content. What isn’t already in my data set that I need to observe, process, and put on the page?

> **译文：** 写散文时也会发生类似的现象。如果你只停留在自己的主观体验、自己的记忆或对世界的先入之见中，你就会自动填充一些内容。但你没有真正观察；你没有看到实际存在的东西。学习视觉艺术教会了我专注于实际呈现的东西。我错过了什么？我的数据集中还没有什么是我需要观察、处理并写到纸上的？

AI doesn’t do that. There’s no spark; it’s simply a facsimile of many other created things that people tend to like. > **译文：** 人工智能做不到这些。没有火花；它只是许多其他受人喜爱之物的复制品。它也许能骗你一次，但如果没有持续的人类输入，它永远无法突破极限，也无法为艺术创新创造可能性。这就是为什么我对人工智能取代作家、动画师或插画师既不特别感兴趣，也不感到担忧。

> **译文：** 关于缓慢这一点，可以追溯到Verlyn Klinkenborg的观点：任何过于轻易呈现的东西都不会突破界限。人工智能只能提供现成的词语来表达现成的想法，因为它是在现有数据集上训练的。

> **译文：** 他的职业生涯始于用俄语写作，他通晓多种语言，后来移居美国。在那里，他决定精通英语，并用他的第二语言创作出了杰作。在每一处，你都能看到他对某些英语词汇组合的奇特之处和可能性的着迷。他不断地实验头韵、押韵和令人捧腹的并置。

> **译文：** 他以一种母语为英语的作家不会采用、或者说对他们来说很难做到的方式去玩弄、拉伸和创新英语，因为他拥有局外人的视角。他的书是丰富、有趣、极具娱乐性的语言实验，同时又保持着可读性。

AI will never achieve that kind of play because it doesn’t view English as a foreign body to be brute-forced into something new, to be turned into building blocks for unprecedented creations. AI models are functionally designed to reproduce what everyone has already done. While the output can sound good because humans have done amazing things, you won’t get your next Pale Fire from an LLM.

> **译文：** 人工智能永远无法实现那种文字游戏，因为它不把英语视为一个需要通过强力改造而变成新事物、变成前所未有的创作积木的外来体。人工智能模型在功能上就是为了复现所有人已经做过的事情而设计的。虽然输出听起来可能不错，因为人类已经做出了惊人的成就，但你不会从大语言模型那里得到你的下一部《微暗的火》。

> **译文：** 我们来谈谈你的草稿写作过程。你会经历多轮草稿，往往始于一个想法，最终却以别的东西收尾。请带我回顾一下这个过程。

Rebecca (41:04-45:16):

It’s a horrible process, not something I would advise any of my students to do, but it’s the only way I know how to write. I start with a “word vomit draft,” or a “zero draft.” This stage is really about brainstorming and research, feeling my way around the story to discover which moments are emotionally resonant and where the beating heart of the story lies.

> **译文：** 这是一个糟糕的过程，我不会建议我的任何学生这样做，但这是我所知道的唯一写作方式。我从“文字呕吐稿”或“零稿”开始。这个阶段实际上关乎头脑风暴和研究，在故事中摸索前行，去发现哪些时刻在情感上能引起共鸣，以及故事跳动的核心在哪里。

> **译文：** 我不按大纲写作，也不会先列大纲。我只是从大量的研究开始。以我目前正在创作的小说为例，整个第一部分设定在19世纪末的德国。它探讨了普法战争、德国民族主义，以及古典音乐在该语境中的作用。

I read monograph after monograph on these topics. Every time I encounter an interesting historical development, an encounter, or something surprising and “crunchy,” I build a scene around it. I might weave it into a line of dialogue or create a brief character interaction to introduce that friction. These scenes aren’t linked chronologically; there’s no order or purpose. These are just things that interest me.

> **译文：** 我一本接一本地阅读关于这些主题的专著。每当我遇到一个有趣的历史发展、一次相遇，或某些令人惊讶且“有嚼劲”的东西，我就会围绕它构建一个场景。我可能会把它编织进一句对话中，或者创造一段简短的人物互动来引入那种摩擦。这些场景在时间上并不连贯；没有顺序，也没有目的。它们只是让我感兴趣的东西。

> **译文：** 我凭直觉行事；我跟着感觉走。我的大量研究和头脑风暴就是去发现什么是有光彩的东西，像喜鹊一样把它收集起来，然后放进巢里。在这个阶段，我的写作过程就像向读者呈献随机的物件：“我找到了一枚五分硬币，我找到了一个用过的卫生棉条，我找到了一颗纽扣。”

> **译文：** 一旦我有了那堆收集物，我就会退后一步问：“这里的故事在哪里？那张从石头里探出的脸在哪里？”这就像做一名雕塑家，辨别出需要从这团混乱中解放出来的东西。拥有一份零稿比仅仅做研究并在抽象层面思考要清晰得多。

That’s where the technical structural work begins. I start considering plot structures, possible templates, and global choices like the distance between the reader, narrator, story, and characters. > **译文：** 这时，技术性的结构工作就开始了。我开始考虑情节结构、可能的模板，以及一些全局性的选择，比如读者、叙述者、故事和人物之间的距离。我决定视角——现在时还是过去时——以及如何操控时间顺序来呈现那个慢慢成形故事。

Even this will be incredibly messy and filled with placeholder sentences. Character faces and descriptions, for example, come last for me, because a person’s visual appearance is often the least interesting thing about them, just as it is with people I meet in real life.

> **译文：** 然后，我会回过头去，基于所有有趣的时刻写一份逐拍大纲。之后，我会尝试写一份按时间顺序排列的草稿。即使这一稿也会非常混乱，充满了占位句。例如，人物的面孔和外形描写对我来说总是放在最后，因为一个人的视觉外观往往是最无趣的部分，就像我在现实生活中遇到的人一样。

In the first pass, there won’t be comments on architectural style, furniture, or drape color. In the final pass, I make intentional choices about those details.

> **译文：** 我把这个初始阶段描述为动画制作过程中的图层，在很长一段时间里你只有“棍子上的球”。在后续的几轮修改中，我会充实内容，包括场景设定。第一稿中不会出现关于建筑风格、家具或窗帘颜色的描写。在最后一稿中，我才会对这些细节做出刻意的选择。

It’s an extremely time-consuming process. It also means that, until the very end, I don’t have pages I’d be comfortable sharing with anyone. This is why it’s so difficult when people ask, “Oh, you’ve been working on this for a year; can I see the first few chapters?” I feel like I have everything and nothing at the same time. I can say, “Give me a month and you’ll have the entire novel,” but until all those polishing passes are complete, it’s a horrendous Frankenstein mess.

> **译文：** 这是一个极其耗时的过程。这也意味着，直到最后一刻，我都没有可以放心与任何人分享的页面。这就是为什么当人们问“哦，你已经在这本书上花了一年时间了；我能看看前几章吗？”时会如此困难。我感觉自己同时拥有一切又一无所有。我可以说：“给我一个月时间，你就能看到整本小说了。”但在所有打磨的轮次完成之前，它就是一个可怕的弗兰肯斯坦式的烂摊子。

David (45:16-45:18):

How do dreams play into that process?

Rebecca (45:19-46:24):

I often won’t write a scene if it feels too artificial, if it seems like I’m constructing everything that’s happening. If I have to consciously ask myself, “What are the characters doing here? What are their goals? What does the atmosphere feel like?” and answer those questions from scratch in the moment, then my subconscious isn’t ready to approach that scene yet.

> **译文：** 如果某个场景感觉太刻意，感觉我是在凭空构建正在发生的一切，我通常就不会去写它。如果我必须有意识地问自己：“这些人物在这里做什么？他们的目标是什么？氛围感觉如何？”并在当下从零开始回答这些问题，那么我的潜意识还没有准备好去接近那个场景。

Instead, a scene must feel like recounting a daydream or a previous memory of a life I never had for it to come organically. This comes from brainstorming, feeding the inputs, and allowing the ideas to linger in my subconscious long enough that I’m fully immersed with the characters, feeling what they feel, and sensing the drive towards the conflict emerging in the scene.

> **译文：** 相反，一个场景必须感觉像是在复述一个白日梦，或者一段我从未经历过的生活的记忆，它才能自然地涌现。这来自于头脑风暴、输入素材的喂养，以及让想法在潜意识中停留足够长的时间，直到我完全沉浸在人物之中，感受他们所感受的，并感知到场景中正在浮现的冲突驱动力。

I rely heavily on the instincts that come from dreams and daydreams before I allow myself to approach anything from a more analytical, bird’s-eye perspective.

> **译文：** 在我允许自己从更具分析性、鸟瞰式的视角来处理任何东西之前，我非常依赖来自梦境和白日梦的直觉。

David (46:25-46:28):

Rebecca (1:01:46-1:04:37):

The main takeaway, or the attitude I hope everyone leaves my classroom with, is an attention to the rhetorical choices in all the texts they read. I want them to learn to dissect a piece of fiction, even one they picked up for enjoyment. They shouldn’t see it as a fluid delivery of feelings, a story, or an idea, but rather as a series of thousands, millions, of little choices made on every level to create a certain effect or to stimulate the reader’s brain in a particular way.

> **译文：** 我希望每个人离开我课堂时带走的主要收获，或者说态度，是对他们所读所有文本中修辞选择的关注。我希望他们学会剖析一篇小说，即使是一本为了消遣而拿起的小说。他们不应该把它看作是一种情感、故事或想法的流畅传递，而应该看作是一系列成千上万、数以百万计的选择，在每一个层面上做出，以创造某种效果或以特定方式刺激读者的头脑。

That is what good writing is: understanding those thousands of choices and then learning to employ them deliberately yourself. Once you’re reading and writing at that level, you move past the attitude of, “This book makes me feel good, though I don’t really understand why,” or “Writing this story just made me feel good; it poured out of me, and if it felt good, that’s great.” These are mystical, vague, and extremely unhelpful ways to think about reading and writing.

> **译文：** 这就是好写作的意义：理解那成千上万的选择，然后学会自己刻意地运用它们。一旦你在那个层面上阅读和写作，你就超越了“这本书让我感觉很好，虽然我不太明白为什么”或“写这个故事只是让我感觉很好；它从我身上涌出来，如果感觉好，那就很棒”的态度。这些是思考阅读和写作的神秘、模糊且极其无益的方式。

I believe this also contributes to myths that writing is fundamentally unteachable, or that there’s some special something separating great writers and efficient storytellers from people with no writing talent. What I truly try to do, especially in my beginner-level courses, is instill the attitude that writing is a technical activity. You are a craftsman, tinkering with words.

> **译文：** 我相信这也助长了一些神话，即写作从根本上说是无法教授的，或者存在某种特殊的东西将伟大的作家和高效的故事讲述者与没有写作天赋的人区分开来。我真正努力去做的，尤其是在我的初级课程中，是灌输这样一种态度：写作是一项技术活动。你是一个工匠，在文字上精雕细琢。

> **译文：** 有些人可能比其他人更有创新性，或者对工具有更本能的掌握。然而，归根结底，你可以看一段文字，把它分解成各个组成部分，并问自己它在每个层面是如何运作的。你可以学会自己做这件事。这就是持续教育的意义所在。

Even if they don’t become professional writers—the majority of my students won’t pursue writing as a career, but rather to scratch an itch or fulfill a humanities requirement—this training will still improve their reading. It will make the experience as an audience member so much more interesting when you can see how the sausage is made and understand the choices involved.

> **译文：** 即使他们不成为职业作家——我的大多数学生不会以写作为职业，而是为了满足兴趣或完成人文学科的要求——这种训练仍然会提高他们的阅读水平。当你能看到“香肠是如何制作的”并理解其中的选择时，作为受众的体验会变得有趣得多。
