The AI industry’s frontier models require vast amounts of computing power, electricity, and money, which puts them out of reach for much of the world. But in fact, smaller, specialized models may be far more useful in many settings.

> **译文：** AI行业的前沿模型需要庞大的算力、电力和资金支持，这使得世界上大部分地区难以企及。但事实上，在许多应用场景中，更小、更专用的模型可能远比大模型有用得多。

In our featured article, Brooklyn-based science journalist David Berreby looks at AI built for parts of the world where broadband and electricity can’t be taken for granted. His opening example is a handheld scanner used to detect counterfeit medications across Africa and Asia. When a slow connection to a distant U.S. data center made the device nearly unusable, its developers created a smaller version of the AI model that could run entirely on a phone.

> **译文：** 在我们的专题文章中，布鲁克林的科学记者David Berreby关注了那些不能将宽带和电力视为理所当然的地区所构建的AI。他开篇的例子是一款手持扫描仪，用于在非洲和亚洲检测假药。当与遥远的美国数据中心连接缓慢导致设备几乎无法使用时，开发者创建了一个更小版本的AI模型，可以完全在手机上运行。

But Berreby thinks the appeal of this approach extends far beyond places with limited infrastructure. Large general-purpose AI models have become bound up with fears about jobs, human relationships, resource use, and even human survival. By contrast, he told me, “None of these fears arise out of an AI that does one thing well, and stays in its lane.” AI’s physical footprint matters too: Data centers draw protests over their electricity and water use, while small models can run quietly on devices people already own.

> **译文：** 但Berreby认为，这种方法的吸引力远不止于基础设施有限的地区。大型通用AI模型已经与人们对就业、人际关系、资源使用乃至人类生存的担忧紧密相连。相比之下，他告诉我：“这些担忧都不会源于一个能把一件事做好、并且安分守己的AI。”AI的物理足迹也很重要：数据中心因电力和水资源消耗而招致抗议，而小型模型可以在人们已有的设备上安静运行。

Berreby increasingly thinks this kind of specialized AI will be “the AI that lasts and changes people’s lives,” not only in the developing world but everywhere. He points to systems that can identify disease in a crop, help an autistic child communicate, or create a model of a patient’s heart. His examples suggest a different way to measure AI progress: by what a system can do reliably and affordably for someone who needs it.

> **译文：** Berreby越来越认为，这种专用AI将成为“持久存在并改变人们生活的AI”，不仅是在发展中国家，而是在所有地方。他指出了能够识别作物病害、帮助自闭症儿童沟通或创建患者心脏模型的系统。他的例子提出了一种衡量AI进展的不同方式：即一个系统能为需要它的人可靠且经济地做到什么。

Conversations About AI

Should Researchers Write Papers for AI Instead of People?

> **译文：** 研究人员应该为AI而不是为人写论文吗？

Should scientists stop writing papers for people—and start presenting their research in an “AI native” format? Jiachen Liu, cofounder of the Agent Native Research Lab and lead author of “The Last Human-Written Paper,” talks to IEEE Spectrum about a proposed alternative that would capture the data, code, failed attempts, and decisions that traditional papers leave out.

> **译文：** 科学家是否应该停止为人写论文——转而以“AI原生”格式呈现他们的研究？Agent Native Research Lab联合创始人、论文《最后一篇人类撰写的论文》的第一作者Jiachen Liu与IEEE Spectrum讨论了这一提议的替代方案，该方案将捕捉传统论文遗漏的数据、代码、失败尝试和决策过程。

IEEE Spectrum: You and your colleagues say the traditional scientific paper has fundamental flaws from AI’s point of view. Can you explain what those are?

> **译文：** IEEE Spectrum：您和您的同事认为，从AI的角度来看，传统科学论文存在根本性缺陷。您能解释一下这些缺陷是什么吗？

Jiachen Liu: One is the “storytelling tax.” Once we write everything into a paper, 80 percent of the information about the work is lost. We only write down the last 20 percent. All the process, a lot of important decision-making, the failures, the attempts that didn’t work out, they are all gone. In my work, I might spend a lot of time on fine-tuning a small component, maybe just a parameter or several lines of code to make the system perform better. Yet none of that is shown in my final paper. Someone can read the paper, think the work is great, but they won’t learn what is actually the trick that makes it perform better on a certain workload. So many side branches get left out in creating the story of how the work was done.

> **译文：** Jiachen Liu：一个是“叙事税”。一旦我们把所有内容写成论文，关于这项工作的80%信息就丢失了。我们只写下了最后的20%。所有的过程、大量重要的决策、失败、没有成功的尝试，全都消失了。在我的工作中，我可能会花大量时间微调一个小组件，也许只是一个参数或几行代码，让系统表现得更好。但这些都不会出现在我的最终论文中。有人读了论文，觉得工作很棒，但他们不会学到真正让它在特定工作负载下表现更好的诀窍。在构建工作如何完成的叙事过程中，太多旁支被遗漏了。
