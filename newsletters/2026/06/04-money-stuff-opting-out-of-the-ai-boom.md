Why is there not a no-AI index fund?

I mean, not literally. Keep Nvidia and Alphabet and the flavoring company and 
the toilet company; every company is an AI company now. But:
我的意思是，并非字面意义上的。英伟达、Alphabet、调味品公司，还有……

厕所公司；现在每家公司都成了人工智能公司。但是：

 * In the next few months, SpaceX, Anthropic and OpenAI will all probably go 
public at massive valuations. 
 * They will be fast-tracked into the major stock indexes, because those 
indexes are designed toreflect the stock market <https://bloom.bg/4g000xv>, and 
reflecting the stock market, in 2027, will absolutely require big allocations 
to those three companies. 
 * A lot of people are happy about this, because they like AI, Elon Musk, 
rockets, etc., and are excited to finally be able to own 
SpaceX/Anthropic/OpenAI in their index funds. 
 * A lot of people, however, are unhappy about this, because they do not like 
AI, Elon Musk, etc., and/or because they are morally offended that the big 
indexes are adjusting their rules to fast-track these companies, and/or because 
they think that the whole process is about pumping up an AI bubble and then
dumping it on index investors <https://bloom.bg/4o5zXar>. 
 * If you’re in the happy camp, life is easy: Your index funds will buy these 
stocks in fairly short order (notthat short, in the case of the S&P 500), and 
you will own them. 
 * If you’re in the sad camp, there is very little you can do about it. Your 
retirement money is probably in index funds. You will have to write a sternly 
disappointed letter to, like, Vanguard, saying “I have been a loyal user of 
index funds for 20 years but I cannot abide SpaceX, please send me my money 
back.” And then invest it in stocks you pick that are not SpaceX. (Not 
investing advice!) 
This is a market failure, all those stern letters are inefficient, and someone 
—Vanguard and BlackRock — should offer, like, the Total Market But Not SpaceX 
or OpenAI or Anthropic Fund, or the S&P 500 But None of Those Guys Fund, or 
maybe even the S&P 500 But Under the Old Rules Where You Had to Be Profitable 
<https://bloom.bg/4g000xv> and Wait 12 Months to Get Into the Index Fund. I

ESG-flavored versions of their index funds. The ESG version is, like, “the S&P 
but without the bad companies,” based on some set of ESG exclusion rules. The 
exclusion rule here is very easy! “No SpaceX, no Anthropic, no OpenAI.”

Exotics

Theory would tell you that the expected financial return from owning a sports 
team is low. Owning a sports team is cool and a lot of people want to do it, so 
they will pay more for sports teams than would be justified by the teams’ 
expected cash flows\. Things that everyone want trade at a high price, which 
means that they trade at a low expected return. [1]  <> The cost of capital of 
a sports team is low.
理论会告诉你，拥有运动队的预期财务回报很低。拥有运动队很酷，很多人都想拥有，所以他们愿意为运动队支付高于运动队预期现金流所能支撑的价格。人人都想要的东西价格很高，这意味着它们的预期回报很低。[1] <> 运动队的资本成本很低。

In fact, sports teams trade at such high prices that they are increasingly 
syndicated: Instead of one rich guy buying a sports team, teams now might be 
owned by multiple special-purpose vehicles, funds, etc. One might wonder, like, 
what is going on there. Owning a sports team is very cool — you get to hang out 
with the players and tell them what to do, etc. — but is owning 1% of an SPV 
that owns 13% of a sports team proportionally cool? One possible answer is that 
the joy and cachet of telling everyone that you own a sports team is fully 
divisible into fractional shares, so in fact syndicated equity financing of 
sports teams makes sense even at high luxury prices and low expected financial 
returns. Another possibility is that everyone is somewhat confused.

分割成零碎的股份，因此，即使价格高昂且预期财务回报低，体育俱乐部的联合股权融资实际上也是合理的。另一种可能是，大家都对此感到有些困惑。

Similarly, there are businesses that sell fractional ownership of art. If you 
own 100% of a painting, then (1) maybe you can resell it at a profit one day 
and (2) you can hang it over your couch now. The price you pay for it should 
reflect both benefits. If you own 0.1% of a painting, then (1) maybe the person 
managing the investment for you will resell it at a profit one day and give you 
0.1% of the proceeds but (2) no couch. It seems strictly worse. And yet the 
business exists, implying that the cheapest funding for art might be not one 
rich person with a wall to fill but a bunch of investors with purely financial 
motivatios.
同样，有些公司出售艺术品的部分所有权。如果你拥有一幅画作100%的所有权，那么（1）也许有一天你可以把它转售获利；（2）现在你就可以把它挂在沙发上方。你支付的价格应该反映出这两种好处。如果你拥有一幅画作0.1%的所有权，那么（1）也许有一天，为你管理投资的人会把它转售获利，并给你0.1%的收益，但（2）你却买不到沙发。这看起来明显更糟。然而，这种商业模式依然存在，这意味着艺术品最便宜的融资方式可能并非来自一位需要装饰墙面的富人，而是来自一群纯粹出于经济动机的投资者。

you generally have (1) a levered exposure to home price appreciation plus (2) 
somewhere to live. An investor who buys a portion of your home equity gets the 
levered exposure to home prices, but doesn’t generally move in with you. It is 
pretty clear that the investors in these products arenot confused: Those

homeowners complain. (The homeowners are confused.) But the products have to be 
structured in an investor-favorable way, because — as theory would tell you — 
the expected financial returns from home price appreciation are low, because 
people do not buy houses principally to get price appreciation. (They buy them 
to get a place to live.) Simply selling 20% of your home equity to a rational 
economic investor, at the price you paid, wouldn’t work. You need to give the 
investor something extra, to make up for the fact that you’re not giving her a 
place to live.

Alpha capture

The big multi-manager multistrategy hedge funds are essentially in the 
business of evaluating, hiring, motivating, supervising and firing portfolio 
managers. There are people in the world (portfolio managers) who can do some 
trades (picking tech stocks, index arbitrage, Treasury/futures basis, 
whatever), and they are inputs to the process. The process is acquiring the 
right mix of those people in the right quantities. The hedge fund has a series 
of quantitative and qualitative mechanisms that ultimately produce something 
like “a $1 billion bet on Alice and a $2 billion bet on Bob and a $3 billion 
bet on Claire and Dave is fired.”
大型多经理多策略对冲基金的本质业务是评估、招聘、激励、监督和解雇投资组合经理。世界上有一些人（投资组合经理）可以进行一些交易（例如挑选科技股、指数套利、国债/期货基差交易等等），他们是这一过程的投入。这个过程的关键在于找到合适数量的合适组合的人才。对冲基金拥有一系列定量和定性机制，最终产生类似这样的结果：“投资10亿美元给Alice，投资20亿美元给Bob，投资30亿美元给Claire，Dave被解雇了。”

Any quantitative hedge fund manager knows that <https://bloom.bg/4dOMMCx> your

decisions you make with that skill. If your alpha comes from being very good at 
evaluating portfolio managers, you should try to find more portfolio managers 
to evaluate. The obvious way to do this is to scale up your hedge fund — 
instead of having five portfolio managers, hire 100 — but that is expensive. 
There are other ways. Bloomberg’sLiza Tetley and Nishant Kumar report 
<https://bloom.bg/439H1sW>:
任何量化对冲基金经理都知道，你的超额收益与以下两点成正比：(1) 你的技能；(2) 你运用该技能做出的独立决策的数量。如果你的超额收益来自于非常擅长评估投资组合经理，那么你应该尝试寻找更多投资组合经理进行评估。最直接的方法是扩大你的对冲基金规模——与其雇佣五位投资组合经理，不如雇佣一百位——但这成本很高。

SpaceX IPO price

The dollar price per share of a company’s initial public offering is somewhat 
arbitrary: While it is still private, the company can split or combine its 
shares however it wants to get a price per share that is most appealing to 
public investors. You don’t want to go public at $5,000 per share, or $5, and 
you’d never have to. A 2-for-1 pre-IPO stock split would get SpaceXso close to 
$69 <https://bloom.bg/4ujbkbN>:
公司首次公开募股（IPO）的每股价格在某种程度上是任意的：在公司仍处于私有状态时，它可以随意拆分或合并股票，以获得对公众投资者最具吸引力的每股价格。你肯定不想以每股 5000 美元或 5 美元的价格上市，而且你也永远不必这样做。SpaceX 在 IPO 前进行 1 比 2 的股票拆分，其股价将接近 69 美元。

SpaceX is planning to offer shares at $135 apiece to raise $75 billion in its

Musk rejects another Wall Street convention by setting a fixed price ahead of 
the marketing phase of the deal. ...
据知情人士透露，SpaceX计划以每股135美元的价格发行股票，通过首次公开募股（IPO）筹集750亿美元。埃隆·马斯克打破了华尔街的又一项惯例，在交易的营销阶段之前就设定了固定价格。

The move adds to the unconventional aspects of a deal that’s set to be the 
biggest ever listing. Most companies listing in the US typically announce a

IPO in 2020.
此举更增添了这笔交易的非常规性，而这笔交易也注定将成为有史以来规模最大的IPO。在美国上市的大多数公司通常会在投资者推介会上公布一个价格区间，只有极少数小型公司会选择固定价格。这种发行方式在欧洲和亚洲更为常见。例如，Hut Group在2020年于伦敦进行的IPO中筹集了25亿美元。

Still I suppose:

 * You’re not getting $1.5 trillion of demand. This is the biggest IPO ever by 
quite a distance, and maybe that undercuts the usual gamesmanship of an IPO. An 
investor in a hot IPO who wants $100 million might put in an order for $500 
million, expecting to be cut back. An investor in a $75 billion IPO who wants 
$1 billion might be more hesitant to put in an order for $5 billion, because 
that’s just too big. It’s possible that reducing the number of variables in 
this IPO will make it easier to get done. 
 * As a corporate finance matter, knowing how many shares you’re going to 
issue and how much money you’re going to raise is a lot more convenient than 
having a wide range and finding out the answer on the night of pricing. If 
you’re immediately spending that $75 billion on flinging data centers into 
space, it helps to know that you’re getting exactly $75 billion. 
 * The typical IPO process is a negotiation between investors and the company, 
mediated by the banks. Plausibly Elon Musk simply doesn’t want to haggle over 
price with a bunch of investors. Just tell them the price and wait; fear of 
missing out should do the rest. 
* 你不可能获得1.5万亿美元的需求。这是有史以来规模最大的IPO，
而且差距相当大，这或许会削弱IPO中常见的博弈。

一个在750亿美元IPO中想要投资10亿美元的投资者可能会更犹豫是否要下单50亿美元，
因为这实在太大了。

减少IPO中的变量可能会使发行更容易。

从公司财务的角度来看，知道你要发行多少股票以及要筹集多少资金，远比
有一个很大的范围，然后在定价当晚才知道答案要方便得多。

由银行进行调解。很可能埃隆·马斯克只是不想和一群投资者就价格讨价还价。
