crypto (say, $500 million of Bitcoin or Ether or Solana or whatever), and 
investors would get so excited that its stock would go up and it would be worth 
$1 billion. “The US public stock market will pay $2 or more for $1 worth of 
crypto,”as I put it <https://bloom.bg/4v10abL>, and a lot of companies noticed. 
There were many,many DATs <https://bloom.bg/4aFr4Pt>, and the idea expanded 
beyond crypto. There was agold treasury company <https://bloom.bg/4eHakIt>, and 
aGameStop one <https://bloom.bg/4eHZi62>. [1]  <>

It is a strange transaction. Triller signed a deal <https://bloom.bg/4v1glWT> 
with a Bahamas company called Capital Truth Holdings Ltd.to buy 
<https://bloom.bg/4v10aZj> “100% of the membership interests … of SAC1, a 
Bahamian investment vehicle (‘Holdings’”) that owns certain common stock 
equivalent interests (the ‘Share Equivalents’), through the Holdings investment 
in the Fortune Offshore Fund – Gigafund in and to 3,917,185 shares of Space 
Exploration Technologies Corp.” That is, Triller is a sort of SPV-cubed: It is 
buying shares of SAC1, which owns shares of Fortune Offshore Fund, which owns 
shares of SpaceX. [3]  <> If you buy shares of Triller, you own shares of a 
company that owns shares of a company that owns shares of a company that owns 
shares of SpaceX. Presumably the Fortune Offshore Fund, as a pre-IPO 
shareholder of SpaceX, issubject to a lockup agreement 
<https://bloom.bg/4uUqZi0> preventing it from selling its SpaceX shares, but 
presumably Capital Truth Holdings, as an SPV-cubed itself, didn’t sign any 
lockups and can sell Triller its SPV-squared shares.

> 这是一笔奇怪的交易。Triller 与一家名为 Capital Truth Holdings Ltd. 的巴哈马公司签署了一项协议，收购了 SAC1（一家巴哈马投资机构，简称“控股公司”）的 100% 成员权益。SAC1 通过其对 Fortune Offshore Fund – Gigafund 的投资，持有 Space Exploration Technologies Corp. 的 3,917,185 股普通股等值权益（简称“股份等值权益”）。也就是说，Triller 是一种特殊目的实体（SPV）的“立方”结构：它购买了 SAC1 的股份，而 SAC1 持有 Fortune Offshore Fund 的股份，Fortune Offshore Fund 又持有 SpaceX 的股份。[3] <> 如果您购买了 Triller 的股份，您就拥有了一家公司的股份，这家公司持有另一家公司的股份，而这家公司又持有 SpaceX 的股份。Fortune Offshore Fund 作为 SpaceX 上市前的股东，可能受到禁售协议的约束，

<https://bloom.bg/4uUqZi0> 禁止其出售 SpaceX 的股份，但 Capital Truth Holdings 作为一家特殊目的实体，可能没有签署任何禁售协议，因此可以将其持有的特殊目的实体的股份出售给 Triller。

because the shares are locked up and (2) I’m not sure why they’d lend you 100% 
of the purchase price. “Retains a portion of the position as treasury” suggests 
that the “secured financing arrangement” involves giving the lender some 
(much?) of the upside in the SpaceX shares, since after all the lender is 
putting up all the money. [4]  <> Possibly Triller is not so much buying the

There must have been a moment in the 1980s when most financial firms employed 
teams of professionals who went around valuing companies using pencil and 
paper, but a few small scrappy startup firms started using Lotus 1-2-3 and 
bragging to journalists “it’s amazing, we can replace teams of a dozen analysts 
with a single spreadsheet, and we can do stuff like change our margin 
assumptions and see how that affects cash flows, a whole new world has opened 
up to us.” I wasn’t there. But here’s a2020 paper <https://bloom.bg/4eHLXKO> in 
the IEEE Annals of the History of Computing, by William Deringer of MIT, titled 
“Michael Milken's Spreadsheets: Computation and Charisma in Finance in the 
Go-Go ’80s,” which rounds up a bunch of those stories:

> 上世纪 80 年代，肯定有过那么一段时间，大多数金融公司都雇佣了专业的团队，用纸笔四处评估公司价值。但一些小型创业公司开始使用 Lotus 1-2-3，并向记者吹嘘：“太棒了，我们用一张电子表格就能取代十几名分析师的团队，还能做一些事情，比如改变利润率假设，看看这对现金流有什么影响，一个全新的世界向我们敞开了大门。” 我当时不在场。不过，麻省理工学院的 William Deringer 在 IEEE 计算机历史年鉴上发表了一篇 2020 年的论文（https://bloom.bg/4eHLXKO），题为《迈克尔·米尔肯的电子表格：80 年代金融业的计算与魅力》，其中总结了许多这样的故事：

Asked to explain the unprecedented financial turmoil of the 1980s, [Michael] 
Milken once claimed the true culprits were the creators of VisiCalc. Versions 
of this story appear in multiple print sources. Management researcher J. 
Christopher Westland describes how “Michael Milken once commented that 
Bricklin’s invention had single-handedly paved the way for the 1980s corporate 
megadeals.” Innovation guru Michael Schrage recounts how “Michael Milken … has 
privately credited spreadsheet software with making it possible to model and 
market so swiftly ‘high-yield’—aka junk—bonds he pioneered at Drexel Burnham 
Lambert.”

> 当被问及如何解释20世纪80年代前所未有的金融动荡时，迈克尔·米尔肯曾声称真正的罪魁祸首是VisiCalc的开发者。这一说法在多家印刷媒体上都有出现。管理研究员J·克里斯托弗·韦斯特兰描述了“迈克尔·米尔肯曾评论说，布里克林的这项发明为20世纪80年代的企业巨额交易铺平了道路”。创新大师迈克尔·施拉格回忆道，“迈克尔·米尔肯私下里认为，正是电子表格软件使得他能够如此迅速地建模和销售他在德崇证券(Drexel Burnham Lambert)率先推出的‘高收益’（即垃圾）债券。”

And:

According to business journalist George Anders, KKR executive Donald Herdrich 
stopped by a Manhattan electronics store in 1980 to purchase a personal 
computer as entertainment for his children. While there, an enterprising sales 
associate offered Herdrich a demonstration of VisiCalc. “Enthralled” by 
VisiCalc’s ability to recalculate projections instantaneously upon changing one 
cell, Herdrich purchased KKR’s first Apple IIe. It proved a “decisive 
advantage.” “In the 1970s, KKR couldn’t rapidly stalk several companies at 
once, because its financial blueprints required weeks of calculations by hand,” 
Anders explained. “Then microchips came to Wall Street. ... All of a sudden, 
giant companies’ finances could be picked apart in an afternoon.”

> 据商业记者乔治·安德斯 (George Anders) 所述，KKR 高管唐纳德·赫德里奇 (Donald Herdrich) 于 1980 年造访曼哈顿一家电子商店，想为孩子们购买一台个人电脑作为娱乐设备。在那里，一位颇具进取心的销售员向赫德里奇演示了 VisiCalc 软件。赫德里奇被 VisiCalc 只需更改一个单元格即可瞬间重新计算预测结果的功能深深吸引，于是购买了 KKR 的第一台 Apple IIe 电脑。事实证明，这台电脑带来了“决定性的优势”。安德斯解释说：“在 20 世纪 70 年代，KKR 无法同时快速跟踪多家公司，因为其财务蓝图需要数周的手工计算。后来，微芯片进入华尔街……突然之间，只需一个下午就能剖析大型公司的财务状况。”

Buyout firms began invested heavily in computing technology, upgrading 
hardware and shifting to more sophisticated packages like Lotus’s 1-2-3 and 
Symphony. Wall Street Journal advertisements touted “$99 ‘LBO software 
packages’ that let even a neophyte race through the types of calculations that 
had once taken Herdrich and Kravis weeks.” This ability to “stalk” buyout 
targets through spreadsheets empowered upstart firms. As Ander put it: “the 
personal computer accomplished for Wall Street’s buyout boutiques what the 
advent of gunpowder did for Mongol warriors.”

> 收购公司开始大力投资计算机技术，升级硬件并转向更复杂的软件包，例如 Lotus 1-2-3 和 Symphony。《华尔街日报》的广告吹捧“售价 99 美元的‘杠杆收购软件套装’，即使是新手也能快速完成赫德里奇和克拉维斯过去需要数周才能完成的计算”。这种通过电子表格“追踪”收购目标的能力赋予了新兴公司强大的力量。正如安德所说：“个人电脑之于华尔街的精品收购公司，正如火药的出现之于蒙古战士。”

In the olden days, a giant institution would take weeks to calculate by hand 
how much a company was worth. But thanks to modern technology, for just $99, a 
couple of smart scrappy entrepreneurs could do it in minutes. The possibilities

How’s private credit going?

One possibility is that funds that offer quarterly liquidity have more 
liquidity risk than funds that offer daily liquidity. Like: If you invest in a 
mutual fund, you can take your money out every business day, so you probably 
don’t. “Ehh, it’s fine, if things get bad I can take my money out tomorrow,” 
you think, so you leave your money in. Leaving your money in is not an 
irrevocable decision — you can change your mind tomorrow — so it has low stakes.

> 一种可能性是，按季度提供流动性的基金比按日提供流动性的基金流动性风险更高。例如：如果你投资于共同基金，你可以每个工作日取出资金，但你可能不会这么做。“嗯，没关系，如果情况恶化，我明天可以取出资金，”你心想，于是你把钱留在基金里。把钱留在基金里并不是一个不可撤销的决定——你明天可以改变主意——所以风险很低。

Whereas, if you invest in a non-traded business development company or 
interval fund, you can ask for your money back once per quarter, so maybe you 
do. “Things are a little dicey,” you think, “and if I don’t take my money out 
now who knows what will happen in the next three months, so I’d better take it 
out now.” Leaving your money in feels like an irrevocable decision, which might 
make you nervous enough to take it out.

> 然而，如果你投资于非上市的商业发展公司或区间基金，你可以每季度要求赎回一次，所以你可能会这么做。“情况有点不妙，”你心想，“如果我现在不取出资金，谁知道未来三个月会发生什么，所以我最好现在就取出来。”把钱留在里面感觉就像一个不可逆转的决定，这可能会让你感到紧张，从而选择取出。

From the fund’s perspective, keeping your money is good: It is bad for the 
fund to have to give you back your money; that can lead to forced sales and 
self-reinforcing declines in value. The fund would rather keep your money 
forever; if its liquidity terms could be “you can never get your money back,” 
it would do that. And of course some funds — publicly traded BDCs and 
closed-end funds — have terms like that. But there are tradeoffs: Those funds 
typically trade at a discount, and “you can never get your money back” is not a 
great marketing pitch. Instead, there is a norm for many type of funds — 
reinforced by US Securities and Exchange Commission regulation — that they 
offer to give investors (some of) their money back every three months. In 
normal times, this sounds like good prudent liquidity management: Investors can 
only occasionally ask for their money back, so the fund can rely on keeping 
most of it for a reasonable amount of time. Letting the investors get their 
money backmore often would be riskier.

> 从基金的角度来看，持有您的资金是好事：对基金而言，不得不将资金返还给您是件坏事；这可能导致强制出售和价值的自我强化式下跌。基金宁愿永远持有您的资金；如果流动性条款可以写成“您永远无法取回您的资金”，它肯定会这么做。当然，一些基金——例如公开交易的商业发展公司（BDC）和封闭式基金——确实有这样的条款。但这其中有利有弊：这些基金通常以折价交易，“您永远无法取回您的资金”并不是一个好的营销策略。相反，许多类型的基金——受到美国证券交易委员会监管的强化——通常会每三个月向投资者返还（部分）资金。在正常情况下，这听起来像是审慎的流动性管理：投资者只能偶尔要求取回资金，因此基金可以依靠在合理的时间内持有大部分资金。让投资者更频繁地取回资金风险更大。

SpaceTeslaX

I wrote yesterday <https://bloom.bg/4405OA3> about the ominous rumbling that 
SpaceX might merge with Tesla. One thing I said is that, while SpaceX investors 
(1) won’t and (2) can’t object to any such merger on any terms that Elon Musk 
wants, Tesla investors might. Not only does Tesla have some shareholder rights; 
it also has big outside shareholders (index funds, etc.) who are not devoted 
Musk fans, and who might stick up for themselves if the deal is unfair to them. 
Don’t count on it or anything, but maybe.

> 我昨天写了一篇文章（<https://bloom.bg/4405OA3>），谈到了SpaceX可能与特斯拉合并的不祥传闻。我当时提到，SpaceX的投资者（1）不会，（2）也无法反对埃隆·马斯克提出的任何合并条款，

但特斯拉的投资者可能会反对。特斯拉不仅拥有一定的股东权利，

而且它还有一些重要的外部股东（指数基金等），这些股东并非马斯克的忠实拥趸，

如果交易对他们不公平，他们可能会维护自身权益。

别抱太大希望，但也许会这样。

Along those lines, law professor Ann Lipton makes another important point 
<https://bloom.bg/4oOl8ta>:

> 基于此，法学教授安·利普顿提出了另一个重要观点：

<https://bloom.bg/4oOl8ta>:

Assuming Texas law tracks Delaware on this, if SpaceX acquires Tesla, that’s a 
conflict transaction of the sort shareholders on both sides might sue over 
(when Shari Redstone caused CBS to combine with Viacom, both sets of 
shareholders sued, for example). But on the SpaceX side, that’s a derivative 
lawsuit; shareholders must have 3% to file, and that’s, you know, in excess of 
$52 billion depending on where SpaceX closes today. But on the Tesla side, it’s 
not derivative – that’s direct, and that means, there is no 3% barrier to 
suing. Plus, Tesla has not (yet) attempted to bar class actions the way SpaceX 
has. So there’s much more litigation risk on the Tesla side, even if the 
standard of proof would require plaintiff-shareholders to show fraud or 
intentional misconduct.

> 假设德克萨斯州的法律与特拉华州的法律一致，如果SpaceX收购特斯拉，这将是一笔利益冲突交易，双方股东都可能就此提起诉讼（例如，莎莉·雷石东促成CBS与维亚康姆合并时，双方股东都提起了诉讼）。但就SpaceX而言，这是一起衍生诉讼；股东必须持有3%的股份才能提起诉讼，而根据SpaceX今天的收盘价，这相当于超过520亿美元。但就特斯拉而言，这不是衍生诉讼——这是直接诉讼，这意味着没有3%的持股门槛。此外，特斯拉（目前）还没有像SpaceX那样试图阻止集体诉讼。因此，即使举证标准要求原告股东证明存在欺诈或故意不当行为，特斯拉方面的诉讼风险也更大。

That is: If you’re a shareholder of a public company, and your company makes 
an acquisition at too high a price, that harms the company, and you can sue the 
company’s directors for breaching their fiduciary duty to the company. But the 
bar for that lawsuit is high: Directors have a lot of leeway to run the company 
however they think best, and in the case of Tesla and SpaceX you need to own 3% 
of the company to sue.

> 也就是说：如果您是一家上市公司的股东，而您的公司以过高的价格进行收购，这会损害公司利益，您可以起诉该公司董事违反其对公司的信托义务。但是，提起此类诉讼的门槛很高：董事拥有很大的自主权，可以按照他们认为最好的方式经营公司，而且以特斯拉和SpaceX为例，您需要持有公司3%的股份才能提起诉讼。

But if you’re a shareholder of a public company, and your company gets acquired
 at too low a price, that harmsyou: You are forced to sell your stock too 
cheap. You can sue the company for that, and the bar is lower. If you own one 
share, and your share gets bought out too cheap, you’ve got a lawsuit.

> 但如果你是一家上市公司的股东，而你的公司被收购，

价格过低，这对你不利：你被迫低价出售股票。你可以就此起诉公司，而且门槛也较低。如果你只持有一股，

而你的股票被低价收购，你就可以提起诉讼。

This assumes that the buyer in any merger would be SpaceX, and the target 
would be Tesla. That seems like a reasonable assumption. For one thing, 
SpaceX’s market capitalization is bigger, and usually the bigger company is the 
buyer. For another thing, SpaceX has dual-class stock that gives Elon Musk 
total control, while Tesla doesn’t, and Musk would probably want to preserve 
his total control of the combined company.

> 这假设在任何并购中，买方都是SpaceX，目标公司是特斯拉。这似乎是一个合理的假设。首先，SpaceX的市值更大，通常情况下，市值更大的公司才是买方。其次，SpaceX采用双重股权结构，赋予埃隆·马斯克完全的控制权，而特斯拉则没有，马斯克很可能希望保持对合并后公司的完全控制权。

Passive income

“Passive income” is, as far as I can tell from Instagram, largely a scam. 
People will sell you online courses teaching you how to sell online courses to 
teach people how to sell online courses, in an infinite recursion of 
uselessness. Or they will sell you online courses teaching you how to buy 
rental properties with 99% leverage, which I guess is not exactly a scam — that 
is a thing you can do — but which has some obvious risks.

> 就我从Instagram上了解到的情况来看，“被动收入”基本上是个骗局。

有人会卖给你一些在线课程，教你如何卖在线课程，然后你再去教别人如何卖在线课程，如此循环往复，毫无意义。

或者他们会卖给你一些在线课程，教你如何用99%的杠杆购买出租房产，

这或许不能算是骗局——毕竟这

确实可行——但其中也存在一些显而易见的风险。

This stuff, on Instagram, always offends me as a believer in capitalism. The 
point of capitalism is that you do something useful for society, and society 
gives you money. The point of “passive income” on social media is mostly “here 
are ways to get money without doing anything for society.” Bad.

> 作为资本主义的信奉者，我在Instagram上看到这些东西总是感到反感。资本主义的本质在于你为社会做出贡献，社会给你报酬。而社交媒体上所谓的“被动收入”大多是“教你如何不劳而获”。太糟糕了。

Here is a Wall Street Journal story — “Forget Work. Passive Income Is the New 
American Dream <https://bloom.bg/4405Rfd>” — that mostly confirms my crotchety 
view of passive income. But not entirely. It does start with this uplifting 
anecdote:

> 以下是《华尔街日报》的一篇文章——“忘记工作吧。被动收入才是新的美国梦 <https://bloom.bg/4405Rfd>”——这篇文章基本证实了我那略显古怪的被动收入观。但并非完全如此。文章开头讲述了一个令人振奋的轶事：

Greg Keogh couldn’t stand commuting, office attire and feeling depleted at the 
end of a workday. To find a way out of his corporate 9-to-5, he decided to 
break into the world of so-called passive income. ...

> 格雷格·基奥无法忍受通勤、办公室着装以及一天工作结束后精疲力竭的感觉。为了摆脱朝九晚五的上班生活，他决定进军所谓的被动收入领域。

His escape hatch came from a chance conversation with a dog owner who was 
excited to have found a bigger-than-normal lint roller. A mechanical engineer

> 他的转机源于与一位狗主人的一次偶然对话，这位狗主人兴奋地发现了一个比普通粘毛滚筒更大的产品。作为一名受过机械工程训练的工程师，他设计了一个几乎和纸巾卷筒一样宽的粘毛滚筒，并将其放在亚马逊上销售，销量迅速增长。

Keough could have tried to build a lint-roller empire. Instead, he decided to 
coast. Seven years later, he works on it two hours or less in a typical month 
and personally nets about $50,000 to $115,000 a year.

> 基奥本可以尝试打造一个粘毛滚筒帝国。但他却选择了安逸度日。七年后，他每月花在粘毛滚筒上的时间通常不超过两小时，个人年收入约为5万至11.5万美元。

See that’s great, that’s how the world is supposed to work. He did something 
modestly useful for society — invented a big lint roller — and now makes a 
perpetual modest living from it. If all of the passive income schemes on social 
media were like “invent a useful thing and sell it on Amazon” that would be 
great. We’d have better lint rollers, probably.

> 你看，这很棒，世界本该如此运转。他为社会做了一件

略有助益的事——发明了一种大型粘毛滚筒——现在靠它

维持着一份稳定的、略有收入的生活。如果社交媒体上所有被动收入计划都像“发明一件有用的东西，然后在亚马逊上卖”那样，

那就太好了。我们或许就能用上更好的粘毛滚筒了。
