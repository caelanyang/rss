HELLO DISCOVERERS!

At some point in your thirties, you often start carrying a particular
kind of grief. You find yourself mourning the years when friends were
a constant, ambient presence – when mundane moments felt significant
simply because they were shared. Beneath that grief sits a layer of
guilt: for the calls you never made, the messages you meant to send,
and for letting it get to the point where reaching out now feels like
an awkward imposition.

> 到了三十岁，你往往会开始背负一种特殊的

悲伤。你会怀念那些朋友如影随形、如影随形的岁月——那时，平凡的瞬间也因彼此分享而显得意义非凡。

在这份悲伤之下，还隐藏着一层

愧疚：为那些从未拨出的电话，那些本该发出的信息，
以及为如今主动联系朋友都感觉像是一种尴尬的负担而感到愧疚。

Pranav Jain has captured all of this in a short, tender essay [3]
about how friendships fray in adulthood and how little language we
have for that kind of loss.

> 普拉纳夫·贾恩在一篇简短而温柔的文章[3]中捕捉到了这一切，

文章讲述了成年后友谊是如何破裂的，以及我们对于这种失去的语言是多么匮乏。



With romantic heartbreak we can usually rely on a kind of support
infrastructure. The dissolving of a close friendship, however, usually
goes strangely unmourned. There are no dramatic breakups.

> 面对爱情的伤痛，我们通常可以依靠某种支持体系。

然而，亲密友谊的破裂却往往出奇地不引人惋惜。没有戏剧性的分手。

“Most friendships dissolve through unattended accumulation –
postponed calls, exhausting jobs, geographic distance, emotional
fatigue, (...) One day you realise the person who once knew your
thoughts now only knows what you accidentally reveal on Instagram
stories.”

> “大多数友谊都是因为疏于处理而逐渐消散的——

拖延的通话、疲惫的工作、地理上的距离、情感上的

疲惫，（……）有一天你会意识到，曾经了解你

想法的人，现在只知道你在Instagram

故事里不小心透露的那些信息。”

When we were younger, school and university did the work of friendship
for us – proximity bred intimacy, repetition bred familiarity.
Adulthood dismantles that scaffolding:

> 年轻的时候，学校和大学帮我们培养了友谊——

近距离接触培养了亲密感，重复接触培养了熟悉感。

成年后，这种关系架构被拆毁了：

“Today’s young professionals live inside systems that quietly
erode friendship while pretending to celebrate connection. Work
consumes emotional bandwidth. Cities stretch distances cruelly.
Weekends become recovery periods rather than social spaces. Ambition
transforms everyone into project managers of their own lives. (...)

> “如今的年轻职场人士生活在这样的体系中：

这些体系表面上颂扬人与人之间的联结，却在悄然侵蚀着友谊。

工作耗尽了他们的情感能量。
城市残酷地拉长了人与人之间的距离。

周末变成了休养生息的时间，而非社交场所。

雄心壮志让每个人都成了自己人生的项目经理。(…)

We are perhaps the first generation to possess uninterrupted access to
each other while simultaneously becoming emotionally inaccessible. We
maintain ambient awareness of one another’s existence without
participating meaningfully in each other’s lives. I know what my
friends eat. Which cafés they visit. Which things they complain
about. I know when they get promoted because LinkedIn informs me
before they do. And yet sometimes I hesitate before calling because I
no longer know the emotional weather of their lives.”

> 我们或许是第一代能够随时随地联系彼此，却同时在情感上变得疏离的一代。我们时刻感知着彼此的存在，却无法真正参与到彼此的生活中。我知道我的朋友们吃什么，去哪家咖啡馆，抱怨什么。我知道他们升职了，因为LinkedIn会在他们自己之前通知我。然而，有时我却犹豫要不要打电话，因为我不再了解他们生活中的情绪起伏。

One of Jain’s best observations is that adult friendship has
gradually absorbed the same logic we apply to everything else. We tend
to weigh our relationships through a kind of invisible cost-benefit
analysis:

> 贾恩最精辟的见解之一是，成年人的友谊

逐渐吸收了我们应用于其他一切事物的相同逻辑。我们倾向于

通过一种无形的成本效益分析来衡量我们的人际关系：



“Somewhere along the way, friendships too began absorbing the
language of management. We now discuss emotional bandwidth like data
plans. Even affection sometimes feels evaluated through invisible
cost-benefit analysis: Who texts first? Who makes more effort? Who is
emotionally available? Who drains energy?

> “不知从何时起，友谊也开始吸收了

管理的语言。我们现在讨论情感带宽，就像讨论流量套餐一样。

甚至连感情有时也像是经过了无形的
成本效益分析：谁先发短信？谁付出更多？谁情感上更投入？谁消耗精力？”

Friendship, however, has always depended on a certain irrational
generosity. A willingness to waste time together magnificently. To
listen to the same anxiety for the fifth time. To sit through silence.
To remain available without agenda.”

> 然而，友谊始终依赖于某种非理性的

慷慨。一种愿意一起消磨时光的意愿。

第五次倾听同样的焦虑。

静静地享受沉默。

不带任何目的，随时待命。

Like idleness (see DD392 [4]), friendship is antithetical to the
economic system we live inside, because so much of it is gloriously
unproductive and unpaid. Friendship doesn’t show up on any balance
sheet, so it gets discounted, forgotten, filed under things that can
wait.

> 就像懒惰一样（参见 DD392 [4]），友谊与我们所处的经济体系格格不入，因为友谊中的很多部分都是无生产力且无回报的。友谊不会出现在任何资产负债表上，因此它被忽略、被遗忘，被归类为可以等待的事情。

“It resists the transactional logic modern life rewards everywhere
else. Because a real friend offers something profoundly rare:
unoptimised presence. Family is structured by blood. Marriage by
institution. Work relationships by utility. Friendship survives purely
through mutual choosing. Nobody has to stay. And yet some people
do.”

> 它抵制现代生活其他地方普遍奉行的交易逻辑。

因为真正的朋友能提供极其珍贵的东西：

不加修饰的陪伴。家庭由血缘构成。婚姻由
制度构成。工作关系由功利性构成。友谊的维系完全依赖于

彼此的选择。没有人必须留下。然而，有些人却

选择留下。

The good news is that many of these friendships aren’t dead so much
as dormant. The thread is still there; sometimes a single late-night
call is enough to pull it taut again and find, briefly, an earlier and
more reachable version of yourself waiting on the other end.

> 好消息是，很多这样的友谊并非消亡，而是暂时沉寂。

纽带依然存在；有时，一个深夜的

电话就足以让它重新紧绷，让你短暂地发现，电话那头是更早、更亲切的自己。

And now to this week’s discoveries. – Kai

THE QUIET GRIEF OF ADULT FRIENDSHIP [3]

READ

A lovely, tender essay on how adult friendships tend to dissolve not
through dramatic fallouts but through slow, unattended accumulation
– postponed calls, exhausting jobs, distance and fatigue – and how
we struggle to name the grief that comes with it. Pranav Jain
poignantly describes this modern erosion of friendship: we have
constant digital access to one another, yet very little real presence.

> 这是一篇优美而细腻的文章，探讨了成年人的友谊是如何逐渐消逝的，并非源于

激烈的争吵，而是源于缓慢而疏离的积累——

拖延的通话、疲惫的工作、距离和倦怠——以及

我们如何难以启齿随之而来的悲伤。普拉纳夫·贾恩

深刻地描绘了现代友谊的这种衰落：我们

拥有随时随地的线上联系，却鲜少真正地相聚。

“Somewhere along the way, friendships too began absorbing the
language of management. We now discuss emotional bandwidth like data
plans. Even affection sometimes feels evaluated through invisible
cost-benefit analysis: Who texts first? Who makes more effort? Who is
emotionally available? Who drains energy?”

> “不知从何时起，友谊也开始吸收了

管理的语言。我们现在讨论情感带宽，就像讨论流量套餐一样。

甚至连感情有时也像是经过了无形的
成本效益分析：谁先发短信？谁付出更多？谁情感上更投入？谁消耗精力？”

Activist and writer Mariame Kaba with a moving letter to young

we practise. She urges us to focus on ‘critical connections’ over
critical mass, and to keep taking small, constructive actions even
when outcomes are unclear. It’s a steadying piece about how to keep
going without numbing out, and why small, stubborn efforts still
matter.

> 活动家兼作家玛丽亚姆·卡巴（Mariame Kaba）写了一封感人至深的信，献给那些身处危机中的年轻组织者们。

希望并非天真，而是一种需要我们不断实践的自律。

她敦促我们关注“关键联系”而非“临界规模”，并坚持采取小而有效的行动，

即便结果尚不明朗。

这是一篇振奋人心的文章，讲述了如何在不麻木的情况下继续前行，

以及为何微小而坚持不懈的努力依然至关重要。



“I’m with Audre Lorde, who wrote that ‘despair is a tool of our
enemies’. Why do I believe this? Because despair has a way of
distorting, it often pairs well with cynicism, which I see as a way of
being that contracts what’s possible rather than expanding
possibilities. Rather than being enabling, I have experienced it as
corrosive. Are cynical people builders? I haven’t experienced them
as such.”

> “我赞同奥德丽·洛德的观点，她写道‘绝望是我们敌人的工具’。我为什么这么认为？因为绝望会扭曲现实，它常常与愤世嫉俗相伴而生。在我看来，愤世嫉俗是一种限制而非拓展可能性的生存方式。它非但没有带来积极的影响，反而具有腐蚀性。愤世嫉俗的人是建设者吗？我并没有遇到这样的人。”

THE SHARED FEELING OF BEING HARVESTED BY THE FUTURE [28]

> 被未来收割的共同感受[28]

READ

Yi-Ling Liu is a journalist who splits her time between the US and
China. Here she cuts through the AI race narrative showing how
ordinary people in both countries feel like powerless bystanders in
someone else’s game. Everyone feels equally tracked, helpless and
anxious. The real story isn’t who wins the race, she argues, but how
both countries have abandoned the people running it. An insightful,
humanising perspective on the usual geopolitics framing.

> 刘怡玲是一位常年往返于美国和中国的记者。

她以独特的视角剖析了人工智能竞赛的叙事，揭示了两国普通民众如何感到自己如同旁观者一般，在别人的游戏中无能为力。

每个人都感到被追踪、无助和焦虑。

Here’s one way to put a trillion dollars into perspective. A million
seconds is 11 days. A billion seconds is 31 years. A trillion seconds
is 31,700 [37] years. You’d have needed to start counting when
Neanderthals went extinct.

> 这里有一个方法来理解万亿美元。一百万
秒是11天。十亿秒是31年。一万亿秒
是31700年。你得从
尼安德特人灭绝的时候开始算起才行。
