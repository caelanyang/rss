Fake Polymarket traders

In traditional finance, there are stock exchanges and there are brokerages. 
Stocks are bought and sold on the stock exchange, but you can’t just walk in 
and buy some stock. Only certain selected professionals can trade stocks on the 
stock exchange. If you want to trade stocks, you have to open an account with a 
brokerage; your broker will buy the stock for you on the stock exchange. [1]  <>
 You can’t just open an accounton the stock exchange and buy stock. The stock 
exchange doesn’t have accounts for retail investors.

> 在传统金融领域，有证券交易所和经纪公司。

股票在证券交易所进行买卖，但你不能直接走进去

购买股票。只有特定的专业人士才能在证券交易所进行股票交易。

如果你想交易股票，你必须在经纪公司开户；你的经纪人会代表你在证券交易所购买股票。[1]

你不能直接在证券交易所开户就购买股票。证券交易所不为散户投资者提供账户。

This is more or less a matter of historical contingency, though there are some 
benefits <https://bloom.bg/4xI2Opr> to this division of labor, and stock 
exchanges and regulators are sort ofattached to it <https://bloom.bg/4w2tGin> 
now. One arguable advantage of this division is that retail brokerages can be 
kind of embarrassing, without thereby rendering the whole stock market kind of 
embarrassing. Like: Robinhood used to show little animations of confetti when 
its customers bought stock, andregulators found that embarrassing 
<https://bloom.bg/4xJ7oE2>, but it was embarrassing for Robinhood. If you were 
a stock trader at Goldman Sachs or Jane Street or BlackRock or whatever, moving 
tens of millions of dollars of stock at a clip, and your uncle was like 
“heheheh what about the confetti,” you could reasonably reply “that has nothing 
to do with me, I do not use Robinhood, that’s a retail brokerage, I am a 
sophisticated professional and there is almost no confetti at the New York 
Stock Exchange.”

> 这或多或少是一个历史偶然性的问题，尽管这种分工也有一些好处，而且证券交易所和监管机构现在也或多或少地依赖于它。这种分工的一个可论证的优势是，零售经纪公司可能会让人感到尴尬，但不会因此让整个股票市场都感到尴尬。例如：Robinhood 过去会在客户购买股票时显示一些彩带动画，监管机构认为这很尴尬，但对 Robinhood 来说却很尴尬。如果你是高盛、简街、贝莱德或其他公司的股票交易员，一次就能经手数千万美元的股票，而你的叔叔却说：“呵呵，那彩带呢？”你可以理直气壮地回答：“那跟我没关系，我不使用Robinhood，那是零售经纪公司，我是个经验丰富的专业人士，纽约证券交易所几乎没有彩带。”

Nobody who started a retail financial market from scratch today, or even 10 
years ago, would set it up this way, though. Nobody would be like “I am going 
to set up a central venue for trading of Pokémons, and offer a limited number 
of memberships to professional Pokémon trading firms, who can sign up customers 
and trade on their behalf on my venue.” What? No. You set upa website, and you 
letpeople open accounts on the website, and they can trade with each other. 
Perhaps professional trading firms will come to your website, perhaps you will 
enter into special arrangements with professional market makers to provide 
liquidity, but in general you are going to have an account-opening function and 
let people open accounts.

> 如今，甚至十年前，没有人会从零开始创办零售金融市场。没有人会说：“我要建立一个宝可梦交易中心，并向专业的宝可梦交易公司提供数量有限的会员资格，让他们注册客户，并代表客户在我的平台上进行交易。”什么？不。你会建立一个网站，让人们在网站上开设账户，然后他们可以互相交易。

或许会有专业的交易公司来你的网站，或许你会与专业的做市商达成特殊协议来提供流动性，但总的来说，你会提供开户功能，让人们可以开设账户。

In financial history this is more or less an innovation of crypto — crypto 
exchanges were the big financial institutions that most notably combined the 
roles of exchanges and retail-facing brokerages — but realistically it is an 
innovation ofwebsites. The New York Stock Exchange started as a building where 
people met; the building could only fit so many people, and they all had to be 
in New York. A financial platform starting in the 2020s is naturally an online 
platform, and those constraints do not apply, so there is no real need for a 
separation of the exchange and brokerage functions.

> 从金融史的角度来看，这或多或少是加密货币的一项创新——加密货币交易所是大型金融机构，它们最显著地融合了交易所和面向零售客户的经纪业务——但实际上，这更像是网站的一项创新。纽约证券交易所最初是一栋人们聚集的建筑；这栋建筑的容量有限，而且所有人都必须身处纽约。而2020年代兴起的金融平台自然是线上平台，这些限制不再适用，因此也就没有必要将交易所和经纪业务的功能分开。

On the other hand, if your retail customer acquisition functions are 
embarrassing, that in a sense makes the whole market embarrassing. Here’s aWall

SpaceX bonds

We talked a few weeks ago <https://bloom.bg/4xEAzrX> about debt financing for 
artificial intelligence. People want to build huge amounts of AI infrastructure 
(data centers, chip fabs, nuclear plants, etc.), which might require borrowing 
trillions of dollars. The lenders want to see some steady cash flows. A 
data-center builder who strikes a leasing deal with Anthropic might have a hard 
time borrowing billions of dollars to fund development, “because Anthropic is 
still a startup <https://bloom.bg/4xNDMpa> and lacks the earnings to support 
such a large debt deal.” And so a custom has developed in which some big 
investment-grade-rated public company — Broadcom, Nvidia,Meta 
<https://bloom.bg/4xHUDcH> — backstops the borrowing. With that backing, the 
bonds get an investment-grade rating and investors are happy to buy them.

> 几周前，我们讨论过人工智能领域的债务融资问题。人们希望建设大量的AI基础设施（数据中心、芯片制造厂、核电站等），这可能需要借贷数万亿美元。贷款方希望看到稳定的现金流。一家数据中心建设商如果与Anthropic达成租赁协议，可能很难借到数十亿美元用于开发，“因为Anthropic仍然是一家初创公司，缺乏足够的盈利来支撑如此庞大的债务交易。” 因此，一种惯例逐渐形成：一些大型的、具有投资级评级的上市公司——例如博通、英伟达、Meta——为借款提供担保。有了这种担保，债券就能获得投资级评级，投资者也乐于购买。

There might, however, be a shortcut that bypasses some of this complexity. The 
leading AI labs are at this point trillion-dollar-ish companies, even if they 
don’t have a lot of earnings. (Anthropicexpects net income 
<https://bloom.bg/4eBLeLf> of $559 million on revenue of $10.9 billion this 
quarter, its first profitable quarter.) “This data center is leased to a 
money-losing startup, but it’s really cool”: No, bad, not what bondholders 
want. “This data center is leased to a trillion-dollar company”: Yes, better.

> 然而，或许存在一条捷径可以绕过这些复杂性。目前，领先的人工智能实验室都是市值万亿美元的公司，即便它们的盈利能力并不强。（Anthropic预计本季度净收入为5.59亿美元，营收为109亿美元，这将是该公司首次实现盈利。）“这个数据中心租给了一家亏损的初创公司，但它真的很酷”：不行，这不好，这不是债券持有人想要的。“这个数据中心租给了一家市值万亿美元的公司”：好多了。

Some of that — about the rockets and satellites — is about SpaceX’s actual 
current ability to generate cash. Some of it — “the AI segment addresses a 
large total addressable market … and represents significant long-term revenue

> 其中一些——关于火箭和卫星——关乎SpaceX目前实际的现金流能力。另一些——“人工智能业务面向庞大的潜在市场……并代表着巨大的长期收入和盈利增长潜力”——则仅仅是“这是一个大型人工智能实验室”。还有一些——“可以进入股权和债务资本市场”——则仅仅是“这是一家大型上市公司，人们喜欢它的股票”。

It’s possible the first component — the money-generating rockets — is 
essential to SpaceX’s investment-grade ratings, but I’m not sure. “This is a 
public company with plausible plans to make trillions of dollars selling 
enterprise AI, and people are lining up to buy tens of billions of dollars of 
its stock at whatever price it demands”: Doesn’tthat sound like a good credit? 
Also doesn’t that describe Anthropic and OpenAI? I mean, not now, but when they

这难道不也适用于Anthropologie和OpenAI吗？我的意思是，现在还不是，但当它们上市的时候呢？

I guess the point here is that if you’re a big AI startup, you should go 
public not only toraise tens of billions of dollars of equity 
<https://bloom.bg/4b6nkq7>, but also to raise tens of billions of dollars of 
debt. Being a trillion-dollar startup is cool, but it might not move ratings 
agencies or bond investors; being a trillion-dollar public company will. Once 
there is an established public market price for your equity, and that price is 
$2 trillion, it is easier to persuade people that you’re a good credit risk.

> 我想重点是，如果你是一家大型人工智能初创公司，你应该上市，

不仅是为了筹集数百亿美元的股权融资

<https://bloom.bg/4b6nkq7>，也是为了筹集数百亿美元的债务融资。

成为一家万亿美元的初创公司固然很酷，但这可能不会打动评级机构

或债券投资者；

而成为一家万亿美元的上市公司则会。

一旦你的股权在公开市场上有了确定的价格，

比如2万亿美元，

就更容易说服人们相信你是一个良好的信用风险。

In that vein, here’s a 2025 working paper <https://bloom.bg/4epeHZX> by Andres 
Almazan, Nathan Swem, Sheridan Titman and Gregory Weitzner titled “Access to 
Capital and the IPO Decision: An Analysis of US Private Firms,” with basically 
that takeaway:

Stretch unpeg

One of the coolest weirdest worst financial instruments in recent memory is 
Strategy Inc.’s “Stretch” preferred stock. Stretch is a floating-rate preferred 
stock, but its dividend rate floats not with some benchmark interest rate but 
rather with Strategy’s own market-clearing interest rate. Stretch pays a 
monthly dividend, and it trades on the stock exchange, and each month Strategy 
resets the dividend to try to make Stretch trade at $100 per share. If the 
dividend rate is 10% and Stretch trades at $80, for a 12.5% yield, then next 
month Strategy should reset the dividend rate to 12.5% to make it trade at par.

> 近年来最酷炫、最奇葩、最糟糕的金融工具之一，当属Strategy Inc.的“Stretch”优先股。Stretch是一种浮动利率优先股，但其股息率并非与某个基准利率挂钩，而是与Strategy公司自身的市场出清利率挂钩。Stretch每月支付股息，并在证券交易所交易。Strategy公司每月都会调整股息率，力求使Stretch的交易价格达到每股100美元。如果股息率为10%，Stretch的交易价格为每股80美元，收益率为12.5%，那么下个月Strategy公司应该将股息率调整为12.5%，使其交易价格与面值持平。

When Strategy launched this thing last year, I compared it to auction-rate 
securities <https://bloom.bg/4eYD9S0>, which were the pre-2008 implementation 
of this idea. What is the idea? The idea is something like “a long-term 
fixed-income instrument with no duration.” [2]  <> Stretch remains outstanding 
forever, but it is nonethelessessentially one-month financing: From Strategy’s 
or an investor’s perspective, it is economically equivalent to selling 
one-month debt and rolling it over every month. Of course, if you have to roll 
your debt over every month, you take the risk that one month you won’t be able 
to: Debt markets will get bad, or investors will get nervous about your credit, 
and they won’t want to buy your new debt.Strategy also takes that risk: Even 
though it doesn’t actually sell new debt every month (the Stretch is 
perpetual), its interest expense can go up rapidly if markets, or its credit, 
get worse.

> 去年Strategy推出这项产品时，我曾将其与拍卖利率证券（https://bloom.bg/4eYD9S0）进行比较，后者是2008年之前该理念的实现方式。那么，这个理念是什么呢？它类似于“一种没有久期的长期固定收益工具”。[2] Stretch将永远有效，但本质上仍然是一种为期一个月的融资：从Strategy或投资者的角度来看，其经济意义等同于出售一个月期的债务并每月展期。当然，如果你必须每月展期债务，你就要承担这样的风险：某个月你可能无法展期：债务市场会恶化，或者投资者会对你的信用状况感到担忧，他们可能不愿意购买你的新债务。这种策略也存在同样的风险：即使它实际上并非每月都出售新债务（展期是永久性的），但如果市场或其信用状况恶化，其利息支出也会迅速上升。

Stretch, though, is a softer and safer version of one-month financing. It’s 
perpetual, it’s preferred stock, and the dividend reset is in the discretion of 
the board. If Strategy decides “never mind, we’re going to let it trade at 
$80,” or even “never mind, we’re not going to pay dividends to conserve cash,” 
it can do that. The dividends on a preferred stock are not mandatory the way 
interest on debt is. But, of course, if you stop paying dividends on preferred 
stock, gettingnew financing — selling more preferred stock, for instance — gets 
much harder. You can’t exactly default on preferred stock, but if you stop 
paying it people will ask questions.

> 不过，Stretch 是一种比一个月融资更灵活、更安全的融资方式。它是永久性的，属于优先股，股息重置由董事会酌情决定。如果战略部门决定“算了，我们让它以 80 美元的价格交易”，或者甚至“算了，为了节省现金，我们不支付股息”，它都可以这样做。优先股的股息不像债务利息那样是强制性的。但是，当然，如果你停止支付优先股股息，获得新的融资——例如出售更多优先股——就会变得困难得多。你不能直接违约优先股，但如果你停止支付股息，人们就会提出疑问。

Anyway, Strategy sells this thing to buy Bitcoin. At some level, the story 
here is “Strategy sells an assortment of weird preferred stocks to buy 
Bitcoin,” fine. But I think that the more useful analysis is: “Strategy has
recreated bankingto buy Bitcoin.” I kind of think that is Strategy’s analysis, 
and Iwrote last month <https://bloom.bg/4eYDcgE>:

> 总之，Strategy 出售这些东西是为了购买比特币。从某种程度上来说，这个故事是“Strategy 出售各种奇怪的优先股来购买比特币”，这没问题。但我认为更有价值的分析是：“Strategy 重塑了银行业务来购买比特币。” 我感觉这正是 Strategy 的分析，而且我上个月也写过这篇文章：<https://bloom.bg/4eYDcgE>

The story of banking is that it is a magic trick for transmuting risky assets 
into safe liabilities. A bank lends money to companies and homeowners, which is 
risky (they might not pay back the money). The bank gets the money by issuing 
deposits, which are safe. A $1 deposit at a bank should always be worth $1. …

> 银行业的故事就是一个将风险资产转化为安全负债的魔术。银行向公司和房主放贷，这本身就存在风险（他们可能无法偿还贷款）。银行通过发行存款来获得资金，而存款是安全的。在银行存入的1美元应该永远价值1美元。

You might think: “If people put their money somewhere safe, like a bank 
deposit, and then the bank invested the money in Bitcoin, that would make 
everyone happy. The people with money would feel safe, but also Bitcoin would 
go up. The magic transmutation of banking increases society’s capacity to take 
risk on Bitcoin. There will be more productive investment in Bitcoin, because 
of this magic.” …

> 你可能会想：“如果人们把钱存到安全的地方，比如银行，然后银行把这些钱投资到比特币，那岂不是皆大欢喜？有钱人会感到安全，比特币的价格也会上涨。这种神奇的银行业务转型提升了社会承担比特币风险的能力。由于这种神奇的转变，会有更多高效的比特币投资涌现。”……

Essentially Strategy is issuing dollar-denominated one-month financing (at 
11.5%!) designed to always pay back par, and using that financing to buy a big 
chunk of all the Bitcoins that are for sale. So that’s neat!

> 本质上，Strategy 发行的是以美元计价的一个月期融资（利率高达 11.5%！），旨在始终按面值偿还，并利用这笔融资购买大量待售比特币。这真是个妙招！

Strategy is not actually issuing demand deposits to buy Bitcoin, because 
Stretch has perpetual maturity and Strategy can turn off the dividend. But it 
has approximately the economic shape of issuing demand deposits to buy Bitcoin, 
in the sense that, if the market gets nervous about Strategy’s balance sheet — 
for instance, because the value of its assets (Bitcoin) goes down — then

> Strategy 实际上并没有发行活期存款来购买比特币，因为

Stretch 具有永续到期特性，而且 Strategy 可以停止分红。但它
在经济形态上与发行活期存款来购买比特币大致相同，

因为，如果市场对 Strategy 的资产负债表感到担忧——

例如，由于其资产（比特币）价值下跌——那么

Strategy 的融资成本将立即上升，就像一家

区域性银行一样。<https://bloom.bg/3SmYaNE>

Coben analogizes Stretch to a “death spiral convertible,” an instrument that

More stock

We have talked a couple <https://bloom.bg/4xEAzrX> of times 
<https://bloom.bg/4b6nkq7> around here about the re-equitization of the stock 
market. That is: For a few decades now, US public companies have bought back 
more stock than they have sold, because they were generating more money than 
they needed and returned the extra to shareholders. But now we are seeing a 
shift, where companies are projected to sell more stock than they buy.

> 我们之前已经讨论过几次股票市场的重新股权化问题。也就是说，几十年来，美国上市公司回购的股票数量超过了售出的股票数量，因为它们盈利过剩，并将多余的资金返还给了股东。但现在我们看到一种转变，预计公司售出的股票数量将超过其购入的股票数量。

I have explained that shift <https://bloom.bg/4b6nkq7> in a pretty traditional 
way: Companies need more money than they generate, so they sell stock to raise 
it. We are near the start of a giant buildout of artificial intelligence 
infrastructure; companies expect that AI buildout to eventually generate huge 
profits, but for now it is expensive. Companies need to invest more in AI than 
they can generate organically from their cash flows, so they sell stock to 
raise the money.

> 我曾以一种相当传统的方式解释过这种转变<https://bloom.bg/4b6nkq7>：公司需要的资金比其自身产生的资金更多，因此它们会出售股票来筹集资金。我们正处于人工智能基础设施大规模建设的初期；公司预期人工智能的建设最终会带来巨额利润，但就目前而言，成本很高。公司需要在人工智能方面的投资超过了其自身现金流所能产生的资金，因此它们会出售股票来筹集资金。

But there is an alternative, also very traditional trading story. That story 
is: “Companies sell stock when they think their stock is overpriced.” At the 
Wall Street Journal,James Mackintosh tells that story <https://bloom.bg/43SiDMN>
:

> 但还有另一种说法，也非常传统，那就是：“公司会在认为股票价格过高时抛售股票。”《华尔街日报》的詹姆斯·麦金托什讲述了这个故事。<https://bloom.bg/43SiDMN>



When companies as a group turn into sellers, it’s a reasonable sign that 
stocks are very overpriced. Indiscriminate demand from investors incentivizes 
companies to step up to supply them, both with secondary offerings, like 
Alphabet’s recent record-breaking issue, or IPOs.

> 当公司整体转向抛售股票时，这通常表明股票价格过高。投资者不加区分的需求会促使公司加大力度满足这些需求，包括通过二次发行（例如Alphabet最近创纪录的发行）或首次公开募股（IPO）。

That story has sort of opposite implications. The 
we-need-lots-of-money-to-build-super-profitable-AI story suggests that 
companies expect the present value of all of this AI investment to be very 
high, so they are willing to sell stock to build it. The 
lol-our-stock-is-too-high-so-let’s-sell story suggests something else.

> 这个故事的含义恰恰相反。“我们需要大量资金来打造高利润的人工智能”的故事表明，公司预期所有这些人工智能投资的现值会非常高，因此他们愿意出售股票来开发人工智能。而“哈哈，我们的股票太高了，所以我们卖掉吧”的故事则暗示了另一种情况。
