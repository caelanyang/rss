Blind Extrapolation as a Powerful Force in Finance

A few weeks ago, The Diff wrote about building a market simulation to see if simple rules could produce complex emergent properties like value and momentum strategies producing excess returns [https://www.thediff.co/r/9fead6e1?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. There must be something in the air (specifically: agentic coding dramatically speeds up the loop of writing a simulation, running it, looking at what’s weird in the results, tweaking it, and running it again). Here’s another team, Victor Haghani and Rich Dewey, with a look at their market simulation.

> 几周前，《The Diff》撰文介绍了他们构建市场模拟系统，旨在验证简单的规则是否能够产生复杂的涌现特性，例如价值策略和动量策略带来的超额收益[https://www.thediff.co/r/9fead6e1?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。看来市场趋势确实存在（具体来说：智能体编码显著加快了编写模拟程序、运行程序、分析结果异常、调整程序并再次运行的循环速度）。接下来，Victor Haghani 和 Rich Dewey 团队将带来他们的市场模拟系统。

Earlier this year we gave a two-part talk at the Numercon Conference titled Who Killed The Random Walk?, which is based on ideas in a recently released research paper of the same title [https://www.thediff.co/r/67f166c2?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. The idea was to explore how agents with different expectations, beliefs, and constraints interact with each other to shape prices in financial markets. We let them trade with each other over decades of simulated time.

> 今年早些时候，我们在Numercon大会上发表了题为《谁扼杀了随机游走？》的两部分演讲，该演讲基于近期发表的同名研究论文[https://www.thediff.co/r/67f166c2?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。演讲旨在探讨具有不同预期、信念和约束的参与者如何相互作用，从而影响金融市场的价格。我们模拟了数十年的交易过程。

What emerged was realistic and illuminating. The interaction of these simple agents produced excess volatility, bubbles and crashes, fat-tailed returns, and persistent trends—all phenomena that have puzzled market observers for centuries. These are not merely theoretical curiosities. In 2007, quantitative equity funds suffered sudden, correlated losses when crowded strategies unwound simultaneously [https://www.thediff.co/r/432c4966?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]—one set of agents' forced selling triggering another's. During the 2021 meme-stock episode, coordinated retail options buying generated gamma squeezes that forced market makers into destabilizing hedging flows. In 2025, the "Rolling Thunder" drawdown [https://www.thediff.co/r/bbba7dbd?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] ($, FT) revealed how quickly algorithmic strategies can cascade when they collide.

> 结果既现实又富有启发性。这些简单主体之间的互动产生了过度波动、泡沫和崩盘、厚尾收益以及持续趋势——所有这些现象都困扰了市场观察者几个世纪之久。这些并非仅仅是理论上的奇闻异事。2007年，量化股票基金遭受了突如其来的、相关的损失，原因是拥挤的策略同时平仓[https://www.thediff.co/r/432c4966?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]——一组主体的被迫抛售引发了另一组主体的抛售。在2021年的“模因股”事件中，协调一致的散户期权买入造成了伽玛挤压，迫使做市商进行破坏市场的对冲操作。 2025 年，“滚雷”下跌 [https://www.thediff.co/r/bbba7dbd?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] ($, FT) 揭示了算法策略在相互碰撞时会以多快的速度级联。

In this model, one of the magic ingredients was the inclusion of extrapolators, who naïvely assume that the market’s return is higher if it’s been high recently, and vice-versa. This is not quite the same thing as momentum, which doesn’t make a claim that future returns will be similar to the recent past, only that on average excess returns persist for a bit. The extrapolators tend to participate more in the market when it’s gone up recently, and then to put more of their money in cash when the market has declined. So they tend to keep both trends moving.

> 在这个模型中，关键要素之一是引入了外推者。他们天真地认为，如果市场近期走高，那么未来的收益也会更高，反之亦然。这与动量理论并不完全相同。动量理论并不声称未来的收益会与近期收益相似，而只是说平均而言，超额收益会持续一段时间。外推者倾向于在市场近期上涨时更多地参与其中，而在市场下跌时则更多地持有现金。因此，他们往往会维持这两种趋势的持续。

At one level, this is just directly adding some excess volatility and time-varying risk premia to the model in a direct way, by telling investors: have your equity risk premium be whatever the most recent realized equity risk premium was, even if that is far outside of historical norms. On the other hand, that’s what the literature says many investors do! Sometimes, it really is that easy.[1] [https://www.thediff.co/r/df101b0b?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]

> 从某种程度上来说，这只是直接地向模型中添加了一些额外的波动性和随时间变化的风险溢价，即告诉投资者：即使远超历史平均水平，也要将股票风险溢价设定为最近实际发生的股票风险溢价。另一方面，文献表明，许多投资者正是这样做的！有时候，事情真的就是这么简单。[1] [https://www.thediff.co/r/df101b0b?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]

We think this research is important to financial markets for two reasons. First, the character of markets is changing rapidly. New instruments such as zero-day to expiration options, perpetual futures and leveraged and inverse ETFs (often based on a single stock or commodity) have grown in popularity. Prediction markets and tokenized assets are likewise adding new dimensions to markets. Second, the composition of market participants is changing, with retail traders—whose actions can become correlated whether by design or contagion—and quantitative trading firms both growing in size and influence.

> 我们认为这项研究对金融市场至关重要，原因有二。首先，市场性质正在迅速变化。诸如零日到期期权、永续期货以及杠杆和反向ETF（通常基于单一股票或商品）等新型金融工具日益普及。预测市场和代币化资产也为市场增添了新的维度。其次，市场参与者的构成也在发生变化，散户交易者（他们的行为可能因有意或无意而产生关联）和量化交易公司的规模和影响力都在不断扩大。

Modeling agent behavior can help us understand market dynamics and hopefully make them more efficient and less prone to crises. We don’t have good historical precedents for how large-scale use of zero-day options interacts with extreme volatility. We have some suggestive examples, like looking at the rollercoaster around Liberation Day. But that illustrates why this is so hard: how do you tease out what was Liberation Day-specific from what was caused by options? And if you’re predicting the next period of extreme volatility, how do you decide whether market participants repeat their mistakes or make equal and opposite ones instead?

> 对代理人行为进行建模可以帮助我们理解市场动态，并有望提高市场效率，降低危机发生的可能性。我们缺乏大规模使用零日期权与极端波动相互作用的良好历史先例。虽然有一些启发性的例子，例如解放日前后市场的剧烈波动，但这恰恰说明了问题的难点：如何区分解放日特有的波动和期权交易造成的波动？此外，如果要预测下一轮极端波动，又该如何判断市场参与者是会重蹈覆辙，还是会采取相反的策略？

For active market participants, identifying tipping points is crucial not just for profits, but for survival. Some firms involved in the episodes described above did not survive, suffered continuity-threatening losses, or, most embarrassingly, joined the category of asset managers whose compounded returns are decent and whose total lifetime alpha in dollars is negative because they were managing so much at the peak.

> 对于活跃的市场参与者而言，识别临界点不仅关乎盈利，更关乎生存。上述事件中的一些公司未能幸存，遭受了足以威胁持续经营的损失，或者更令人尴尬的是，由于在市场高峰期管理了过多的资产，它们最终沦为资产管理公司行列，尽管其复合收益率尚可，但由于管理规模过大，其终生总阿尔法收益（以美元计）为负值。

The importance of this research goes beyond financial markets as it might shed light on what Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang recently remarked will be the coming “age of Agentic AI.” The explosive growth of tools such as OpenClaw is suggestive of a future where agents take on many responsibilities for us, for example helping with healthcare decisions, financial planning and organizing our travel and entertainment activities.The full implications are uncertain, but we believe financial markets offer a particularly instructive lens for observation and understanding.

> 这项研究的重要性远不止于金融市场，它或许能为英伟达首席执行官黄仁勋近期所言的即将到来的“智能体人工智能时代”提供一些启示。诸如OpenClaw之类的工具的爆炸式增长预示着未来智能体将承担起我们诸多职责，例如协助我们进行医疗保健决策、财务规划以及安排旅行和娱乐活动。其全部影响尚不明朗，但我们相信，金融市场为我们提供了一个极具启发性的观察和理解视角。

Financial markets possess three properties that make them an unusually good laboratory for studying multi-agent systems: clearly defined rules, powerful incentives (money, reputation, survival), and lots of data. Add to that relentless selection pressure—agents that consistently underperform are diminished, and those that outperform gain influence. Markets even capture subtleties like the difference between good outcomes on average and good outcomes for the average person. And they tend to incubate surprisingly complex emergent behaviors that can then be checked for realism against the real world: we can generate all the synthetic data we want and check it for realism against historical data that goes back centuries.

> 金融市场具备三大特性，使其成为研究多智能体系统的绝佳实验室：清晰明确的规则、强大的激励机制（金钱、声誉、生存）以及海量数据。此外，还有持续不断的筛选压力——表现持续不佳的智能体会被淘汰，而表现优异的智能体则会获得更大的影响力。市场甚至能够捕捉到一些细微差别，例如平均而言的良好结果与对普通人而言的良好结果之间的差异。而且，市场往往会孕育出出人意料的复杂涌现行为，我们可以用现实世界的数据来检验这些涌现行为的真实性：我们可以生成任意数量的合成数据，并将其与追溯到几个世纪前的历史数据进行比对，以验证其真实性。

Researchers working at the intersection of finance and reinforcement learning have already shown that market-making algorithms, when left to learn and adapt, can converge on collusive equilibria—improving their own profits at the expense of everyone else, without any explicit coordination. If that can happen in a regulated, transparent market, what happens when AI agents negotiate prices, allocate resources, or manage logistics in less structured environments? At the Santa Fe institute collaborations between behavioral economists and physicists including Doyne Farmer, Brian Arthur and Blake LeBaron have examined [https://www.thediff.co/r/2656191b?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] agent interactions and how irrationality can cascade into socio-economic systems.

> 金融和强化学习交叉领域的研究人员已经证明，市场算法在自主学习和适应的过程中，会趋向于合谋均衡——即在没有任何明确协调的情况下，以牺牲其他所有人的利益为代价来提高自身利润。如果这种情况在受监管、透明的市场中都会发生，那么当人工智能代理在结构性较弱的环境中进行价格谈判、资源分配或物流管理时，又会发生什么呢？在圣塔菲研究所，包括多恩·法默、布莱恩·阿瑟和布莱克·勒巴伦在内的行为经济学家和物理学家合作，研究了代理之间的互动以及非理性行为如何蔓延到社会经济系统中。

The frontier AI labs are also wrestling with these problems in an attempt to align increasingly powerful LLMs with humanity’s best interests and their designers’ stated intentions. The work on alignment and interpretability has received increasing focus and made encouraging progress. However, financial market episodes suggest that even thoughtfully designed systems and strategies can encounter unexpected outcomes in complex and dynamic systems—though they also suggest that a population of heterogeneous agents can achieve a sort of rock-paper-scissors equilibrium where one of them never quite dominates.[2] [https://www.thediff.co/r/d85212db?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]

> 前沿人工智能实验室也在努力解决这些问题，试图使功能日益强大的逻辑逻辑模型（LLM）与人类的最佳利益及其设计者的既定意图保持一致。关于一致性和可解释性的研究日益受到关注，并取得了令人鼓舞的进展。然而，金融市场事件表明，即使是精心设计的系统和策略，在复杂多变的系统中也可能遇到意想不到的结果——尽管这些事件也表明，由不同主体组成的群体可以达到一种类似石头剪刀布的平衡状态，其中一方永远不会完全占据主导地位。[2] [https://www.thediff.co/r/d85212db?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]

We believe these lines of inquiry all have merit and can help develop our understanding of agents acting in noisy and competitive systems. We think this is critical given widespread expectations that algorithmically driven agents will soon take on work previously done by humans; we’re at a brief moment in history where fairly autonomous software agents exist, but don’t yet outnumber humans by orders of magnitude. (That sounds implausible, but there was a similar point in the industrial revolution, when you could have observed that hydrocarbons are powering useful work and will clearly do a lot more of it than humans ever did. The amount of energy used to move an average car the distance of an average commute is about 10,000 calories, or about four full days’ worth of human energy consumption)

> 我们认为这些研究方向都很有价值，有助于我们更好地理解在嘈杂且竞争激烈的系统中行动的智能体。鉴于人们普遍预期算法驱动的智能体很快将取代人类的工作，我们认为这一点至关重要；我们正处于历史上一个短暂的时期，虽然软件智能体已经相当自主，但它们的数量尚未远远超过人类。（这听起来难以置信，但在工业革命时期也曾出现过类似的情况，当时人们观察到碳氢化合物正在驱动各种有用的工作，而且其作用显然将远远超过人类。一辆普通汽车行驶一段普通通勤距离所需的能量约为10,000卡路里，相当于人类四天的能量消耗。）

Financial markets have been confusing since José de la Vega wrote the first book about stock trading in 1688. Our simulations suggest the confusion is not noise—it is the predictable consequence of diverse agents pursuing different goals with different beliefs. Understanding those interactions will not eliminate the confusion, but it can transform it from a source of anxiety into a source of opportunity to better design and manage mission-critical systems.

> 自何塞·德·拉·维加于1688年撰写第一本关于股票交易的书籍以来，金融市场就一直令人困惑。我们的模拟表明，这种困惑并非噪音，而是不同主体怀揣不同信念、追求不同目标的必然结果。理解这些互动并不能消除困惑，但可以将其从焦虑的根源转化为机遇，从而更好地设计和管理关键任务系统。

Midjourney Medical

Late last week, Midjourney, better known for image generation, announced that they're getting into the medical business, specifically whole-body 3D ultrasound tomography (a different kind of image generation!) [https://www.thediff.co/r/def8d86f?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. Their goal is to make these scans ubiquitous, cheap, convenient, and, based on their render of the Midjourney Spa, perhaps luxurious. Doctors are not so enthused; Scott Alexander has a guarded take [https://www.thediff.co/r/c653450c?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c], and plenty of them on Twitter are pretty annoyed. Here [https://www.thediff.co/r/2ed69638?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] is a representative case, though that doctor also walked it back [https://www.thediff.co/r/91a92674?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. The key concern is: if we usually scan people who have symptoms, and we start doing precautionary scans, then people will request biopsies, which risk medical complications. And even if they don't, they'll be worried sick over what doctors call an incidentaloma, i.e. a growth that you weren't looking for, that doesn't have any symptoms, and that is almost certainly benign. The pro-Midjourney argument is: why are you treating the conditional probabilities as the same under different conditions!? Maybe there are some particularly pushy patients, but at some point a doctor is either a trustworthy medical professional, who will tell people no, or a doctor is a service provider who is selling to willing customers who will at least say they're comfortable with the risk they're taking. The more interesting outcome is that if scans get cheap and lots of asymptomatic people keep doing them, we'll get a rich set of historical data that will eventually be great training data once some of them develop illnesses. Now is just a really bad time to bet against anyone who is trying to capture snapshots of data and then predict the contents of the next snapshot.

> 上周晚些时候，以图像生成技术闻名的 Midjourney 公司宣布进军医疗领域，具体来说是全身 3D 超声断层扫描（一种不同的图像生成技术！）[https://www.thediff.co/r/def8d86f?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。他们的目标是让这种扫描技术普及、价格低廉、方便快捷，而且根据他们对 Midjourney 水疗中心的渲染图来看，或许还能带来奢华体验。然而，医生们对此并不热衷；斯科特·亚历山大对此持谨慎态度[https://www.thediff.co/r/c653450c?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]，而许多医生在推特上对此表示不满。这里[https://www.thediff.co/r/2ed69638?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]是一个典型案例，尽管那位医生后来又收回了之前的说法[https://www.thediff.co/r/91a92674?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。关键问题在于：如果我们通常只对有症状的人进行扫描，而现在我们开始进行预防性扫描，那么人们就会要求进行活检，这会带来并发症的风险。即使他们不要求活检，他们也会对医生所说的“偶发瘤”忧心忡忡，也就是那些你原本没有注意到、没有任何症状、而且几乎可以肯定是良性的肿块。支持“中途之旅”理论的观点是：为什么要把不同条件下的条件概率视为相同？！或许有些病人特别坚持，但医生要么是值得信赖的医疗专业人士，会拒绝病人；要么是服务提供者，向自愿接受治疗的顾客推销服务，而这些顾客至少会表示他们能够承受风险。更有意思的是，如果扫描费用降低，大量无症状者持续进行扫描，我们将获得丰富的历史数据，一旦其中一些人患病，这些数据最终将成为极佳的训练数据。现在，任何试图捕捉数据快照并预测下一快照内容的人，都不应该轻易下注。

The less-spoken subtext of this is that programmers and doctors shouldn't be fighting with each other when they could be going after their common enemy, the lawyers. Medical malpractice risk warps doctors' incentives, particularly around questions that are probabilistic when they're posed but that eventually have a definite answer. Which makes the doctors' situation a lot more sympathetic. For one thing, they don't want a bunch of people showing them scans and exaggerating the risk, then asking for treatment. If they treat, there's a risk that something goes wrong with the biopsy. If they don't, eventually one of their patients really will turn out to have cancer, and will be able to point to the scan and show—only with the benefit of hindsight!—that they gave their doctor a picture of it. And, at another level: they can't say this, either! If they say "I don't want my patients to take tests lest they get a superfluous operation, something goes wrong, and they sue me," that statement can get read back to them in court when they're being sued because they didn't order a test. So doctors have an unfortunate incentive to 1) resist any situation where some kind of diagnostic was previously run on sick people and is now being run on healthy ones, and 2) to be a little vague about why. The high-handed attitude that anyone who Googles a symptom or tries to learn about their own body is voiding their warranty is actually the next-best option.

> 这背后的潜台词是，程序员和医生不应该互相争斗，而应该联手对抗他们的共同敌人——律师。医疗事故风险扭曲了医生的激励机制，尤其是在那些看似概率性但最终却有明确答案的问题上。这使得医生的处境更加令人同情。首先，他们不希望一群人给他们看扫描结果，夸大风险，然后要求治疗。如果他们治疗，活检就有可能出错。如果他们不治疗，最终他们的某个病人可能会真的患上癌症，并能指着扫描结果——当然，这只能是事后诸葛亮！——证明他们曾给医生看过扫描结果。而且，从另一个层面来说：他们也不能这么说！如果医生说“我不想让我的病人做检查，以免他们接受不必要的手术，万一出了什么问题，他们还会起诉我”，那么当他们因为没有要求病人做检查而被起诉时，这句话就会在法庭上被反复提及。因此，医生们不幸地倾向于：1）抵制任何之前只对病人进行过诊断，现在却对健康人进行检查的情况；2）对原因含糊其辞。而那种认为任何在网上搜索症状或试图了解自身身体状况的人都会失去“保修”的傲慢态度，实际上是次优之选。

But Midjourney is not the only company that's trying to use AI for medical research, and we'll probably see many similarly-shaped problems. To someone with a lot of computer hardware or a penchant for deep learning, every problem is easiest when you turn it into a data problem, and turning a healthcare problem into a data problem often means running a test on someone who isn't sick, and then telling them something that probably makes a lot of sense to a professional poker player or an ML engineer but that will be easy for a regular person to misinterpret. Doctors are obviously not afraid of new technology, and are actually surprisingly early and aggressive users of AI [https://www.thediff.co/r/10171209?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c].

> 但 Midjourney 并非唯一一家尝试将人工智能应用于医学研究的公司，我们很可能会看到许多类似的问题。对于拥有大量计算机硬件或精通深度学习的人来说，将任何问题转化为数据问题都最为容易解决。而将医疗保健问题转化为数据问题通常意味着对健康人进行测试，然后告诉他们一些对职业扑克玩家或机器学习工程师来说可能很有道理，但对普通人来说却很容易误解的信息。医生们显然并不惧怕新技术，而且实际上他们是人工智能的早期积极用户[https://www.thediff.co/r/10171209?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。

Chips

A rough guide to the chip business is that marginal cost goes down when fixed cost goes up, which 1) leads to some exciting swings in profitability if your new, low-cost capacity comes on line right as demand starts falling, and 2) means that a chip business can be more profitable if it can find more ways to guarantee demand. Google is taking some lessons from Nvidia in that respect [https://www.thediff.co/r/74210a8b?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] ($, WSJ), by backstopping datacenters that use their TPUs. One way or another, Google captures upside from a lower marginal cost for TPUs: it can sell them through Google cloud, offer strategic partnerships, give big partners an alternative to Nvidia (or to Amazon), and, as a backup, use them in its own search, YouTube, etc. business. For now, many of Google's capital-allocation decisions are downstream from the fact that it's its own reserve bidder for inference.

> 芯片行业的一条粗略准则是：固定成本上升时，边际成本下降。这导致两点：1）如果你的低成本新产能恰好在需求开始下降时上线，那么盈利能力将出现令人兴奋的波动；2）芯片企业如果能找到更多方法来保障需求，就能获得更高的利润。谷歌在这方面正从英伟达那里汲取一些经验[https://www.thediff.co/r/74210a8b?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] ($, WSJ)，通过为使用其TPU的数据中心提供支持。无论如何，谷歌都能从TPU边际成本降低中获益：它可以通过谷歌云销售TPU，提供战略合作伙伴关系，为大型合作伙伴提供英伟达（或亚马逊）之外的替代方案，并且作为备用方案，将其用于自身的搜索、YouTube等业务。目前来看，谷歌的许多资本配置决策都源于这样一个事实：它是自身推理能力的储备竞标者。

Disclosure: long NVDA, GOOGL, AMZN.

Financial Engineering

A few years ago, some clever deal types realized that instead of going through the arduous process of acquiring a company, they could offer a hybrid deal where they paid off investors (partly), hired the team (also partly), and left behind some well-funded but mostly inert remnants. But a look back at the historical record for these deals shows that often, the acquired team doesn't stick around long [https://www.thediff.co/r/634f4ce7?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. When there's a new financial maneuver, it can unlock some transactions that weren't possible before. But it also hasn't had time to accrete the kinds of stipulations, slow payouts, representations, etc. that traditional deals do.

> 几年前，一些精明的交易者意识到，与其经历繁琐的收购流程，不如提供一种混合交易：他们既可以部分偿还投资者，又可以部分聘用原团队，留下资金雄厚但基本停滞不前的残余部分。但回顾这些交易的历史记录，就会发现，被收购的团队往往不会长期留任[https://www.thediff.co/r/634f4ce7?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。新的金融运作模式或许能够促成一些以前无法实现的交易，但它也缺乏传统交易中常见的各种附加条款、缓慢的付款周期、陈述等等。

Rigged Games

A pretty obvious way to market a gambling service is to show a video of someone placing a bet and winning. It's the rare case where a pure financial service can actually be pitched in a pleasantly direct way. However, this leads to temptation: make a video that isn't quite identified as an ad, and that shows a winning bet by using a dummy version of the site in question. That's what Polymarket appears to be doing [https://www.thediff.co/r/30f59393?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] ($, WSJ): they've paid online influencers to post clips that portray winning bets, but at least some of these bets were made on "poiymarket.com" instead, and referenced events where the resolution would have been different had the user actually made the bet in question. This is just incredibly sloppy on Polymarket's part: they've created a huge surface area and deliberately posted misleading news snippets on their branded marketing channel. It would be very hard for them to get away with this kind of thing.

> 推广博彩服务最直接的方法之一就是展示一段有人下注并赢钱的视频。纯粹的金融服务很少能以如此直接有效的方式进行推销。然而，这很容易让人产生诱惑：制作一段不明显带有广告性质的视频，并用该网站的虚拟版本来展示赢钱的场景。Polymarket 似乎就是这么做的[https://www.thediff.co/r/30f59393?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]（$，华尔街日报）：他们付费请一些网络红人发布视频，展示赢钱的场景，但至少其中一些赌注是在“poiymarket.com”网站上进行的，而且视频中提到的事件，如果用户真的在网站上下注，结果会截然不同。 Polymarket的做法实在太草率了：他们利用其品牌营销渠道，大肆发布误导性新闻片段。这种行为很难逃脱惩罚。

Reality is the Complement

Getty Images shares have lost 95% of their value during the company's stint as a public company, at least partly because generative AI is such a great solution for the I-need-a-picture-of-something-here issue that leads to so much demand for stock photos. But that also means that even though every article with an AI-generated stock image has a customized illustration, they all tend to look the same. Which means that genuine photographs are, in a sense, more valuable than they were before because they signal that the content in question is not being mass-produced at minimal cost. And how better to distribute such images than through the same tools that make the substitutes? So Getty Images is worth twice what it was last week after announcing a licensing deal with OpenAI [https://www.thediff.co/r/608dc945?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. There are more companies that fit this template, where AI is in one sense a substitute but in another sense turns what they compete with into a premium product by default.
