Today, recursive self-improvement is starting to look less like philosophy and more like ordinary engineering practice.

2008年，当IEEE Spectrum发表关于奇点的特别报告<https://connect.ieee.org/NzU2LUdQSC04OTkAAAGiLMPiXznaWfc8U8r59OCdEqwd9V5WyhU2mNCJAexg6AiDFZs8O3P5R0gOKJtU5lnSTEGXkdI=>时，递归自我改进——即人工智能系统创造更智能的人工智能系统——的概念在很大程度上还只是一个思想实验。这一概念长期以来既是乌托邦式愿景的灵感来源，也是末日预言的根源：一种是超级智能解决人类最棘手难题的科技狂潮，另一种则是失控的智能爆炸将人类远远抛在身后。如今，递归式自我改进正逐渐从哲学层面转向普通的工程实践。

As freelancer Matthew Hutson reports, pieces of the AI-development loop are already becoming automated <https://connect.ieee.org/NzU2LUdQSC04OTkAAAGiLMPiX2LMhjXRTtyM5iWlYCP5NLKkP3Pd9v6t1fO6lI0n6eq_VKMBNnStajwG9bQOeTAVLrc=>. Large language models now write code at frontier AI labs. Systems such as Google DeepMind’s AlphaEvolve are optimizing algorithms and chip designs. Research agents can generate ideas, run software experiments, and critique results. Humans still define the goals, allocate resources, and decide what changes to keep. But the shift underway is significant: AI is no longer just the object of research. Increasingly, it’s part of the research workforce itself.
正如自由撰稿人马修·哈特森报道的那样，人工智能开发流程中的部分环节已经开始自动化<https://connect.ieee.org/NzU2LUdQSC04OTkAAAGiLMPiX2LMhjXRTtyM5iWlYCP5NLKkP3Pd9v6t1fO6lI0n6eq_VKMBNnStajwG9bQOeTAVLrc=>。大型语言模型如今已能在前沿人工智能实验室中编写代码。诸如谷歌DeepMind的AlphaEvolve之类的系统正在优化算法和芯片设计。研究代理可以生成想法、运行软件实验并对结果进行评估。人类仍然负责定义目标、分配资源并决定保留哪些更改。但正在发生的转变意义重大：人工智能不再仅仅是研究对象，它正日益成为研究队伍的一部分。

That doesn’t necessarily mean we’re headed toward one runaway supermind. The emerging picture looks more like an ecosystem of interacting systems that help researchers design algorithms, chips, experiments, and infrastructure for the next generation of AI. As Hutson told me after reporting the story, recursive self-improvement may emerge “not from one program improving itself but from a whole society of them acting collectively.” 
这并不一定意味着我们会迎来一个失控的超级智能体。目前呈现的图景更像是一个由相互作用的系统组成的生态系统，这些系统帮助研究人员为下一代人工智能设计算法、芯片、实验和基础设施。正如哈特森在报道此事后告诉我的那样，递归式自我改进可能“并非源于单个程序的自我改进，而是源于整个程序群体的集体行动”。

What counts as “human-only” cognitive labor is already changing. Tasks once considered core intellectual work are gradually being shared with machines. The real question may not be whether AI can improve AI, but how much of the loop humans ultimately choose to hand over.
 
Eliza Strickland, AI editor
