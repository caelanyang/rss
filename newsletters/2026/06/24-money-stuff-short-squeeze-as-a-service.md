Avis/Pentwater

If you run a public company and you want to raise money, you can sell stock, 
but you probably won’t. Many companies feel a certain squeamishness about 
selling stock, and we have talked a couple <https://bloom.bg/4w6c7Ou> of  times 
<https://bloom.bg/4vY1Gwq> recently about the “de-equitization” of public 
companies over the last few decades. Companies mostly buy back stock; they 
don’t sell it.

如果你经营一家上市公司，想要筹集资金，你可以出售股票，

但你可能不会这么做。许多公司对出售股票感到有些犹豫，

我们最近也多次讨论过过去几十年里上市公司的“去股权化”现象。

公司大多选择回购股票，而不是出售。

One problem with selling stock is the price. If you run a public company, you 
probably think your stock price is too low. If you sell stock to new 
shareholders, you are diluting your existing shareholders: You are selling too 
cheap, transferring some of the company’s existing value to new shareholders at 
the expense of old ones.

出售股票的一个问题是价格。如果你经营一家上市公司，你可能认为你的股价太低了。如果你把股票卖给新股东，你就是在稀释现有股东的权益：你卖得太便宜了，把公司的一部分现有价值转移给了新股东，而牺牲了老股东的利益。

Occasionally, though, a public company will think its stock price is too high: 
The stock price is above the present value of all the cash flows that 
management expects the company to have. This is a more pleasant problem: If you 
sell overpriced stock to new shareholders, that isaccretive for your existing 
shareholders. [1]  <> But it is still, I think, a problem. You are in some 
sense ripping off thenew shareholders, selling them stock for more than it is 
worth. You might feel some sense of fiduciary duty to them too, and feel bad 
about giving them a bad deal. In the big meme-stock wave of 2021, companies 
were initially nervous about selling stock into meme frenzies: Are you even
allowedto sell stock at prices you know are too high? [2]  <>

不过，有时上市公司会认为其股价过高：股价高于管理层预期公司所有现金流的现值。这是一个比较令人愉快的问题：如果你以过高的价格将股票出售给新股东，这对现有股东来说是有利的。[1] 但我认为这仍然是一个问题。在某种程度上，你是在坑害新股东，以高于股票价值的价格出售给他们。你可能也会对他们负有某种信托责任，并对给他们不公平的交易感到愧疚。在2021年的“梗图股票”热潮中，公司最初对在“梗图热潮”中出售股票感到紧张：你是否有权以明知过高的价格出售股票？[2]

The ideal, most pleasant solution would be to sell overpriced stock to 
non-shareholders, people to whom you have no fiduciary responsibilities and 
whom you don’t mind ripping off. (“I would tax foreigners living abroad” is
Monty Python’s <https://bloom.bg/43W927E> ideal tax policy 
<https://bloom.bg/4vY1H3s>.)

最理想、最令人愉悦的解决方案是将价格过高的股票卖给

非股东，也就是你无需承担任何信托责任，并且

你也不介意坑害他们的人。（“我要对居住在国外的外国人征税”是

蒙提·派森的理想税收政策。）

For instance: Your stock is trading at around $100 per share, though you think 
it’s really worth more like $200. Then magic occurs, and for a little while you 
have the opportunity to sell stock at $700 per share. If you sold stock at $700 
to nice enthusiastic retail investors who love your company, you’d feel bad: 
They’re overpaying for the stock and will lose a lot of money when the magic 
ceases and the stock reverts to $100, or perhaps to $200. But what if you sold 
stock at $700 to evil hedge funds who hate your company? Evil hedge funds who 
haveshorted your stock to bet that it will go down: They’re not shareholders; 
they’re the opposite of shareholders. Wouldn’t that be nice? You’d get money by 
selling stock at high prices, which is accretive to your real long-term 
shareholders. The short-selling hedge funds would lose money by buying the 
stock at high prices, but youwant that. Everybody wins, except the people who 
should lose. It’s a perfect trade. All you have to do is figure out how to make 
the magic occur.

例如：你的股票目前交易价格在每股 100 美元左右，但你认为它的实际价值应该在 200 美元左右。然后，奇迹发生了，在一段时间内，你有机会以每股 700 美元的价格出售股票。如果你以 700 美元的价格将股票卖给那些热情洋溢、热爱你公司的散户投资者，你会感到愧疚：他们为股票支付了过高的价格，当奇迹消失，股价回落到 100 美元，甚至 200 美元时，他们会损失惨重。但是，如果你以 700 美元的价格将股票卖给那些憎恨你公司的邪恶对冲基金呢？这些邪恶的对冲基金做空你的股票，押注股价下跌：他们不是股东；他们与股东截然相反。这岂不是很棒？你可以通过高价出售股票赚钱，这有利于你真正的长期股东。做空对冲基金如果以高价买入股票会亏钱，但这正是你想要的。除了那些应该亏钱的人之外，人人都是赢家。这是一笔完美的交易。你只需要想办法让奇迹发生。

今年早些时候，在 Pentwater Capital 披露已持有大量 Avis 股份后，Avis 股价在一个月内飙升超过 600%，创下历史新高。但股价下跌的速度远超上涨速度，短短两天内就抹去了约 70% 的涨幅，Avis 管理层将此归咎于 Pentwater 的抛售行为。

We talked about Avis’s rally <https://bloom.bg/3SWArUy> in April, and then a 
few weeks later we talked aboutPentwater’s sales <https://bloom.bg/44mIQTQ>. 
The bones of the story are:

我们四月份讨论了 Avis 的股价上涨<https://bloom.bg/3SWArUy>，几周后又讨论了 Pentwater 的销售情况<https://bloom.bg/44mIQTQ>。

故事梗概如下：

 * Avis’s stock traded down from around $130 at the start of the year to 
around $100 in mid-March. 
 * Pentwater had been buying Avis stock <https://bloom.bg/4oMhVtZ> for a 
while; it was a6.8% shareholder <https://bloom.bg/3SglMn8> last year. This 
April, itannounced <https://bloom.bg/43SwSBa> that, between its stock and 
derivative positions, Pentwater owned a bit more than 50% of Avis’s stock, 
around 18 million shares. 
 * Another big shareholder — SRS Investment Management, a fund run by an Avis 
board member — also owned more than 50% of the stock. So more than 100% of the 
stock was owned by two big insiders. [3]  <> 
 * How is this possible? There were a lot of short sellers who were betting 
against the stock; something like 49% of the floatwas shorted 
<https://bloom.bg/4vY1JZ8> as of mid-March. Pentwater and SRS owned, roughly 
speaking, all of the outstanding sharesplus some of the shares that had been 
borrowed and shorted. 
 * This was alarming news for short sellers: If all of the stock is owned by a 
few big insiders, there could be a short squeeze in which stock lenders demand 
their stock back and short sellers have to buy it back at any price. 
 * And so, in fact, Avis’s stock shot up, closing as high as $713.97 on April 
21, as short sellers bought back stock. 
 * Then Pentwater, over a few days at the height of the short squeeze, sold 
about 4.3 million shares for about$1.75 billion <https://bloom.bg/4oMhVtZ>, an 
average price of around $400 per share. If you assume that its average purchase 
price was $100 per share or so, then it made a profit of more than $1 billion 
on those 4.3 million shares. 
 * The stock dropped as Pentwater sold; it ended April at $180.67, and has 
mostly been in the $145 to $190 range since. (It closed yesterday at $186.28 
and was up this morning.) 
One somewhat perverse thing to take from this story might be that Pentwater 
caused Avis’s stock price todrop, from the $700s to the $180s. That is Avis’s 
takeaway,Fitzgerald notes <https://bloom.bg/4oTSUNB>:

* 年初时，Avis 的股价约为 130 美元，到 3 月中旬跌至约 100 美元。

Pentwater 一直在买入 Avis 的股票<https://bloom.bg/4oMhVtZ>，

去年，它持有 Avis 6.8% 的股份<https://bloom.bg/3SglMn8>。今年 4 月，Pentwater 宣布<https://bloom.bg/43SwSBa>，通过其持有的股票和衍生品，

Pentwater 持有 Avis 超过 50% 的股份，

约 1800 万股。

另一位大股东——SRS Investment Management，一家由 Avis 董事会成员管理的基金——也持有超过 50% 的股份。

因此，超过 100% 的股份由两位大股东持有。[3]

* 这怎么可能？当时有很多卖空者做空这只股票；截至3月中旬，大约49%的流通股被做空。<https://bloom.bg/4vY1JZ8> Pentwater和SRS大致持有所有流通股，以及一些被借入并做空的股票。

* 这对卖空者来说是个令人担忧的消息：如果所有股票都掌握在少数几位大股东手中，就可能出现空头挤压，届时股票借出方会要求回购股票，而卖空者则不得不不惜一切代价买回股票。

* 事实上，随着卖空者买回股票，Avis的股价飙升，4月21日收盘价高达713.97美元。 * 随后，在空头挤压最猛烈的几天内，Pentwater 抛售了约 430 万股股票，套现约 17.5 亿美元<https://bloom.bg/4oMhVtZ>，平均每股价格约为 400 美元。假设其平均买入价约为每股 100 美元，那么它仅凭这 430 万股就获利超过 10 亿美元。

* 随着 Pentwater 抛售股票，Avis 的股价下跌；4 月底收于 180.67 美元，此后股价大多在 145 美元至 190 美元之间波动。（昨日收于 186.28 美元，今早上涨。）

从这个故事中或许可以得出一个略显讽刺的结论：Pentwater 的抛售导致 Avis 的股价从 700 美元左右跌至 180 美元左右。这就是艾维斯得出的结论。

菲茨杰拉德指出<https://bloom.bg/4oTSUNB>：

“Given the quantum of shares sold in such a short span of time, our stock 
price experienced a significant decline,” Avis CEO Brian Choi said on the
company earnings call <https://bloom.bg/3StckNa> in April. “It seems the only 
insider active during this period of excess volatility was Pentwater Capital.”

“鉴于在如此短的时间内抛售了如此大量的股票，我们的股价出现了大幅下跌，”Avis首席执行官Brian Choi在4月份的公司财报电话会议上表示。“似乎在这段股价剧烈波动的时期，唯一活跃的内部人士是Pentwater Capital。”

Another, more informative thing to take from this story is that Pentwater 
caused Avis’s stock price torise, from the $100s to the $700s. It’s still in 
the $180s! Avis’s stock price is durably higher than it was before Pentwater’s 
intervention. Seems weird for Avis to be like “ugh they drove down our stock 
price.”

从这个故事中我们还可以得出另一个更有价值的结论：Pentwater 的介入使 Avis 的股价从 100 美元飙升至 700 美元。目前股价仍在 180 美元以上！Avis 的股价远高于 Pentwater 介入之前的水平。Avis 抱怨“唉，他们把我们的股价拉低了”似乎有点奇怪。

But as we also discussed <https://bloom.bg/44mIQTQ>, there is a problem with 
these trades. The problem is that US securities law — the “short-swing profit 
rules” ofSection 16 <https://bloom.bg/4tokzXP> — do not allow Pentwater to (1) 
buy a ton of stock in Avis, (2) sell it a few weeks later at a profit and (3) 
keep the profit. Once Pentwater became a 10% holder of Avis’s stock, it was an 
“insider” for purposes of these rules, and it could not keep any profit it made 
on “short-swing” trades (buying and selling within six months). It couldmake 
the profit — it could buy at $100, or $200, and sell at $400, or $700 — but it 
would have to give up the profit to the company.

但正如我们之前讨论过的（https://bloom.bg/44mIQTQ），这些交易存在一个问题。问题在于美国证券法——第16条的“短期交易获利规则”（https://bloom.bg/4tokzXP）——不允许Pentwater公司(1)大量买入Avis公司的股票，(2)几周后卖出获利，以及(3)保留利润。一旦Pentwater公司持有Avis公司10%的股份，根据这些规则，它就属于“内部人士”，因此不能保留任何通过“短期交易”（六个月内买卖）获得的利润。它可以赚取利润——它可以以100美元或200美元的价格买入，然后以400美元或700美元的价格卖出——但它必须将利润上缴给公司。

Still, you can work with that. Pentwater bought a bunch of stock in the $100 
to $200 range, which had the effect of igniting a short squeeze and pushing the 
stock to $700. Then it sold a bunch of stock in the $400 range, which had the 
effect of defusing the short squeeze and making, like, a billion dollars of 
profit. So far, so good. And then the third stage of the trade is returning the 
profit to Avis.

不过，这招还是管用的。Pentwater 在 100 到 200 美元之间买入了一批股票，结果引发了一场空头挤压，把股价推高到了 700 美元。然后，它又在 400 美元左右卖出了一批股票，结果平息了空头挤压，赚了大约 10 亿美元。到目前为止，一切顺利。接下来，交易的第三阶段就是把利润返还给 Avis。

But, you know. For one thing, the Section 16 rules are sort of complicated, 
and Pentwater had an argument that it didn’t actually owe Avis much of its 
profits. It argued that itbought the stock in some funds and accounts that it 
managed, andsold the stock out of other funds and accounts that it managed, and 
you couldn’t “match” most of the buys and sells under the Section 16 rules. 
When it initially announced its sales,Pentwater said <https://bloom.bg/4t7l7kG> 
that it was “engaged in discussion with the Issuer and [has] agreed to 
voluntarily disgorge to the Issuer any short-swing profits realized from these 
matchable transactions.” But it also said that most of its trades werenot 
matchable transactions: Only about94,000 shares <https://bloom.bg/4oGrhr7>, in 
its telling, violated the short-swing profit rules and thus had to give up 
their profits — on the order of tens of millions of dollars — to Avis. The 
rest— on the order of $1 billion of profits — were Pentwater’s to keep. Avis 
apparently disagreed, but that was a starting point for negotiations.

但是，你知道，首先，第 16 条规则相当复杂，

而且 Pentwater 辩称，它实际上并不欠 Avis 多少利润。

它辩称，它是通过其管理的某些基金和账户买入股票，

又通过其管理的其他基金和账户卖出股票，

而根据第 16 条规则，你无法“匹配”大部分买卖交易。

Pentwater 最初宣布出售股票时表示，

它“正在与发行人进行磋商，并已同意

自愿向发行人返还从这些可匹配交易中实现的任何短期利润。”

但该公司也表示，其大部分交易并非可匹配交易：据其所述，只有约9.4万股股票<https://bloom.bg/4oGrhr7>违反了短期交易利润规则，因此必须将数千万美元的利润上缴给Avis。其余约10亿美元的利润则归Pentwater所有。Avis显然对此表示异议，但这成为了双方谈判的起点。

For another thing, Pentwater’s pitch, in those negotiations, was pretty good. 
It goes something like this: “Hi. We have created a billion dollars of profit
from nothing. We did it by trading your stock, buying it low and selling it 
high. But we sold it highto short sellers, whom you should hate. This trade 
cost you nothing: no money, no dilution. And it cost your shareholders nothing: 
When we sold at $700, it was to evil short sellers who got squeezed, not to 
your good and loyal long-term shareholders. This is just a free transfer of a 
billion dollars from your enemies to … well, to us. And you. We’ll give you 
most of it. But we want a few hundred million dollars as a tip for raising all 
this free money for you.”

另外，彭特沃特在谈判中的说辞相当不错。

大概是这样的：“嗨。我们凭空创造了十亿美元的利润。我们通过买卖你们的股票，低买高卖。但我们把股票高卖给了你们应该恨之入骨的卖空者。这笔交易对你们来说没有任何损失：没有资金投入，也没有股权稀释。对你们的股东来说也没有任何损失：当我们以每股700美元的价格出售股票时，买家是那些被挤压的邪恶卖空者，而不是你们忠诚的长期股东。这相当于把十亿美元从你们的敌人那里免费转移到……嗯，转移到我们这里。还有你们这里。我们会把大部分给你们。但我们需要几亿美元作为小费，感谢我们为你们筹集了这么多‘免费’的资金。”

This pitch is not entirely provably true: Who knows who was buying at $700? 
Every “short squeeze” is some combination of (1) short sellers actually feeling 
squeezed and being forced to buy back stock and (2) retail traders (and 
momentum-trading professionals) betting that short sellers will get squeezed 
and buying stock themselves. But the pitch is probably roughly true and quite 
appealing. “Short squeeze as a service,” a reader called it in an email to me 
in April; I think that’s right.

这种说法并非完全属实：谁知道是谁在700美元买入的？

每一次“空头挤压”都是以下两种情况的某种组合：(1) 卖空者确实感到被挤压，被迫回购股票；(2) 散户交易者（以及动量交易专业人士）押注卖空者会被挤压，并买入股票。但这种说法可能大致正确，而且相当有吸引力。一位读者在四月份给我的邮件中称之为“空头挤压即服务”；我认为这个说法很贴切。

Anyway Avis said in April <https://bloom.bg/4oTSUNB> that it “will go after 
every last dollar that our shareholders are owed,” and itsaid yesterday 
<https://bloom.bg/3QYgEUc> that the $650 million settlement “is subject to 
court approval,” which will require a court to find that “the Company has 
diligently pursued the claims raised in the Section 16(b) Action and that the 
Settlement Amount is fair, reasonable and adequate.” [4]  <>

总之，Avis在4月份表示<https://bloom.bg/4oTSUNB>，它将“追讨股东应得的每一分钱”，并且昨天表示<https://bloom.bg/3QYgEUc>，6.5亿美元的和解协议“尚需法院批准”，这意味着法院需要认定“公司已尽职尽责地追讨了第16(b)条诉讼中提出的索赔，并且和解金额公平、合理且充分。”[4]

Is $650 million every dollar of profit that Pentwater made on this trade? I 
hope not! It’s a great trade and they deserve to make money from it. Is $650 
million a good and fair result for Avis? Absolutely! That’s like 10% of its

Carried interest loans

If you run a hedge fund, and your portfolio goes up by $1 billion in a year, 
you can charge performance fees on that return. Conventionally those fees are 
20% of returns, so you’d get $200 million, which you can use to pay traders and 
buy yachts and stuff.

如果你经营一家对冲基金，你的投资组合一年内增长了10亿美元，

你可以就这部分收益收取业绩费。通常情况下，这些费用是

收益的20%，所以你会得到2亿美元，你可以用这笔钱来支付交易员的薪酬，

购买游艇之类的东西。

If you run a private equity fund, things are different. You do not generally 
charge performance fees on mark-to-market returns, in part because there’s not 
much of a market to mark to. If you buy a company in a leveraged buyout for $5 
billion, and next year it’s worth $6 billion, how do you know? There’s no 
public market for its stock; any valuation that you give the company would be 
subjective and conflicted. Instead, you charge performance fees — generally 
called “carry” or “carried interest” — when yousell the company: If you buy a 
company for $5 billion and sell it for $6 billion, then you have $1 billion of 
gains and collect your $200 million of carry. Instead of collecting fees every 
year on mark-to-market performance, you wait several years to sell the company 
and collect the carry all at once.

如果你管理的是私募股权基金，情况就不同了。你通常不会按市值计价的回报收取业绩费，部分原因是市值计价的市场规模很小。如果你以 50 亿美元通过杠杆收购买入一家公司，而明年它的价值达到 60 亿美元，你怎么知道呢？它的股票没有公开市场；你对公司的任何估值都将是主观的，并且存在利益冲突。相反，你会在出售公司时收取业绩费——通常称为“业绩分成”或“附带权益”：如果你以 50 亿美元买入一家公司，然后以 60 亿美元卖出，那么你将获得 10 亿美元的收益，并收取 2 亿美元的业绩分成。你无需每年按市值计价的业绩收取费用，而是等待几年后出售公司，一次性收取业绩分成。

In a hypothetical steady state, these approaches shake out roughly the same 
way: If you run a big private equity business, you are constantly buying new 
companies in new funds and selling old ones from old funds, so you are 
collecting carry each year. But that’s not guaranteed, and if you have a spell 
of good mark-to-market performance (your companies keep getting more valuable) 
but norealizations (you don’t sell them), you won’t collect very much money, 
and will have trouble paying for upkeep on your yacht.

在假设的稳定状态下，这些方法的最终结果大致相同：

On the one hand, if you think that the core product of an investment bank is

一方面，如果你认为投资银行的核心产品是推介材料，那么银行就永远无法真正摆脱中介作用：客户不会自己制作推介材料。另一方面，（1）推介材料并非投资银行的核心产品，甚至根本算不上产品；（2）无论如何，客户完全有理由期望人工智能能够更快、更好地制作推介材料。

Prediction market index

Last week I made some jokes <https://bloom.bg/4oH6SlQ> about a prediction 
market index, which is just a funny thing to think about. If a stock market 
index goes up, that means that people think the aggregate present value of 
future cash flows to public companies is higher than it was yesterday. If a 
prediction market index goes up, does that mean that people think that events 
are more likely than they were yesterday? Just in general? What could that 
mean? “This is pretty stupid,” I wrote, “but on the other hand I bet there will 
be an absolutely straight-faced launch of a prediction markets index in like 
two weeks.”

上周我开了个玩笑，说了个关于预测市场指数的事儿，这事儿想想就挺有意思的。如果股票市场指数上涨，那就意味着人们认为上市公司未来现金流的现值总和比昨天高。如果预测市场指数上涨，是不是意味着人们认为某些事件发生的可能性比昨天高？或者说，就总体而言？这到底意味着什么？我写道：“这想法挺蠢的，但另一方面，我敢打赌，大概两周后就会有人一本正经地推出一个预测市场指数。”

Good news and bad news: Someone did launch a prediction market index, and here 
it is <https://bloom.bg/3QdSnJG>, but I don’t think it would be fair to call it 
absolutely straight-faced. The index was down a bit as of this morning, so I 
suppose things will happen less.

有好消息也有坏消息：有人确实推出了一个预测市场指数，链接在这里：<https://bloom.bg/3QdSnJG>，但我认为称其“绝对可靠”并不公平。截至今天早上，该指数略有下跌，所以我估计市场波动会减少。
