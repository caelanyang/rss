Universal basic AI stocks

One view of the artificial intelligence boom is that AI will replace all of 
human economic endeavor <https://bloom.bg/4okVT1s>. Robots will make the stuff, 
and robots will manage the factories that make the stuff, and robots will set 
corporate strategy about what stuff to make, and robots will run the hedge 
funds that allocate capital to the companies whose robots make the stuff, etc., 
just robots all the way down. All of the corporate profits that used to go to 
companies that did stuff — manufacturing, retailing, consulting, whatever — 
will in the future go to the meta-companies that own the robots, and all of the 
labor income that used to go to people who had jobs will in the future also go 
to the robot companies, because the robots will take all the jobs.

一种关于人工智能繁荣的观点认为，人工智能将取代所有人类的经济活动。机器人将生产产品，机器人将管理生产产品的工厂，机器人将制定企业战略，决定生产什么产品，机器人将运营对冲基金，这些基金将资金分配给那些使用机器人生产产品的公司，等等，一切都将由机器人掌控。过去流向那些从事生产制造、零售、咨询等各种业务的公司的所有利润，未来都将流向拥有机器人的“元公司”，而过去流向人们的劳动收入，未来也将流向机器人公司，因为机器人将取代所有工作。

I’m sort of kidding but also sort of not. This is, for one thing, the way the 
leaders of the big AI labs talk, possibly because they believe it. The reason 
that the big AI labs have gone from nothing to trillion-dollar valuations in 
the blink of an eye is thatinvestors believe some toned-down version of this 
story. They believe it about the AI labs (thus the valuations) and also about 
thevictims of the AI labs; thus the frequent occasions earlier this year 
<https://bloom.bg/4oiG37o> when an AI lab would announce some product that 
might disrupt some industry, and the stocks of all the companies in that 
industry would drop.SpaceX says <https://bloom.bg/43ZN1Vi> that the total 
addressable market for AI “enterprise applications” is $22.7 trillion, perhaps 
20% of theworld’s economic output <https://bloom.bg/4evrPg0> and perhaps 40% of 
therevenue of the world’s corporations <https://bloom.bg/3Q2g33x>. That is not 
quite “we will replace all human economic endeavor,” but it is closer than any 
non-AI company has ever gotten.

我这话有点开玩笑，但也不完全是。一方面，大型人工智能实验室的领导者们就是这样说话的，或许是因为他们自己也相信。大型人工智能实验室之所以能在一夜之间从一无所有跃升至万亿美元估值，是因为投资者们相信了这个故事的某种淡化版本。他们相信人工智能实验室（因此估值如此之高）的真相，也相信人工智能实验室的受害者们的真相；因此，今年早些时候经常出现这样的情况：人工智能实验室宣布推出可能颠覆某个行业的新产品，导致该行业所有公司的股价下跌。SpaceX 表示，人工智能“企业应用”的潜在市场规模高达 22.7 万亿美元，约占全球经济产出的 20%，以及全球企业收入的 40%。这并非意味着“我们将取代所有人类经济活动”，但比任何非人工智能公司都更接近这一目标。

Another view is “lol this is all dumb, of course the people leading AI labs 
talk like this, they need to raise umpteen bazillion dollars to build data 
centers and pay themselves, and the AI-will-take-over-everything thesis is 
vastly overstated.”

另一种观点是“哈哈，这一切都很愚蠢，当然，领导人工智能实验室的人会这么说，

他们需要筹集数不清的资金来建设数据中心并给自己发工资，而人工智能将统治一切的论点被严重夸大了。”

But let us just suspend disbelief for a moment and assume that the maximalist 
view is correct and soon everything will be AI. What … uh … what should one do 
about that? There was a brief moment when a semi-plausible answer might have 
been “have faith in our benevolent AI overlord, Sam Altman”: For a while, the 
clear leader in AI was OpenAI, which was run as a nonprofit organization with 
the goal ofproducing AI for the benefit of humanity <https://bloom.bg/4esKNE2>, 
not for profit. If that had, you know, worked out, then AI would take all the 
jobs and businesses and produce all the goods and services, but maybe it would 
give away all the goods and services to whoever needed them, a socialist 
paradise overseen by Altman and his nonprofit board of trustees and his helpful 
robots. But more recently OpenAI (1) has a lot more competition for the lead in 
building AI and (2) has become rapaciously capitalist, so never mind.

但我们不妨暂时放下怀疑，假设极端主义者的观点是正确的，那么很快一切都将由人工智能主导。那么……呃……我们该如何应对呢？曾经有一段时间，一个看似合理的答案可能是“相信我们仁慈的人工智能统治者萨姆·奥特曼”：一段时间以来，人工智能领域的领军者是OpenAI，它以非营利组织的形式运营，其目标是开发造福人类的人工智能，而非以盈利为目的。如果一切顺利，人工智能将会取代所有工作和企业，生产所有商品和服务，但或许它会将所有商品和服务免费赠送给任何需要的人，一个由奥特曼、他的非营利董事会和他那些乐于助人的机器人共同管理的社会主义天堂。但最近，OpenAI (1) 在人工智能开发领域面临着更激烈的竞争，(2) 并且变得贪婪无度，所以还是算了吧。

The more self-help answer is that you had better own some robots yourself. The 
ideal way to do that is to start an AI company right this minute, if that’s at 
all plausible, but for most of us it isn’t. Failing that, maybe:

更实用的建议是，你最好自己拥有一些机器人。理想的做法是现在就创办一家人工智能公司，如果这有可能的话，但对我们大多数人来说，这并不现实。如果这行不通，或许可以试试：

 * Sell all your investments and put the money in a portfolio of the big AI 
companies. This takes care of the risk that the AI companies will render all of 
the other companies worthless. 
 * That’s still not enough, though! AI will also render your human capital 
worthless; you need to offset your loss of labor income. 
 * Maybe borrow a bunch more money to invest in AI stocks? 
I emphasize that this is a goofy hypothetical and not at all investment 
advice. But I think it is the logical consequence of believing the AI 
maximalist thesis? In the current world, humans consume goods and services and 
pay for them with some combination of labor income and rents from capital. In 
the AI maximalist future world, you will still want goods and services, but you 
will have to pay for them with rents from the capital you have invested in AI 
stocks, because AI stocks will be the only source of value.

* 卖掉你所有的投资，把钱投入到大型人工智能公司的投资组合中。这样就能规避人工智能公司让其他所有公司变得一文不值的风险。

* 但这还不够！人工智能还会让你的人力资本变得一文不值；你需要弥补劳动收入的损失。

* 或许可以再借一大笔钱投资人工智能股票？

我强调这只是一个异想天开的假设，并非投资建议。但我认为这是相信人工智能至上主义论点的必然结果。在当今世界，人类消费商品和服务，并用劳动收入和资本租金的组合来支付。在人工智能至上主义的未来世界里，你仍然需要商品和服务，但你必须用投资人工智能股票的资本租金来支付，因为人工智能股票将成为唯一的价值来源。

Again, the counterargument is: “This is all dumb, and the AI people only talk 
like this to scare us into giving them money.” I can see both sides.

当然，反驳的观点是：“这一切都是愚蠢的，人工智能领域的人这么说只是为了吓唬我们，让我们给他们钱。” 我觉得双方的观点都有道理。

I feel like a lot of stories in financial markets these days are about 
essentially this tension, “everyone must prepare for apocalypse by owning AI 
stocks” vs. “you are trying to trick us into owning AI stocks.” The most 
literal version might bethis story from Notus last week 
<https://bloom.bg/4xgVYaq>:

我觉得最近金融市场里很多故事都围绕着这样一个矛盾展开：一方面是“人人都应该通过持有人工智能股票来为世界末日做准备”，另一方面则是“你们这是在骗我们买人工智能股票”。最直白的例子或许就是上周Notus的这篇报道：

<https://bloom.bg/4xgVYaq>

Senior U.S. officials have held preliminary discussions with major artificial 
intelligence companies about the potential for the federal government to 
acquire some shares in their firms, according to three people familiar with the 
matter.

据三位知情人士透露，美国高级官员已与多家大型人工智能公司就联邦政府可能收购这些公司部分股份的可能性进行了初步讨论。

Sam Altman, the CEO of OpenAI, has discussed the idea with senior Trump 
administration officials periodically since the president began his second 
term, said two of the sources, all of whom spoke on the condition of anonymity 
to reflect private deliberations. Altman first pitched the concept directly to 
President Donald Trump in a conversation in early 2025, and has discussed it 
again with senior administration officials in recent weeks as a way to more 
broadly distribute the economic benefits of AI to the public, they said.

据两位消息人士透露，OpenAI首席执行官萨姆·奥特曼自特朗普总统第二个任期开始以来，就定期与特朗普政府高级官员讨论过这一想法。这两位消息人士均要求匿名，以反映内部讨论的情况。他们表示，奥特曼在2025年初的一次谈话中首次直接向特朗普总统提出了这一概念，并在最近几周再次与政府高级官员讨论了这一想法，旨在更广泛地将人工智能的经济效益惠及公众。

While planning is ongoing and details are in flux, discussions have centered 
on having the firms voluntarily cede the shares to the government, the people 
said. The returns on the investment could then be directed to public purposes, 
one of the people said, such as distributing a dividend payment to all American 
households. …

据知情人士透露，虽然计划仍在制定中，细节也尚未最终确定，但讨论的重点是让这些公司自愿将股份转让给政府。其中一位知情人士表示，投资收益随后可以用于公共用途，例如向所有美国家庭发放股息。

This instinct is bipartisan: Sen. Bernie Sanders called this week for the U.S. 
government to acquire 50 percent equity stakes in the AI companies, effectively 
bringing the companies under federal control. The Vermont independent’s 
forthcoming bill also would tax at 50% the stock of OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI and 
other AI firms, with the proceeds being placed in a sovereign wealth fund for 
the public.

这种想法并非党派之争：参议员伯尼·桑德斯本周呼吁美国政府收购人工智能公司50%的股权，实际上是将这些公司置于联邦政府的控制之下。这位来自佛蒙特州的独立参议员即将提出的法案还将对OpenAI、Anthropic、xAI和其他人工智能公司的股票征收50%的税，所得款项将存入一个主权财富基金，供公众使用。

Arguably what Altman, Trump and Sanders agree on is something like “AI will 
displace a lot of human economic activity so we’d better make sure everyone 
gets a piece of it.” They share, broadly, the AIoptimist view. 

可以说，奥特曼、特朗普和桑德斯在一点上达成了共识，那就是“人工智能将取代大量的人类经济活动，所以我们最好确保每个人都能从中受益”。他们总体上认同人工智能乐观主义的观点。

Meanwhile a surprising proponent of the AI pessimist view is S&P Dow Jones 
Indices. We havetalked <https://bloom.bg/4ohNvzC> a lot 
<https://bloom.bg/4g5zJOm> in recent weeks <https://bloom.bg/4euIEYv> about how 
index providers are falling over themselves to add the big upcoming AI initial 
public offerings — SpaceX, OpenAI and Anthropic — to their indexes. The most 
important index is the S&P 500, which normally waits to add companies until 
they (1) have been public for at least a year and (2) are profitable. S&P 
published a consultation about waiving some of those requirements so that the 
big AI companies could join the index in six months and before they are 
profitable. It was widely assumed — certainly by me — that this is what S&P’s 
customers wanted, and for fairly obvious reasons:

与此同时，标普道琼斯指数公司竟然也加入了对人工智能持悲观态度的行列，这着实令人惊讶。

最近几周，我们一直在讨论指数提供商如何争先恐后地将即将上市的大型人工智能公司——SpaceX、OpenAI 和 Anthropic——纳入其指数。最重要的指数是标普 500 指数，该指数通常会等到公司 (1) 上市至少一年且 (2) 实现盈利后才会将其纳入。标普发布了一份咨询文件，建议豁免部分要求，以便大型人工智能公司能够在上市六个月后，甚至在实现盈利之前就加入该指数。人们普遍认为——当然也包括我——这正是标普的客户所希望的，原因显而易见：

 * Index investors want to own all the big companies, and SpaceX, OpenAI and 
Anthropic will certainly be big companies, so index investors want to own them. 
 * If you give any credit to the AI maximalist view, then you might worry 
about therest of the S&P 500. Like all those widget companies and financial 
services companies and so forth are gonna get eaten by the robots, if the 
maximalist view is right. Excluding the big AI companies from the S&P 500 makes 
that index, to some extent, a betagainst the AI maximalist thesis. (Not 
entirely — Alphabet and Nvidia are in the index — but to a meaningful extent.) 
If you are a passive investor, your goal is probably not to make a big 
high-stakes bet against a popular thesis of the current stock market. You just 
want the market. Excluding the AI portion of the market is maybe a stronger bet 
than you want to make. 
I wrote once <https://bloom.bg/4okVT1s> that a natural hedge to the AI 
maximalist thesis was to make sure that everyone owned a lot of index funds. 
“Universal basic index fund ownership,” I called it: “You can buy index funds 
that include all of the big US public companies; probably some of them will 
capture the profits from AI.” If the index funds mostly exclude the big AI 
companies, that won’t quite work.

* 指数投资者想要持有所有的大公司，而SpaceX、OpenAI和Anthropic无疑都是大公司，所以指数投资者想要持有它们。

* 如果你认同人工智能至上主义的观点，那么你可能会担心标普500指数中的其他公司。如果至上主义的观点正确，那么所有这些小部件公司、金融服务公司等等都将被机器人吞噬。将大型人工智能公司从标普500指数中剔除，使得该指数在某种程度上与人工智能至上主义的论点背道而驰。（并非完全如此——Alphabet和Nvidia仍在指数中——但确实有相当程度的背离。）

如果你是被动投资者，你的目标可能并非是要对当前股市的流行论点进行高风险的押注。你只是想参与市场。剔除人工智能板块或许比你预想的要冒险得多。我曾在<https://bloom.bg/4okVT1s>上写道，对冲人工智能极端主义论点的一个天然策略是确保每个人都持有大量指数基金。

我称之为“全民基本指数基金持有”：“你可以购买包含所有美国大型上市公司的指数基金；其中一些公司很可能会从人工智能中获利。”但如果这些指数基金大多排除了大型人工智能公司，那么这种策略就行不通了。

On the other hand, a lot of people were really mad at the idea of S&P waiving 
its rules for the big AI IPOs. That was partly a matter of fairness, the look 
on Elon Musk’s face, etc., but it was also partly a matter of valuations. Some 
people just think the AI maximalist thesis is silly and don’t want 10%, or 
100%, or 500% of their money to be allocated to AI companies at their current 
huge valuations, and they don’t want index providers to force their hands.

另一方面，很多人对标普豁免大型人工智能IPO的规则感到非常愤怒。这部分是出于公平考虑，也与埃隆·马斯克的表情等有关，但部分原因也是估值问题。有些人认为人工智能至上主义的理论很荒谬，他们不希望自己10%、100%甚至500%的资金被配置到目前估值过高的人工智能公司，也不希望指数提供商强迫他们这样做。

I guess the skeptics won that one. Last Thursday, Bloomberg’s Isabelle Lee and 
Neil Campling reported <https://bloom.bg/4xyG3Vd>:

看来怀疑论者这次赢了。上周四，彭博社的伊莎贝尔·李和尼尔·坎普林报道了此事（https://bloom.bg/4xyG3Vd）：



S&P Dow Jones Indices will keep its existing eligibility requirements for 
benchmarks including the S&P 500, closing the door to fast entry for big tech 
IPOs like SpaceX and delaying billions of dollars in flows from passive funds.

标普道琼斯指数公司将维持其现有的基准指数（包括标普500指数）的资格要求，这将关闭大型科技公司（如SpaceX）快速上市的大门，并延迟数十亿美元被动型基金的资金流入。

The index provider in a press release Thursday said it will not shorten the 
12-month seasoning period for newly public companies it currently has or waive 
existing profitability and public-float requirements based on a company’s size, 
diverging from a broader industry shift embraced by rivals Nasdaq Inc. and FTSE 
Russell. …

指数提供商周四在一份新闻稿中表示，不会缩短目前对新上市公司实行的12个月“成熟期”，也不会根据公司规模豁免现有的盈利能力和公众持股比例要求，这与竞争对手纳斯达克和富时罗素等公司所采取的更广泛的行业变革背道而驰。

“I am genuinely surprised,” said James Seyffart, ETF analyst at Bloomberg 
Intelligence. “But S&P is the market leader and they can buck the trend.”

彭博资讯ETF分析师詹姆斯·塞法特表示：“我确实感到惊讶。但标普是市场领导者，他们有可能逆势而行。”

And on Friday, Lu Wang, Justina Lee and Isabelle Lee added 
<https://bloom.bg/4xyG8bt>:

周五，陆望、Justina Lee 和 Isabelle Lee 也加入了

“It’s a calculated gamble,” said Campbell Harvey, a finance professor at Duke 
University and partner at Research Affiliates. “If they’re in late and their 
investors do not participate in the explosive upside that happens after June 
12th, that’s the downside. And the upside is they’re late because SpaceX goes

“这是一场经过深思熟虑的赌博，”杜克大学金融学教授、Research Affiliates合伙人坎贝尔·哈维表示。“如果他们入场太晚，他们的投资者就无法参与到6月12日之后SpaceX股价的爆炸性上涨中，这就是风险所在。而好处在于，他们入场晚了，SpaceX的股价在未来12个月内可能会下跌50%。所以这是一个主动的决定。”

[It will] leave investors confronting a dilemma passive investing was designed 
to avoid: buy the stocks and risk chasing hype at stretched valuations, or 
stick with the benchmark and risk missing some of the market’s most 
consequential new companies.

这将使投资者面临被动投资旨在避免的两难困境：

买入股票，冒着被高估值炒作所迷惑的风险，还是

坚持基准指数，冒着错过市场上一些最

重要新公司的风险。

It could be years <https://bloom.bg/4dUURFL> before the big AI companies are 
profitable enough to get into the S&P. And then a few years after that, maybe 
they’ll take over the world. In a sense, S&P is betting they won’t.

大型人工智能公司可能需要数年时间才能实现足够的盈利，从而被纳入标普500指数。而在此之后几年，它们或许会统治世界。从某种意义上说，标普押注它们不会这样做。

Public markets are the new private markets
