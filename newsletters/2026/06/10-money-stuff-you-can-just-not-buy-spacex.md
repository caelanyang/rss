Exit vs. voice

The simple model is that, if you don’t like SpaceX, you don’t have to buy it. 
For whatever reason. If you think that SpaceX is worth $1 trillion, or$780 
billion <https://bloom.bg/4xkd0Em>, or zero, and it sells shares in its initial 
public offering at a $1.78 trillion valuation, you can just not buy any shares. 
(If the price later drops, you can revisit that decision.) Or if you think that 
SpaceX’s governance — in whichElon Musk has total control of the company 
<https://bloom.bg/4ursEv9> and can’t be sued or second-guessed by shareholders 
— is oppressive for shareholders, then just don’t buy any shares. Nobody is 
forcing you to become an oppressed SpaceX shareholder. [1]  <>

简单来说，如果你不喜欢SpaceX，你完全可以不买。

无论出于什么原因。如果你认为SpaceX的价值是1万亿美元，或者7800亿美元，或者根本不值钱，即使它在首次公开募股（IPO）中的估值是1.78万亿美元，你也可以选择不买。

（如果股价之后下跌，你可以重新考虑这个决定。）或者，如果你认为SpaceX的治理模式——埃隆·马斯克完全掌控公司，股东无法起诉或质疑他——对股东构成压迫，那么你完全可以不买。没有人强迫你成为SpaceX的股东。[1]

If you want to go around telling people that SpaceX is bad and that they 
shouldn’t buy it either, that’s your prerogative. But your main defense against 
SpaceX’s governance and valuation is not to buy the stock.

 * Public companies are owned by public shareholders. 
 * Big institutional public shareholders — and particularly widely diversified 
indexed or quasi-indexed public shareholders — have a sort of stewardship 
responsibility for public companies. A private company “belongs” to its 
founders, but in some meaningful sense a public company “belongs” to BlackRock 
and Vanguard and the big public pension funds. They are the large universal 
repeat players; they take responsibility for the companies as a whole. 
 * Those big public shareholders, in their role as stewards of public 
companies, have a grim obligation to own some shares of every company andtell

fraud or fiduciary violations or overpaying their chief executive officers,

 * SpaceX’s governance breaks this social compact: The big public pensions can 
buy shares of SpaceX in or after its IPO, but they won’t be able to supervise 
SpaceX. If they ask for a meeting with Elon Musk to express their concerns as

Alpha capture gig economy

One thing that you sometimes hear is that the modern multimanager 
multi-strategy “pod shop” hedge funds are an evolution of the fund-of-funds. A 
fund-of-funds is a fund that raises money from investors and allocates it to 
different hedge funds. Ideally, the fund-of-funds does some due diligence to 
make sure that the hedge funds are doing a good job; it constructs a portfolio 
of hedge funds with good independent strategies and sources of returns; it 
monitors the hedge fund managers’ performance and moves money out of the funds 
that stop performing well. For this — and for giving investors access to the 
underlying funds — the fund-of-funds charges its investors fees, on top of the 
fees that the underlying funds charge.

你有时会听到这样一种说法：现代多经理多策略“独立基金”对冲基金是基金中基金的演变。基金中基金是指从投资者那里募集资金，并将其分配给不同的对冲基金的基金。理想情况下，基金中基金会进行尽职调查，以确保这些对冲基金运作良好；它会构建一个由具有良好独立策略和收益来源的对冲基金组成的投资组合；它会监控对冲基金经理的业绩，并将资金从业绩不佳的基金中撤出。为此，以及为了让投资者能够投资于这些底层基金，基金中基金除了收取底层基金的费用外，还会向投资者收取额外的费用。

A pod shop performs a similar function but keeps the underlying hedge funds 
in-house: Instead of allocating capital to a bunch of independent hedge funds, 
the pod shophires the managers of those hedge funds, sits them in the same 
office, and allocates capital to them. The advantages of this are numerous. The 
pod shop can move capital among portfolio managers much more quickly, rapidly 
cutting off managers who perform badly and scaling up managers with good 
opportunities. Its due diligence and performance monitoring can be more 
thorough and continuous. It can be more thoughtful about making sure that the 
managers’ sources of return are independent, and about hedging shared risk. 
Maybe it can save money on data subscriptions and office space. Themain feature 
of a fund-of-funds — paying the portfolio managers fees on their returns, and 
adding an extra layer of fees on top — is preserved, but the discipline and 
measurement and risk management are tightened up.

基金组合公司（pod shop）的功能与基金中的基金类似，但它将底层对冲基金

置于公司内部：基金组合公司不将资金分配给众多独立的对冲基金，而是聘用这些对冲基金的经理，让他们在同一间办公室办公，并将资金分配给他们。这样做有很多优势。基金组合公司可以更快地在投资组合经理之间调配资金，迅速

剔除表现不佳的经理，并

扩大具有良好机会的经理的规模。其尽职调查和绩效监控可以更加彻底和持续。它可以更周全地确保经理的收益来源独立，并对冲共同风险。

或许还可以节省数据订阅和办公空间方面的成本。基金中基金的主要特征——向投资组合经理支付收益费用，

并在此基础上增加额外费用——得以保留，但纪律、衡量和风险管理都更加严格。

I think the judgment of the market has been pretty clear that the pod shop is 
a better mousetrap, and a lot of money has flowed into them. There are, 
however, some disadvantages, which have become more apparent in recent years. A 
key difference between a pod shop and a fund-of-funds is that a portfolio 
manager who manages money for a fund-of-funds is her own boss and can manage 
money for other investors too, while a portfolio manager who manages money for 
a pod shop is an employee of that pod shop. [2]  <> This means that there are 
bidding warsfor portfolio managers: If some portfolio manager is very good, she 
can only work at one of Point72, Millennium, Citadel, etc.; they will compete 
to hire her, and she will have a lot of leverage. She will exercise that 
leverage to demand a very large cut of her returns with a large guarantee, 
which will make her very expensive for her employer and its investors. Thefixed 
costs <https://bloom.bg/3Q9hoWg> of a pod shop are enormous. Also, whichever 
fund does hire her will demand that she sign a long non-compete preventing her 
from working for another fund for a year or two after she leaves. That, 
combined with the short-term and mercenary nature of pod-shop employment, means 
that a lot of the best portfolio managers will beon gardening leave 
<https://bloom.bg/4oqakRS> at any time.

我认为市场已经相当明确地表明，独立基金公司（pod shop）是一种更优的选择，因此大量资金涌入。然而，它也存在一些缺点，近年来这些缺点变得更加明显。独立基金公司与基金中的基金（fund-of-funds）的一个关键区别在于，基金中的基金经理是自己的老板，也可以为其他投资者管理资金，而独立基金公司的基金经理则是该基金公司的雇员。[2] 这意味着基金经理的竞争非常激烈：如果一位基金经理非常优秀，她只能在Point72、Millennium、Citadel等公司工作；这些公司会竞相聘用她，而她将拥有很大的议价能力。她会利用这种议价能力，要求获得非常高的收益分成和丰厚的保证，这将使她的雇主及其投资者付出高昂的代价。投资组合经理的固定成本非常高昂。此外，无论哪家基金聘用她，都会要求她签署一份长期竞业禁止协议，禁止她在离职后的一两年内为其他基金工作。再加上投资组合经理工作性质的短期性和功利性，这意味着许多优秀的投资组合经理随时都可能处于“休假”状态。

So perhaps the next natural evolution is a step back toward the fund-of-funds 
model. What you might want is a pod shop model where:

因此，或许下一步的自然演变是回归基金中的基金模式。

你可能想要的是一种小型投资小组模式，其中：

 * The pod shop can still rapidly move capital among portfolio managers, 
scaling up good managers and turning off bad ones. 
 * The pod shop still has minute-by-minute visibility into the portfolio 
managers’ trades, and can risk-manage and hedge them. 
 * The pod shop still pays the portfolio managers a big cut of their returns, 
and charges some extra fees for itself. 
 * But the pod shop doesn’t have to pay huge fixed guarantees to the portfolio 
managers, because they are not exclusive employees of the pod shop and there 
are no auctions for their services. 
 * And, because the portfolio managers aren’t employees of the pod shop or of 
its competitors, the pod shop doesn’t have to wait out two years of gardening 
leave before it can start allocating capital to a new portfolio manager. 
From the portfolio manager’s side, this is sort of a gig economy: You run a 
team, your team produces investment ideas, those ideas get fed into the 
computers of like four big pod shops, some or all of the four big pod shops 
allocate capital to your ideas based on how much their computers like them and 
how much capital they have already allocated to similar ideas, and you get paid 
a cut of the returns that your ideas generate on the capital allocated to them. 
[3]  <> The capital can come and go on a moment’s notice, and your job is not 
really to implement the trades (the pod shops will do that). You might not even 
know how much capital is being traded based on your ideas — the pod shops do 
the trading, not you — but you get paid if the ideas work. Andyou can come and 
go on a moment’s notice: If you sign up another pod shop to pay for your ideas, 
your current stable of pod shops won’t object; if you stop selling your ideas 
to one pod shop, it will just go buy from someone else. The pod shop’s 
advantage is not inemploying you; it’s in having a good model for figuring out 
how much capital to allocate to your ideas and when.

* 投资平台仍然可以快速地在投资组合经理之间调配资金，

从而扩大优秀经理的规模，淘汰不合格的经理。

* 投资平台仍然可以实时掌握投资组合经理的交易情况，

并进行风险管理和对冲。

* 投资平台仍然会向投资组合经理支付高额的收益分成，

并收取一些额外的费用。

* 但投资平台无需向投资组合经理支付巨额的固定保证金，

因为他们并非投资平台的专属雇员，而且他们的服务也无需竞标。

* 而且，由于投资组合经理既不是投资平台的雇员，也不是其竞争对手的雇员，

因此投资平台无需等待两年的“休假期”即可开始向新的投资组合经理分配资金。

从投资组合经理的角度来看，这有点像零工经济：你管理一个团队，你的团队提出投资理念，这些理念会被输入到四家大型投资平台的计算机系统中，这四家大型投资平台中的部分或全部会根据计算机对你理念的偏好程度以及他们已经分配给类似理念的资金量，将资金分配给你的理念，而你则从你的理念在分配给它们的资金上产生的收益中抽取一部分作为报酬。

[3] 资金可以随时进出，你的工作实际上并非执行交易（投资平台会负责执行）。你甚至可能不知道有多少资金是基于你的理念进行交易的——投资平台负责交易，而不是你——但如果理念奏效，你就能获得报酬。而且你可以随时加入或退出：如果你与另一家投资平台签约，为你的想法付费，你目前合作的投资平台不会反对；如果你停止向一家创业孵化器出售你的创意，

它就会转而从其他孵化器购买。创业孵化器的优势不在于让你失业；而在于它拥有一个良好的模式，可以帮助你确定应该为你的想法分配多少资金以及何时分配。

 from external discretionary managers,” use those signals in its own trading, 
and pay the external managers for the signals. “If your alpha comes from being 
very good at evaluating portfolio managers, you should try to find more 
portfolio managers to evaluate,” I wrote. And on Friday, Bloomberg’sLiza Tetley 
and Nishant Kumar reported <https://bloom.bg/4ojF2fo>:

我猜这种模式，粗略地说，可以称为“买方阿尔法捕获”。我们上周讨论过 Citadel 如何开发一项新的阿尔法捕获计划，该计划旨在“收集来自外部全权委托基金经理的交易信号”，并利用这些信号进行自身交易，同时向外部基金经理支付相应的费用。我当时写道：“如果你的阿尔法收益来自于你非常擅长评估投资组合经理，那么你应该尝试寻找更多投资组合经理来进行评估。”周五，彭博社的 Liza Tetley 和 Nishant Kumar 报道了此事。

AI lawyers 

If you run a big alternative asset management firm, you will frequently go out 
and raise funds from institutional investors. Each fund will be structured as a 
limited partnership, and there will be a limited partnership agreement setting 
out the terms of the investments, the rights of the investors, the fees, etc. 
Some of the investors will also have side letters that modify the terms for 
their investment.

如果你经营一家大型另类资产管理公司，你会经常向机构投资者募集资金。每支基金都将以有限合伙的形式构建，并签订有限合伙协议，其中会列明投资条款、投资者权利、费用等。部分投资者还会签署补充协议，对其投资条款进行修改。

You will do a lot of these — each fund will have its own LP agreement and side 
letters with several investors — and, while they will not all beexactly the 
same, there will be a certain family resemblance. In part because that makes 
sense — your relationship with your investors shouldn’t change all that much 
from fund to fund, or from investor to investor — but also in part for the 
practical reason that, when you set up a new fund, the associate at your law 
firm will open the final Word version of the LP agreement for your most recent 
fund, change the names, and circulate that as the LP agreement for the new 
fund. They’re not, like, sitting down with a blank piece of paper and waiting 
for inspiration to strike. They’re notjust copying and pasting, oh no, the 
market evolves and your lawyers are undoubtedly staying up to speed on the 
latest legal and commercial developments and drafting new artisanal contract 
language to reflect those developments. But are theymostly copying and pasting? 
Well.

你会做很多这样的事情——每个基金都会有自己的有限合伙协议和与几位投资者的补充协议——虽然它们不会完全相同，但总会有一些相似之处。部分原因是这很合理——你与投资者的关系不应该因基金或投资者的不同而发生太大变化——部分原因也是出于实际考虑，当你设立新基金时，你律师事务所的律师会打开你最近一个基金的有限合伙协议的最终Word版本，更改名称，然后将其作为新基金的有限合伙协议分发出去。他们不会对着一张白纸坐等灵感降临。他们不会只是简单地复制粘贴，不，市场瞬息万变，你的律师无疑会密切关注最新的法律和商业动态，并根据这些动态起草新的、更具针对性的合同条款。但他们大多只是复制粘贴吗？

嗯。

This is obviously a process that evolves with technology. In the very olden 
days, the associate would have to pull down a bound volume with the old LP 
agreement and hand it to a secretary to retype; the cutting (with scissors) and 
pasting (with paste) were literal. In modern times, Microsoft Word can “replace 
all” to change the names. But also AI.The Financial Times reported last week 
<https://bloom.bg/4dQ48yX>:

这显然是一个随着技术发展而演变的过程。在过去，合伙人必须取下装订好的旧版有限合伙协议，交给秘书重新录入；剪切（用剪刀）和粘贴（用胶水）都是实实在在的操作。如今，微软 Word 可以“全部替换”来更改名称。人工智能也发挥了作用。《金融时报》上周报道：
