The five-year-old startup sells software that can protect companies’ data from hackers equipped with increasingly powerful AI tools—a threat highlighted by Anthropic’s warnings about the cyber capabilities of its Mythos model.For the VC fund Cyberstarts, the Cyera round gives it more bragging rights after its blockbuster Wiz exit last year. The fund wrote Cyera’s first $4.2 million seed funding check five years ago at a $15 million valuation; on paper, Cyberstarts has returned more than 224 times its original investment in Cyera, says Cyberstarts founder Gili Raanan. Its total stake in the company including follow-on investments is worth more than $1.2 billion, Raanan told me. That performance could help Cyberstarts entice backers for its eighth fund. The firm is planning to raise a seed fund of about $60 million, according to people familiar with its plans. Last year it raised a $380 million fund to invest in the firm’s mature startups, riding the tailwinds of Google’s $32 billion acquisition of Wiz, which returned about $1.2 billion to the VC firm.Cyera’s recent growth coincides with rising demand for cybersecurity tools built to protect against AI threats. Cyera sells software that automatically scans companies’ cloud storage, databases, email systems and software such as Microsoft 365 to keep an inventory of sensitive data. Cyera’s software then alerts customers’ IT staff to data that could be exposed or stolen. Cyera competes with Sentra, which was valued at $205 million last year, as well as Palo Alto Networks and Proofpoint, which bought rivals to Cyera in 2023 and 2024.Preventing data theft is a top priority for large companies amid fears that hackers will deploy increasingly sophisticated AI .“Pre-AI, we saw that thesis proving out and bet on it early, but now it's an obvious necessity,” said Cyberstarts managing director Lior Simon, who led the firm’s first investment in Cyera.Cyera has pivoted its strategy several times over the years with input from Cyberstarts and its unique network of cybersecurity executives who offer advice to its portfolio companies in exchange for a share of the fund’s profits. Cyera’s founders were originally thinking of selling products to protect a narrower category of enterprise software, but shifted after feedback from Cyberstarts.“We went to the market and asked our CISO network what pain point we should focus on and they said data security,” Raanan said. “So we had them build a data security product.”Cyera has said that its revenue tripled in each of the past three years—though it hasn’t disclosed total revenue—and it counts large enterprises like BlueCross BlueShield, Paramount and Chipotle as customers. But not every AI-adjacent cybersecurity firm has seen similar good fortune. Some AI security companies that sold themselves to larger firms in the past year were generating only modest revenue when they were acquired. Still, Cyberstarts is betting that the AI security market will only grow and has been making more investments in startups focused on defending against AI-related threats, Simon said.“Everyone investing in AI talks about chips and models, and that's obvious and well-funded,” Simon said. “But what will truly build out the potential of this coming wave is: How secure can you be against whatever is coming up next?”
