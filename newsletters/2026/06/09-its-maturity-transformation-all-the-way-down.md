It's Maturity Transformation All The Way Down

Years ago, I worked in the business of "alternative data"—buying things like anonymized spending data, web scraping, web traffic, app usage, etc. and blending all of these together into fundamental predictions. (This is "alternative" in almost exactly the same sense that "alternative" music is—i.e. a recognizable subcategory of mainstream.) Once, I was commiserating with a friend about how one data delivery delay from a vendor led to a cascading series of problems that eventually meant that we missed a delivery date for a report. We’d baked in some time to get the data, clean it, analyze it, and write it up, and while we usually wound up with a little buffer, this time our run of bad luck meant that we missed the deadline. My friend sagely responded: "this industry is maturity transformation all the way down."

几年前，我从事“另类数据”行业——购买匿名消费数据、网络爬虫数据、网络流量数据、应用使用数据等等，并将所有这些数据融合起来，做出一些基础预测。（这里的“另类”几乎和“另类”音乐的含义完全相同——即主流音乐的一个可识别的子类别。）有一次，我和一位朋友抱怨，供应商一次数据交付延迟引发了一连串问题，最终导致我们错过了报告的交付日期。我们预留了一些时间来获取数据、清理数据、分析数据并撰写报告，虽然通常我们最终都会留出一些缓冲时间，但这次运气不好，我们还是错过了截止日期。我的朋友一针见血地指出：“这个行业从里到外都在经历成熟度的转变。”

Maturity transformation is the alchemy by which banks and other financial institutions can own a portfolio of long-duration assets, but offer depositors (or repo counterparties, or prime brokers, or whoever) theoretically instantaneous liquidity. Calling it "alchemy" is not just poetic—at various points in human history, gold and money have been synonymous, so being able to synthesize a portfolio of mortgages, long-term bonds, business loans, etc. into something that can back a checking account or credit card is a way to literally create what is metaphorically equivalent to gold. This is the closest we'll get to alchemy unless certain approaches to fusion pan out [https://www.thediff.co/r/d96c6f79?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c].

期限转换是一种神奇的技巧，它使银行和其他金融机构能够持有长期资产组合，同时为存款人（或回购交易对手、一级经纪商或其他相关方）提供理论上的即时流动性。称之为“炼金术”并非仅仅富有诗意——在人类历史的某些时期，黄金和货币曾是同义词，因此，能够将抵押贷款、长期债券、商业贷款等资产组合合成一种可以作为支票账户或信用卡担保的资产，实际上就是一种创造出与黄金在象征意义上等同的东西的方法。除非某些融合方法取得成功，否则这大概是我们所能达到的最接近炼金术的境界了[https://www.thediff.co/r/d96c6f79?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。

Depending on the details of your economic orientation, the fact that the financial system sometimes backs demand deposits and short-term borrowings with longer-term debt is a) just a prosaic fact about the way the world works, b) a marvelous wealth-creation engine when used appropriately, or c) a multi-trillion dollar scam. But if you look beyond the literal case of banking, maturity transformation is everywhere—the modern economy is awash with arrangements that, formally or informally, promise more of something on-demand than the provider can always guarantee. And yet, the system mostly holds together.

根据你对经济的看法，金融体系有时会用长期债务来支持活期存款和短期借款这一事实，要么是a) 世界运行的平淡事实，要么是b) 一个运用得当的绝妙财富创造引擎，要么是c) 一个数万亿美元的骗局。但如果你跳出银行业这个具体案例，就会发现期限转换无处不在——现代经济充斥着各种各样的安排，无论正式与否，它们都承诺按需提供超出提供者实际能力范围的产品或服务。然而，这个体系大多依然运转良好。

One of the ways this shows up is in retailers' inventories and quick-service restaurants' preparation speeds. These are not guarantees; you aren't going to sue McDonald's because the drive-through took eleven minutes one time. But the value proposition is that what you get will consistently meet expectations; they're mostly selling a standard deviation of outcomes, not a mean. For mass merchants, part of their pitch is that they'll have what you're looking for, even if there's a surge in demand—candy and flowers on February 13th (or the morning through afternoon of the 14th), burger patties and hot dogs ahead of July 4th and Labor Day, etc. Given that this kind of demand is not just serially-correlated but sometimes self-fulfilling—people preemptively stock up on what they think stores will run out of—it's surprising that the system stays in balance. For quick-service restaurants, part of how they manage it is precisely the opposite of how pre-FDIC banks did; at a McDonald's seeing a big line means you're less likely to participate in a run on their burger inventory. Retailers have more limits, but they also have some tricks that fast food can't match. Seasonal products are a mix of things that basically only sell in certain weeks (e.g. Christmas wreaths, holiday cards) and things that sell continuously but have demand spikes around particular holidays. So they can avoid the empty-shelf problem by restocking seasonally-fluctuating-demand goods to replace season-specific ones—sell the last black cat costume, and you can replace its shelf spot with a jumbo bag of Snickers that has at least a little demand later on. Inventory also exists beyond the level of individual stores; fulfillment centers can keep inventory balanced across a region, and they can shuffle goods between stores if needed. And in both the restaurant and store examples, app-based omnichannel ordering makes things easier. If someone opens an app, and your guess is that they're likely to buy the last of item X, you might intercept that by giving them a big discount on a more in-stock substitute Y. Essentially, these stores get to perform a shell game where they always present as fully-stocked, by cleverly redefining what that means on the fly—but this ends up being what consumers expected in the first place.

这种现象体现在零售商的库存和快餐店的出餐速度上。当然，这些并非绝对保证；你不会因为一次得来速花了11分钟就起诉麦当劳。但其价值主张在于，你得到的商品和服务将始终符合预期；他们主要销售的是结果的标准差，而非平均值。对于大型零售商而言，他们的卖点之一是，即使需求激增，他们也能满足你的需求——比如2月13日（或14日上午至下午）的糖果和鲜花，7月4日和劳动节前的汉堡肉饼和热狗等等。考虑到这种需求不仅具有序列相关性，有时还具有自我实现的效应——人们会提前囤积他们认为商店会缺货的商品——令人惊讶的是，整个系统竟然能够保持平衡。对于快餐店而言，他们应对这种平衡的方式与联邦存款保险公司成立之前的银行的做法截然相反；在麦当劳看到长长的队伍，意味着你不太可能参与抢购汉堡。零售商虽然受到的限制更多，但他们也有一些快餐店无法比拟的技巧。季节性产品包括一些基本上只在特定几周销售的商品（例如圣诞花环、节日贺卡），以及一些常年销售但在特定节假日需求量会激增的商品。因此，零售商可以通过补充季节性需求波动的商品来避免货架空空的问题——比如，卖掉最后一件黑猫服装后，可以用一包之后至少还有点需求的士力架巧克力来填补空缺。库存管理不仅仅局限于单个门店；配送中心可以保持区域内库存平衡，并在需要时在门店之间调配商品。无论是餐厅还是商店，基于应用程序的全渠道订购都让一切变得更加便捷。如果有人打开一个应用，而你猜测他们很可能会买走最后一件商品 X，那么你可以通过提供库存更充足的替代品 Y 的大幅折扣来促成交易。本质上，这些商店玩着一种障眼法，通过巧妙地重新定义“库存充足”的含义，始终营造出库存充足的假象——但这最终却恰恰是消费者最初的预期。

It's easier to deliver bits than burgers, but demand swings can be bigger. It's likely that the biggest single win from the Covid era was Operation Warp Speed, but the second-biggest was that billions of stir-crazy people never lost access to endless high-quality video streaming entertainment. It probably saved us a few riots and revolutions. But Netflix and YouTube weren't designed in advance to keep that many people happily couchlocked at once, nor was the infrastructure through which they're delivered. Netflix agreed to reduce the quality of their streaming in the EU, partly to avert congestion for other parts of the network [https://www.thediff.co/r/992c0d08?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. YouTube did the same [https://www.thediff.co/r/91d55178?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c], and video game companies reduced their download speed at that time as well. There was just a lot of streaming, both of entertainment and of work-related conference calls, at a blurrier and choppier resolution than in January of 2020. And yet, the streaming continued. The relevant finance analogy is probably banks imposing withdrawal limits; you can get your pixels, just not all of your pixels all at once.

配送视频比配送汉堡包容易得多，但需求波动也更大。新冠疫情时代最大的胜利或许是“曲速行动”，但第二大胜利是数十亿被困在家中的人们从未失去无限畅享高质量视频流媒体娱乐的机会。这或许避免了一些骚乱和革命。但Netflix和YouTube并非预先设计成能让如此多的人同时乐于宅在家中，它们赖以生存的基础设施也并非如此。Netflix同意降低其在欧盟的流媒体质量，部分原因是为了避免网络其他地区出现拥堵[https://www.thediff.co/r/992c0d08?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。 YouTube也采取了同样的措施[https://www.thediff.co/r/91d55178?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]，当时游戏公司也降低了下载速度。无论是娱乐内容还是工作相关的视频会议，视频质量都比2020年1月时更加模糊卡顿。然而，流媒体服务依然在继续。这或许可以类比于银行设置取款限额；你可以提取你所需的像素，但不能一次性提取所有像素。

Some services have had a more pleasant, but no less stressful, demand spike. OpenAI had a big one when they released ChatGPT and created a new category, which they promptly had to throttle and paywall. In their case, revenue was an important consideration, but the other one was finding some way to ensure that users didn't feel that they were getting ripped off—paying customers still got their tokens, and free users couldn't be too annoyed that their research preview didn't come with a rock-solid SLA. In OpenAI's case, they implicitly offered users much more than they could deliver, but that's a nice problem to have. (It was an especially nice problem back then, when the supply of GPUs was a bit more elastic.)

有些服务的需求激增过程虽然令人愉悦，但也同样压力巨大。OpenAI 在发布 ChatGPT 并创建了一个新类别时就经历了一次巨大的需求高峰，他们不得不立即限制访问量并设置付费墙。对他们来说，收入固然重要，但另一个关键因素是如何确保用户不会感到被宰——付费用户仍然能够获得代币，而免费用户也不能因为研究预览版没有可靠的服务级别协议 (SLA) 而感到不满。OpenAI 的情况是，他们实际上向用户提供了远超其能力范围的服务，但这算是一个甜蜜的烦恼。（尤其是在当时，GPU 的供应弹性更大，这更是一个甜蜜的烦恼。）

Maturity transformation doesn't exist entirely at the level of companies, or entirely in the world of commercial transactions. In fact, one of the places it's most relevant is in the tradeoff between professional and personal obligations. It's generally possible to be on-call for one of these, but not both. Somewhere or another, either there's a Bagehotian source of emergency liquidity, or something has to give. And if that hasn't come up yet, it means that you're more reliant on credit than you think.

成熟度转变并非完全存在于公司层面，也并非完全存在于商业交易领域。事实上，它最相关的领域之一在于职业义务和个人义务之间的权衡。通常情况下，你可以随时履行其中一项义务，但不可能两者兼顾。总而言之，要么存在某种巴格霍特式的紧急流动资金来源，要么就必须做出取舍。如果这种情况尚未发生，则意味着你对信贷的依赖程度可能超出你的想象。

Weather

A few years ago, The Diff wrote about parametric insurance [https://www.thediff.co/r/46c04707?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] ($), particularly applied to flooding. Traditional insurance covers some specific loss (your house burned down, you got sued, etc.), but parametric insurance covers some statistical event that leads to a high probability of loss, like flooding of X inches over Y minutes at location Z. It doesn't hedge as perfectly as traditional insurance, but it can be a lot faster to administer, and it's less prone to adverse selection.

几年前，《The Diff》杂志曾撰文介绍参数保险[https://www.thediff.co/r/46c04707?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]（$），尤其适用于洪水灾害。传统保险承保的是特定损失（例如房屋被烧毁、被告上法庭等），而参数保险则承保的是导致高概率损失的统计事件，例如Z地点在Y分钟内发生X英寸的洪水。虽然参数保险的对冲效果不如传统保险，但其管理速度更快，且不易受到逆向选择的影响。

One reason for that is that it can be traded on a market, as is happening with monsoon-linked parametric insurance in India [https://www.thediff.co/r/f0c4f7fc?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] ($, Economist). Different people face different monsoon risks, and some might either be insulated against them or see the monsoon as an uncorrelated risk they can profit from insuring. One of the most interesting features of this kind of insurance is that it reveals some mix of a best guess about what future people expect and a best guess about what future they're dreading, weighted by how much they dread it. And that's a valuable signal for policymakers, if they choose to use it.

其中一个原因是它可以像印度季风相关的参数型保险那样在市场上交易[https://www.thediff.co/r/f0c4f7fc?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]（《经济学人》）。不同的人面临不同的季风风险，有些人可能已经具备抵御风险的能力，或者将季风视为一种与季风无关的风险，可以通过投保获利。这类保险最有趣的特点之一是，它反映了人们对未来预期和恐惧程度的某种混合预测。如果政策制定者选择利用这一信息，这将是一个有价值的信号。

Adverse Selection

Polymarket has been running an undisclosed influencer marketing campaign using controversial influencers [https://www.thediff.co/r/69a30a33?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. This is an unfortunate market opportunity. Historically, one of the tradeoffs an online political entertainer faced was that they could be blandly mainstream and have a small audience, or they could throw some bombs, get a big audience, and lose their advertisers. You used to be able to measure just how controversial a commentator was by what kind of health or workout supplement they were shilling. But in the case of prediction markets, that kind of controversy-courting is great for business! The kinds of people who like fringe opinions are the kinds of people who will take the long side of long-shot bets and persistently bleed money to liquidity providers (or, occasionally, have a big win that they then talk about on social media). There are plenty of advertisers who deliberately target less sophisticated customers, but few are in such a good position to systematically extract money from them. All of this is particularly unfortunate given that prediction markets are such an effective tool for aggregating informed opinion; there are probably more socially-optimal ways to subsidize the price-discovery side of these services without encouraging them to part the foolish from their money, even if that's an inevitable side effect of this kind of betting venue.

Polymarket一直在进行一项未公开的网红营销活动，利用一些颇具争议的网红[https://www.thediff.co/r/69a30a33?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。这真是一个令人惋惜的市场机会。过去，网络政治娱乐人士面临的权衡之一就是：要么保持平庸的主流风格，受众群体小；要么抛出一些爆炸性言论，吸引大量受众，但也会失去广告商。过去，人们可以通过评论员代言的保健品或健身补剂来衡量他们的争议程度。但在预测市场中，这种博取争议的做法却能带来巨大的商业利益！那些喜欢极端观点的人，往往会押注于胜算渺茫的赌注，不断地向流动性提供商投入资金（或者偶尔大赚一笔，然后在社交媒体上大肆宣扬）。很多广告商都刻意瞄准那些不太精明的消费者，但很少有广告商能像预测市场那样，如此有效地从他们身上榨取金钱。考虑到预测市场是汇集理性观点的有效工具，这一切尤其令人遗憾；或许有更符合社会效益的方式，既能补贴这类服务的价格发现环节，又不会鼓励消费者掏钱，即便这是这类博彩场所不可避免的副作用。

Compute, and P/S Ratios

Google is temporarily buying $920m/month of compute from SpaceX [https://www.thediff.co/r/c9fc1d32?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. Since Google owns about 5% of SpaceX, and since SpaceX plans to go public at more than 20x revenue, this is technically accretive—but it's year-one accretive, not indefinitely so. Google is not really at a point where they're dependent on getting markups like this, and it's also not likely that SpaceX investors are going to mechanically mark up the company's valuation by x% for every x% revenue increases—the sophisticated investors are putting different multiples on different lines of business (and would put a low one on this one, given that it can be terminated by either side with 90 days' notice). And the Musk fans are betting more on a narrative—an accurate one!—rather than specific current numbers. FY26's cloud computing revenue number matters insofar as it gives SpaceX the cash flow to hit FY36's orbital computing revenue number, but is not that important on its own. Overall, this is a pretty mysterious deal, but the Occam's Razor interpretation is that there really is a compute shortage, and SpaceX is one of the rare companies that can fill in the gap.

谷歌暂时从SpaceX购买价值9.2亿美元的每月计算资源[https://www.thediff.co/r/c9fc1d32?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。由于谷歌持有SpaceX约5%的股份，而SpaceX计划以超过20倍营收的估值上市，因此从技术上讲，这笔交易会提升谷歌的估值——但这种提升仅限于第一年，并非长期有效。谷歌目前并不依赖于这种估值加成，而且SpaceX的投资者也不太可能机械地随着公司营收每增长x%，就将公司估值提高x%——成熟的投资者会对不同的业务线采用不同的估值倍数（考虑到双方都可以提前90天通知终止这项交易，他们给出的估值倍数会比较低）。马斯克的粉丝们更看重的是一个故事——一个准确的故事！——而不是具体的数字。2026财年的云计算收入之所以重要，是因为它能为SpaceX提供足够的现金流，以实现2036财年的轨道计算收入目标，但就其本身而言，意义并不重大。总的来说，这笔交易相当神秘，但根据奥卡姆剃刀原理，我们可以得出这样的结论：计算能力确实存在短缺，而SpaceX是少数几家能够填补这一缺口的公司之一。

Software PE

Historically, for many software companies there's a level of spending on sales and marketing, and on R&D, that makes sense if they expect to grow indefinitely. And there's a much lower level of spending that implies that they're going to keep getting more revenue from existing customers, but won't actually take over the world. Some of them can gracefully shift to the second model over time, but the natural way to do it quickly is to sell to PE. Which is rarer this year than any time since the start of Covid [https://www.thediff.co/r/b1c94f2d?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] ($, FT). We're at a very strange time in the software industry, because AI offers one of the most legible paths to radical cost cuts for software businesses, which ought to make PE deals easier to underwrite. But no PE firm wants to acquire a company thinking they'll harvest high cash flows for a decade only to find out that the business evaporates in a few years. And this caution also means that software companies aren't spending as much on one another's products. The current setup means that there's a lot of pent-up demand, for both software and software equities, that can be unlocked as soon as deals start happening.

从历史上看，许多软件公司在销售、市场营销和研发方面的投入水平，对于预期无限增长的公司而言是合理的。而投入水平则要低得多，这意味着它们将继续从现有客户那里获得更多收入，但实际上并不会垄断市场。有些公司可以随着时间的推移优雅地过渡到第二种模式，但快速转型的自然途径是出售给私募股权公司。然而，自新冠疫情爆发以来，今年的私募股权交易比以往任何时候都更加罕见[https://www.thediff.co/r/b1c94f2d?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] ($, FT)。软件行业正处于一个非常特殊的时期，因为人工智能为软件企业提供了一条最清晰的大幅削减成本的途径，这本应使私募股权交易更容易获得承销。但没有哪家私募股权公司愿意收购一家公司，指望它能带来十年的高现金流，结果却发现这家公司几年后就倒闭了。这种谨慎也意味着软件公司之间互相购买产品的费用有所减少。目前的市场格局意味着，无论是软件还是软件股权，都存在着巨大的潜在需求，一旦交易开始发生，这些需求就能得到释放。

Automation and Utilization

One of the things that makes big consumer packaged goods companies hard to compete with is that their delivery network is denser, so they can manage inventory more efficiently and have a lower cost of delivery. This also makes them ideal early adopters for driverless trucks, which Pepsico is now using [https://www.thediff.co/r/003405a7?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] ($, WSJ). The best place for a driverless truck is somewhere that trucks are running 24/7, where the fixed cost of automation can be amortized over as many ton-miles as possible. That also means that each vehicle is picking up more training data per day of deployment, so it's a data subsidy to future use cases that can't support quite as many hours per day.

大型消费品公司难以匹敌的原因之一是其配送网络密度更高，因此能够更高效地管理库存并降低配送成本。这也使它们成为无人驾驶卡车的理想早期采用者，百事公司目前正在使用这项技术[https://www.thediff.co/r/003405a7?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]（华尔街日报报道）。无人驾驶卡车的最佳应用场景是全天候运行的场所，这样可以将自动化系统的固定成本分摊到尽可能多的行驶里程上。这也意味着每辆车每天的运行时间更长，从而为未来那些无法支持如此长时间运行的应用场景提供数据支持。
