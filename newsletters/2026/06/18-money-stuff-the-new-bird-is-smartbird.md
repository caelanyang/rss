AIbirds

It does seem like the preferred way to raise money for an idea in artificial 
intelligence is with institutional venture capital. Some once quite far-fetched 
AI ideas have paid off richly and rapidly in recent years, and there’s a lot of 
venture capital looking for more. We’vetalked about  
Mira Murati’s AI startup, Thinking Machines, which apparently raised $2 billion 
from top venture firms without telling them anything about its product or 
business plan. It had people with good AI credentials, it was doing something 
in AI, good enough!

看来，人工智能领域筹集资金的首选方式是借助机构风险投资。近年来，一些曾经看似遥不可及的人工智能创意获得了丰厚的回报，而且进展迅速，许多风险投资机构仍在寻找更多机会。我们曾讨论过米拉·穆拉蒂 (Mira Murati) 的人工智能初创公司 Thinking Machines，该公司据称从顶级风险投资公司筹集了 20 亿美元，却没有向他们透露任何关于其产品或商业计划的信息。该公司拥有优秀的人工智能从业人员，并且正在做一些人工智能方面的工作，这就足够了！

Probably if you’re one or two tiers down from Murati, in terms of your AI 
pedigree, you can still get a pretty good deal from good VCs. Not everyone can, 
though. Presumably there are people with (1) decent AI credentials and (2) a 
desire to raise a pot of money to do something in AI, who nonetheless can’t get 
$1 billion or even $100 million from VCs even in this hot market.

如果你在人工智能领域的资历比穆拉蒂低一两个档次，你仍然有可能从优秀的风险投资机构那里获得相当不错的投资。但并非人人都能如此。想必有些人具备以下条件：(1) 拥有不错的人工智能资历；(2) 渴望筹集资金在人工智能领域有所作为；

即便在如今火热的市场中，他们也难以从风险投资机构那里获得10亿美元甚至1亿美元的投资。

There are other sources of money? For instance, retail investors in the public 
market seem interested in AI. You can’t necessarily start a company, raise no 
venture capital, and do an immediate initial public offering with a proposal 
like “ehhhh gonna do some AI stuff, trust me.” But there are plenty of existing 
small US public companies that don’t have much going on. You could take control 
of one, announce “ehhhh gonna do some AI stuff,” and the stock will pop:Before, 
the company was mostly an empty shell,or a deli , but 
now it is a speculative bet on your AI plans and talents. It’s an AI lottery 
ticket, and a lot of people are in the market for AI lottery tickets.

还有其他资金来源吗？例如，公开市场上的散户投资者似乎对人工智能很感兴趣。你不可能创办一家公司，不筹集任何风险投资，然后就带着“嗯……我要做一些人工智能方面的工作，相信我”之类的提案立即进行首次公开募股。但是，有很多现有的小型美国上市公司并没有太多实质性进展。你可以收购其中一家，宣布“嗯……我要做一些人工智能方面的工作”，然后股价就会飙升：以前，这家公司大多只是一个空壳公司，或者一家熟食店，但现在，它变成了对你人工智能计划和才能的投机性赌注。这就像一张人工智能彩票，而很多人都在寻找人工智能彩票。

Then you do an at-the-market stock offering — or a floating-strike convertible 
 — to sell stock, at new AI-inflated prices, to 
retail investors. Then you have $100 million to do your AI thing. Then you can 
do your AI thing. Will your AI thing be as good as Murati’s AI thing? I mean. 
It is tempting to think that the top venture capitalists have better sorting 
and vetting capacities than microcap retail stock investors do. But everyone’s 
moving pretty fast and making lottery-like bets, and what do I know?

Oops

The main modern way to invest is to buy the index, or most of it. Most 
investors should assume that they’renot smarter than the market 
, or at least that they’re not much smarter than the 
market; they have at most specific narrow advantages over the market. Buying 
all of the stocks gets you the market return. Buying most of the stocks, but 
adjusting a bit — buying more of a few stocks that you like; avoiding a few 
stocks that you don’t like — might get you a bit better than the market return. 
The baseline is owning the market, and then you tinker from there. My Bloomberg 
Opinion colleagueJohn Authers writes today :

现代主要的投资方式是购买指数，或者购买其中的大部分。大多数投资者应该假设自己并不比市场更聪明，或者至少比市场聪明不了多少；他们至多拥有一些相对于市场的特定优势。购买所有股票就能获得市场平均回报。购买大部分股票，但稍作调整——多买一些你喜欢的股票，少买一些你不喜欢的股票——可能会获得比市场平均回报略高一些的收益。

投资的基准是持有整个市场，然后在此基础上进行微调。我的彭博观点专栏同事约翰·奥瑟斯今天写道：

Thirty years ago, a fund manager would say they “owned” a stock. Now, they 
tend to say they’re “overweight.” Once, if you didn’t like a stock, you simply 
wouldn’t buy it. With the rise of indexing and benchmarking — judging active 
funds by comparing them to an index — that no longer follows. Put 5% of your 
fund into Nvidia Corp., and that’s a big bet on the world’s largest chipmaker. 
You’d be desperate for the company to succeed. But withNvidia 
 now 7.9% of the S&P 500, a 5% holding becomes a big 
“underweight,” and the manager bizarrely would prefer a company they own to do 
badly. This was labeled the“curse of the benchmarks”  
by Paul Woolley of the London School of Economics. …

三十年前，基金经理会说他们“持有”某只股票。现在，他们倾向于说他们“超配”。过去，如果你不喜欢某只股票，你根本不会买它。但随着指数化和基准化的兴起——通过将主动型基金与指数进行比较来评估其表现——这种情况已经不复存在。将你基金的5%投入英伟达公司，这无疑是对这家全球最大芯片制造商的一次重仓押注。你会迫切希望这家公司取得成功。但由于英伟达目前在标普500指数中占比高达7.9%，5%的持仓就变成了严重的“低配”，而基金经理却出人意料地更希望自己持有的公司表现不佳。伦敦政治经济学院的保罗·伍利将此称为“基准的诅咒”。

Thus investors move ever more in crowds, out of a basic instinct for 
self-preservation. Just likewildebeest on the savannah 
, there is more safety in the middle of the herd than 
as an outlier at the front or back, where they will be far more vulnerable to 
predators. That tendency toward herding gnaws away further at market efficiency.

因此，出于基本的自我保护本能，投资者越来越倾向于群体行动。就像草原上的角马一样，待在群体中间比待在队伍的前面或后面更安全，因为在队伍前面或后面，它们更容易受到捕食者的攻击。这种群体行为进一步削弱了市场效率。

“Quasi-indexing,” you might call  this style of 
investing. But it is not theonly way to invest. Another, more old-fashioned way 
to invest is to buy, like, 20 stocks that you like and think will go up. Your 
starting point is not owning the market; your starting point is a blank sheet 
of paper. This has perhaps three advantages over the quasi-indexing style of 
investing:

你可以把这种投资方式称为“准指数投资”。但这并非唯一的投资方式。另一种更传统的投资方式是购买大约20只你喜欢且认为会上涨的股票。你的起点并非拥有整个市场；你的起点是一张白纸。这种方式相比准指数投资或许有三个优势：

 * For the quasi-indexing style to work for everyone else, somebody has to be 
doing the first-principles work of figuring out what companies are good and how 
much to pay for their stocks. You start from a blank piece of paper so that all 
the indexers and quasi-indexers don’t have to. 
 * If you are good at this style, it is very restful. You only have to buy 
your 20 stocks, hold them, and wait for them to go up. 
 * If you are good at this style, it is very lucrative. Most 
 of the market’s  returns 
 come from just a few stocks. You can just buy those, 
and not the other ones, and then you’ll significantly outperform the market. 
We talk occasionally about professional investors who follow roughly this 
approach and have nice lives. Bill Ackman at Pershing Square Capital Management 
has a lot of stuff going on, butthe main thing he has going on 
 is buying and holding like a dozen stocks that go up.
Chris Hohn  at the Children’s Investment Fund is 
another guy who makes concentrated long-term bets, is a billionaire, and goes 
aroundsaying stuff like  “close your eyes, and in the 
darkness you can know God and God is consciousness.” Plenty of other hedge fund 
managers don’t have time to close their eyes; they might miss a crucial market 
move.

* 要想让准指数投资策略对所有人都有效，就必须有人从头开始，找出哪些公司值得投资，以及应该为它们的股票支付多少价格。你从一张白纸开始，这样所有指数投资者和准指数投资者就不必再从头开始了。

* 如果你擅长这种投资策略，它会非常轻松。你只需要买入20只股票，持有它们，然后等待它们上涨。

* 如果你擅长这种投资策略，它会非常赚钱。市场的大部分收益都来自少数几只股票。你只需要买入这些股票，而不用买其他股票，这样你就能显著跑赢市场。

我们偶尔会谈到一些专业投资者，他们大致遵循这种投资方法，并且过着优渥的生活。潘兴广场资本管理公司的比尔·阿克曼

有很多事情要做，但他主要做的事情

是买入并持有十几只上涨的股票。

儿童投资基金的克里斯·霍恩是另一位进行集中长期投资的人，他是一位亿万富翁，并且经常

说一些诸如“闭上眼睛，在黑暗中你可以认识上帝，上帝就是意识”之类的话。许多其他对冲基金经理

没有时间闭上眼睛；他们可能会错过关键的市场波动。

There is a significant selection bias here, though. If you are a professional 
concentrated buy-and-hold investment manager and you buy the 20 stocks that go 
up, you will make a lot of money and get on lists and have profiles written 
about you in which you can muse about the nature of consciousness. If you are a 
professional concentrated buy-and-hold investment manager and you buy 20other 
stocks — ones that go down, or even ones that go up less than the overall 
market — you mostly won’t.

然而，这里存在显著的选择偏差。如果你是一位专业的集中买入并持有策略的投资经理，并且你买入了那20只上涨的股票，你就会赚很多钱，登上各种榜单，甚至会有专门为你撰写的传记，让你有机会思考意识的本质。但如果你是一位专业的集中买入并持有策略的投资经理，并且你还买入了另外20只股票——那些下跌的股票，甚至是那些涨幅低于整体市场的股票——你基本上就赚不到钱了。

Those people have to be out there, though. The whole premise of modern 
investing is that not everyone can pick the 20 best stocks. If all the other,

GP vs. LP

Hedge funds are pretty good at aligning incentives between their managers and 
their investors (who are generally called limited partners, or LPs). The 
manager manages the investors’ money and tries to make it go up; for this, the 
manager traditionally gets a management fee (traditionally 2% of assets each 
year) and a performance fee (traditionally 20% of returns each year). This does 
not create perfect alignment of interests. For one thing, 2% of a large pool of 
assets is real money, and a hedge fund manager might reasonably think — andI 
have argued  — that her main job is not getting high 
returns(and earning 20% of the returns) but rather keeping the investors’ money 
forever(and earning 2% of it, plus some optionality if the returns are good).

对冲基金非常擅长协调基金经理和投资者（通常称为有限合伙人，或LP）之间的利益。基金经理管理投资者的资金并力求使其增值；为此，基金经理通常会收取管理费（通常为每年资产的2%）和业绩费（通常为每年收益的20%）。但这并不能完全实现利益一致。首先，2%的资产是一笔不小的数目，对冲基金经理可能会合理地认为——我也曾论证过——她的主要职责并非获得高回报（以及20%的收益），而是永远持有投资者的资金（并赚取2%的收益，如果收益良好，还可以获得一些期权）。

But pretty good alignment:

 * Getting 20% of good returns is good. 
 * Even if the manager’s goal is just to raise and keep a lot of money and 
earn 2%, good returns are a good way to raise and keep the money. 
 * The hedge fund manager will normally have a lot of her own money in the 
fund, and her own wealthwill often depend  more on 
getting good returns on that money than it does on the fees. 
 * The manager is a hedge fund manager; she has a certain self-confidence and 
risk tolerance. Even if the expected value of being cautious and clipping 2% of 
a lot of money is higher than the expected value of doing lots of trades to try 
to earn high returns, she might try to earn high returns anyway, because that 
is what she is socially conditioned to do. If she wanted to clip management 
fees on a giant pot of money, she’d be a long-only manager. 
On the other hand, sometimes a hedge fund manager will sell shares in her 
management company — that is, in the stream of fees charged to limited partners 
— to outside investors. Those outside investors — “GP investors,” you might 
call them; they invest in the management company, which acts as the general 
partner in the hedge fund — do not have quite the same incentive alignment as 
the actual hedge fund manager, in two ways:

* 获得20%的良好回报是好事。

* 即使基金经理的目标只是募集并持有大量资金，赚取2%的佣金，良好的回报也是募集和持有资金的好方法。

* 对冲基金经理通常会在基金中投入大量自有资金，

而她的财富往往更多地取决于这些资金的良好回报，而不是管理费。

* 这位基金经理是位对冲基金经理；她有一定的自信和风险承受能力。

即使谨慎行事并收取2%的管理费的预期价值高于进行大量交易以尝试获得高回报的预期价值，

她仍然可能会尝试获得高回报，因为这是她受到的社会环境影响而做出的选择。

如果她想从巨额资金中赚取管理费，

她就会成为一名只做多的基金经理。

另一方面，有时对冲基金经理会将她管理公司的股份（也就是有限合伙人收取的费用的一部分）出售给外部投资者。这些外部投资者——你可以称他们为“普通合伙人投资者”；他们投资于管理公司，而管理公司则作为对冲基金的普通合伙人——在以下两个方面与实际的对冲基金经理的利益并不完全相同：

 * They don’t necessarily have their own money in the fund. You can be a GP 
investor in the management company without being an LP investor in the fund. 
 * They are not managing the fund themselves; they do not necessarily share all
of the manager’s self-confidence and risk tolerance. 
Stereotypically, investors in asset management businesses prefer stable 
recurring management fees over lumpy at-risk incentive fees. Bloomberg’s Hema 
Parmar, Preeti Singh, and Layan Odehcheck in on Bridgewater 
:

* 他们不一定在基金中持有自己的资金。您可以成为管理公司的普通合伙人 (GP)

投资者，而无需成为基金的有限合伙人 (LP) 投资者。

* 他们不亲自管理基金；他们不一定拥有基金经理的所有自信和风险承受能力。
