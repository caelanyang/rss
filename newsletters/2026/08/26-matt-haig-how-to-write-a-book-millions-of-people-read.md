Story ideas. Sometimes, it’s just a title hanging in my head. I’ll think, “That’s a good title,” but I need an idea for it. For example, for ages, I had the title The Memory Thief, but I wondered, “Whose memory? Who’s the thief? Why is this even in my head?”

You can have a title without an idea, or sometimes the title is the last thing that comes to me. It just depends. A lot of the process is simply percolating in my brain. There’s a lot of work that doesn’t feel like work, and I’ll beat myself up, thinking, “Why aren’t I working?” But it turns out to be the most important work because I am actually thinking about things.

It’s that classic situation where you’re sitting in front of a Word document and can’t find inspiration. You need to be away from it. Often, when I’m in another country or traveling, an idea will just come to me. You need to be taken out of yourself a little bit to get it.

So, I swing from that state of thinking, percolating, and secretly working—despite beating myself up for not “working”—to then getting an idea. Then I worry that I won’t remember it all, which creates an impulse to rush and write, to get it all down while it’s still in my head.

> **译文：**

You learn when to listen to them and when to defend your position. You understand when they’re speaking as a general reader versus when it’s their specific taste.

> **译文：**

We have a lot of conversations, many phone calls, and plenty of back and forth. My editor, Francis Bickmore, is a nice guy. Sometimes he’s a bit too nice, so I have to dig to understand what he’s truly saying. I tell him to be more brutal. I’ve learned how to translate his feedback.

> **译文：**
> 我们有很多交流，很多电话，也来来回回沟通。我的编辑弗朗西斯·比克莫尔人很好。有时候他太和善了，所以我得费一番功夫才能明白他真正的意思。我告诉他要更直言不讳。我已经学会了如何解读他的反馈。

Sometimes in editing, it’s easier to be brutal because there’s a temptation to correct individual sentences, lines, or small scenes when there’s actually a much bigger problem. It’s not just about a specific page; it’s about addressing a larger structural issue.

> **译文：**
> 编辑时，有时为了避免陷入更大的问题，反而更容易采取严厉的批评，因为人们往往会倾向于修改个别句子、行或小场景。这不仅仅关乎某一页的内容，而是要解决更大的结构性问题。

What famously happens with my editor and me is he’ll suggest a few changes, perhaps a paragraph here or there. But then I’ll suddenly find myself wiping out 50,000 words. If he spots a small problem, I’ll pull at that thread and think, “What is this problem really about?” Often, it turns out that an entire character or even the whole premise doesn’t need to be there.

> **译文：**
> 我和我的编辑之间经常发生这样的事：他会提出一些修改意见，也许是一两段话。但之后我却会突然发现自己删掉了五万字。如果他发现了一个小问题，我会仔细琢磨，琢磨“这个问题到底是什么？”
> 通常情况下，结果发现，一个完整的角色，甚至整个故事的前提，其实都是多余的。

For instance, with my novel The Humans, I think I deleted 40,000 words. It was a small novel to start with, around 80,000 words, so 40,000 of those went. Then another 55,000 words were added between the first and second drafts.

> **译文：**
> 例如，在我的小说《人类》中，我删掉了大约4万字。这部小说最初篇幅不长，大约8万字，所以删掉了其中的4万字。然后，在第一稿和第二稿之间，我又增加了5.5万字。

It’s strange because you’ll click “send” on your first draft, convinced it’s the most perfect, complete, finished thing you can do. You feel like it isn’t a first draft because you’ve lived inside it and been editing as you go.

> **译文：**
> 这很奇怪，因为你点击“发送”按钮提交初稿时，会确信这是你能写出的最完美、最完整、最最终的作品。你感觉它不像是初稿，因为你已经沉浸在其中，并且一直在修改完善。

> **译文：**

Of course, that’s not what happens. There’s always work to be done, but it requires those outside eyes to see it. Editing is an interesting art because you have to approach it knowing there’s no such thing as perfection. You understand there’s no perfect novel you can reach.

> **译文：**
> 当然，实际情况并非如此。总有工作要做，但这需要局外人的视角来审视。编辑是一门有趣的艺术，因为你必须明白，完美并不存在。你要明白，你不可能写出一部完美的小说。

You also learn that there comes a point where, even if each draft improves things, further revisions can start to make it worse. For me, diminishing returns typically set in around the fourth or fifth round. After that, you’re getting so far away from your original vision that the work begins to suffer. You see this in the film industry all the time when projects have been meddled with too much.

> **译文：**
> 你还会发现，即使每一稿都有所改进，但进一步的修改反而会适得其反。对我来说，这种边际效益递减的现象通常在第四稿或第五稿左右出现。之后，你离最初的设想越来越远，作品的质量也开始下降。这种情况在电影行业屡见不鲜，很多项目都因为被过度修改而走向失败。

From a creative standpoint, it’s interesting to consider the fundamentally different nature of AI compared to human evolution. Life famously evolves through mistakes and mutations; that’s how DNA works. In contrast, AI is designed to be a perfectionist. It weeds out all mistakes and halts any equivalent of mutation. > 的运作方式正是如此。相比之下，人工智能的设计目标是追求完美。它会剔除所有错误，并阻止任何形式的突变。因此，生命和技术的进化方式截然相反。
