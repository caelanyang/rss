You might be shocked to learn there is a world outside AI! And we got a glimpse of it in an Oakland, Calif., courtroom today, at the opening of the trial in a lawsuit brought by California and several other states against Meta Platforms over the alleged harms its social media apps have done to kids. This case, part of a deluge of such lawsuits against Meta and other social media companies that started going to trial in January, may be existential for the companies involved, given the damages sought and the operational changes requested of them.

Meta, for instance, has said that the states in the Northern California case are seeking $1.4 trillion in penalties—equivalent to its market capitalization—although the states put the number at closer to $200 billion, according to Reuters. Let’s assume Meta is being hyperbolic. The point is there are thousands of cases, many filed by individuals rather than states, so the costs could quickly add up. As Meta’s finance chief, Susan Li, said on an earnings call last month, the trials scheduled for this year in the U.S.

> **译文：** 例如，Meta公司曾表示，北加州案件中各州寻求1.4万亿美元的罚款——相当于该公司的市值——但据路透社报道，各州给出的数字更接近2000亿美元。我们姑且假设Meta的说法有些夸张。关键在于，此类案件数以千计，其中许多是由个人而非州政府提起的，因此诉讼费用可能迅速累积。正如Meta首席财务官苏珊·李上个月在财报电话会议上所说，今年在美国进行的审判……

“may ultimately result in a material loss.” When Meta uses the word “material,” you know it’s serious. And look, it’s hard to dismiss the worst-case scenarios, given that Meta has already lost a couple of cases, including one in California brought by a young person, which was tried over the winter, and one in New Mexico that resulted in a total of $942 million in penalties levied against Meta. The Northern California case is more akin to the New Mexico lawsuit, as it’s brought by states rather than individuals.

> **译文：** “最终可能导致重大损失。”当Meta使用“重大”一词时，你就知道事情非同小可。而且，考虑到Meta已经输掉了几起官司，包括一起在加州由一名年轻人提起的诉讼（该案在冬季审理），以及一起在新墨西哥州的诉讼（该案最终导致Meta被判处总计9.42亿美元的罚款），我们很难忽视最坏的情况。北加州的这起案件与新墨西哥州的诉讼更为相似，因为它是由州政府而非个人提起的诉讼。

Both cases broadly make similar allegations: that Meta designed its apps to addict young people. As California’s deputy attorney general said on Tuesday, Meta’s business model was designed to “hook the users, hold them for as long as they can, harvest their data and then hide the truth from the public.”Things are never as simple as they appear, however. For one thing, Meta and other social media firms have so far lost attempts to defeat the lawsuits by pointing to the protection they enjoy under Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act, which gives them immunity from liability for what their platforms publish.

> **译文：** 这两起案件的指控大体上类似：Meta 设计其应用程序的目的是为了让年轻人上瘾。正如加州副检察长周二所说，Meta 的商业模式旨在“吸引用户，尽可能长时间地留住他们，收集他们的数据，然后向公众隐瞒真相”。然而，事情远没有表面看起来那么简单。首先，Meta 和其他社交媒体公司迄今为止都未能成功援引《通信规范法》第 230 条来驳回诉讼，该条款赋予他们免于承担平台发布内容责任的豁免权。

You can foresee a scenario in which an appeals court reverses the verdicts found so far on exactly those grounds. (So far, however, one appeals court has not done so.) Leaving aside that issue, Meta also has some defenses. After all, as one Meta lawyer pointed out on Tuesday, the Facebook app is overwhelmingly used by adults, not children. (It’s no secret that the predominance of very old people has put kids off Facebook in recent years.) Instagram skews younger than Facebook, but neither of these apps is like Snapchat, for instance, which is better known for its appeal to kids.

> **译文：** 你可以预见，上诉法院可能会基于这些理由推翻迄今为止的判决。（然而，到目前为止，还没有上诉法院这样做。）撇开这个问题不谈，Meta也有一些辩护理由。毕竟，正如Meta的一位律师周二指出的那样，Facebook应用程序的绝大多数用户是成年人，而不是儿童。（众所周知，近年来，老年人的大量涌入让孩子们对Facebook望而却步。）Instagram的用户群体比Facebook更年轻，但这两款应用程序都不像Snapchat那样，后者因其对儿童的吸引力而闻名。

That reality might undercut the claim that Meta designed its apps to trap kids. Still, that’s the bull case for Meta. The bear case is that the New Mexico verdict demonstrates how much evidence the states can marshal against Meta that won’t sit well with the jury. We’ll have to see how this plays out. Anthropic Goes for SupervotesThis is going to be interesting. The Information scooped the news on Tuesday that Anthropic was preparing to give its co-founders, including CEO Dario Amodei, a class of stock with additional voting power—typically known as supervoting shares—to give them more ability to deal with “outside shareholder pressure.”Supervoting stock is common in tech and in parts of the media sector.

> **译文：** 这一事实或许会削弱Meta设计其应用程序旨在诱骗儿童的说法。不过，这仍然是Meta的利好之处。而其不利之处在于，新墨西哥州的判决表明，各州可以收集到大量不利于Meta的证据，而这些证据很可能无法被陪审团接受。我们拭目以待。Anthropic寻求超级投票权 这将会很有意思。《The Information》周二独家报道，Anthropic正准备向其联合创始人（包括首席执行官Dario Amodei）授予一种具有额外投票权的股票——通常被称为超级投票权股票——以增强他们应对“外部股东压力”的能力。超级投票权股票在科技行业和部分媒体行业很常见。

Theoretically it allows founders to take a long view in their management of a company rather than being beholden to very short-term–oriented investors. The theory is that without these protections, a founder-CEO could be vulnerable to an activist investor unhappy with their management and wanting a quick fix. In practice, that means founders armed with supervoting shares sometimes do things that are unpopular with investors in the short term. Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg, who has supervoting shares giving him control of his company, is a good example: He’s spending a fortune on AI right now, which has not exactly enhanced his standing on the street.

> **译文：** 理论上，超级投票权允许创始人从长远角度管理公司，而不是受制于只关注短期利益的投资者。其理论依据是，如果没有这些保护措施，创始人兼首席执行官可能会受到不满其管理、希望快速解决问题的激进投资者的攻击。实际上，这意味着拥有超级投票权的创始人有时会做出一些短期内不受投资者欢迎的事情。Meta 首席执行官马克·扎克伯格就是一个很好的例子，他拥有超级投票权，从而控制着自己的公司：他目前正在人工智能领域投入巨资，但这并没有提升他在投资者心目中的地位。

The same is true for Snap CEO Evan Spiegel and his quixotic pursuit of augmented reality glasses. (An activist has tried to persuade Spiegel of the error of his ways, so far unsuccessfully.) One issue with Anthropic, though, is that the company already has a trust that has the power to elect a majority of its board. The trust is meant to “address the unique challenges and long-term opportunities we believe transformative AI will present,” as Anthropic has put it. Our story said the new voting rights would “balance the trust’s powers”—but will they simply curtail them?

> **译文：** Snap首席执行官埃文·斯皮格尔(Evan Spiegel)及其对增强现实眼镜的异想天开的追求也面临着同样的问题。（一位活动人士曾试图劝说斯皮格尔放弃他的想法，但迄今为止尚未成功。）然而，Anthropic公司面临的一个问题是，该公司已经设立了一个信托基金，该基金有权选举其董事会的大多数成员。正如Anthropic公司所说，该信托基金旨在“应对我们认为变革性人工智能将带来的独特挑战和长期机遇”。我们的报道指出，新的投票权将“平衡信托基金的权力”——但它们真的会限制信托基金的权力吗？

The devil, however, will be in the details.
