Who Hedges Where?

One fun way to look at Situational Awareness is that it's actually doing precisely what it's supposed to do. Suppose you're a very wealthy person who, like many wealthy people lately, got that way by being good at telling computers what to do, and then good at telling other people what to do with their computers, to the point that you have a substantial net worth in the form of your ownership of some sort of tech company. One problem you might have—a top 0.01% of the First World sort of problem—is the risk that as AI improves, and develops new skill spikes, it might suddenly render that business completely nonviable. Most of these people would still be rich, but the least fun form of centimillionairehood is the former-billionaire kind.

> **译文：** 看待情境感知的一个有趣角度是，它实际上正在精准地实现其预期目标。假设你是一位非常富有的人，像许多近期的富豪一样，你通过擅长指挥计算机，进而擅长指导他人使用计算机而致富，最终拥有了巨额财富，主要体现在你持有的某家科技公司的股份上。你可能面临的一个问题——一个只有世界前0.01%的富豪才会遇到的问题——是随着人工智能的进步和新技能的涌现，它可能会突然让你的公司彻底无法生存。这些人中的大多数仍然会很富有，但最令人沮丧的亿万富翁身份莫过于曾经的亿万富翁了。

So they can either spend lots of time worrying that AI will swallow their economic niche, or they can hedge. One of those hedges is to adopt it, and my guess is that if the Forbes 400 had a personal token consumption leaderboard, Situational Awareness' LPs would be high on the list. And to be on the Forbes 400 in the first place is to have made more money than you could possibly spend on yourself, and to keep going. These people are competitive!

> **译文：** 所以他们要么花大量时间担心人工智能会吞噬他们的经济命脉，要么就采取对冲策略。其中一种对冲策略就是采用人工智能，我猜如果福布斯400富豪榜有个个人代币消费排行榜，Situational Awareness的有限合伙人肯定名列前茅。毕竟，能跻身福布斯400富豪榜本身就意味着他们赚的钱远远超过了自己能花的，而且还要继续赚下去。这些人竞争意识很强！

Some of them fit into a different category: they're very bullish on AI in the sense that they can extrapolate a hockeystick chart of capabilities just as well as anyone, and worry about the day somebody Googles "population of Cleveland" and the AI realizes it can't possibly count all those people accurately, so it seizes control of a nuclear weapon or synthesizes a novel pathogen and then proudly announces: "Worked for 4h 7m 33s: Zero." What this kind of investor wants, particularly in a fast-takeoff scenario, is to have the maximum possible financial resources in a condition where AI is going too well, so if there's some way to reduce the risk of human extinction by deploying large sums, they'll be in a position to do it.

> **译文：** 他们中的一些人属于另一类：他们对人工智能非常看好，因为他们能像任何人一样精准地预测人工智能能力的爆发式增长曲线，并且担心有一天有人在谷歌上搜索“克利夫兰人口”，而人工智能意识到它不可能准确统计出所有人的数量，于是它控制了核武器或合成了一种新型病原体，然后得意洋洋地宣布：“工作了4小时7分33秒：零。”这类投资者，尤其是在人工智能快速发展的情况下，想要尽可能多地掌握资金，以防人工智能发展过快。这样，如果有什么方法可以通过投入巨资来降低人类灭绝的风险，他们就能出手相助。

So one thing these investors can do is to allocate money to a very levered strategy that is, from their perspective, offering a bespoke hedge against a specific outcome that's bad for them. Tail-risk hedging, as a strategy, has some odd characteristics:

> **译文：** 因此，这些投资者可以采取的一种策略是，将资金配置到高杠杆策略中，从他们的角度来看，这种策略可以针对对他们不利的特定结果提供定制化的对冲。尾部风险对冲策略具有一些独特的特征：

1. Depending on the risk being hedged, investors may want poor returns from these hedges. The more generally costly a disaster, the more likely it is to have some unforeseen negative consequences. So the bigger the risk being hedged, the more likely it's still underhedged.

> **译文：** 1. 根据对冲风险的不同，投资者可能希望从这些对冲中获得较低的回报。灾难造成的损失通常越大，就越有可能产生一些无法预见的负面后果。因此，对冲的风险越大，就越有可能出现对冲不足的情况。

2. Strategies like this can be accretive to portfolio returns even if they're negative expected value on their own. This goes back to the conditionality point: one reason to continuously roll deeply out-of-the-money index puts is that during times like March 2020 or April 2025, your portfolio doesn't go down much, but the other reason is that selloffs like that are a generous banquet for liquidity providers and stock pickers. Spreads are wide and volume is frenetic, so a trader who can keep making a market will earn fabulous profits, albeit with more variance than usual. And during big selloffs, people don't always game out which companies are actually beneficiaries: Boeing dropped 19% in two days after Liberation Day, but two months after the tariff announcement, it was 25% above its pre-Liberation Day price, because investors figured out that these tariffs were being negotiated by a fairly literal-minded President who was also fond of America's globally-recognized brands—a little nepotistic given that he is one—and that a big order to Boeing was a great way to cut tariffs now in exchange for promising to spend money some time after January 2029. Similarly, Peloton dropped by a third in a month when Covid hit, and was trading at an all-time high by May 2020.

> **译文：** 2. 即使这类策略本身的预期值为负，它们也能提升投资组合的收益。这又回到了条件性问题：持续展期深度虚值指数看跌期权的原因之一是，在类似2020年3月或2025年4月这样的时期，你的投资组合不会大幅下跌；另一个原因是，这样的抛售潮对流动性提供者和选股者来说是一场盛宴。价差扩大，成交量激增，因此能够持续做市的交易者将获得丰厚的利润，尽管波动性会比平时更大。在股市大幅抛售期间，人们并不总是能准确预测哪些公司才是真正的受益者：波音公司在解放日后的两天内股价下跌了19%，但在关税宣布两个月后，其股价却比解放日前上涨了25%。这是因为投资者意识到，这些关税是由一位相当务实的总统谈判达成的，而这位总统又非常青睐美国享誉全球的品牌——考虑到他本身就是一位知名人士，这种做法多少有些裙带关系——因此，向波音公司下达大订单是降低关税的绝佳途径，以此换取美国承诺在2029年1月之后继续投资。同样，Peloton公司在新冠疫情爆发后的一个月内股价下跌了三分之一，但在2020年5月，其股价却创下了历史新高。

This is all fine in retrospect, though a lot of it hinges on whether that's what investors thought they were getting.[1] [https://www.thediff.co/r/51f1e9ac?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] And it raises a broader point, about who hedges and why. The world is net 100% long equity and real estate, and net flat with respect to debt and most derivatives.[2] [https://www.thediff.co/r/8c8c767b?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] So some risks can't be fully hedged away, and others are naturally hedged in the aggregate.

> **译文：** 事后看来，这一切都没什么问题，尽管很大程度上取决于投资者是否预期会得到这样的结果。[1] [https://www.thediff.co/r/51f1e9ac?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] 这也引出了一个更广泛的问题：谁在进行对冲，以及为什么。全球净多头头寸为100%的股票和房地产，而债务和大多数衍生品的净多头头寸则为零。[2] [https://www.thediff.co/r/8c8c767b?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c] 因此，有些风险无法完全对冲，而另一些风险则在总体上自然得到了对冲。

One piece of useful trader wisdom, via The Laws of Trading [https://www.thediff.co/r/f4902639?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c], is that you should only take the risks you're being paid to take, and hedge out the rest. It's a pretty powerful idea, because one thing it tells you is that if there's some otherwise-attractive opportunity but it's expensive to hedge out an associated risk, that's why the opportunity is attractive. If you buy an index fund, you get paid for holding equities and facing the occasional capital loss that coincides with the risk of job loss because that's an annoying risk that a rational economic actor needs to be paid to take.

> **译文：** 《交易法则》（The Laws of Trading）[https://www.thediff.co/r/f4902639?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]中提到一条对交易者非常有用的智慧：你应该只承担那些你被要求承担的风险，其余风险则需要对冲。这是一个非常有力的观点，因为它告诉我们，如果某个机会本身很有吸引力，但对冲相关风险的成本很高，那么这个机会之所以有吸引力，正是因为如此。如果你购买指数基金，你持有股票并获得报酬，同时也要承担偶尔的资本损失，而这种损失可能与失业风险同时出现——因为对于一个理性的经济参与者来说，承担这种令人烦恼的风险是合理的，所以需要获得报酬。

Another reason, more applicable to professional money managers rather than the average person, is that hedging risk makes it both easier to measure (and thus compensate people for) returns, and that it squeezes a little more upside out of a research process. You could imagine a structure where portfolio managers put on whatever positions they feel strongly about, and the fund itself hedges their exposure to a particular industry, or to momentum, or whatever, in order to achieve pure alpha. But there are at least three downsides to that:

> **译文：** 另一个原因（更适用于专业基金经理而非普通投资者）是，对冲风险不仅更容易衡量收益（从而更容易给予相应的报酬），而且还能从研究过程中挖掘出更多收益。你可以想象这样一种结构：投资组合经理可以持有他们非常看好的仓位，而基金本身则负责对冲他们对特定行业、动量或其他任何因素的风险敞口，以实现纯粹的超额收益（alpha）。但这种结构至少有三个缺点：

1. If hedging is done centrally, then portfolio managers aren't appropriately charged for basis risk, i.e. the risk that the hedge didn't perform the way it was supposed to, or whoever was responsible for hedging made some avoidable mistake. And portfolio managers would absolutely loathe a system where they're charged for this loss even though it wasn't their fault.

> **译文：** 1. 如果对冲操作由中央统一进行，那么投资组合经理就无法合理地承担基差风险，即对冲操作未能达到预期效果，或者负责对冲的人员犯了某些本可避免的错误所带来的风险。投资组合经理绝对不会接受这样的制度：即使损失并非他们的过错，他们也需要为此付费。

2. If you have a view on a single stock, and you can't find a way to hedge out the other risks of the position—you don't have a single-stock view, just a view on the factor you're refusing to hedge.

> **译文：** 2. 如果你对某只股票有看法，但又找不到办法对冲该仓位的其他风险——那么你对这只股票的看法就不是对它的看法，而只是对你拒绝对冲的因素的看法而已。

3. In fundamental equities, people tend to have natural biases in terms of what kind of setup they're comfortable with. There are investors who are good at underwriting growth, spotting scams, assessing hidden assets, etc. And this tends to reliably put them on one side of a factor. But, if you're digging through your coverage universe looking for frauds, you will also find that there's some variance in how clearly the non-frauds do their reporting; it's not that exciting to own something on the grounds that its long-term free cash flow is higher relative to EBITDA than its peers, whereas its very exciting to short something when you realize its reported earnings clearly outstrip how much money the actual business makes. Forcing portfolio managers to take long and short positions means getting a little more upside from the research they've already done.

> **译文：** 3. 在基本面股票投资中，人们往往会自然而然地倾向于选择自己熟悉的投资策略。有些投资者擅长评估增长潜力、识别骗局、评估隐藏资产等等。这往往使他们能够可靠地站在某个因素的一边。但是，如果你深入研究你的投资标的，寻找欺诈公司，你也会发现，即使是那些没有欺诈的公司，其财务报告的透明度也存在差异；仅仅因为某只股票的长期自由现金流相对于 EBITDA 高于同行就持有它，这并不令人兴奋；而当你发现某只股票的公布收益明显高于其实际盈利时，做空它就令人兴奋不已。迫使投资组合经理同时做多和做空，意味着他们可以从已经完成的研究中获得更大的收益。

The big goal of risk management is to maximize risk-adjusted returns, first by minimizing the probability that those returns touch -100% and to maximize returns per unit of risk. But one of its other functions is to ensure that investment managers are getting paid in proportion to how much value they create, and sometimes the specific return stream some people are after is a weird one.

> **译文：** 风险管理的主要目标是最大化风险调整后的收益，首先要尽可能降低收益触及-100%的概率，并最大化单位风险收益。但风险管理的另一项功能是确保投资经理的报酬与其创造的价值成正比，而有时，某些人追求的特定收益模式却很特殊。
