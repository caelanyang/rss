Ad-Supported Platforms are a Unique Antitrust Problem

> **译文：** 广告支持型平台是一个独特的反垄断问题

To most of the people who interact with the big ad-supported platforms, they're basically an infinite free-money glitch. These companies' employees are some of the best-paid people on earth, their investors have been well-rewarded for backing them, and the typical user experiences either an affordable online ordering experience or free video, social media, or search (often all three!).

> **译文：** 对于大多数与大型广告支持平台互动的人来说，这些平台基本上就是一个无限的免费赚钱漏洞。这些公司的员工是世界上收入最高的人群之一，他们的投资者也因支持他们而获得了丰厚的回报，而普通用户要么能享受价格实惠的在线订购服务，要么能免费观看视频、使用社交媒体或搜索服务（通常三者兼得！）。

Something doesn't add up. Where is all that money coming from!?

> **译文：** 这其中肯定有问题。这些钱都是从哪儿来的？！

The answer is that these companies are an ideal test case for an increasingly common business meta-model, where companies try to create a consumer surplus at one end in order to maximize their negotiating leverage for capturing the producer surplus everywhere else in their supply chain.

> **译文：** 答案是，这些公司是日益普遍的商业元模型的理想测试案例，在这种模型中，公司试图在供应链的一端创造消费者剩余，以最大限度地提高其在供应链其他环节获取生产者剩余的谈判筹码。

This is a peculiar model. Typically the way you'd think about a monopoly is to imagine a dynamic market and then take away some of the dynamism. Shrink the number of sellers in a given market, and you’d eventually have a number small enough that they can coordinate to raise prices, or, with a single seller, set whatever price they want.

> **译文：** 这是一个奇特的模型。通常，人们会这样理解垄断：想象一个充满活力的市场，然后削弱其中的一些活力。减少特定市场中的卖家数量，最终卖家数量会少到足以让他们协调起来抬高价格，或者，如果只有一个卖家，他就可以随意定价。

And the big platforms have some room to do this: people have been complaining that Google has been getting worse for over a decade, and to the extent that they're right it hasn't gotten so much worse that a meaningful number of people have switched. Similarly, lots of people know YouTube is full of ads because they spend a lot of time watching videos on YouTube and getting interrupted by the ads.

> **译文：** 大型平台在这方面确实有一定的空间：人们抱怨谷歌的服务越来越差已经超过十年了，即便他们的说法有一定道理，但谷歌的服务恶化程度也不足以让大量用户转投其他平台。同样，很多人都知道YouTube广告泛滥，因为他们经常在YouTube上看视频，并不断被广告打断。

Amazon is in the paradoxical position where it's ostensibly cluttered with junk from brands unpronounceable in any human language, but also the first place you'd shop across many different categories. So, in theory these companies could try to monetize their lock on certain consumers by turning to you, the consumer, and demanding whatever price they can get away with.

> **译文：** 亚马逊的处境颇为矛盾：表面上看，它充斥着各种晦涩难懂、难以发音的品牌商品，但实际上，它却是你购买众多品类商品的首选之地。因此，理论上，这些公司可以通过向消费者抛出橄榄枝，尽可能压低价格，从而利用其对特定消费者的垄断地位来牟利。

Instead, these monopolists are cynically looking for opportunities to give you more: Amazon wants to offer the Universal Bundle of convenient access to consumer goods plus a decent streaming service and some music; Google decided not to let AI exist as a separate category from search, when they'd already been converging for a while ; Meta conceded that short video was a meaningful category and that they had no choice but to compete in it (and also dropped a few tens of billions on making the metaverse the next big category).

> **译文：** 相反，这些垄断者却在暗中寻找机会，为你提供更多：亚马逊想要提供便捷的消费品购买渠道、不错的流媒体服务和一些音乐的“通用捆绑包”；谷歌决定不再让人工智能作为搜索之外的一个独立类别存在，尽管它们已经融合了一段时间；Meta 承认短视频是一个重要的类别，他们别无选择，只能参与竞争（并且还投入了数百亿美元，将元宇宙打造为下一个重要类别）。

The predominant way these companies use their monopolistic power is at the other end of the business: all that generosity to the consumer is monetized through ruthless exploitation of their business counterparties. It makes sense: businesses respond to marketing based on return on investment, but consumers just don't model the world that way most of the time. And even when they do, it's rare for consumers to find a (healthy, non-vice) category where, once they've sampled it a bit, they'll happily increase their spending. But for a business, that's the whole idea: figure out which kind of spending leads to the most contribution margin bang for the buck, and max it out. If a consumer is spending 50% more on the same category each year, something is going wrong; if a business is spending 50% more on something every year, the usual bet is that things are going great.

> **译文：** 这些公司运用垄断权力的主要方式体现在业务的另一端：它们对消费者的慷慨，最终都通过对商业伙伴的残酷剥削而转化为利润。这不难理解：企业会根据投资回报率来调整营销策略，但大多数时候，消费者并非如此看待世界。即便他们确实如此，也很难找到一个（健康的、非不良嗜好的）品类，让他们在尝试之后乐于增加支出。但对企业而言，这正是关键所在：找出哪种支出能带来最高的利润率，并最大化其收益。如果消费者每年在同一品类上的支出增加50%，那肯定有问题；但如果企业每年在某个品类上的支出增加50%，通常意味着一切进展顺利。

This dynamic gives platform operators a few imperatives:

> **译文：** 这种动态给平台运营商带来了一些迫切需要解决的问题：

1. They need to care about users, generally some mix of the average existing user and the theoretical marginal user.

> **译文：** 1. 他们需要关心用户，通常是现有普通用户和理论上的边缘用户的某种混合体。

2. They want to support as broad a mix of activities as they can over time. The more points of contact they have with their users, the better they understand them, and the better the ad targeting will be.

> **译文：** 2. 他们希望随着时间的推移，尽可能支持多种多样的活动。他们与用户的接触点越多，就越了解用户，广告投放的精准度也就越高。

3. They want advertisers' businesses to be as commoditized as possible. For them, the ideal business is one that's high-margin, but with minimal competitive differentiation, such that the main way competitors distinguish themselves from one another is how much they're willing to bid for an audience (e.g, personal injury, insurance, DTC, increasingly sports betting, etc.). This means that they want to make things easy for new entrants and harder for incumbents.

> **译文：** 3. 他们希望广告商的业务尽可能地同质化。对他们而言，理想的业务是高利润率但竞争差异最小的业务，这样一来，竞争对手之间的主要区别就在于他们愿意为获取受众付出多少代价（例如，人身伤害、保险、直接面向消费者、日益流行的体育博彩等）。这意味着他们希望新进入者更容易进入市场，而现有企业则更难生存。

4. On a related note, the platforms want to avoid letting people go direct, for whatever definition might apply: if someone is clicking a search ad or tapping a social one, that interaction is worth more if they've already made up their mind, and worth even more if there's no chance that looking for the product somewhere else would reveal that it has a lower price.

> **译文：** 4. 另外，平台希望避免让用户直接购买，无论这种直接购买的定义是什么：如果有人点击搜索广告或社交广告，那么如果他们已经下定决心购买，这种互动就更有价值；如果他们在其他地方搜索该产品不会发现价格更低，那么这种互动就更有价值。

The best way to understand the result of this is to consider the funniest section of Tim Wu's The Age of Extraction . He talks about the experience of someone who started selling pomade on Amazon, got great results, ultimately depended on Amazon for 90% of his business, and then found that Amazon kept raising fees and cutting into his margins, while competitors kept competing on price and bidding up ads.

> **译文：** 要理解这一结果，最好的方法是看看蒂姆·吴的《提取时代》中最有趣的部分。他讲述了一个人在亚马逊上销售发蜡的经历，生意兴隆，最终90%的业务都依赖于亚马逊，但随后他发现亚马逊不断提高费用，压缩他的利润空间，而竞争对手则不断打价格战，抬高广告价格。

Wu is a major figure in the neo-Brandeisian movement, which wants to limit how much companies can take advantage of pricing power, and whose members tend to be progressive on other issues. And here he is, looking at a market where incumbents can't slack off, where everyone competes on a level playing field, and where the taxes the platform levies are precisely calibrated based on ability to pay—and hates it!

> **译文：** 吴是新布兰代斯主义运动的重要人物，该运动旨在限制企业利用定价权的程度，其成员在其他问题上往往持进步立场。然而，他却对这样一个市场深恶痛绝：在这个市场里，现有企业无法懈怠，每个人都在公平的竞争环境中竞争，平台征收的税费也根据支付能力进行精准调整！

Amazon basically implemented the neo-Brandeisian wishlist within their platform, and the neo-Brandeisians aren't having it![1]

> **译文：** 亚马逊基本上在其平台上实现了新布兰代斯式的愿望清单，但新布兰代斯主义者们并不买账！[1]

Some of this is the result of deliberate planning, but a lot of it is the accumulation of a natural evolutionary process. You don't have to set out to create the closest approximation to perfect competition and optimal taxation. All you have to do is ask what the best way to increase ad revenue is, and think a few steps ahead.

> **译文：** 其中一些是精心策划的结果，但很多是自然演化过程的积累。你不必刻意追求完全竞争和最优税收，只需思考如何才能最大程度地提高广告收入，并提前考虑几步即可。

It's no wonder it's so frustrating to regulate these companies. You can talk a good game about powerful, unaccountable businesses, but it rings a little hollow when they're all in the business of providing an accountability layer: they spend a lot of effort on policing the ads they run, and in an ad-driven business, spam is not just a bad user experience but direct competition. These businesses have accidentally ended up running a kind of populist strategy: 99% of people experience Amazon as increasingly convenient and reasonably cheap, and a small fraction of them are small business owners who struggle to survive in Amazon's world of high taxes and perfect competition.

> **译文：** 难怪监管这些公司如此令人沮丧。你可以高谈阔论那些强大且不受约束的企业，但当它们都在努力提供问责机制时，这种说法就显得有些空洞了：它们投入大量精力监管投放的广告，而在广告驱动的商业环境中，垃圾广告不仅会降低用户体验，还会直接构成竞争。这些企业无意间采取了一种民粹主义策略：99% 的用户认为亚马逊越来越方便且价格合理，而其中只有一小部分是小企业主，他们在亚马逊的高税收和完全竞争的环境下苦苦挣扎。

This model leaves plenty of unanswered questions. For one thing, the cost of ads is economically similar to a tax from the perspective of the business buying the ads, but it's equivalent to a tax in the sense of how it's spent. Right now, those taxes are invested in building datacenters; if your preference is for more economic redistribution to the elderly rather than better and cheaper AI, you might prefer that someone other than big platforms aggressively tax small businesses.

> **译文：** 这种模式留下了许多悬而未决的问题。首先，从购买广告的企业的角度来看，广告成本在经济上类似于税收，但就其用途而言，两者本质上是相同的。目前，这些税收被用于建设数据中心；如果你更倾向于将更多经济资源重新分配给老年人，而不是用于开发更好更便宜的人工智能，那么你或许更希望由大型平台以外的机构来大力向小型企业征税。

On the other hand, suppose you take the view that these companies have been biding their time for the last few decades, only pretending to vigorously compete against one another, relentlessly attempt to commoditize one another's products, and dump vast sums into speculative new products, etc.—one of these days, the thinking goes, they'll start taking advantage of consumers and raking in profits.[2]  But if they actually do that, instead of stacking up capex that will give them a big depreciation shield, they'll be paying taxes! (And even if they spend enough to reduce their tax burden, that shows up as tax revenue somewhere else—an incremental GPU sale has about a 75% gross margin, so those retained earnings are still generating tax revenue somewhere.

> **译文：** 另一方面，假设你认为这些公司在过去几十年里一直在蛰伏，只是假装激烈竞争，不断试图将对方的产品同质化，并将大量资金投入投机性新产品等等——人们认为，总有一天，他们会开始利用消费者，大赚一笔。[2] 但如果他们真的这么做了，他们就无法积累资本支出以获得巨大的折旧抵免，反而要缴税！（即使他们支出足够多以减少税负，这些支出也会在其他地方转化为税收——GPU 的增量销售毛利率约为 75%，因此这些留存收益仍然会在其他地方产生税收。）

The other unanswered question is how far this can go. A broad platform that puts itself between millions of businesses and billions of consumers wields enormous influence, albeit in a subtler way than earlier generations of maximum-breadth businesses like monopoly newspapers or the big three TV networks. Consolidated media companies have a lot of discretion about what does and doesn't count as news, and thus perform an agenda-setting function for legislature.

> **译文：** 另一个悬而未决的问题是，这种影响力究竟能延伸到何种程度。一个横跨数百万企业和数十亿消费者的庞大平台，拥有巨大的影响力，尽管其影响力方式比以往那些覆盖面极广的企业（例如垄断性报纸或三大电视网）要微妙得多。媒体巨头在新闻的定义上拥有很大的自由裁量权，因此它们在立法机构的议程设置中扮演着举足轻重的角色。

Search, social, and e-commerce probably have more influence on people's lives, in the aggregate, but it's through subtle differences in their behavior, like how they weight aggregate popularity against individual preferences in product recommendations, or which kinds of content they're willing to host but not willing to make easy to find. But this is also more diffused internally. When the big three networks were dominant, it took three people saying "that's not news" for a story not to be news.

> **译文：** 搜索、社交和电子商务总体上可能对人们的生活影响更大，但这体现在它们行为上的细微差别，例如它们在产品推荐中如何权衡整体受欢迎程度和个人偏好，或者它们愿意发布哪些类型的内容，却不愿意让用户轻易找到。但这种影响在内部也更加分散。在三大网络巨头占据主导地位的时期，只要有三个人说“这不是新闻”，一条新闻就不算新闻了。

But now, things are less autocratic, and the collectively influential decisions are happening more frequently and lower down the org chart.

> **译文：** 但现在，专制程度有所降低，具有集体影响力的决策发生的频率更高，而且决策层级也更低。

This is a historically unusual setup. There are surprisingly powerful businesses that devote a great deal of their energy to either making consumers better-off in order to exploit more businesses, and trying to kneecap one another's efforts to do this. The companies in question are a bigger share of the economy every year, and a bigger share of effective state capacity. Which means that if there existence is, or will lead to, a problem, it needs to get fixed—but also, their size and disproportionate share of economic growth means that botched regulations make the world a lot poorer.

> **译文：** 这是一种历史上不寻常的局面。一些实力惊人的企业投入大量精力，一方面致力于提升消费者福祉以攫取更多利润，另一方面又试图阻挠彼此的类似努力。这些企业在经济中所占的份额逐年增长，对国家有效资源的利用也日益增多。这意味着，如果它们的存在本身存在问题，或者将来会造成问题，就必须加以解决——但同时，它们庞大的规模和不成比例的经济增长份额也意味着，监管不力会让世界变得更加贫困。

They get excited about random gimmicks like publicly-owned grocery stores or price controls on beer at athletic events, and simply don't engage in any kind of detailed discussion of how those policies might work and what the more efficient alternatives are.

> **译文：** 1. 这么说可能有点不客气，但说实话，我觉得政策圈里那一小撮人几乎都出奇地缺乏见识。他们对一些花里胡哨的噱头，比如公有制超市或者体育赛事上的啤酒价格管制之类的，就兴奋不已，却根本不去深入探讨这些政策到底是怎么运作的，以及有没有更有效的替代方案。

It is, in that world, very low-status to know what the typical profit margin of a grocery store is, and what the low- and high-margin products are. If you're going to make that case, you need to absolutely obsess over how businesses work and how pricing evolves under different scenarios. 2. Companies do, of course, introduce products at low prices in order to get widespread adoption and kickstart a network effect before raising prices. The cleanest cases of that are companies like Netflix and Uber. Uber operates in a duopoly, so to the extent that their plan was to subsidize rides until they had a monopoly and could charge whatever they wanted, they just ended up transferring lots of venture dollars to their customers and drivers and then stopped.

> **译文：** 2. 当然，公司确实会以低价推出产品，以获得广泛的用户接受度，并在提价前启动网络效应。最典型的例子就是 Netflix 和 Uber。Uber 处于双寡头垄断的市场，因此，如果他们的计划是补贴乘车直到形成垄断并可以随意定价，那么他们最终只是将大量风险投资转移给了用户和司机，然后停止了补贴。

Netflix is trickier: in one sense, they're operating in the fragmented market for video entertainment. > **译文：** Netflix的情况比较复杂：一方面，它身处一个分散的视频娱乐市场；另一方面，它和所有竞争对手一样，对某些特定节目拥有垄断权。在这个行业，规模最大的运营商往往对定价拥有最大的控制权，因为他们可以将固定成本分摊到更多观众身上。

But only for a little while: the forgone profits from keeping prices low are proportionate to market share, so the winner of one round of price competition is the target for the next one. ↩︎

> **译文：** 但这只是暂时的：为了维持低价而放弃的利润与市场份额成正比，因此，一轮价格竞争的赢家将成为下一轮的目标。
