Our featured article takes us to the dark side. Its subject is “jailbreaking”: using cleverly framed prompts to persuade an AI system to ignore its safety rules. Cybersecurity researcher David Kuszmar recounts how he jailbroke an AI-powered Darth Vader in the video game Fortnite, persuading the Sith lord to explain card counting and provide instructions for making napalm. And the exploit was more than an amusing stunt. Kuszmar has devised a variety of methods for talking LLMs around their safety rules, with some attacks working across nearly every major commercial chatbot.

> **译文：** 我们的专题文章将带我们走进“黑暗面”。文章的主题是“越狱”：利用巧妙设计的提示语，诱使人工智能系统无视其安全规则。网络安全研究员大卫·库兹马尔讲述了他如何在电子游戏《堡垒之夜》中“越狱”了由人工智能驱动的达斯·维达，成功说服这位西斯尊主解释了算牌技巧，并提供了制造凝固汽油弹的方法。而这不仅仅是一次有趣的尝试。库兹马尔已经设计出多种方法来诱导低级逻辑（LLM）系统绕过其安全规则，其中一些攻击几乎适用于所有主流商业聊天机器人。

In an email for AI Alert, I asked Kuszmar whether newer models have become harder to manipulate. Companies have strengthened their defenses, he told me, but his recent tests suggest that the underlying attacks still work. “Some of the guardrails and intermediary components require a little more coaxing,” he said, “but a lot of them still amount to blacklisted words and phrases you can easily circumnavigate.”

> **译文：** 在给AI Alert的一封邮件中，我询问库兹马尔，新模型是否更难被操纵。他告诉我，各公司已经加强了防御措施，但他最近的测试表明，底层攻击仍然有效。“一些防护措施和中间组件需要更多技巧才能奏效，”他说，“但很多攻击仍然只是一些被列入黑名单的词语和短语，很容易绕过。”

The fundamental problem, as Kuszmar sees it, is that an attacker can use language itself to change the apparent meaning and context of a request until the model no longer recognizes it as dangerous. That makes individual jailbreaks patchable, without necessarily solving the broader vulnerability. “Ultimately,” he said, “I don’t think we can make this architecture stable with the direction things are heading.”

> **译文：** 库兹马尔认为，根本问题在于攻击者可以利用语言本身来改变请求的表面含义和上下文，直到模型不再将其识别为危险。这使得单个越狱漏洞可以被修补，但未必能解决更广泛的漏洞。“最终，”他说道，“我认为照目前的发展方向，我们无法使这种架构保持稳定。”

Conversations About AI

Emily Bender Sets the Record Straight on “Stochastic Parrots”

> **译文：** 艾米莉·本德澄清关于“随机鹦鹉”的真相

In March 2021, a group of four researchers—a collaboration of linguists and computer scientists—published their now legendary paper “On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots: Can Language Models Be Too Big? ” Lead author Emily M. Bender , a professor of computational linguistics at the University of Washington, discussed common misconceptions about the paper and the field of AI with IEEE Spectrum. Question: What are the most common misconceptions about the “stochastic parrots” metaphor? Emily M. Bender: “Stochastic parrot” got picked up and interpreted by other people as a minimization or an insult.

> **译文：** 2021年3月，由四位语言学家和计算机科学家组成的研究团队发表了他们如今已成为经典的论文《论随机鹦鹉的危险：语言模型会不会过大？》。论文的第一作者、华盛顿大学计算语言学教授艾米丽·M·本德(Emily M. Bender)与IEEE Spectrum探讨了关于这篇论文和人工智能领域的一些常见误解。问：关于“随机鹦鹉”这个比喻，最常见的误解是什么？艾米丽·M·本德： “随机鹦鹉”这个比喻被一些人误解为贬低或侮辱。

It was not meant that way. Other people might be using it that way, but that’s not how I intended it, because it’s just a description of what these systems actually are. To see it as an insult requires either the belief that the large language model is the kind of thing that can take offense, which it isn’t, or that these large language models should be understood as steps toward this grand ideal that I don’t hold of artificial intelligence.

> **译文：** 我本意并非如此。或许其他人会那样理解，但这并非我的本意，因为这只是对这些系统实际运作方式的描述。若将其视为一种冒犯，要么是认为大型语言模型会生气（事实并非如此），要么是认为这些大型语言模型应该被理解为迈向某种宏大理想的步骤（而我并不认同这种理想）。
