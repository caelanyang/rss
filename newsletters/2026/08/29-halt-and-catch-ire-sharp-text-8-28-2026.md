Halt and Catch Ire

Friday, August 28, 2026

Natalie Behring/Getty Images

It was only back in May that Ben Thompson and I recorded an episode of Sharp Tech titled “Much Ado About Data Centers.” That was a fairly comprehensive look at the brewing backlash to building local infrastructure to sustain the American AI ecosystem, and three months later, our conversation looks quaint. I’m amazed by how much more widespread the anger has become, and how quickly it’s happened.

> **译文：** 就在今年五月，Ben Thompson 和我录制了一期题为《无事生非话数据中心》的 Sharp Tech 播客节目。当时我们相当全面地探讨了围绕建设本地基础设施以支撑美国 AI 生态系统所引发的日益高涨的反对声浪，而仅仅三个月后，我们的对话就显得有些过时了。我惊讶于这股愤怒情绪蔓延之广、速度之快。

This has, needless to say, become a messy topic, with a bunch of relevant details to wade through. At the end, though, I’m going to explain why I’m less alarmed by the long-term implications of this fight than most of the tech community seems to be. I think the speed and virality of the backlash reflects the internet’s role as its primary driver, and social media-addled political movements tend to be less durable than moral panics of the past (compared to, say, 40 years of opposition to nuclear power). But before we get there, let’s survey the chaos.

> **译文：** 毋庸置疑，这已经成为一个棘手的话题，其中充斥着大量需要梳理的相关细节。不过，在文章结尾，我将解释为什么我对这场争斗的长期影响不像科技界大多数人那样感到担忧。我认为，反对声浪的速度和病毒式传播反映了互联网作为其主要驱动力的角色，而受社交媒体影响的政治运动往往不如过去的道德恐慌那样持久（例如，与持续了40年的反核能运动相比）。但在此之前，让我们先来审视一下这片混乱局面。

Midterms and Moratoriums

Midterm elections are upon us, and data centers are now a political flashpoint all over the country. As the New York Times reports :

> **译文：** 中期选举即将来临，数据中心如今已成为全国各地的政治引爆点。据《纽约时报》报道：

One new ad in Texas begins with someone typing into a chatbot, “Why is my electric bill going up?” Another in Ohio blames one of the state’s Republican senators for a proliferation of data centers, calling them “a total scam.” And in top House battlegrounds in Michigan, Wisconsin and Pennsylvania, the Democratic challengers are all running ads against the server warehouses powering the artificial intelligence boom.

> **译文：** 得克萨斯州一则新广告以某人在聊天机器人中输入“为什么我的电费账单在上涨？”开头。俄亥俄州另一则广告则将该州数据中心激增归咎于该州一位共和党参议员，称其为“彻头彻尾的骗局”。而在密歇根州、威斯康星州和宾夕法尼亚州的关键众议院选区，民主党挑战者都在投放广告，抨击那些支撑 AI 繁荣的服务器仓库。

Meanwhile, the National Republican Senate Committee warned AI companies that support for data centers is collapsing nationwide and will cost Republicans races in Ohio and elsewhere, and politicians from both parties are updating their platforms accordingly.

> **译文：** 与此同时，全国共和党参议院委员会警告 AI 公司，对数据中心的支持正在全国范围内崩塌，这将使共和党在俄亥俄州等地失去选票，两党政客也相应更新了他们的竞选纲领。

In total, 15 states across the political spectrum have now enacted or proposed temporary bans on data center construction. While a blue state like New York was the first to issue a moratorium on hyperscale data center construction , a purple state like Pennsylvania now sees both sides of its governor race fighting to outflank one another in the race to distance themselves from past support for data center projects. Even a red, builder-friendly state like Texas has announced a pause to all new data center approvals amid contested races for governor and the Senate, where Democratic candidates are hammering their Republican opponents on the issue. For reference, Texas is home to 335 data centers with “at least 248 more in the works , ” and just 10 months ago was hailed by Governor Greg Abbott as the “epicenter of AI development.” That branding is no longer a political winner.

> **译文：** 总体而言，目前已有15个政治立场各异的州颁布或提议了数据中心建设临时禁令。虽然像纽约这样的蓝州率先对超大规模数据中心建设发布了暂停令，但像宾夕法尼亚这样的紫色州，如今州长竞选的双方都在竞相与过去对数据中心项目的支持划清界限，试图在这一点上超越对方。甚至像得克萨斯这样一个对建设友好的红州，也在州长和参议员竞选激烈之际，宣布暂停所有新数据中心的审批，民主党候选人正就此问题猛烈抨击其共和党对手。作为参考，得克萨斯州拥有335个数据中心，“至少还有248个正在规划中”，而就在10个月前，州长 Greg Abbott 还称赞该州是“AI 发展的中心”。这个标签如今已不再是政治上的加分项。

Lies, Half-Truths and China

Building more data centers and computing capacity will bring down the cost of AI for everyone and likely lead to more innovation in the long run, in addition to incentivizing the buildout of new electricity resources that will benefit Americans all over the country, including with lower prices. Pausing AI data center construction in the U.S. won’t stop AI from transforming our economy; models will still be trained and deployed, and there will be enough inference to run them at America’s biggest companies. That said, while building one new AI data center won’t meaningfully alter the environment, building hundreds of them, and bringing more power online to sustain that growth, will likely lead to more emissions. Hardcore environmentalists who oppose that outcome are doing so for logically coherent reasons (with the caveat that stopping pollution in one country doesn’t stop it in another).

> **译文：** 建设更多的数据中心和计算能力将降低每个人的 AI 使用成本，从长远来看可能会带来更多创新，同时还能激励新电力资源的建设，这将使全美各地的美国人受益，包括降低电价。暂停美国 AI 数据中心建设不会阻止 AI 改变我们的经济；模型仍将被训练和部署，并且将有足够的推理能力来支持美国大公司的运营。话虽如此，虽然新建一个 AI 数据中心不会对环境产生重大影响，但建设数百个这样的中心，并增加电力供应以维持这种增长，可能会导致更多排放。强烈反对这一结果的环保主义者有其逻辑上自洽的理由（但需要注意的是，在一个国家停止污染并不能阻止另一个国家的污染）。

Beyond that fundamental tension, one obvious observation about the contemporary furor is that a few of the most popular arguments are trivially disproven. The claim that data centers use dangerous amounts of water has been debunked so many times that it’s now cliche to call it misinformation, and in general, the claims that data centers impose unique environmental harms relative to other industrial projects are wrong and misguided (in fact they impose much fewer). Researcher and Substack author Andy Masley has done a terrific job tethering this discussion to facts, and for anyone who’s skeptical, I recommend this piece on water , this piece on land use , and this tweet responding to a variety of stated concerns from data center opponents.

> **译文：** 除了这一根本性的矛盾之外，对当前这场喧嚣的一个显而易见的观察是，一些最流行的论点很容易被证明是站不住脚的。关于数据中心消耗大量危险用水的说法已被辟谣多次，以至于称其为 misinformation 已成老生常谈。总的来说，声称数据中心相对于其他工业项目会造成独特环境危害的说法是错误的、具有误导性的（事实上，它们造成的危害要小得多）。研究员兼 Substack 作者 Andy Masley 在将这一讨论与事实挂钩方面做得非常出色，对于任何持怀疑态度的人，我推荐这篇关于用水的文章、这篇关于土地使用的文章，以及这条回应数据中心反对者各种担忧的推文。

Alongside that now-robust economy of data center myths and mythbusting, there’s also the actual economy. Obviously AI spending and speculation has almost single-handedly powered a growing stock market (and millions of retirement accounts) for the past several years, and that presents its own set of systemic questions . But regardless of how precarious the financing becomes, every inch of compute that’s built is being utilized, and on the ground, data centers themselves have created a variety of winners beyond Nvidia. Last week Reuters highlighted a wide spectrum of attendant construction industries that are booming , while this week the New York Times ran an op-ed from an electricians union whose membership has doubled in the past 10 years.

> **译文：** 除了如今围绕数据中心神话与辟谣形成的成熟生态外，还有实体经济。显然，过去几年，AI 的支出和投机几乎单枪匹马地推动了股市的增长（以及数百万人的退休账户），这本身就带来了一系列系统性问题。但无论融资变得多么不稳定，每一寸已建成的算力都在被利用，而在实际层面，数据中心本身也创造了除 Nvidia 之外的众多赢家。上周，路透社重点报道了各类蓬勃发展的配套建筑行业，而本周《纽约时报》则发表了一篇来自电工工会的专栏文章，该工会成员在过去10年中翻了一番。

As to everyone else, alongside the $27 billion in nationwide tax revenue that data centers are projected to generate for counties and states by the end of the decade, another popular concern is the risk of rising electricity costs for residential customers. On that one, the answer is tricky. Yes, the insatiable demand for computing power is adding strain to already-strained electric grids , but many new data centers are being built with their own power sources, rendering some of these concerns moot.

> **译文：** 至于其他方面，除了预计到本十年末数据中心将为各县和各州带来270亿美元的税收收入外，另一个普遍担忧是居民用户电价上涨的风险。关于这一点，答案很棘手。是的，对计算能力永不满足的需求正在给本已紧张的电网增加压力，但许多新建数据中心都配备了自有电源，这使得其中一些担忧变得无关紧要。

Moreover, using tech as a bogeyman to explain America’s steadily rising electricity prices oversimplifies the problem and risks forestalling potential solutions. In a comprehensive op-ed published by the New York Times in April, the authors found “[AI] is actually a small part of a much bigger problem,” because “[o]ur grid is too old and our supply of electricity too small.” Indeed, we inexplicably stopped building new electricity generation 15 years ago , well before the AI boom started. In this environment, data centers can benefit both short and medium term prices by spreading a grid’s operating costs across industrial clients (as opposed to residential customers) while also incentivizing the construction of new power. As that Times op-ed continues:

> **译文：** 此外，用科技作为替罪羊来解释美国电价稳步上涨，既过于简化了问题，也可能阻碍潜在的解决方案。在《纽约时报》四月发表的一篇综合性专栏文章中，作者发现“[AI] 实际上是一个更大问题中的一小部分”，因为“[我们的]电网太老旧，电力供应也太少”。确实，我们在15年前就莫名其妙地停止了新建发电设施，这远早于 AI 热潮开始。在这种环境下，数据中心可以通过将电网运营成本分摊给工业客户（而非居民客户），同时激励新电力建设，从而对短期和中期电价产生积极影响。正如那篇《纽约时报》专栏文章继续指出的：

The concern around these data centers is understandable. But at least when thinking about the power system, we need to walk a fine line: We should welcome the growth of electricity demand, but seek to slow electricity inflation. Although it sounds counterintuitive, when a big new electricity customer shows up on the grid, it can sometimes help keep power costs down for everyone else, because it buys so much power that it can cover much more of the system’s costs, keeping a lid on rising power rates.

> **译文：** 对这些数据中心的担忧是可以理解的。但至少在考虑电力系统时，我们需要把握好分寸：我们应该欢迎电力需求的增长，但力求减缓电价上涨。虽然听起来有悖常理，但当一个大额新电力客户接入电网时，有时反而有助于降低其他所有人的电力成本，因为它购买了大量电力，可以覆盖更多的系统成本，从而抑制电费上涨。

Cases will vary by market, but those counterintuitive benefits are real . And as a general matter, short-circuiting U.S. data center construction won’t solve our electricity inflation problems. To the contrary, the current AI investment cycle may be our best hope in a generation for expanding grids and building new power that will benefit businesses in any industry, as well as residential consumers.

> **译文：** 具体情况因市场而异，但这些反直觉的好处是真实存在的。总的来说，中断美国数据中心建设并不能解决我们的电价上涨问题。恰恰相反，当前的 AI 投资周期可能是我们这一代人扩大电网、建设新电力设施的最大希望，这将惠及任何行业的企业以及居民消费者。

Finally, returning to the category of viral claims that lack supporting evidence and tend to confuse the issues, I’m skeptical of the popular argument that the violent shift in American public opinion is being driven by Chinese propagandists. On that point, let me recommend one more piece from Andy Masley , because earlier this month he wrote exactly what I’ve been thinking all year:

> **译文：** 最后，回到那些缺乏证据支持且容易混淆视听的病毒式传播言论，我对一种流行说法持怀疑态度，即美国公众舆论的剧烈转变是由中国宣传分子推动的。关于这一点，让我再推荐一篇 Andy Masley 的文章，因为本月初他写下了我这一整年都在思考的内容：

There have been a few high-profile reports and claims purporting to show foreign involvement, and lots of people are sharing them as if they’re smoking gun evidence that China’s had significant impact. I’m worried that a lot of people are reading those headlines and nodding along without poking at the claims. When you do, they mostly collapse into a few specific examples of tiny organizations who happen to be based in China and other countries running some ineffective bot farms and behaving in ways that make them seem unlikely to be directed by the CCP, some negative coverage of data centers from Chinese state media, and a CCP-aligned billionaire funding networks of goofy goober fringe groups in the US like CodePink … All of these examples seem to be completely dwarfed by the organic local backlash driven by American media coverage, social media, and ordinary land-use politics. […]

> **译文：** 有一些高调的报告和说法声称显示了外国势力的介入，很多人像分享确凿证据一样分享它们，仿佛证明中国产生了重大影响。我担心很多人只是读了那些标题就点头附和，而没有深入探究这些说法。当你深入探究时，它们大多会瓦解为几个具体例子：一些恰好位于中国和其他国家的小型组织在运行一些无效的机器人水军，其行为方式看起来不太可能受中共指挥；中国官方媒体对数据中心的一些负面报道；以及一个亲中共的亿万富翁资助美国国内像 CodePink 这样的愚蠢边缘团体的网络……所有这些例子似乎都完全被由美国媒体报道、社交媒体和普通土地使用政治所驱动的本地自发反弹所掩盖。[……]

All this made me pretty temperamentally skeptical of the idea that the data center panic is too intense and targeted to be an organic result of local American politics. American politics is pretty wild right now! I had also been following the panic from the start, and knew that many of the most popular talking points and ways of thinking had come from specific overconfident or clumsy reporting from established US papers, and spread quickly because their claims were shocking and alarming when read in isolation, and also sometimes exciting to talk about on social media (it’s very easy to buy a water bottle to hold in front of a camera on TikTok and compellingly repeat a fake statistic ).

> **译文：** 这一切让我在性情上对以下观点持怀疑态度：数据中心恐慌过于强烈和具有针对性，不可能是美国地方政治自发产生的结果。美国政治现在确实很疯狂！我也从一开始就关注这场恐慌，知道许多最流行的论调和思维方式都源自美国老牌报纸某些过于自信或笨拙的报道，并因其说法在孤立阅读时令人震惊和警觉，有时在社交媒体上讨论起来也很刺激（在 TikTok 上买一个水瓶对着镜头，令人信服地重复一个虚假统计数据是非常容易的），而迅速传播开来。

China’s most effective propaganda tool is buying off elites, and while some have argued that various climate organizations belong in that category and many of those same orgs now oppose data centers and grid expansion, we’re getting awfully attenuated at that point.

> **译文：** 中国最有效的宣传工具是收买精英，虽然有些人认为各种气候组织属于这一类，而且许多此类组织现在反对数据中心和电网扩张，但到了那一步，我们的论证就变得非常牵强了。

I’ve omitted citations from most of that Masley excerpt, but I retained the link to the fake statistic he highlights—in which the Washington Post uses graphics to incorrectly tell readers that one AI search query uses more water than a 16.9 oz bottle of water —because I find it wonderfully illustrative of the point: the American information ecosystem is definitely screwed up enough on its own, independent of China, to power a months-long moral and political panic over data centers.

> **译文：** 我在马斯利那段摘录中省略了大部分引用，但保留了他所强调的虚假统计数据的链接——其中《华盛顿邮报》用图表错误地告诉读者，一次AI搜索查询消耗的水比一瓶16.9盎司的瓶装水还多——因为我觉得这完美地说明了我的观点：美国的信息生态系统本身就足够混乱，无需中国插手，就足以引发一场持续数月、围绕数据中心的道德与政治恐慌。

Regardless of China’s intent to interfere in U.S. politics (I have no doubt they’re trying), I don’t think it’s a primary driver of the backlash. Nor do I think it’s useful to exoticize Chinese capabilities in lieu of better understanding our own politics.

> **译文：** 无论中国是否有意干预美国政治（我毫不怀疑他们在尝试），我不认为这是这场反弹的主要驱动力。我也不认为，用神秘化的眼光看待中国的能力、而不去更好地理解我们自己的政治，是有什么益处的。

How This Story Will End

In broad strokes, I do see how we got here. Data centers are massive, hulking buildings that can make dystopian humming sounds. In the face of rampant warnings about their environmental harms and the consequences of widespread AI adoption, voting no on constructing more of these buildings is a straightforward and honestly pretty rational response to both the past 35 years and the past 35 months.

> **译文：** 从宏观上看，我确实理解我们是如何走到这一步的。数据中心是庞大笨重的建筑，会发出反乌托邦式的嗡嗡声。面对铺天盖地的关于其环境危害和AI广泛采用后果的警告，投票反对建造更多这样的建筑，是对过去35年和过去35个月的一种直接且相当理性的回应。

Tech has already atomized and disrupted our lives in ways that no one ever voted for and many people don’t love, while AI’s most visible leaders spent the three years after ChatGPT’s launch promising on one hand to remake the economy without humans, while warning of dire safety risks on the other.

> **译文：** 科技已经以无人投票支持、许多人不喜欢的方式将我们的生活原子化和颠覆，而AI最显眼的领导者在ChatGPT发布后的三年里，一边承诺在没有人类的情况下重塑经济，一边又警告严峻的安全风险。

In April I said on Sharp Tech that I can sympathize with anyone who instinctively opposes the continued buildout of data centers on these grounds, and Ben echoed the point in both our May episode and a Stratechery Update from the same week.

> **译文：** 四月份我在Sharp Tech节目上说过，我能理解任何基于这些理由本能反对数据中心继续建设的人，本也在我们五月的节目和同周的Stratechery更新中呼应了这一观点。

Furthermore, to the extent a large portion of the opposition is not that principled or rational, and in fact this movement has spread rapidly across the American electorate thanks to the proliferation of half-truths or outright lies that citizens have seen on the internet, that’s of course poetic in its own way. Tech is now getting the pointy end of the stick after a generation of profiting from disruption of both the literal economy and our information economy.

> **译文：** 此外，如果很大一部分反对力量并非那么有原则或理性，事实上这场运动之所以在美国选民中迅速蔓延，是因为公民在互联网上看到了大量半真半假或彻头彻尾的谎言，那这本身在某种意义上是具有讽刺意味的。科技在从对实体经济和信息经济的颠覆中获利了一代人之后，现在终于尝到了苦头。

The latter has been upended, probably irrevocably, at the hand of algorithms that amplify conspiratorial thinking and erode faith in institutions across the political spectrum.

> **译文：** 后者（信息经济）已经被算法彻底颠覆，可能是不可逆转的，这些算法放大了阴谋论思维，侵蚀了各政治派别对机构的信任。

This is the water we’ve been swimming in for a while now; anyone who’s been awake for the past 15 years shouldn’t be surprised that an informative op-ed from USA Today is less powerful than a hundred Instagram videos about water and noise pollution, or that a local government desperate for revenue now encounters a woman who’s convinced data centers will make everyone sick .

> **译文：** 这就是我们一直以来身处的环境；任何在过去15年里保持清醒的人都不应该感到惊讶：《今日美国》的一篇信息丰富的专栏文章，不如一百个关于水和噪音污染的Instagram视频有影响力；一个急需税收的地方政府，现在会遇到一个坚信数据中心会让所有人得病的女人。

And all of that could, of course, be understood as a crushingly depressing reality check. Our leaders have been lamenting the disappearance of American factories for as long as I’ve been alive, and now that a new and enormously valuable category of factory is spurring blue collar job growth and incentivizing brick and mortar buildouts across the country, America is hand-wringing over electricity and bizarre lies about water. (Please, no one tell Instagram about the environmental costs of a rare earth refinery).

> **译文：** 而所有这些，当然可以被理解为一个令人沮丧的现实检验。从我出生起，我们的领导人就一直在哀叹美国工厂的消失，而现在，一种新的、极具价值的工厂类别正在推动蓝领就业增长，激励全国各地的实体建设，美国却在为电力和关于水的离奇谎言而焦虑不安。（拜托，千万别让Instagram知道稀土精炼厂的环境成本。）

But I wouldn’t over-read this moment, either. This comes back to my lack of alarm about the long run implications.

> **译文：** 但我也不会过度解读这一刻。这又回到了我对长期影响并不感到恐慌的态度。

While it’s certainly true that a nihilistic sort of populism is on the rise in both parties and de-growth impulses are especially common on the left—two trends that aren’t going away—it’s worth keeping in mind that policy movements that draw their momentum from stuff people are saying on social media often lose steam just as quickly as they seemed to accelerate to the forefront of every political conversation in the country.

> **译文：** 虽然两党中确实都在兴起一种虚无主义的民粹主义，去增长冲动在左翼尤其常见——这两个趋势不会消失——但值得记住的是，那些从社交媒体上人们的声音中汲取动力的政策运动，往往在看似迅速冲到全国政治话题前沿之后，又以同样快的速度失去动力。

Or to put it differently: of all the things that have taken their turn as The Current Thing , the track record for sustained outrage or support is not great.

> **译文：** 换句话说：在所有轮流成为“当下热点”的事情中，持续愤怒或持续支持的战绩并不好。

No one talks about de-funding the police anymore, Donald Trump is not building the wall, no one is flying Ukrainian flags today (well, except my parents), the MeToo movement is now seen as excessive in a variety of ways, and Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, who today sponsors a bill to impose a federal moratorium on data center buildouts, recently made news for blithely admitting, “Woke 1 was crazy.”

> **译文：** 现在没人再谈削减警察经费了，唐纳德·特朗普没有建墙，今天没人再挥舞乌克兰国旗了（好吧，除了我父母），MeToo运动现在在很多方面被视为过激，而亚历山德里娅·奥卡西奥-科尔特斯——如今提出一项联邦暂停数据中心建设法案的议员——最近因轻描淡写地承认“觉醒1.0太疯狂了”而上了新闻。

Let me be clear that I’m not offering prescriptions to tech companies and telling them to just wait it out; they all need to calibrate their messaging a lot more carefully, and recognize that American towns are no longer falling all over themselves to hand out tax breaks and land. But I imagine incentives will continue to point the tech industry in the right direction without my help. Siting these plants needs to be done thoughtfully and power costs need to be borne by the companies themselves, and all of that is achievable, particularly when the benefits are undeniable.

> **译文：** 我要说明的是，我不是在给科技公司开药方、告诉它们只要熬过去就行；它们都需要更谨慎地调整自己的信息传达，并认识到美国城镇不再争相提供税收优惠和土地。但我猜想，即使没有我的帮助，激励机制也会继续引导科技行业朝着正确的方向前进。这些设施的选址需要深思熟虑，电力成本需要由公司自己承担，而这些都是可以实现的，尤其是在好处不可否认的情况下。

In lieu of offering obvious advice, I’m more interested in cultural observation. Many have cited the 5G panic as a parallel for the kind of mass delusion around data center harms that has taken root this year, but personally, I think back to the conversations we used to have about fracking for oil and natural gas. That, too, was once branded as a uniquely harmful practice that imperiled our water supply, and while there were isolated incidents in which that was true , Obama’s EPA found that as a general matter those fears were overblown (although the agency later softened its exculpatory language after receiving criticism from activists and scientists).

> **译文：** 与其提供显而易见的建议，我更感兴趣的是文化观察。许多人将5G恐慌视为今年围绕数据中心危害的大规模集体错觉的类比，但就我个人而言，我想起了我们过去关于水力压裂开采石油和天然气的讨论。那也曾被标榜为一种危害独特、威胁我们供水安全的做法，虽然确实有个别事件如此，但奥巴马政府的环保署发现，总体而言这些担忧被夸大了（尽管该机构后来在受到活动人士和科学家的批评后，软化了其免责措辞）。

Those EPA findings and decades of mostly safe fracking didn’t stop Elizabeth Warren and Bernie Sanders from promising to impose a federal ban on fracking when they ran for president in 2019 (which could have cost millions of jobs ), so sure, there’s your reminder we will always have radical ideas to contend with.

> **译文：** 环保署的这些发现和几十年来基本安全的水力压裂实践，并没有阻止伊丽莎白·沃伦和伯尼·桑德斯在2019年竞选总统时承诺实施联邦水力压裂禁令（这可能使数百万工作岗位流失），所以没错，这提醒我们，我们永远要面对激进的想法。

But the salience of fracking in political life has subsided, and along the way, while many companies engaged in the practice lost astounding amounts of money, the consumer surplus they delivered is undeniable. The shale revolution has made America the biggest energy supplier in the world and led to the mass adoption of natural gas as a replacement for coal, which went from supplying 50% of U.S. electricity generation in 2005 to 15% in 2026 .

> **译文：** 但水力压裂在政治生活中的突出地位已经消退，在此过程中，虽然许多从事该业务的公司亏损惊人，但它们为消费者带来的剩余价值是不可否认的。页岩革命使美国成为世界上最大的能源供应国，并推动了天然气大规模取代煤炭，煤炭在美国发电量中的占比从2005年的50%下降到2026年的15%。

And most importantly, Americans who once opposed fracking don’t care or even notice. There was never any grand public debate where one side won and the other lost; the work got done in spite of vocal critics, and then the critics mostly forgot about it.

> **译文：** 最重要的是，曾经反对水力压裂的美国人现在不在乎了，甚至根本没注意到。从来没有什么盛大的公开辩论，一方赢了另一方输了；工作是在批评者的声音中完成的，然后批评者大多就忘了这件事。

Today those sorts of shifts in sentiment and attention happen across months and years, not decades. An infinite supply of memes means every meme has a shorter life span, and a constant flood of news and opinion means it’s easier than ever to wash away political movements that aren’t rooted in the lived experience of voters. To that end, I think what will undermine the cause of activists opposing new data centers is not anecdotes about their positive impact on local communities , but the simple reality that the vast majority of people are not affected by local data centers one way or the other. The endgame might be different if environmental risks really were dire, or tax benefits were all a sham, but all things considered, the objects of today’s populist outrage are arguably the cleanest and most profitable factories that America has ever seen.

> **译文：** 如今，这种情绪和注意力的转变发生在数月和数年间，而不是数十年。无限供应的梗意味着每个梗的寿命更短，而源源不断的新闻和观点意味着，抹去那些不植根于选民真实经历的政治运动比以往任何时候都容易。就此而言，我认为最终削弱反对数据中心建设活动人士事业的，不是关于它们对当地社区积极影响的轶事，而是一个简单的现实：绝大多数人根本不受当地数据中心的影响。如果环境风险真的很严重，或者税收优惠全是骗局，那么结局可能会不同，但综合考虑，今天民粹主义愤怒的对象，可以说是美国有史以来最清洁、最赚钱的工厂。

So midterms will come and go. Opportunistic states like Texas will find a way to return to building. As entertaining or distressing as the current panic has been, the future of American AI infrastructure will not in fact be relocated to the UAE or outer space. Some of this controversy will probably be revived for a presidential election in 2028, but I expect the incentives for both companies building data centers and local governments attracting them will stubbornly prevail. And over time, voters won’t care.

> **译文：** 所以中期选举会来也会去。像得克萨斯这样机会主义的州会找到恢复建设的方法。无论当前的恐慌多么有趣或令人不安，美国AI基础设施的未来实际上不会转移到阿联酋或外太空。这场争议的一部分可能会在2028年总统选举中被重新提起，但我预计，建设数据中心的公司和吸引它们的地方政府双方的激励机制，都会顽固地占据上风。而随着时间的推移，选民们不会在乎了。

I could cite polling as evidence that a shift in sentiment is foreseeable ( support swings by 31 points when voters learn how modern data centers use water ), but in truth, this argument is all instinct and experience. Everything on the internet is ephemeral, and that includes most of the political movements that start there.

> **译文：** 我可以引用民调作为证据，表明情绪转变是可以预见的（当选民了解现代数据中心如何用水时，支持率会波动31个百分点），但说实话，这一论点完全基于直觉和经验。互联网上的一切都是短暂的，包括大多数在那里兴起的政治运动。
