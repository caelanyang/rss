* Here's a 2000 Landon Thomas Jr. profile of Ryan Jacobs, who started managing an Internet-focused fund with $200k in assets, ran it up to $600m in assets under management two years later, and ultimately lost two thirds of his investors’ money [https://www.thediff.co/r/c2e52c5c?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. Which is a good reminder that these stories don't have to rhyme in any particulars other than the volatility: Jacobs got to run the fund because he was working at a small asset manager and had a lot of hustle, and the willingness to run what was then a failing strategy. He made the canonical 90s mistake of dreaming of total addressable markets without thinking of the mechanics of getting there. (Ironically, the bubble helped solve a lot of this problem by making distribution easier, and then un-solved it as the companies that controlled that distribution exercised their pricing power.) When Jacobs was plying his trade, it wasn't cheap to get exposure to particular kinds of beta, and you could do well by giving investors a high-fee vehicle to express a generally positive view on a sector. Today, it's still possible to sell a strategy that is structurally long some factor, but investors will want to know how you're adding alpha on top of that.

> **译文：** 以下是兰登·托马斯二世 (Landon Thomas Jr.) 2000 年撰写的一篇关于瑞安·雅各布斯 (Ryan Jacobs) 的报道。雅各布斯最初管理着一只专注于互联网领域的基金，资产规模仅为 20 万美元。两年后，基金管理规模飙升至 6 亿美元，但最终却损失了投资者三分之二的资金 [https://www.thediff.co/r/c2e52c5c?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。这提醒我们，这类故事除了波动性之外，其他方面未必相同：雅各布斯之所以能管理这只基金，是因为他当时在一家小型资产管理公司工作，而且非常积极主动，并愿意尝试当时并不成功的投资策略。他犯了 90 年代常见的错误，那就是只顾着幻想拥有庞大的潜在市场，却忽略了实现这一目标的具体方法。 （讽刺的是，泡沫通过简化分销渠道，在很大程度上解决了这个问题，但随后又因为控制分销渠道的公司行使定价权而使问题再次浮出水面。）在雅各布斯从事交易的年代，获得特定类型的贝塔收益并不便宜，你可以通过向投资者提供高收费工具来表达对某个行业的总体乐观观点，从而获得丰厚的回报。如今，你仍然可以出售一种结构性做多某些因子的策略，但投资者会想知道你是如何在此基础上增加超额收益的。

* Tuhin Chakrabarty, Xinyue Liu, Jane C. Ginsburg, and Paramveer Dhillon on how AI-written books are crowding out human-written ones, but at different paces in different genres [https://www.thediff.co/r/1d5cc0c1?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. But one of the takeaways is that this is happening in the genres you'd already think of as long-tail, quantity-over-quality domains. There is some amazing genre fiction out there, but there are plenty of authors who are churning out one replacement-level sci-fi book, business profile, or romance novel after another. There are different audiences for different books, and in some categories books are more a scratch-the-itch phenomenon than something that's supposed to be life-changing. So this is partly a market efficiency story. There's a segment of the book-reading audience that liked certain topics but wasn't that sensitive to quality, and there's now an automated way to meet that demand. Serious literature will be safe for a while yet, though writers may face some harsh truths about what the average reader really wants.

> **译文：** 图欣·查克拉巴蒂、刘欣悦、简·C·金斯伯格和帕拉姆维尔·迪隆探讨了人工智能写作的书籍如何挤占人类写作的书籍，但不同类型书籍的挤占速度各不相同[https://www.thediff.co/r/1d5cc0c1?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。其中一个重要结论是，这种情况发生在那些你通常认为是长尾、重数量轻质量的领域。市面上确实有一些非常优秀的类型小说，但也有很多作者在源源不断地炮制平庸的科幻小说、商业人物传记或言情小说。不同类型的书籍有不同的读者群体，在某些类别中，书籍更多的是一种满足阅读需求的途径，而不是改变人生的读物。因此，这在某种程度上也是一个市场效率的故事。有一部分读者喜欢某些题材，但对作品质量并不十分在意，而现在有一种自动化的方式可以满足他们的需求。严肃文学在未来一段时间内仍将保持安全，尽管作家们或许会面临一些关于普通读者真正喜好的残酷现实。

* In the Center for Educational Progress Substack, Justin Rosentover writes about his experience with the Montgomery Blair High School magnet program [https://www.thediff.co/r/48921a90?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. This is a fun piece because it's a great example of a local government running an experiment and getting good results: you can offer high school students more advanced coursework in parallel to the standard offerings, and you can do it in a way where there are students who default to more challenging coursework, and students who do it ad hoc. People vary tremendously in their academic ability and motivation, both in terms of average skill and in terms of subject-specific competence, but it's also helpful to be surrounded by peers who genuinely care about the robotics team more than the football team, and vice-versa. There's an opportunity for schools to flex a little and benefit their students a lot; hopefully more of them take it.

> **译文：** 在教育进步中心（Center for Educational Progress）的Substack专栏中，贾斯汀·罗森托弗（Justin Rosentover）撰写了一篇关于他在蒙哥马利·布莱尔高中磁校项目（Montgomery Blair High School magnet program）的经历的文章[https://www.thediff.co/r/48921a90?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。这篇文章很有意思，因为它很好地展现了地方政府开展实验并取得良好成果的案例：学校可以为高中生提供与标准课程并行的更高级的课程，并且可以采用一种模式，让一些学生默认选择更具挑战性的课程，而另一些学生则根据自身情况选择。人们的学术能力和学习动力差异巨大，无论是在平均水平还是学科专长方面，但如果身边有真正关心机器人团队而不是橄榄球队的同龄人，反之亦然，那将大有裨益。学校有机会灵活调整教学方式，从而极大地惠及学生。希望更多人接受它。

* Joshua Rothman in The New Yorker on lookism, i.e. discrimination based on personal appearance [https://www.thediff.co/r/1f2059e5?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. Speaking strictly as a haver-of-takes, lookism is a great phenomenon to be long, because it's obviously a huge issue (not only do attractive people get paid more, but there's a lot they simply don't have to pay for), it's somewhat contrarian to explicitly advocate for People of Unattractiveness, and such people will be overrepresented in textual media due to selection effects. If you're in the business of reading and synthesizing people's views, this is an absolute gold mine: you can make the clear argument that it's deeply unfair that certain face and body proportions will give someone better jobs, lower rent, a lower chance of being convicted of a crime, etc., and you can also explore the question of how much "attractiveness" just refers to body fat, and whether this discrimination is somewhat rational—a landlord might indirectly make the judgment that someone who skips appealingly flavorful potato chips would also be less likely to write a check that's going to bounce. Within lookism, heightism seems like the easiest moral case to make (disclosure: 6'3", taken), since for people raised in the developed world it's almost entirely genetic luck, and it's strange that at least for now it's socially acceptable to discriminate on this basis. It's a topic that will generate endless takes once it gets big.

> **译文：** * Joshua Rothman 在《纽约客》上发表了一篇关于外貌歧视（即基于个人外貌的歧视）的文章[https://www.thediff.co/r/1f2059e5?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。仅从个人角度来看，外貌歧视是一个值得深入探讨的现象，因为它显然是一个巨大的问题（不仅长相俊美的人收入更高，而且他们还能享受很多无需付费的服务），公开倡导“相貌平平的人”在某种程度上是逆潮流而行的，而且由于选择性偏差，这类人群在文本媒体中的代表性也会过高。如果你从事阅读和综合分析人们观点的工作，这简直就是一座金矿：你可以清晰地论证，某些面部和身材比例能让人获得更好的工作、更低的房租、更低的犯罪概率等等，这极其不公平；你还可以探讨“吸引力”在多大程度上仅仅指代体脂，以及这种歧视是否在某种程度上是合理的——房东可能会间接地判断，不爱吃美味薯片的人，开空头支票的可能性也更小。在“外貌歧视”中，身高歧视似乎是最容易论证的道德问题（声明：身高1米9，已婚），因为对于在发达国家长大的人来说，身高几乎完全取决于基因，而令人费解的是，至少目前社会上竟然可以接受基于身高的歧视。一旦这个话题流行起来，它必将引发无穷无尽的讨论。

* Dylan Matthews in Asterisk on John and Mack Rust, who spent years trying to commercialize a mechanical cotton picker, as part of a political mission to empower workers [https://www.thediff.co/r/358d9ec8?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. Picking cotton by hand is laborious and unpleasant—my grandmother mentioned having to do this as a kid, and the main thing she emphasized was that you'd be amazed at how long it takes to fill up a bag with cotton, and then amazed again when you learned how little it weighed. But that's part of the story of why it took so long to automate: if you're in a part of the world where kids need to do agricultural labor to support their families, it's a part of the world where labor is very cheap, and a machine needs to be very efficient indeed to displace that. But, at the same time, automation begets more automation: as output rises in other sectors, the friendly version of Baumol's Cost Disease shows up: employers in a rich country find that they can't afford not to automate drudgery.

> **译文：** * 迪伦·马修斯在《星号》（Asterisk）一书中讲述了约翰和马克·拉斯特兄弟的故事。他们多年来致力于将机械采棉机商业化，以此作为赋予工人权力的政治使命的一部分[https://www.thediff.co/r/358d9ec8?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。手工采棉既费力又令人不快——我的祖母曾提到她小时候不得不做这件事，她强调的重点是，你会惊讶于装满一袋棉花需要多长时间，然后当你发现棉花的重量如此之轻时，你会再次感到惊讶。但这正是自动化进程如此缓慢的原因之一：如果你生活在一个孩子们需要从事农业劳动来养家糊口的地方，那么那里的劳动力非常廉价，机器必须非常高效才能取代人工。但与此同时，自动化又会催生更多的自动化：随着其他部门产量的增加，鲍莫尔成本病的温和版本出现了：富裕国家的雇主发现，他们无法承受不将繁重体力劳动自动化的后果。

* In Capital Gains this week, we look at why basically all statistics about the share of humans versus bots in some domain are bogus [https://www.thediff.co/r/b0a3520f?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. It's not that they don't measure something, it's that they measure too many things at once: how much work can be automated, how much work people choose to automate, and the fraction of low-stakes interactions that can happen without a human in the loop. There are already fields where bots technically make 90% of the decisions but humans are actually in charge (like big online ad buys or big stock trades) and decisions where humans make almost all of the decisions but the bots are actually in control (like when people feed some algorithm data on what fraction of interactions with an app should have a particular call-to-action).

> **译文：** * 本周的《资本收益》栏目将探讨为什么几乎所有关于某些领域中人类与机器人比例的统计数据都是虚假的[https://www.thediff.co/r/b0a3520f?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。问题不在于这些统计数据没有衡量某些东西，而在于它们同时衡量了太多东西：有多少工作可以自动化，人们选择自动化多少工作，以及在无需人工参与的情况下，低风险交互的比例是多少。实际上，在某些领域，机器人理论上做出了90%的决策，但最终掌控者仍然是人类（例如大型在线广告投放或大型股票交易）；而在另一些领域，几乎所有决策都由人类做出，但最终控制权却掌握在机器人手中（例如，当人们向算法输入数据，决定应用程序交互中应该包含哪些特定行动号召时）。

* A very fun Read.Haus question this week: is pricing more efficient for individual stocks or for broader factors [https://www.thediff.co/r/3ca5ce2d?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]. This one is really hard! On one hand: factors cover multiple assets, and if every asset is being priced reasonably well, factors should get some benefit from diversification and be even better priced. On the other hand, within organizations there's a correlation between thinking about factors and wanting to have zero exposure to them: there is probably more money in figuring out which AI or Iran war play is mispriced relative to some other one than figuring out whether AI and Iran are big deals or not, even though there's more dollar alpha in the latter category. It's entirely possible to go through life adding a lot of efficiency to the market by determining which overpriced company in a sector is least-overpriced, while being blissfully agnostic as to whether or not the sector in question is overpriced. And sector bets, or country bets, will have lower sharpe ratios than relative value trades within them, simply because there's a bigger sample size and more room to isolate risks that are worth taking. If you want to make a living picking stocks, be indifferent to broader trends and very attentive to which companies are over- or under-indexed to them. If you want to be a legend, focus on the trends; you'll have a better story. (But this, too, is part of the market-efficiency story. It's just less fun to say that you made your money betting on the gap between Hut 8 and Nebius, versus realizing that Nebius and all its peers will earn great returns on the GPUs they buy. So you have to choose whether you'll optimize for a great story about being a contrarian, or a less fun story about how you survived the grind.)

> **译文：** * 本周 Read.Haus 提出了一个非常有趣的问题：个股定价和更广泛的因子定价哪个更有效率？[https://www.thediff.co/r/3ca5ce2d?m=5ad25eba-11f2-4b23-b354-f63de0d99a2c]。这个问题真的很难回答！一方面：因子涵盖多种资产，如果每种资产的定价都相当合理，那么因子应该能从分散化中获益，定价也会更好。另一方面，在企业内部，人们往往既想考虑因子，又想完全避免接触这些因子：与其纠结于人工智能和伊朗战争是否真的重要，不如去弄清楚哪个人工智能或伊朗战争相关的投资标的定价存在偏差，这样可能更有利可图，尽管后者能带来更高的超额收益。完全有可能通过找出某个行业中估值过高的公司中估值最低的那家，同时对这个行业本身是否估值过高漠不关心，从而为市场带来巨大的效率提升。而且，行业或国家投资的夏普比率会低于行业内相对价值交易，原因很简单：前者样本量更大，更容易筛选出值得承担的风险。如果你想靠选股谋生，就不要理会大趋势，而是要密切关注哪些公司在大趋势中被过度或不足地反映出来。如果你想成为传奇，那就专注于趋势；这样你的故事会更精彩。 （但这同样也是市场效率的一部分。只不过，与其说你靠押注 Hut 8 和 Nebius 之间的价格差距赚了钱，不如说你意识到 Nebius 及其所有同行都会从他们购买的 GPU 中获得丰厚回报，这样更有意思。所以你必须做出选择：是追求一个关于逆向思维的精彩故事，还是追求一个关于你如何熬过漫长岁月的略显乏味的故事。）
