Hey there,

In the past year, it feels like Asia has bubbled over. News of major protests and uprisings came in waves. Civil unrest in Jakarta, Gen Zs rising up in Nepal, and anti-corruption protests in the Philippines. More recently, the Cockroach Janata Party saw more young folk in India demanding changes to the national exam system. Images of thousands taking to the streets dominated news coverage. Commentators chimed in—this is Gen Z’s moment, the end of an era, and so on.

> **译文：** 过去一年，亚洲仿佛沸腾起来。大规模抗议和起义的消息接踵而至：雅加达爆发了社会动荡，尼泊尔的Z世代奋起反抗，菲律宾爆发了反腐败抗议。最近，印度的“蟑螂人民党”也吸引了更多年轻人，他们要求改革国家考试制度。成千上万人走上街头的画面占据了新闻报道的头条。评论员们纷纷发表看法——这是Z世代的时刻，一个时代的终结，等等。

As a Singaporean, I admit these images of street protests can sit a little uneasily. In a country where protests require permits, or are reduced to a little patch of green called Hong Lim Park, protests feel risky and often also foreign–seemingly an imported “Western” ideal. But disagreement can find unique forms. Back in 2011, a Chinese family in Singapore complained [https://kawan.kontinentalist.com/r/1b6e5420?m=ea23cb80-9b47-4aa7-ad2c-da3cd6873059] about the strong curry smells that came from the home of their Indian neighbours; after mediation, the two families settled on the Indian family cooking only when their neighbours were out. In protest [https://kawan.kontinentalist.com/r/b0de71af?m=ea23cb80-9b47-4aa7-ad2c-da3cd6873059] of what seemed like intolerance, thousands of Singaporeans organised over social media and made curry in their homes on a weekend that same month. So did I.

> **译文：** 作为一名新加坡人，我承认这些街头抗议的画面确实让我感到有些不安。在这个国家，抗议活动需要获得许可，或者只能在芳林公园这片小小的绿地上进行，因此抗议活动显得既冒险又陌生——似乎是一种舶来的“西方”理念。但分歧也可以有其独特的表达方式。早在2011年，新加坡的一户华人家庭就曾抱怨印度邻居家飘出的浓烈咖喱味；经过调解，双方最终达成协议：印度邻居家只能在邻居外出时做饭。为了抗议这种看似不宽容的行为，成千上万的新加坡人在当月的某个周末通过社交媒体组织起来，在家做咖喱。我也参与了。

In preparing for this newsletter, I shared this anecdote with my colleagues. “Collective pettiness”, one of them quipped. I love the term for what it represents. Pettiness is what’s left when the room for disagreement is small. Thousands of pots of curry, their smell spreading through neighbourhoods. For a weekend, kitchens became a space for stubbornness, and solidarity with a minority community in Singapore.

> **译文：** 在准备这期简报时，我跟同事们分享了这个轶事。“集体斤斤计较”，其中一位打趣道。我喜欢这个词，因为它恰如其分地表达了这种感受。当分歧的空间所剩无几时，斤斤计较便成了唯一的选择。成千上万锅咖喱，香味弥漫整个街区。在一个周末，厨房成了固执己见和对新加坡少数族裔群体团结一致的地方。

Cooking a pot of curry costs nothing next to street protests, but disobedience takes different shapes. Tang ping–lying flat–in China to refuse competitive work environments. Women hanging up sarongs [https://kawan.kontinentalist.com/r/441431a7?m=ea23cb80-9b47-4aa7-ad2c-da3cd6873059] and using local superstition to scare military officers in Myanmar. Embracing a cockroach [https://kawan.kontinentalist.com/r/2d60a19b?m=ea23cb80-9b47-4aa7-ad2c-da3cd6873059] insult as the identity for a movement in India. While the constraints across the region are not shared, the wit of protestors endures, like a cockroach that cannot be stamped out.

> **译文：** 与街头抗议相比，煮一锅咖喱的成本微不足道，但反抗的形式却多种多样。在中国，人们平躺（唐平）以示对竞争性工作环境的拒绝。在缅甸，妇女们挂起纱笼，并利用当地的迷信来恐吓军官。在印度，人们甚至将蟑螂（蟑螂）这种带有侮辱性的词汇作为一场运动的象征。尽管该地区各地面临的限制不尽相同，但抗议者的智慧却像蟑螂一样顽强不屈，难以根除。

Doing less to disrupt societal expectations

Instead of marching or striking against the burden of societal expectations, there are young people in Asia who are protesting in a quieter way: by simply refusing to try. In China, tang ping or "lying flat" [https://kawan.kontinentalist.com/r/f8bd83a0?m=ea23cb80-9b47-4aa7-ad2c-da3cd6873059] means doing only the bare minimum at work and not chasing money or promotions. In South Korea, the "N-po generation" [https://kawan.kontinentalist.com/r/7a10e474?m=ea23cb80-9b47-4aa7-ad2c-da3cd6873059] takes this even further, giving up not just on hard work, but on dating, marriage, or owning a home. In Japan, the satori generation [https://kawan.kontinentalist.com/r/e2e9e289?m=ea23cb80-9b47-4aa7-ad2c-da3cd6873059] which literally means the “enlightened generation” follows similar patterns, where they show little interest in career advancement, materialism, and romantic relationships. These movements are part of a response to looming economic despair and the unsustainable working culture of the modern world.

> **译文：** 亚洲的一些年轻人并没有走上街头游行或罢工来反抗社会期望的重负，而是以一种更为静默的方式进行抗议：他们干脆拒绝尝试。在中国，“吃平”指的是工作中只做最低限度的工作，不追求金钱或晋升。在韩国，“N-po一代”则更进一步，他们不仅放弃努力工作，甚至放弃恋爱、婚姻和购房。在日本，被称为“觉悟世代”（satori generation）的群体（字面意思是“开悟的一代”）也遵循着类似的模式，他们对事业发展、物质主义和恋爱关系兴趣寥寥。这些运动是对迫在眉睫的经济困境和现代社会不可持续的工作文化的一种回应。
