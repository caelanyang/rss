The Briefing

By Martin Peers

Aug 26, 2026

Thanks for reading The Briefing, our nightly column where we break down the day’s news. If you like what you see, I encourage you to subscribe to our reporting here. Greetings! You couldn’t miss the disconnect in the AI discussion among major tech figures on Wednesday. Microsoft co-founder Bill Gates warned in a lengthy essay about the dangers posed by the technology, noting that he would support a global slowdown in AI advances if someone had a “credible plan” for doing so.

> **译文：**
> 感谢您阅读我们的每日新闻专栏“简报”，我们会在此为您解读当日新闻。如果您喜欢我们的内容，欢迎在此订阅。您好！周三，科技界主要人物在人工智能讨论中出现分歧，这一点显而易见。微软联合创始人比尔·盖茨在一篇长文中警告称，这项技术存在风险，并表示如果有人能提出“可信的计划”，他将支持全球放缓人工智能的发展速度。

Then Nvidia, the dominant seller of AI chips, reported that its revenue more than doubled in the July quarter to $96 billion. CEO Jensen Huang’s explanation: “AI has become useful.”Huang didn’t address Gates’ essay, but, as is usually the case, his commentary on the company’s earnings call emphasized AI's benefits for business. Much of those benefits currently accrue to Nvidia, of course, although its quarterly update demonstrated just how far it is going to help potential buyers of its chips.

> **译文：**
> 随后，人工智能芯片领域的领军企业英伟达公布，其7月份季度营收翻了一番多，达到960亿美元。首席执行官黄仁勋解释道：“人工智能已经变得非常有用。”黄仁勋没有回应盖茨的文章，但一如既往，他在公司财报电话会议上的评论强调了人工智能对商业的益处。当然，这些益处目前大多归英伟达所有，但其季度业绩更新也表明，人工智能将如何帮助其芯片的潜在买家。

On top of the investments it’s making in a variety of firms buying its chips or developing AI, and financial support for data center developments, and measures like this one, Nvidia in the July quarter also extended payment terms for some buyers of its chips.

> **译文：** 除了对购买其芯片或开发人工智能的各种公司进行投资，以及为数据中心发展提供财政支持，以及采取类似这样的措施外，英伟达在 7
> 月份的季度还延长了部分芯片买家的付款期限。

Nvidia said it allowed “certain investment-grade customers” to pay over 60 days rather than 45, contributing to a 55% increase in accounts receivable—amounts due from customers who have bought a product. (Who’s betting SpaceX is one of those investment-grade customers?) You have to wonder why on earth a company that supposedly has the money to buy Nvidia’s chips needs a little more time to make its payment.

> **译文：**
> 英伟达表示，他们允许“某些投资级客户”的付款期限从45天延长至60天，这导致应收账款（即已购买产品的客户应付的款项）增加了55%。（谁敢打赌SpaceX是这些投资级客户之一？）人们不禁要问，一家号称有钱购买英伟达芯片的公司，为什么还需要更多时间来付款呢？

Whatever the reason, this move cut Nvidia’s operating cash flow in half from the preceding quarter (more details on the quarter here).

> **译文：** 无论出于何种原因，此举使英伟达的运营现金流比上一季度减少了一半（有关该季度的更多详细信息，请点击此处）。

On the call, Nvidia finance chief Colette Kress addressed the widespread criticism that all of Nvidia’s moves to help its customers amount to “circular financing.” Nvidia’s view is that “we’re going through a major computing platform shift, the creation of one of the most important technologies in human history” and that the returns on the money invested will be “excellent, and our risk is limited,” she said.

> **译文：**
> 在电话会议上，英伟达首席财务官科莱特·克雷斯回应了外界对英伟达所有帮助客户的举措都等同于“循环融资”的广泛批评。她表示，英伟达的观点是“我们正在经历一场重大的计算平台变革，创造人类历史上最重要的技术之一”，而且投资回报将“非常可观，我们的风险也有限”。

Nvidia’s financial risk may be limited—that’s a matter for debate—but as Gates made clear, there’s also a risk that AI’s social harms will outweigh its benefits. Meta’s ConcessionsMeta Platforms’ legal settlement with the states suing it over alleged harm caused by its apps got headlines on Wednesday for the $18 billion price tag. In reality, that payout is the least important part of the agreement.

> **译文：**
> 英伟达的财务风险或许有限——这一点尚待商榷——但正如盖茨所明确指出的，人工智能的社会危害也可能超过其益处。Meta平台与各州就其应用程序涉嫌造成损害而达成的180亿美元和解协议，周三成为各大媒体的头条新闻。但实际上，这笔赔偿金只是协议中最不重要的部分。

Meta pays the money out over a decade (and only 70% is definite, with the rest depending on whether YouTube and TikTok make concessions of their own). Even if it makes all of the payment, that amounts to $1.8 billion a year. In the June quarter alone, Meta generated $32 billion in cash from operations. True, it is spending most of that on AI-related capital expenditures right now.

> **译文：**
> Meta将在十年内分期支付这笔款项（其中只有70%是确定的，其余部分取决于YouTube和TikTok是否做出让步）。即使Meta全额支付，每年也高达18亿美元。仅在6月份的季度，Meta的运营现金流就达到了320亿美元。诚然，目前Meta将其中大部分用于人工智能相关的资本支出。

But that doesn’t change the fact that this legal settlement isn’t expensive for the company in the bigger scheme of things. What could be meaningful are the changes in how Meta operates its apps. Notably, it agreed to turn on by default a number of measures aimed at limiting teen use of its apps. These include a two-hour daily time limit on teen use, an overnight block for teens, a muting of notifications during school hours, and the hiding of likes.

> **译文：**
> 但从长远来看，这笔法律和解费用对该公司而言并不算高昂。真正有意义的是Meta在运营其应用程序方面做出的改变。值得注意的是，该公司同意默认启用一系列旨在限制青少年使用其应用程序的措施。这些措施包括限制青少年每日使用时间不超过两小时、夜间屏蔽青少年使用权限、在校期间静音通知以及隐藏点赞记录。

Leaving these options on by default means users have to take action to turn them off. That’s a big deal. People are much less likely to turn something off that is already on, particularly if it requires going into settings and fiddling around to change it. As we reported in this 2023 story, Meta has previously shied away from giving teen-safety modifications a default status.

> **译文：**
> 默认开启这些选项意味着用户必须手动关闭它们。这很重要。人们不太可能去关闭已经开启的功能，尤其是需要进入设置并费一番功夫才能更改的情况下。正如我们在2023年的报道中所述，Meta此前一直避免将青少年安全设置设为默认开启状态。

In 2020, for instance, Instagram chief Adam Mosseri announced a plan to hide the number of like counts on Instagram, but he backed off on that. State attorneys general claim he did so because of concerns that such a move could reduce how often people visited Instagram and thereby hurt its ad revenue, we reported in that story. (For more on the legal implications of Meta’s settlement, see our story here).

> **译文：**
> 例如，2020年，Instagram首席执行官亚当·莫塞里宣布了一项隐藏Instagram点赞数的计划，但他后来放弃了这一计划。正如我们在当时的报道中所述，各州总检察长声称，他之所以放弃该计划，是因为他担心此举会降低用户访问Instagram的频率，从而损害其广告收入。（有关Meta和解协议的法律影响，请参阅我们之前的报道。）
