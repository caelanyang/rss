The West Seattle hillside home of Richard Rhodes, with views overlooking Puget Sound, is a good place to ponder the end of the world. Walk up a series of steps, through the front door and enter Rhodes’ home office, where his Pulitzer Prize hangs. He won it for his 1986 book “The Making of the Atomic Bomb,” a sprawling, 900-page account of the scientific discoveries that led to one of the greatest weapons ever created.

> **译文：**理查德·罗兹位于西雅图山坡上的家，可以俯瞰普吉特海湾，是思考世界末日的好地方。沿着台阶拾级而上，穿过前门，便可进入罗兹的家庭办公室，他的普利策奖奖杯就挂在那里。他凭借1986年出版的《原子弹的制造》一书荣获此奖，这部长达900页的巨著详细记述了促成人类历史上最强大武器之一——原子弹诞生的科学发现。

I made the trip to Rhodes’ house because his 40-year-old tome has become essential reading for employees at Anthropic and their leader, Dario Amodei, who counts it as one of his favorite books. Amodei has said he identifies with a central character in Rhodes’ book: Leo Szilard, the physicist who first conceived of the nuclear chain reaction and then aggressively advocated to keep it under human control.

> **译文：**我此行拜访罗兹，是因为他那本四十年前的巨著已成为安特罗皮克公司员工及其领导人达里奥·阿莫迪的必读书目，阿莫迪更是将其列为自己最喜欢的书籍之一。阿莫迪曾表示，他与罗兹书中的一位核心人物——物理学家利奥·西拉德——产生了共鸣。西拉德是第一个提出核链式反应理论的人，之后他积极倡导将核反应置于人类的控制之下。

The Anthropic CEO’s affinity for the historic account isn’t surprising if you read the profile of Amodei that my colleagues and I wrote this week. It traces how Amodei has long grappled with the weight of the technology he was building. He used air-gapped computers, machines disconnected from the internet, to compose early OpenAI memos to prevent remote hacking.

> **译文：**如果你读过我和同事本周撰写的关于阿莫迪的人物特写，就不会对Anthropic CEO对这段历史的偏爱感到惊讶了。文章追溯了阿莫迪长期以来如何应对他所开发的技术所带来的巨大压力。为了防止远程黑客攻击，他使用与互联网隔离的计算机（即物理隔离的计算机）来撰写早期的OpenAI备忘录。

He worried about getting kidnapped in China, a U.S. adversary he feared was after key AI research secrets. These days he concerns himself with the security of the data centers that house the clusters of chips training Anthropic’s next frontier-chasing model. He and his sister, Daniela, another Anthropic cofounder, grew up wanting “to save the world together,” Dario has said.

> **译文：**他曾担心在中国被绑架，因为他害怕中国这个美国的对手会觊觎人工智能研究的关键机密。如今，他关注的是数据中心的安全，这些数据中心承载着训练Anthropic下一代前沿模型的芯片集群。达里奥曾说过，他和他的妹妹丹妮拉（Anthropic的另一位联合创始人）从小就梦想着“一起拯救世界”。

Rhodes, 89, has never met Amodei. But he and his wife traveled to downtown San Francisco late last year to speak to Anthropic’s employee book club, which had ordered 100 copies of his book. Rhodes said he gave them a perspective that appears to cut against the “once-in-a-civilization” technology moment that Amodei and employees think they are building. Rhodes, who has also written about the history of technology changes, said he has watched the AI wave with a sense of déjà vu.

> **译文：**现年89岁的罗兹从未见过阿莫迪。但去年年底，他和妻子前往旧金山市中心，为Anthropic公司的员工读书俱乐部做演讲。该俱乐部订购了100本罗兹的书。罗兹说，他向员工们阐述的观点似乎与阿莫迪及其员工所认为的“百年一遇”的技术变革时刻相悖。罗兹也曾撰写过技术变革史，他说，他看着人工智能浪潮的到来，心中却涌起一种似曾相识的感觉。

“The first thing that struck me most of all was what always strikes me with new technologies: the moral panic side. The general sense that this is going to destroy the world. It’s come up every time,” he told me. He went back in history. When 17th century Brits started using coal as fuel for the first time, preachers called it the “devil’s excrement,” he recalled. The rise of the automobile, last century, also stirred protests, he said.

> **译文：**“最让我震惊的，也是每次新技术出现时都会出现的：道德恐慌。人们普遍认为这项技术会毁灭世界。这种感觉每次都会出现，”他告诉我。他回顾了历史。他回忆说，17世纪英国人第一次使用煤炭作为燃料时，牧师们称之为“魔鬼的粪便”。他还说，上个世纪汽车的兴起也引发了抗议。

“We get a new technology. It has its dark side and its bright side, and we slowly surround it with social, legal, moral, cultural controls until we turn it into a human artifact,” he said.

> **译文：**他说：“我们获得了一项新技术。它既有阴暗面，也有光明面，我们慢慢地用社会、法律、道德和文化方面的控制措施来约束它，直到把它变成人类的产物。”

That might not be a stance that Amodei and his employees agree with. Amodei has pegged a 25% chance of AI going “really, really badly” for civilization. A big part of Anthropic’s technological and commercial success stems from the employees’ and Amodei’s deep belief that they are important, historic actors in an existential scientific historical moment. Its sensitive safety culture is an essential part of it.

> **译文：**阿莫迪和他的员工可能并不认同这种观点。阿莫迪认为人工智能有25%的概率会对人类文明造成“非常非常严重的灾难”。Anthropic公司在技术和商业上取得的巨大成功，很大程度上源于员工和阿莫迪深信自己是这个关乎人类存亡的科学历史时刻中的重要参与者。而公司高度重视安全文化，正是这种信念的重要组成部分。

Maybe they’re right, particularly as a series of cybersecurity attacks from AI agents are raising alarm bells recently, and the rest of us will be glad we had such responsible (and newly wealthy) stewards along the way.

> **译文：**也许他们是对的，尤其是最近一系列来自人工智能代理的网络安全攻击敲响了警钟，我们其他人也会庆幸一路走来有这样负责任（且新近致富）的管理者。

Rhodes, meanwhile, has a different, perhaps more optimistic perspective. Rhodes is now working on a work of fiction about robots who take over the world, and all that’s left on Earth is a group of children. But the book is a comedy. “The robots are so stupid and the children are so bright,” he said.—Cory Weinberg

> **译文：**与此同时，罗兹则持有另一种或许更为乐观的观点。他目前正在创作一部关于机器人统治世界的虚构作品，故事讲述的是一群孩子留在地球上的故事。但这本书是一部喜剧。“机器人很愚蠢，孩子们却很聪明，”他说道。——科里·温伯格

Apple’s ‘Confidential’ Files

On Monday of this week, Aaron Tilley wrote a colorful story for us that sought to clear up a mystery: Why do so many former Apple employees end up with access to confidential Apple documents after they leave the company? The answer, he reported, is that the company encourages new hires to mingle their personal and workplace digital identities using iCloud. That practice makes it hard for them to expunge all of their Apple files when they leave the company.

> **译文：**本周一，Aaron Tilley 为我们撰写了一篇精彩的文章，试图解开一个谜团：为什么这么多苹果前员工离职后还能访问苹果的机密文件？他指出，答案在于苹果鼓励新员工使用 iCloud 将个人和工作相关的数字身份混用。这种做法使得他们在离职时很难彻底删除所有苹果文件。

The story was prescient. In the days that followed its publication, an OpenAI blog post and filing in its legal battle with Apple described the very same iCloud issues at the heart of Aaron’s story. OpenAI argued that those issues, among other things, undermine Apple’s accusations that two of its former employees stole trade secrets when they went to work for OpenAI.

> **译文：**这篇报道颇具先见之明。在报道发表后的几天里，OpenAI在其博客文章和与苹果公司的法律诉讼文件中，详细描述了与Aaron报道中核心问题完全相同的iCloud问题。OpenAI辩称，这些问题以及其他一些因素，都削弱了苹果公司对其两名前员工在加入OpenAI后窃取商业机密的指控。

“For years, Apple encouraged employees to use personal iCloud accounts for work, intermingling company and personal data, while failing to manage access cleanly across multiple Apple systems when employees departed,” OpenAI wrote in a motion to dismiss Apple’s suit. “Those practices created precisely the confusion and unwanted access issues that Apple now characterizes as intentional misappropriation.”

> **译文：**OpenAI在一份驳回苹果诉讼的动议中写道：“多年来，苹果鼓励员工使用个人iCloud账户处理工作，将公司数据和个人数据混杂在一起，却未能妥善管理员工离职时跨多个苹果系统的访问权限。这些做法恰恰造成了苹果现在所称的故意挪用行为所导致的混乱和不必要的访问权限问题。”

Apple previously told Aaron the iCloud issues had nothing to do with its allegations in the OpenAI case. We’ll see as the case plays out. Meantime, maybe don’t intermingle your work and personal online accounts just to be safe.—Nick Wingfield

> **译文：**苹果公司此前告诉亚伦，iCloud 的问题与它在 OpenAI 案件中的指控无关。我们将拭目以待案件的进展。与此同时，为了安全起见，或许最好不要将工作和个人在线账户混用。——尼克·温菲尔德

Listening: “What to Carry, What to Burn”

Blair Braverman has enjoyed her fair share of adventures—racing in dog-sledding treks like the Canadian Challenge (300 miles), the Kobuk (440 miles) and in the granddaddy competition of mushing, the Iditarod (1,049 miles).

> **译文：**布莱尔·布雷弗曼 (Blair Braverman) 经历了不少冒险——参加过加拿大挑战赛 (300 英里)、科布克 (440 英里) 等狗拉雪橇比赛，以及狗拉雪橇比赛的鼻祖——艾迪塔罗德 (1,049 英里)。

Braverman, a contributor to The New York Times, Outside magazine and other publications, is also pretty good at recounting other people’s adventures, which she does in her new podcast, “What to Carry, What to Burn.” And it is a testament to her innate likability as a storyteller that she does the pod mostly as a solo narrator—just her recounting various tales of perilous, remarkable survival.

> **译文：**布雷弗曼是《纽约时报》、《户外》杂志和其他出版物的撰稿人，她也很擅长讲述别人的冒险经历，她在新播客节目“携带什么，烧掉什么”中就讲述了这些故事。她作为一名讲故事的人天生就很有魅力，所以这个播客节目大部分时间都是她独自一人讲述各种惊险刺激、令人难忘的生存故事。

So far this has included an early 20th century Arctic expedition to Wrangel Island, a trek that involved, truly, an unpaid internship from hell; a 1970s plane crash in the Sierra Nevadas; and the Salvadoran fisherman lost for 13 months in the Pacific Ocean a decade ago, who was later sued for cannibalism (a charge he denied).

> **译文：**到目前为止，这其中包括 20 世纪初前往弗兰格尔岛的北极探险，这是一次名副其实的无薪地狱式实习；20 世纪 70 年代在内华达山脉发生的飞机坠毁事件；以及十年前在太平洋失踪 13 个月的萨尔瓦多渔民，他后来因食人罪被起诉（他否认了这一指控）。

I appreciated the little details that only a seasoned explorer like Braverman could add to the episodes, like the fact that well-made igloos can indeed get pretty toasty (well past 75 degrees!) and that bear just tastes pretty similar to beef.—Abram Brown

> **译文：**我非常欣赏像布雷弗曼这样经验丰富的探险家才能在节目中添加的那些小细节，比如建造精良的冰屋确实可以非常温暖（远超75华氏度！），以及熊肉的味道和牛肉非常相似。——艾布拉姆·布朗

Reading: “The Secret Dinner” by Raphael Montes

As the world’s tenth largest economy, Brazil exports great quantities of soybeans, coffee and sugar. It has also introduced the world to many other wonderful things, like caipirinhas.

> **译文：**作为世界第十大经济体，巴西出口大量的黄豆、咖啡和糖。它还向世界介绍了许多其他美好的事物，比如凯匹林纳鸡尾酒。

But there’s something else that the country has largely managed to quietly keep and enjoy to itself: the clever, demented talent of thriller-horror writer Raphael Montes, who has been a mainstay on Brazilian bestseller lists for over a decade but remains a non-entity abroad. “People have told me that if I were American, I‘d be a millionaire by now—and I just tell them, ‘Yeah, but then I wouldn’t have Carnival,’” Montes told Folha de São Paulo, Brazil’s largest newspaper, last year. “When a Brazilian author gets translated, their defining trait in the eyes of foreigners is usually just being ‘exotic.’ What I would truly love is to be recognized globally simply as a great thriller author—who happens to be Brazilian.”

> **译文：**但巴西还有一样东西，却一直悄悄地被自己人所珍视：那就是惊悚恐怖小说作家拉斐尔·蒙特斯（Raphael Montes）那令人叹为观止、略带疯狂的才华。十多年来，他的作品一直是巴西畅销书榜上的常客，但在海外却鲜为人知。“有人跟我说，如果我是美国人，现在肯定已经是百万富翁了——我只能告诉他们，‘是啊，但那样我就没法过狂欢节了，’”蒙特斯去年接受巴西发行量最大的报纸《圣保罗页报》（Folha de São Paulo）采访时说道。“当一位巴西作家的作品被翻译成其他语言时，外国人眼中他最显著的特点通常就是‘异域风情’。我真正渴望的是，能够被全世界认可为一位伟大的惊悚小说作家——碰巧我是一位巴西作家。”

His wish may well come true very soon. The 2016 book that first cemented his fame in Brazil, “The Secret Dinner,” has been translated into English for the first time and debuts in the U.S. on Aug. 18. As a gruesome dark comedy, I do not suggest reading it while eating.

> **译文：**他的愿望很可能很快就会实现。2016年让他一举成名的小说《秘密晚餐》首次被翻译成英文，并将于8月18日在美国上市。作为一部黑色幽默的惊悚小说，我不建议在用餐时阅读。

The novel’s title refers to a scheme hatched by four broke young men in Rio de Janeiro: catering an ultra-luxe cannibalistic dinner. (It’s “Rope” meets “Sweeney Todd” meets “Chef’s Table.”) Initially, they envision the soiree as a one-off ploy. But as you might expect, they attract a crowd of unsavory types, including a desperate heir of a prominent family who sees the evening as a great opportunity for capitalistic expansion, with himself as the, well—venture investor.

> **译文：**小说的标题指的是四个身无分文的里约热内卢年轻人策划的一个阴谋：举办一场极致奢华的食人晚宴。（这部小说融合了《绳索》、《理发师陶德》和《主厨的餐桌》的元素。）起初，他们只是把这场晚宴当作一次性的噱头。但不出所料，他们吸引了一群形形色色的不法之徒，其中包括一位出身名门、急于求成的继承人，他将这场晚宴视为扩张资本的绝佳机会，而他自己则扮演着——风险投资人的角色。

Soon, he has strong-armed the boys into running a thriving underground supper club. One of the foursome, Miguel, is a gourmet sous-chef, so he does the cooking. (Where Mrs. Lovett had her pies, Miguel prepares braises, terrines and ossobuco.) The narrator, Dante, an underemployed bookshop clerk with a fondness for weed and Klonopin, takes up maitre d’ duties. The butchering, meanwhile, falls to a chainsaw-wielding girlfriend.

> **译文：**很快，他就强迫这几个男孩经营起一家生意兴隆的地下餐馆。四人中的一个，米格尔，是个美食家副厨，所以他负责烹饪。（洛维特太太吃馅饼的地方，米格尔做的是炖菜、肉冻和炖牛膝。）叙述者但丁，一个工作不顺的书店店员，喜欢吸食大麻和服用氯硝西泮，承担起了餐厅领班的职责。与此同时，屠宰的活儿则落到了他挥舞着电锯的女友身上。

In depicting the wealthy of Rio as rapacious man-eaters, Montes makes obvious his distaste for how his country has grasped for prosperous modernity: Brazil, with its enormous economy, has the third largest disparity between rich and poor on the planet. (“Money is all that matters,” the villainous heir says at one point, practically twirling his moustache. “Nobody gives a damn about the rest.”) To Montes’ credit, he manages to make the point and still delivers a taut page-turner, one complete with a twist that hits like a double-strength caipirinha.—A.B.

> **译文：**蒙特斯将里约热内卢的富人描绘成贪婪的食人魔，这无疑表达了他对巴西追求繁荣现代化的厌恶：巴西拥有庞大的经济，却也是全球贫富差距第三大的国家。（书中那个邪恶的继承人曾一边捻着胡子一边说：“金钱至上，其他的一切都无关紧要。”）值得称赞的是，蒙特斯成功地表达了这一观点，同时又创作了一部情节紧凑、引人入胜的小说，结尾的反转如同双倍劲道的凯匹林纳鸡尾酒般令人震惊。——A.B.

Watching: “Arco”

Many films correspond to a specific time of year. Christmas movies abound, and the scaries populate October. “Ferris Bueller’s Day Off”? Back to school. “The Graduate?” End of school.

> **译文：**许多电影都与特定的季节相契合。圣诞电影层出不穷，恐怖片则在十月盛行。《春天不是读书天》？开学季。《毕业生》？学期结束。

“Arco,” an imaginatively animated feature film from 38-year-old French director Ugo Bienvenu, corresponds to a point on the calendar with increasingly eerie, grim importance: forest fire season, making it a timely watch. (It initially came out last year, caught an Oscar nomination and recently got to Disney+.) The titular character, Arco (voiced by Juliano Krue Valdi in the English dub), is a boy from our far-distant future—the year 2932—where mankind has mastered time travel. It involves Superman-esque flight, with rainbows left in a time-traveler’s wake just as real-life planes emit plumes of white.

> **译文：**由38岁的法国导演乌戈·比安维努执导的动画长片《阿科》充满奇思妙想，恰逢森林火灾季，可谓应景之作。（该片最初于去年上映，获得奥斯卡提名，最近登陆Disney+。）片名主角阿科（英文配音由朱利亚诺·克鲁·瓦尔迪担任）来自遥远的未来——2932年，那时人类已经掌握了时间旅行技术。影片中，时间旅行者可以像超人一样飞行，身后留下彩虹，正如现实生活中的飞机尾迹会喷出白色烟雾一样。

But Arco hasn’t learned how to fly, and when he impishly steals his older sister’s flying suit, he crashes in our near-distant future—the year 2075. In the accident, he loses the suit’s power source, a diamond-shaped crystal; desperate to find it, he enlists the help of Iris (Romy Fay), a girl around his same age. They are pursued by a trio of stooges, Dougie, Stewie and Frankie (Will Ferrell, Andy Samberg, the musician Flea), who previously witnessed another person traveling via rainbow and want proof that time travelers exist.

2075 is no high-water mark for humanity, and the adventure is made more dire when a forest fire engulfs Iris’ suburban town, and the pair flee along with Iris’ robot au pair, Mikki, who has been raising her in her parents’ absence. In this dystopia, adults do not do remote work—they do remote parenting: With their office in a distant city, Iris’ mother and father check in on her occasionally via hologram. (They’re voiced by Mark Ruffalo and Natalie Portman, and in an inventive touch that seems entirely like something that might actually happen in future robotics, the film uses a digital blend of their voices for Mikki—the nanny bot a combination of father and mother.)

“Arco” issues many of the stern environmentalist warnings sounded by Studio Ghibli films, and like a Miyazaki film, the high emotions and dramatic stakes of “Arco” give it a distinctly grown-up register, presenting a prismatic-colored fable that winds to a poignant denouement.—A.B.
