> **译文：*** 财务规划与分析（FP&A）如同遥测与反间谍——良好的财务预测无法拯救一家濒临破产的公司，对于一家足够优秀、不易出错的企业来说，也起不到决定性作用。但有些类型的公司，如果能够敏锐地把握客户的价值、获取客户的成本以及这些数字随时间推移的演变趋势，就能从中获益匪浅。

* Media Incentives—The Twitter algorithm competes with Twitter accounts that repost other people's material. It's very hard to beat them, but a good first step is to stop subsidizing them.
> **译文：*** 媒体激励——推特算法会与转发他人内容的推特账号竞争。虽然很难战胜它们，但停止补贴它们是很好的第一步。

> **译文：*** 基础设施——为行为良好的机器人构建基础设施。

> **译文：*** 福利——威利·旺卡原则：如果你拥有某种独特的东西，以至于难以衡量其市场价格，那就把获得它的机会变成一种抽奖，每一次对你有价值的经济互动都相当于一张彩票。

FP&A as the Telemetry and Counterespionage

The two most common grades a company gets for managing its finances are an F and a gentleman's C. A company gets a failing grade if it had a reasonable business that could have turned a profit, but managed to finance it the wrong way so it ran out of cash. And there are many companies for which you can say all sorts of positive and negative things about their business, and where the best thing you can say about their capital allocation is that they didn't mess up too badly—they didn't do any ill-advised mergers, they gracefully responded to diminishing opportunities to invest by returning more capital to shareholders, they didn't structure some part of the business so GAAP profits were misleadingly front-loaded while later costs more than devoured those gains, and they weren't buying back stock at $30 and issuing it two years later at $5.[1]
> **译文：**公司财务管理最常见的两个评分是F和勉强及格的C。如果一家公司拥有合理的业务，原本可以盈利，但却因为融资方式错误而导致现金流枯竭，那么它就会被评为不及格。而对于很多公司，你可以对他们的业务做出各种各样的评价，但对他们资本配置最好的评价就是：他们没有犯太大的错误——他们没有进行任何不明智的并购，他们优雅地应对投资机会减少的情况，将更多资本返还给股东，他们没有通过结构性地安排业务，导致GAAP利润被误导性地提前计入，而后期成本却远远超过了这些收益，他们也没有以每股30美元的价格回购股票，然后在两年后以每股5美元的价格发行。[1]

And then there’s companies for whom granular financial analysis—at the level of products or end customers—is the defining feature of the business. There are many companies for which the gap between sounding good as a concept and actually working as a business comes down to these granular questions: what fraction of first-time customers convert into regulars, and how do their margins evolve over time?
> **译文：**还有一些公司，对产品或终端客户层面的细致财务分析是其业务的决定性特征。许多公司面临的困境在于，其理念听起来不错，但实际运营起来却并非如此，关键在于这些细致的问题：有多少首次购买的客户会转化为回头客？他们的利润率如何随时间变化？

How long do people stay subscribed, what makes them leave, and how will they react when prices change? When this big piece of capital equipment gets sold, how much will the customer spend on maintenance, what margin will that produce, and how likely are they to switch to some cheaper provider?
> **译文：**用户订阅时长如何？什么原因导致他们取消订阅？价格变动时他们会作何反应？当这台大型设备售出后，客户需要支付多少维护费用？维护费用能带来多少利润？他们转投更便宜的供应商的可能性有多大？

How is foot traffic likely to change over the duration of this lease—and, if whoever's getting that lease drives additional foot traffic to other nearby locations and increases the value of nearby real estate, how airtight can the case for this be?
> **译文：**在此租赁期内，人流量可能会发生怎样的变化？如果获得该租赁权的人能够为附近其他地点带来更多人流量，并提高附近房地产的价值，那么这种租赁的理由是否站得住脚？

In one sense, every company faces these problems, but can't necessarily unbundle these decisions from everyday management choices. Professional services companies of many types have unit economic constraints, but they don't have the sample size necessary to do especially sophisticated modeling, and sometimes other strategic imperatives make the choice for them.
> **译文：**从某种意义上说，每家公司都面临这些问题，但它们未必能将这些决策与日常管理选择完全割裂开来。许多类型的专业服务公司都受到单位经济效益的限制，但它们缺乏进行特别复杂建模所需的样本量，有时其他战略要务会替它们做出选择。

If an accounting firm decides it's not going to audit shakier companies, it's also choosing a longer sales cycle, better customer retention (in the two senses that big companies don't like to change auditors and in the sense that big companies are less likely to go bankrupt), access to better talent, etc.
> **译文：**如果一家会计师事务所决定不再审计那些状况不佳的公司，那么它也选择了更长的销售周期、更好的客户留存率（从两个方面来说，大公司不喜欢更换审计师，而且大公司破产的可能性也更小）、获得更好的人才等等。

And if that accounting firm decides it's going to cross-sell consulting, that process is also going to be naturally fuzzier, with slower feedback, and subject to constraints that don't neatly fit into a spreadsheet.[2] These businesses can't be completely indifferent to unit economics, but they're usually not thinking "let's preserve our net dollar retention." They're thinking "uh-oh, we just lost the Walmart account."
> **译文：**如果会计事务所决定交叉销售咨询服务，那么这个过程自然也会更加模糊，反馈速度更慢，并且会受到一些无法用电子表格精确计算的限制。[2] 这些企业不可能完全忽视单位经济效益，但他们通常不会想着“我们要保持净留存率”，而是会想着“糟了，我们丢了沃尔玛的客户”。

Whereas if you visit Amazon's office the day after somebody canceled their Prime subscription, it's not so funereal. Customers come and go, on average, though of course Amazon wants them to stick around.
> **译文：**然而，如果你在有人取消Prime会员订阅的第二天去亚马逊办公室，就不会觉得气氛那么压抑。顾客来来往往，虽然亚马逊当然希望他们留下来。

But, at a deep level, Amazon's retail business can work backward from the question of: what does a new user spend, how does this spending ramp up, and what are the ways to make them start a larger fraction of their shopping efforts at Amazon and to add a few more things to the cart when they're done?
> **译文：**但从更深层次上看，亚马逊的零售业务可以从以下问题入手：新用户消费多少，消费额如何增长，以及如何让他们在亚马逊上开始更大比例的购物，并在购物完成后向购物车中添加更多商品？

Solving for this underwrites investments in new fulfillment centers, ads, new product lines, inference spending on their AI shopping assistant, and all the rest. (There are problems to solve on the merchant acquisition side, too, but every time customer count goes up by 1 or the net present value of the contribution margin from the typical customer goes up by $0.01, that problem gets easier to solve.)
> **译文：**解决这个问题就能为投资新建物流中心、广告、新产品线、人工智能购物助手等方面提供资金支持。（商家获客方面也存在一些问题需要解决，但每当客户数量增加1个，或者典型客户的贡献毛利净现值增加0.01美元时，这个问题就更容易解决。）

In this wonderful Eugene Wei essay , which is about data visualization in roughly the same sense that Jurassic Park is a story about sometimes it's hard to take kids to the zoo, there's a wonderful line about this:
> **译文：**在尤金·魏这篇精彩的文章中（这篇文章探讨的是数据可视化，其意义大致与《侏罗纪公园》讲述的“有时带孩子去动物园很困难”的故事类似），有一句关于此的精彩论述：

> Late one night in 1997, a few days after I had started, and about my third or fourth time reading the most recent edition of the Analytics Package, a monthly report on every detail of Amazon's financial performance, mostly consisting of a few hundred graphs cover to back, I knew our hidden truth: all the naysaying about Amazon's profitless business model was a lie. Every dollar of our profit we didn't reinvest into the business, and every dollar we didn't raise from investors to add to that investment, would be just kneecapping ourselves. The only governor of our potential was the breadth of our ambition.
> **译文：**1997 年的一个深夜，在我入职几天后，我大概是第三遍或第四遍阅读最新一期的分析报告——这份月度报告详细记录了亚马逊的财务业绩，几乎全是几百张图表——我终于明白了我们隐藏的真相：所有关于亚马逊不盈利商业模式的质疑都是谎言。我们每赚一美元利润，如果不再投资到公司，不从投资者那里筹集一美元来增加投资，就等于是在扼杀我们自己的发展。限制我们潜力的唯一因素，就是我们雄心壮志的广度。

Financial markets in the 90s were capable of underwriting businesses that continuously reinvested in order to grow. This being the era of 15% annualized earnings per share growth targets and multiples far too high to make that achievable through buybacks alone, every growth company was reinvesting something. But if they were buying something tangible, it showed up in the P&L as depreciation.
> **译文：**上世纪90年代的金融市场能够为那些持续再投资以实现增长的企业提供融资。当时，每股收益年化增长目标高达15%，而市盈率也高得离谱，单靠股票回购根本无法实现这一目标，因此，每家成长型公司都会进行再投资。但如果他们购买的是实物资产，那么这些投资就会在损益表中体现为折旧。

Amazon's investments were economically similar, and had different accounting treatment for good reason, but they meant that the economic engine of the company was described to investors qualitatively, and quantification involved repeat-order share rather than pure cohort math. And an explicit plan to aggressively invest in the business in order to earn profits in the future was a pitch made by many other companies that ultimately didn't do that.
> **译文：**亚马逊的投资在经济上与其他公司类似，其会计处理方式也各有不同，这其中自有其道理。但这意味着，公司向投资者描述其经济引擎时，侧重于定性分析，量化指标则侧重于复购率，而非纯粹的用户群体数据。此外，许多其他公司也曾提出过类似的计划，即积极投资业务以期在未来盈利，但最终却未能实现。

Amazon did tell investors this, but on a lag: for example, the headline for their Q4 1999 earnings release was Amazon.com Announces Profitability In U.S.-Based Book Sales, Financial Results for Fourth Quarter 1999 ; solve for solvency, optimize around cohorts, just as it should be. (But a cynic could have pointed out that a high fixed-cost retailer trumpeting the profitability of a subset of their business during the busiest shopping season of the year was a great way to downplay the fact that operating losses had risen from $18m to $175m year-over-year.)[3]
> **译文：**亚马逊确实告诉了投资者这一点，但有所滞后：例如，其 1999 年第四季度财报的标题是“Amazon.com 宣布美国图书销售实现盈利，1999 年第四季度财务业绩”；解决偿付能力问题，围绕客户群体进行优化，这才是应有的做法。（但愤世嫉俗的人可能会指出，一家高固定成本的零售商在一年中最繁忙的购物季大肆宣扬其部分业务的盈利能力，这巧妙地掩盖了其运营亏损同比从 1800 万美元增至 1.75 亿美元的事实。）[3]

From Amazon's perspective, the GAAP-to-reality gap was a blessing for as long as investors believed in the long-term vision: they had to know that the cost of acquiring new customers would rise over time, that finding the right product for someone is easier if you know what they've bought before, that brands accumulate value over time, and brands that thoroughly solve a recurring problem tend to become verbs in a consumer’s lexicon.[4] Knowing all that, and knowing that investors were willing to underwrite the general model without asking too much about the specifics, it was perfectly sensible for them to keep things vague.
> **译文：**从亚马逊的角度来看，只要投资者相信其长期愿景，GAAP与实际情况之间的差距就是一件好事：他们必须知道，随着时间的推移，获取新客户的成本会上升；如果你知道某人以前买过什么，就更容易找到适合他们的产品；品牌会随着时间的推移积累价值；而那些彻底解决反复出现的问题的品牌往往会成为消费者词汇中的动词。[4] 了解了所有这些，并且知道投资者愿意在不过多询问细节的情况下认可这一总体模型，因此，他们保持模糊不清的做法是完全明智的。

There's no sense in sharing their master plan to capture just one market and risk inspiring other people to be the Amazon of DVDs, electronics, and other categories they entered. The people who figured this out were either trying to reverse-engineer Amazon's survival and growth after the dot-com collapse, or licking their wounds and trying to understand how Amazon had beaten them.
> **译文：**分享他们只为占领一个市场的宏伟计划毫无意义，反而可能启发其他人效仿，成为DVD、电子产品以及其他他们涉足领域的亚马逊。那些弄明白这一点的人，要么是试图逆向工程亚马逊在互联网泡沫破灭后生存和发展的成功模式，要么是在舔舐伤口，试图弄明白亚马逊是如何击败他们的。

This wasn't the only problem Amazon had to solve—they were an early enough dot-com that the list of ecommerce-related technical problems they had to deal with was pretty close to "all of them except inventing Unix, C, and Perl." But a keen awareness of the importance of cohort economics, and a ruthless commitment to growing the size of the new ones and the wallet share of the rest of them, was basically what kept Amazon's technology, logistics, and customer service engines humming at full efficiency for so long.
> **译文：**这并非亚马逊唯一需要解决的问题——作为一家早期互联网公司，他们要处理的电商相关技术问题几乎涵盖了“除发明Unix、C和Perl之外的所有问题”。但正是对群体经济重要性的敏锐洞察，以及对扩大新用户群体规模和提升现有用户消费份额的坚定承诺，才使得亚马逊的技术、物流和客户服务体系能够长期高效运转。

A company is in a good position if they're able to spend incremental money on growth and the net present value of the resulting contribution profit is meaningfully higher than what they spent. They're in a great position if the way this shakes out in their financials is that they look like a money furnace, and people assume that competing with them means being better at snookering investors rather than doing whatever it is that the company does.[5]
> **译文：**如果一家公司能够将增量资金投入增长，并且由此产生的贡献利润的净现值显著高于其投入的资金，那么这家公司就处于有利地位。如果最终财务报表显示该公司就像一个烧钱的火炉，人们认为与该公司竞争意味着要更擅长欺骗投资者，而不是去做该公司正在做的事情，那么这家公司就处于非常有利的地位。[5]

All this is not to say that making decisions informed by detailed quantitative analysis is always strictly a good thing. When Bob Iger joined Disney by way of the ABC/Capital Cities acquisition, he was increasingly annoyed to find out that many of his decisions were second-guessed by Disney's planning team, which imposed a slow and uncertain veto on decisions that could have been made directly by business operators.
> **译文：**但这并非意味着基于详细定量分析做出的决策总是绝对的好事。鲍勃·伊格尔通过收购ABC/Capital Cities加入迪士尼后，越来越恼火的是，他发现自己的许多决策都会受到迪士尼规划团队的质疑，该团队对原本可以由业务运营人员直接做出的决策施加了缓慢且不确定的否决权。

GE is a more pathological example, where planning aggressive targets around unrealistic quarterly goals encouraged the business to repeatedly make value-destructive decisions. Planning and finance are useful functions, but just like any other abstraction, they can swallow a company whole. GE's model looked amazing for a while: sell durable equipment, turn that into a predictable stream of maintenance revenue, turn the sale itself into recurring revenue by helping to finance it, etc. But once they had a big enough finance division, there was no end to the number of ideas that looked good on paper, and the returns from esoteric lending are more stable quarter to quarter than the returns from selling consumer durables or capital goods. But not every quarter is any given quarter, and GE ended up being an over-levered specialty credit hedge fund with a few manufacturing businesses bolted on.
> **译文：**通用电气（GE）就是一个更极端的例子。围绕不切实际的季度目标制定激进的计划，导致公司不断做出破坏价值的决策。规划和财务固然重要，但就像任何其他抽象概念一样，它们也可能吞噬整个公司。GE的商业模式一度看起来非常出色：销售耐用设备，将其转化为可预测的维护收入，再通过提供融资服务将销售本身转化为经常性收入等等。但一旦财务部门规模足够大，各种纸面上看似美好的想法就会层出不穷，而且与销售耐用消费品或资本货物相比，来自特殊贷款业务的收益似乎更加稳定。然而，每个季度的情况都不尽相同，最终GE变成了一家过度杠杆化的专业信贷对冲基金，外加几家制造业企业。

FP&A is a kind of business telemetry tool, which, like any such tool, is a lot more valuable when it aggregates lots of backwards-looking information in order to inform future decisions. Which means it's one of those tricky information problems where the meta problem is figuring out how much you can be certain of. When it works well, it's partly a sign that a company has a good model that hasn't yet developed into a visibly good business.[6] So beyond the pass/fail binary, FP&A is particularly effective at helping stealthily great businesses maximize their lead before anyone catches on. It's a weird mix: a lot of finance, a fair amount of strategy, and, in the end, a company that gets an A+ for effective counterintelligence.
> **译文：**财务规划与分析（FP&A）是一种商业遥测工具，与其他此类工具一样，当它能够汇总大量回顾性信息以指导未来决策时，其价值会大大提升。这意味着它属于那种棘手的信息问题，其根本问题在于如何确定信息的准确性。当FP&A运作良好时，部分原因在于它表明公司拥有良好的商业模式，但尚未发展成为一家显而易见的成功企业。[6] 因此，FP&A的作用远不止于简单的通过/失败，它尤其擅长帮助那些默默耕耘的优秀企业在被他人察觉之前最大限度地扩大领先优势。这是一种奇特的组合：大量的财务知识、相当多的战略考量，最终成就了一家在有效反情报方面堪称典范的公司。
